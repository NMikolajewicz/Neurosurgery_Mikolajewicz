Procedure:
R mini craniotomy for SDH evacuation
SDH
Preamble
74M, visiting from India, on no anticoagulation presenting with confusion, some postural imbalance, and left-sided weakness who was found to have a right sided SDH (maximal thickness 2.9 cm) with a remote cerebellar infarct. MAterial risk discussed, etc. He was taken to OR for R mini craniotomy for SDH evacuation.
Procedure.
After induction of general anesthesia and endotracheal intubation, the patient's head was positioned into the crutch. We turned the head to the right side with the left malar eminence to be the highest point.  We shaved the hair and planned a curvilinear incision crossing the superior temporal line. The area was then prepped and draped in a sterile fashion. A skin incision was made using a 20 blade and dissection down to bone using the monopolar electrocautery. We dissected the periosteum as well as the superficial superior portion of the temporalis muscle. Retractor was placed and then we placed an anterior superior bur hole before turning our bone flap. Craniotomy was then completed using the craniotome. We coagulated the dura and placed surgicel into the epidural space. We opened the dura in a cruciate fashion using a 15 blade. We were able to preserve the underlying outer membrane of the subdural hematoma. Dural tack-ups were placed. We then coagulated the outer membrane in a circumferential manner, excising the portion to allow us to further coagulate the free edges. The subdural hematoma came out as a liquid and motor oil consistency in keeping with a chronic hematoma. We were coagulated a the inner membrane representing a located portion of the SDH, which allowed us to further evacuate the underlying SDH. The compartment was vigorously irrigate in the subdural space until the fluid started to run clear. We then tunneled a JP drain and placed this into the subdural space under direct vision. We reapproximated the dural leaflets and then used a Duramatrix to recreate the dura. Cranial flap was placed and fixed using cranial plates. Myocutaneous flap was then closed in the standard multilayer fashion with 3-0 Vicryl for the galea
and staples for the skin. At the end of the case all counts were correct. The patient woke up without any acute complications.

Assistant: Dr. Nicholas Mikolajewicz Anesthesia: General  Preamble: Patient is a 46 female presenting with R temporoparietal bony lesion. It appeared 4 years ago and grew slowly. CT revealed calvarial hemangioma. She was seen in clinic by Dr. Da Costa and plan was for resection by neurosurgery and subsequent reconstruction with Plastics. She presented to SBK on Sept 18 for elective surgery.   Description of procedure: Patient was positioned supine and head was secured on a crutch. Surgical site was prepped and draped in usual fasion. Skin, soft tissue and periosteal dissection was performed by plastic surgery, as described in their operative note. The intraosseous lesion was visualized directly. Healthy bone surrounding the lesion was grossly identified and demarkaded that boundaries of the right temporoparietal craniotomy. 4 burr holes were made using a perforator drill. The two most medial burrholes were made with atleast 5 mm margin away from sagittal sinus. No significant bleeding was encountered in the process. The burrholes were subsequently connected using a craniotome. The dura was stuck the the inner table of the bone flap and was gently dissected away using a periosteal elevator. Dura was inspected with no evidence of defect. Once the bone flap containing the intraosseous lesion was removed, hemostasis at the borders of the craniotomy was achieved using gel foam and patties. There was no evidence of sinus injury. Plastic surgery completed the remainder of the case, as reflected in their operative note.
