## **Basilar Invagination (BI)**

> [!summary] **Overview**  
> Basilar invagination is a craniocervical junction abnormality where the odontoid migrates **superiorly** into the foramen magnum. Classically defined when **>1/3 of the odontoid projects above Chamberlain's line**, indicating cranial settling and potential brainstem/cord compression.

---

## **1. Definition**

### **Chamberlain Line Criterion**

- **>1/3 of the odontoid** lies **above Chamberlain's line**.
- **Chamberlain line:** drawn from the **posterior hard palate** to the **posterior margin of the foramen magnum**.
- Diagnostic validity: **AUROC = 0.97** (excellent accuracy).

> [!note] **Other reference lines** (commonly used in practice):
> 
> - **McGregor line** – slightly more forgiving; from posterior hard palate to lowest occipital curve.
> 
> - **McRae line** – odontoid tip should remain **below** the foramen magnum (basion–opisthion line).
> 
> - **Clivo-axial angle**, **Grabb–Oakes line**, and **Powers ratio** assess secondary ventral compression and instability.

## **2. Pathophysiology**

- Upward migration due to congenital or acquired deformity.
- Causes ventral brainstem and cervicomedullary compression.
- Often associated with:
    - Chiari malformation
    - Atlantoaxial instability
    - Cranial settling (==rheumatoid arthritis==)
    - Platybasia
    - Occipital assimilation of C1

---

## **3. Clinical Presentation**

- Suboccipital headaches
- Neck pain
- Long tract signs (myelopathy)
- Lower cranial nerve dysfunction
- Ataxia or gait imbalance

> [!warning] **Red flags:** progressive myelopathy, dysphagia, respiratory compromise — mandate urgent craniocervical evaluation.

---

## **4. Imaging Evaluation**

> [!tip] **CT for bony anatomy; MRI for brainstem compression and syrinx.**

### **Key Measurements**

- **>1/3 odontoid above Chamberlain line** (primary criterion).
- Evaluate for:
    - Ventral brainstem compression
    - Atlantoaxial instability (ADI >3 mm adults, >5 mm children)
    - Clivo-axial angle (<125° suggests pathology)
    - Grabb–Oakes ≥9 mm (ventral cord compression)
