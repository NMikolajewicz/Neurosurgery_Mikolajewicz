## **AO Spine Classification Systems**

> [!summary] **Overview**  
> The AO Spine classification systems provide a standardized framework for describing spinal injuries across the cervical, thoracolumbar, and sacral regions. They emphasize **injury morphology**, **neurological status**, and **clinical modifiers**, offering a universal language for decision-making and management.

**Reference:** [https://www.aofoundation.org/spine/clinical-library-and-tools/aospine-classification-systems](https://www.aofoundation.org/spine/clinical-library-and-tools/aospine-classification-systems)

---

## **1. Fundamental AO Principles**

### **Core Morphology Types**

- **Type A – Axial/Compression**  
    Involves the ==vertebral body==; posterior tension band typically intact.
    
- **Type B – Bending/Distraction**  
    Failure of the anterior or posterior ==tension band==; frequently unstable.
    
- **Type C – Translation/Displacement**  
    Severe injury with ==displacement or rotation== of one vertebral segment relative to another.

> [!tip] **Mnemonic:**  
> **A**xial load → **A**  
> **B**ending forces → **B**  
> **C**omplete displacement → **C**

## **2. Thoracolumbar Spine (T1–L5) Classification**

> [!info] **Thoracolumbar AO = Morphology (A–C) + Neurologic Grade (N0–N4) + Modifiers**

### **Type A – Compression**

- **A0:** Minor injuries (e.g., TP fractures).
- **A1:** Wedge compression – 1 endplate.
- **A2:** Split/pincer – both endplates.
- **A3:** Incomplete burst – 1 endplate + posterior wall.
- **A4:** Complete burst – both endplates + posterior wall.

> [!tip] **"Points" System to Remember A1–A4:**  
> Endplate = 1 point  
> Posterior wall = 2 points  
> A1 =1, A2 =2, A3 =3, A4 =4

### **Type B – Tension Band Injury**

- **B1:** Transosseous flexion-distraction (Chance). (==bone==)
- **B2:** Posterior ligamentous failure. (==posterior band==)
- **B3:** Hyperextension with anterior band disruption. (==anterior band==)

> [!tip] **B mnemonic:**  
> **B1 – Bone**, **B2 – Back (posterior ligament)**, **B3 – Bend backward (hyperextension)**.

### **Type C – Translation**

- Any displacement or rotational translation of the vertebral column.

### **Neurological Grades (N)**

- **N0:** Intact
- **N1:** Transient deficits
- **N2:** Radiculopathy
- **N3:** Incomplete SCI
- **N4:** Complete SCI

## **3. Subaxial Cervical Spine (C3–C7)**

> [!summary] **Adds Facet Injury Grades + Modifiers**

### **Morphology (A–C)**

Same axial/bending/translation principles as thoracolumbar.

### **Facet Injury Classification (F)**

- **F1:** Non-displaced facet fracture.
- **F2:** Displaced facet fracture.
- **F3:** _Floating lateral mass_.
- **F4:** Locked facets (bilateral or unilateral).

### **Neurological Status (N)**

Same N0–N4 system.

### **Modifiers (M)**

Used to highlight special situations:

- M1 = Indeterminate ligament injury
- M2 = Critical disc herniation
- M3 = Stiff spine (ankylosis)
- M4 = Polytrauma

## **4. Upper Cervical Spine (C0–C2)**

> [!summary] **Region-based system (3 regions × A/B/C patterns)**  
> Applies to the skull–C1–C2 complex where injury patterns differ from the typical subaxial/Thoracolumbar AO framework.

### **Region I – Occipital Condyle / Craniocervical Junction (C0–C1)**

- **Type A:** Isolated condyle fracture.
- **Type B:** Ligamentous injury without displacement.
- **Type C:** C0–C1 dislocation (highly unstable). See also [[Atlantooccipital dislocation]].

### **Region II – C1 (Atlas) & C1–C2 Complex**

- **Type A:** C1 ring fractures (e.g., ==Jefferson==). See also [[C1 atlas fractures]].
- **Type B:** Transverse ligament injury.
- **Type C:** C1–C2 dislocation.

### **Region III – C2 & C2–C3**

- **Type A:** ==Odontoid== fractures (dens). See also [[C2 odontoid fractures]].
- **Type B:** ==Hangman=='s fracture (pars). See also [[C2 ring of axis fractures]].
- **Type C:** C2–C3 dislocation.

> [!tip] **Mnemonic – "Oh A Dandy Hangman's Dislocation"**  
> **Oh** = Occipital condyle (I)  
> **A** = Atlas (II)  
> **Dandy** = Dens (III-A)  
> **Hangman's** = Axis pars (III-B)  
> **Dislocation** = Type C for each region

## **5. Sacral AO Classification**

> [!summary] **Describes sacral fractures by stability + pelvic ring involvement + spino-pelvic dissociation**

### **Type A – Lower Sacrococcygeal (Stable)**

Below SI joint. Spine–pelvis continuity intact.

- **A1:** Coccyx/minor contusion.
- **A2:** Nondisplaced transverse fracture.
- **A3:** Displaced transverse fracture.

### **Type B – Posterior Pelvic Ring (Unilateral)**

Unilateral vertical fracture → pelvic instability.

- **B1:** Medial to foramina (central canal involvement).
- **B2:** Lateral to foramina.
- **B3:** Through foramina.

### **Type C – Spino-Pelvic Instability**

Dissociation between spine and pelvis.

- **C0:** Nondisplaced U-type.
- **C1:** Displaced U-type (no major pelvic instability).
- **C2:** Bilateral vertical fractures.
- **C3:** Displaced U-type with complete dissociation.

> [!tip] **Interpretation:**  
> **A** = Alone (bottom sacrum)  
> **B** = Broken on one side  
> **C** = Completely detached (dissociation)

## **6. Summary Points**

- AO classifications progress from **stable → unstable** (A → B → C).
- Neurologic status (N0–N4) informs prognosis and urgency.
- Modifiers (M) highlight elements influencing treatment (e.g., disc injury, ankylosis).
- Plays a central role in determining **operative vs. non-operative** management and choice of approach.
