> [!summary] **Odontoid Fracture Review**  
> • Classified by **Anderson–D’Alonzo**: Type I (tip avulsion), **Type II (base; highest non-union risk)**, Type III (into C2 body).  
> • **Grauer** further refines Type II: IIA (nondisplaced → collar), **IIB (AS→PI line → anterior screw)**, **IIC (reverse obliquity/comminuted → posterior fusion)**.  
> • **Surgery indications:** >5 mm displacement, age >50, non-union, TAL disruption, or inability to maintain reduction.  
> • ==**Anterior odontoid screw** only for Type IIB ==with good bone and reducibility; contraindicated in non-union, Type IIC, TAL injury, poor trajectory.  
> • **Posterior C1–C2 fusion** preferred in elderly, comminution, IIC fractures, and TAL rupture—highest stability and fusion rates.  
> • CT defines fracture orientation; MRI assesses **TAL integrity**.

## **1. Classification**

![[Image 20231012 095632.png|500]]

### **Anderson–D'Alonzo Types**
- **Type I:** Tip avulsion (alar ligament). Rare; generally stable.
- **Type II:** Fracture at the base of the dens → ==**highest non-union risk**==.
- **Type III:** Extends into the C2 body → better healing potential.

---

### **Grauer Subclassification (for Type II)**

> [!note] Focuses on fracture line orientation and reducibility to guide fixation.

- **Type IIA:** Minimally or nondisplaced; no comminution → ==good candidate== for external immobilization.
- **Type IIB:** Displaced with fracture line **from anterosuperior → posteroinferior** → ==ideal for **anterior odontoid screw**==.
- **Type IIC:** **Reverse obliquity** or comminuted (anteroinferior → posterosuperior) → ==poor screw purchase== → often needs posterior C1–C2 fusion.

## **2. Management Overview**

### **Conservative Management**

- **Rigid collar** for **6–8 weeks** (sometimes up to 12 weeks depending on age/healing potential).
- Appropriate for:
    - Type I
    - Type III
    - Grauer IIA Type II
    - Minimally displaced fractures
    - Patients not fit for surgery

---

### **Operative Management**

> [!important] **Indications for Surgery**
> 
> - **>5 mm displacement**
> 
> - **Age >50** (significantly higher non-union rates)
> 
> - **Non-union or delayed healing**
> 
> - **TAL disruption** (implies instability)
> 
> - **Inability to reduce or maintain alignment** (even in halo)
> 
> - Unfavorable fracture morphology for healing

### **Surgical Options**

---

#### **A. C1–C2 Posterior Fixation/Fusion**

- Go-to intervention for:
    - Type IIC fractures
    - Comminution
    - TAL disruption
    - Chronic non-union
    - Elderly patients
- Techniques include:
    - Goel–Harms screw construct
    - Transarticular screws (Magerl) when anatomy permits
- **High fusion rates**, robust stability.
#### **B. Anterior Odontoid Screw Fixation**

> [!tip] Goal: **internal fixation without fusion**, preserving C1–C2 rotation.

##### **Indications**
- Type ==**IIB** fracture pattern ==(favorable obliquity)
- Adequate bone quality
- Reducible fracture (sagittal alignment correctable intraoperatively)
##### **Contraindications**
- **Non-union**
- ==**Grauer Type IIC**== (reverse obliquity/comminution)
- **TAL disruption** (requires fusion)
- Inability to maintain adequate reduction
- Severe osteoporosis
- Barrel chest or anatomy preventing proper screw trajectory

---

## **3. Clinical Pearls**

> [!tip] Expert Considerations  
> • Elderly patients with Type II fractures have **high non-union rates**—favor posterior fusion early.  
> • Type III fractures heal well non-operatively unless displaced.  
> • Anterior screw requires **neutral alignment**; avoid if kyphotic or irreducible.  
> • CT is critical for evaluating fracture line obliquity (Grauer classification).  
> • MRI helps assess TAL integrity and occult soft tissue injury.
