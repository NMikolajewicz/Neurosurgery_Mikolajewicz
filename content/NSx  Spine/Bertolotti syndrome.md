## **Lumbosacral Transitional Vertebrae (LSTV)**

> [!summary] **LSTV & Bertolotti Syndrome Review**  
> • **LSTV** = congenital segmentation anomaly where L5 sacralizes or S1 lumbarizes; prevalence **4–30%**.  
> • **Castellvi types II–IV** are clinically relevant; **Type II (pseudoarthrosis)** most associated with symptoms.  
> • **Bertolotti syndrome** = symptomatic LBP or radiculopathy due to transitional anatomy; requires **clinical–imaging correlation**.  
> • Pathophysiology: pseudoarthrosis pain, **L4–L5 hypermobility**, adjacent segment degeneration, and **L5 root compression**.  
> • Key exam pearl: symptoms often arise from **L4–L5**, not the transitional segment.  
> • Diagnosis: exclude other pain generators; confirm with **targeted injections** and imaging correlation.  
> • Pitfall: **LSTV is often incidental**—don’t overcall Bertolotti without concordant findings.
---
### **1. Definition**

- Developmental vertebral anomaly at the lumbosacral junction.
- Two main patterns:
    - **Sacralization of L5** (L5 behaves like S1)
    - **Lumbarization of S1** (S1 behaves like L5)
#### **Prevalence**

- **4%–30%**, depending on imaging modality and demographic.

---

### **2. Castellvi Classification (Gold Standard)**

> [!note] **Clinically Relevant:** Types **II–IV** correlate most with symptoms. Type **II (pseudoarthrosis)** is most often implicated in Bertolotti syndrome.
#### **Type I – Enlarged Transverse Process (≥19 mm)**
- **Ia:** Unilateral
- **Ib:** Bilateral
- Usually incidental.
#### **Type II – Incomplete Articulation (Pseudoarthrosis)**
- Anomalous joint between L5 TP and sacrum/ilium
- **IIa:** Unilateral
- **IIb:** Bilateral
- ==**Common source of symptoms==.**
#### **Type III – Complete Bony Fusion**
- **IIIa:** Unilateral
- **IIIb:** Bilateral
- ==May transfer stress to cephalad segments==.
#### **Type IV – Mixed**
- Type II on one side + Type III on the other.

## **Bertolotti Syndrome**

> [!summary] **Definition**  
> Symptomatic low-back pain or radicular symptoms arising from biomechanical or compressive effects of an LSTV. Requires **clinical + imaging correlation**—LSTVs are frequently incidental.

---
### **1. Pathophysiology**

#### **1) Pain From Pseudoarthrosis**

- ==Abnormal articulation between L5 TP and sacrum/ilium== →
    - cartilage-like interface
    - motion-related inflammation
    - localized LBP or buttock pain
- Associated with ==Type II ==
#### **2) Altered Biomechanics**

- Transitional anatomy reduces motion at L5–S1 → ==compensatory **hypermobility at L4–L5**==
- Increased shear forces → early degeneration of the L4–L5 disc and facets
- Potential for ==adjacent segment disease== pattern
- associated with ==Type III==
#### **3) Nerve Root Compression**

- Anomalous articulation → foraminal stenosis
- Bony hypertrophy or osteophytes
- **L5 nerve root** most commonly involved

> [!important] **Clinical Pearl:**  
> The symptomatic level is often **L4–L5**, not the transitional level itself—due to abnormal load transfer.

---

### **2. Clinical Considerations**

- Diagnosis requires **exclusion of more common causes** (disc herniation, facet arthropathy, SI joint pathology).
- Image–symptom concordance is essential:
    - Pain reproduction with palpation over pseudoarthrosis
    - Targeted diagnostic injections can confirm the pain generator.

> [!warning] **Common Pitfall:**  
> Mistaking an incidental LSTV for the pain source leads to misdiagnosis. Not all LSTVs = Bertolotti syndrome.
