> [!summary] **Atlanto-Occipital Dislocation (AOD)**  
> • Highly unstable C0–C1 injury from ligamentous disruption; rapid diagnosis and occiput–C2 stabilization are essential.  
> • Traynelis types: **I anterior**, **II distraction**, **III posterior** displacement.  
> • Key CT metrics: **BAI –4 to 12 mm**, **BDI <8.5 mm**, **AOI <1.4 mm**; Powers ratio **<1**.  
> • ==**AOI is the most sensitive** measurement in children==; values >1.4 mm (CT) are highly suspicious.  
> • ==**BDI >8.5 mm on CT** is one of the most reliable adult indicators.==
> • MRI evaluates tectorial membrane, alar ligaments, apical ligament, and capsular integrity.  
> • Pitfalls: pediatric laxity, condyle fractures, malpositioned head altering measurements.

## **1. Classification**

The Traynelis classification (commonly referenced AOD framework):
### **Type I – Anterior Dislocation**

- Occiput ==displaced **anterior**== to the atlas.
- Most common type.
- Often the result of hyperextension + distraction.
### **Type II – Longitudinal Distraction**

- Craniocervical ==**vertical separation**==.
- Represents the most severe ligamentous disruption (tectorial membrane, alar ligaments, capsular ligaments).
- High risk of neurovascular injury.
### **Type III – Posterior Dislocation**

- Occiput ==displaced **posterior**== to the atlas.
- Less common; typically associated with high-energy trauma.

## **2. Radiographic Metrics (Craniocervical Stability Measurements)**

> [!tip] **CT is preferred** for accuracy; MRI for ligamentous integrity (tectorial membrane, alar ligaments, transverse ligament, capsular complex).

### **Basilar Atlanto-Axial Interval (BAI)**

- **Normal:** –4 mm to +12 mm
- Measured from basion to a line vertical to the posterior axial cortex.

![[Neurosurgery/NSx  Spine/_resources/Atlantooccipital_dislocation.resources/image.2.png|500]]
### **Basion-Dens Interval (BDI)**

- **Normal:**
    - < **12 mm** on X-ray
    - ==< **8.5 mm** on CT==
- Widely used; increased in anterior or posterior displacement.
- ==Most reliable indicator in adults. ==

### **Atlanto-Occipital Interval (AOI)**

- **Normal:**
    - < **2 mm** (X-ray)
    - < **1.4 mm** (CT)
- Measurement of joint space asymmetry; **most sensitive for pediatric AOD**.

![[Neurosurgery/NSx  Spine/_resources/Atlantooccipital_dislocation.resources/image.1.png|500]]
### **Powers Ratio** (BC/AO)

- **Normal:** < **1**
- Historically popular but less sensitive than CT-based measurements.

![[Neurosurgery/NSx  Spine/_resources/Atlantooccipital_dislocation.resources/image.3.png|500]]
### **Dubin Criteria (Mandible-Atlas Relationship)**

Useful adjunct in trauma when other landmarks are obscured.

- Mandible → anterior atlas: **<13 mm**
- Posterior mandible → dens: **<20 mm**

## **3. Imaging Pearls**

> [!tip] **AOD Pearls**  
> • **Never trust the plain film**—CT with metrics (BDI, BAI, AOI) is the diagnostic backbone; MRI confirms ligamentous failure.  
> • **AOI is the most sensitive** measurement in children; values >1.4 mm (CT) are highly suspicious.  
> • **BDI >8.5 mm on CT** is one of the most reliable adult indicators.  
> • **Normal Powers ratio does NOT exclude AOD**—use as a secondary metric only.  
> • AOD often coexists with **occipital condyle fractures** and subtle craniocervical misalignment—don’t anchor on a single measurement.  
> • Head **rotation, flexion, extension**, or poor midline reconstruction can distort values—re-measure on corrected planes.  
> • **Tectorial membrane edema or discontinuity on MRI** is a major red flag for instability, even if CT numbers are borderline.  
> • Avoid over-reduction or traction; maintain neutral alignment until stabilization (occiput–C2 fusion).  
> • Pediatric cases frequently present with **normal metrics but abnormal MRI**—ligamentous laxity reduces sensitivity of CT rules.

> [!important] **MRI**
> 
> - Evaluate the **tectorial membrane**, **alar ligaments**, **apical ligament**, **capsular joints**, and **transverse ligament**.
> 
> - High sensitivity for ligamentous failure—even when CT metrics are borderline.

> [!warning] **Pitfalls**
> 
> - Pediatric ligamentous laxity can mimic widening; AOI values are most reliable in children.
> 
> - Occipital condyle fractures may coexist and mask instability.
> 
> - Head rotation or flexion/extension alters measurements.
