Epidemiology

* 1 in 4000 live births
* most frequent occult dysraphic malformation
* F = M.

* * *

**Classification**

* * *

Intradural subpial lipoma (5% cases)

* dorsal within  spinal cord with an exophytic component
	* may present as an intramedullary tumour
* may have widened spinal canal with deficient posterior neural arch
* may be purely intradural
	* most common at cervical/cervicothoracic

Filum terminale lipoma (20%)

* truly occult - no cutaneous stigmata
* filum is thickened
* may have asymmetric dural attachment or be duplicated
* incidental finding in 1% of all MRIs

Conus lipoma (75%)

* dura deficient in 90% of cases
	* lipoma is continuous with s/c fat
* lipoma arising from the ==dorsal surface of the cord==, medial to the DREZ
* various subtypes:
	* lipomyelocele
		* interface between placode & lipoma lies within spinal canal
	* lipomyelocystocele
		* terminal cord splayed open by cystic dilatation of central canal
		* end of this ependyma-lined sac is plugged by lipoma
	* lipomyelomeningocele
		* terminal placode herniates outside of canal
		* large ventral SAS (since cord typically stuck dorsally)
		* lipoma causes asymmetic tethering
		* placode rotates as it herniates
		* nearly all associated with thickened filum
		* three subtypes: dorsal, transitional, caudal

3 variants

* dorsal variant
	* large dural defect
* caudal variant
* transitional variant

Embryogenesis

* Intradural/conus lipomas
	* premature disjunction theory of McLone & Naidich
		* currently held theory
	* during neural tube folding, the cutaneous ectoderm and neural ectoderm separate too early
		* ==paraxial mesenchyme herniates into the lumen== of the folding NT
		* fat grows,  prevents complete NT closure
* Filum lipomas
	* **elements below S2** are formed by secondary neurulation (or retrogressive differentiation) of caudal cell mass
	* precise mechanism of this is not completely understood
	* this occurs after NT closure
	* can be assoc. with extensive perineal deformities:
		* imperforate anus
		* ==VATER== (vert. defects, anal atresia, tracheoesophageal fistula, esophageal atresia, radial and renal dysplasia)
		* caudal regression syndrome

Pathology

* lipoma consists of mature adipocytes in a dense, fibrous stroma
* can occasionally see straited muscle, epithelial cells, bone, neural tissue within lipoma
* can increase in size in states of general body fat deposition

Clinical

* skin lesions
	* mostly evident in infants
	* ==hairy patch, dermal sinus==, dimple, appendage
	* subcutaneous lipoma most common – 60-90%
	* angioma – 20%
* orthopedic
	* d/t imbalance in antagonist/agonist muscles from tethered cord
	* equinovarus feet, leg length discrepancy
	* hip subluxation
	* scoliosis in 10%
* urologic
	* bowel/bladder ==incontinence==
	* frequent UTI
	* on urodynamics: DSD, flaccid bladder, or detrusor hyperreflexia
* neurologic
	* motor, sensory, pain

Imaging

* **Plain XR**
	* bifid spinous process & widened canal in many cases
* **US**
	* good initial exam for infant with s/c lipoma
	* very echogenic
* **MRI**
	* bright on T1
	* will also r/o assoc. DSM, hydromyelia
	* note: MR does not show much change in conus after de-tethering

Management
**symptomatic**

* surgical untethering (see below)

**NOT symptomatic**\-

* CONTROVERSIAL with two reasonable approaches:

            **1. follow clinically and with urodynamics**
                        - natural history not well known
                        - many will remain asymptomatic for years (or forever)
                        - surgery for conus lipomas has 20% complication rate
                        - even after prophylactic surgery, 20-40% will develop delayed symptoms
            **2. prophylactic untethering**
                        - the natural history is unclear, but likely most patients will deteriorate (?)
                        - once a deficit is established, surgery can’t necessarily correct it         

Surgical untethering for lipomyelomeningocele

* Goals
	* untether cord
	* identify & decompress any intramedullary lipoma (this can expand & cause deficit)
* Adjuncts
	* microscope
	* laser – this is essential for lipoma surgery
	* SSEP and EMG leg/bowel monitoring
		* McLone uses no monitoring
		* at HSC they do not monitor bladder because of  the technical difficulties of  finding appropriately sized monitors for small children
* Procedure
	* Exposure:
		* ==prone== on bolsters
		* midline incision
		* s==eparate bulk of lipoma== from skin
			* leave enough fat on skin side for closure & cosmesis
		* rostrally – find ==plane between lipoma and fascia== and define this circumferentially
		* identify and incise normal fascia in the midline and expose the rostral lamina
			* warning: the first rostral lamina may have only fibrous posterior arch
		* do enough of a laminotomy to be above the lipoma’s intradural component
	* Durotomy:
		* midline incision of dura starting rostrally and extending toward and then circumferentially around the entrance of the lipoma into the thecal sac
			* beware: at the lateral margins of the lipoma, the dura, lipoma, and dorsal nerve roots meet at virtually the same point
			* therefore, ==must preserve intact arachnoid== deep the dural incision and stay a little lateral to lipoma to protect the dorsal roots
		* use laser to amputate any lipoma superficial to lamina level
		* decompress remaining lipoma with laser
	* Intramedullary/Filum:
		* if intramedullary component
			* McLone recommends midline myelotomy and removal with laser
			* HSC much less aggressive: JMD simply removes tethering component, leaves a large fatty mass
		* if fatty filum ® divide this also
	* Closure:
		* watertight dural closure with graft/patch if necessary
		* watertight fascial closure
		* keep child in bed x 3-4 days post-op with a ==abdo tension bandage==

Outcome of Surgery

* 20% - wound problems
	* infection, CSF leak, dehiscence
* 5% - acute neurological worsening
* 20% - delayed neurological worsening requiring repeat de-tethering
	* diff. from the natural history?
* complications much lower for fatty filum transection

**Subpial/intramedullary lipoma**

* * *

Pathophysiology

* premature disjunction of neural ectoderm before formation of neural tube is complete
* mesenchyme enters open ependyma-lined central canal
* mesenchyme forms adipose tissue

Presentation

* dormant for many years
* not associated with spina bifida occulta
* +/- symptomatic lesions
	* myelopathy
	* pain

Management

* surgical debulking of significant mass
	* leave interface between cord and lipoma un-manipulated
