## 1\. DAILY WORKFLOW & FASTHUG

### FASTHUG Checklist

Daily assessment tool for every ICU patient:

* **F - Feeding**: Enteral nutrition started within 12-24 hours; goal rate achieved
* **A - Analgesia**: Pain controlled; use validated pain scales
* **S - Sedation**: Target minimal sedation; daily sedation breaks; RASS/SAS scoring
* **T - Thromboprophylaxis**: DVT prophylaxis unless contraindicated
* **H - Head of bed**: Elevated 30-45° to prevent VAP
* **U - Ulcer prophylaxis**: Stress ulcer prophylaxis if indicated
* **G - Glucose control**: Target 140-180 mg/dL (7.8-10 mmol/L)

### Daily Rounds Structure

* Review overnight events
* Assess organ systems systematically
* Fluid balance review
* Medication reconciliation
* Assess lines/tubes/drains
* Liberation potential (sedation, ventilator, lines)
* Disposition planning

## 2\. AIRWAY MANAGEMENT & INTUBATION

### Indications for Intubation

* **Failure to oxygenate**: PaO₂ <60 mmHg on high-flow O₂
* **Failure to ventilate**: PaCO₂ >50 mmHg with acidosis (pH <7.25)
* **Airway protection**: GCS ≤8, absent gag/cough reflex
* **Anticipated clinical course**: Expected deterioration, transfer
* **Work of breathing**: Exhaustion, respiratory distress

### Pre-intubation Checklist

* **P - Preparation**: Equipment check, suction, backup plan
* **R - Preoxygenation**: 100% O₂ × 3-5 minutes; apneic oxygenation
* **I - Induction**: Sedative + paralytic
* **M - Maintenance**: Post-intubation sedation/analgesia
* **E - Extubation**: Plan early

### Difficult Airway Predictors

* **LEMON** assessment:
	* L - Look externally (facial trauma, obesity)
	* E - Evaluate 3-3-2 rule (mouth opening, thyromental distance)
	* M - Mallampati score
	* O - Obstruction (tumor, epiglottitis)
	* N - Neck mobility

### RSI Medications

**Induction agents:**

* Etomidate: 0.2-0.3 mg/kg IV (hemodynamically stable)
* Ketamine: 1-2 mg/kg IV (bronchospasm, hypotension)
* Propofol: 1.5-2.5 mg/kg IV (status epilepticus; avoid in shock)
* Midazolam: 0.1-0.3 mg/kg IV (backup option)

**Paralytics:**

* Succinylcholine: 1-1.5 mg/kg IV (rapid onset 45-60 sec, duration 5-10 min)
	* Contraindications: hyperkalemia, burns, crush injury, neuromuscular disease
* Rocuronium: 1-1.2 mg/kg IV (onset 60-90 sec, duration 45-60 min)

### Post-Intubation Management

* Confirm tube placement: ETCO₂ capnography (gold standard), CXR
* Target tube depth: 21-23 cm at teeth (males), 19-21 cm (females)
* Initial ventilator settings (volume control):
	* Tidal volume: 6-8 mL/kg ideal body weight
	* Rate: 12-16/min
	* FiO₂: 100% initially, wean to goal SpO₂ 92-96%
	* PEEP: 5 cmH₂O initially
* Post-intubation sedation: fentanyl + propofol or midazolam
* Gastric decompression (OGT/NGT)
* DVT prophylaxis
* Stress ulcer prophylaxis if indicated

## 3\. MECHANICAL VENTILATION & ARDS

### Ventilator Modes

**Volume Control (VC/AC)**

* Set: Tidal volume, rate, FiO₂, PEEP
* Variable: Pressure
* Advantages: Guaranteed minute ventilation
* Disadvantages: Risk of barotrauma

**Pressure Control (PC)**

* Set: Inspiratory pressure, rate, FiO₂, PEEP
* Variable: Tidal volume
* Advantages: Better for ARDS, limits plateau pressure
* Disadvantages: Variable tidal volumes

**SIMV (Synchronized Intermittent Mandatory Ventilation)**

* Combination of mandatory breaths + spontaneous breathing
* Allows weaning

**PSV (Pressure Support Ventilation)**

* Patient-triggered, pressure-limited
* Used for weaning
* Typical settings: PS 5-15 cmH₂O

### PEEP (Positive End-Expiratory Pressure)

**Physiologic effects:**

* Recruits atelectatic alveoli
* Increases functional residual capacity
* Improves V/Q matching
* Reduces intrapulmonary shunt

**PEEP titration:**

* Start at 5 cmH₂O (physiologic)
* ARDS: Use ARDSNet PEEP/FiO₂ table (typically 8-15 cmH₂O)
* Monitor: Plateau pressure <30 cmH₂O, hemodynamics, oxygenation

**Adverse effects:**

* Decreased venous return → hypotension
* Barotrauma/pneumothorax
* Increased ICP (in brain injury)

### ARDS (Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome)

**Berlin Definition:**

* Timing: Within 1 week of known insult
* Imaging: Bilateral opacities not fully explained by effusions/collapse
* Origin: Not fully explained by cardiac failure/fluid overload
* Oxygenation (on PEEP ≥5 cmH₂O):
	* Mild: PaO₂/FiO₂ 200-300 mmHg
	* Moderate: PaO₂/FiO₂ 100-200 mmHg
	* Severe: PaO₂/FiO₂ <100 mmHg

**Pathophysiology:**

* Diffuse alveolar damage
* Increased capillary permeability
* Protein-rich edema
* Hyaline membrane formation
* Impaired surfactant function

**Management - Lung Protective Ventilation (ARDSNet Protocol):**

1. **Low tidal volume:**
	* Target: 6 mL/kg predicted body weight (PBW)
	* PBW (male) = 50 + 0.91 × (height cm - 152.4)
	* PBW (female) = 45.5 + 0.91 × (height cm - 152.4)
	* Minimum: Can reduce to 4 mL/kg if needed
2. **Plateau pressure:**
	* Goal: ≤30 cmH₂O
	* If >30: Reduce tidal volume to 4 mL/kg minimum
3. **PEEP/FiO₂ titration:**
	* Use ARDSNet table for combinations
	* Balance oxygenation vs. overdistension
4. **Permissive hypercapnia:**
	* Accept pH ≥7.20
	* PaCO₂ may rise (often 50-70 mmHg)
	* Consider bicarbonate if pH <7.15
5. **Driving pressure:**
	* ΔP = Plateau pressure - PEEP
	* Goal: <15 cmH₂O (lower = better)

**Adjunctive Therapies:**
**Prone positioning:**

* Indication: PaO₂/FiO₂ <150 on FiO₂ ≥60%
* Duration: Minimum 12-16 hours daily
* Benefit: 10-15% absolute mortality reduction (PROSEVA trial)
* Contraindications: Spinal instability, open abdomen, facial trauma

**Neuromuscular blockade:**

* Early (within 48h) cisatracurium infusion
* Duration: 48 hours
* Consider if: Severe ARDS (P/F <150), difficult ventilation
* Monitor: Train-of-four, prevent awareness

**Conservative fluid management:**

* Target: CVP <4 mmHg or neutral/negative fluid balance
* Reduces ventilator days (FACTT trial)
* Caution: Maintain organ perfusion

**Rescue therapies (severe ARDS):**

* Recruitment maneuvers: Controversial benefit
* Inhaled pulmonary vasodilators: NO or prostacyclin
* ECMO: Consider if P/F <80 despite optimal therapy

### Ventilator Troubleshooting

**High peak pressure alarm:**

* Check: Patient-ventilator dyssynchrony, mucus plug, bronchospasm
* Suction, bronchodilators, adjust sedation

**High plateau pressure:**

* Reduced compliance: ARDS, pneumothorax, pulmonary edema, abdominal compartment syndrome
* Reduce tidal volume, increase PEEP cautiously

**Low tidal volume alarm:**

* Leak: ETT cuff, circuit disconnection
* Increased compliance (improving ARDS)

**Hypoxemia despite high FiO₂:**

* Shunt physiology (increase PEEP)
* Pneumothorax (decompress)
* Mainstem intubation (reposition ETT)
* Mucus plugging (suction)
* Check: Tube position, ventilator circuit

## 4\. RESPIRATORY FAILURE

### Classification

**Type I (Hypoxemic):**

* PaO₂ <60 mmHg, normal/low PaCO₂
* A-a gradient increased
* Causes: Pneumonia, ARDS, PE, pulmonary edema

**Type II (Hypercapnic):**

* PaCO₂ >50 mmHg ± hypoxemia
* A-a gradient normal/mildly elevated
* Causes: COPD, neuromuscular disease, drug overdose, obesity hypoventilation

### Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV)

**Indications:**

* COPD exacerbation (first-line)
* Cardiogenic pulmonary edema
* Immunocompromised patients
* Post-extubation respiratory failure
* Do-not-intubate patients

**Contraindications:**

* Inability to protect airway
* Hemodynamic instability
* Facial trauma/burns
* High aspiration risk
* Inability to cooperate/clear secretions

**CPAP (Continuous Positive Airway Pressure):**

* Single pressure level
* Improves oxygenation (recruits alveoli)
* Settings: 5-10 cmH₂O

**BiPAP (Bilevel Positive Airway Pressure):**

* Two pressure levels (IPAP/EPAP)
* Improves ventilation + oxygenation
* Settings:
	* IPAP: 8-20 cmH₂O
	* EPAP: 4-10 cmH₂O
	* PS (IPAP-EPAP): Typically 8-12 cmH₂O initially

**High-flow nasal cannula (HFNC):**

* Flow: Up to 60 L/min
* FiO₂: Up to 100%
* Benefits: Washout dead space, PEEP effect, comfortable
* Indications: Hypoxemic respiratory failure, pre-oxygenation

## 5\. SHOCK STATES & HEMODYNAMICS

### Classification of Shock

|     |     |     |     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Type | Mechanism | CO  | SVR | PCWP | SvO₂ | Causes |
| Distributive | Vasodilation | ↑↑  | ↓↓  | ↓   | ↑   | Sepsis, anaphylaxis, neurogenic |
| Cardiogenic | Pump failure | ↓↓  | ↑↑  | ↑↑  | ↓   | MI, myocarditis, cardiomyopathy |
| Hypovolemic | Volume loss | ↓   | ↑↑  | ↓   | ↓   | Hemorrhage, dehydration, burns |
| Obstructive | Obstruction | ↓↓  | ↑↑  | Variable | ↓   | PE, tamponade, tension PTX |

### Initial Management - All Shock States

1. **Assess airway/breathing**: Consider intubation if unstable
2. **IV access**: 2 large-bore IVs or central line
3. **Monitoring**: Continuous vitals, arterial line, central line (consider)
4. **Labs**: CBC, electrolytes, lactate, ABG, coags, blood cultures
5. **Imaging**: CXR, bedside echo

### Septic Shock

**Definition (Sepsis-3):**

* Sepsis = Life-threatening organ dysfunction from dysregulated host response to infection
* SOFA score increase ≥2 points
* Septic shock = Sepsis + vasopressor requirement (MAP ≥65) + lactate >2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation

**Surviving Sepsis Campaign (SSC) Bundle - 1 Hour:**

1. Measure lactate; remeasure if >2 mmol/L
2. Obtain blood cultures before antibiotics
3. Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics
4. Rapidly administer 30 mL/kg crystalloid for hypotension/lactate ≥4
5. Apply vasopressors if hypotensive during/after fluid resuscitation to maintain MAP ≥65 mmHg

**Fluid Resuscitation:**

* Initial: 30 mL/kg crystalloid (balanced crystalloids preferred)
* Reassess after each bolus (500-1000 mL)
* Endpoints: MAP ≥65, UOP >0.5 mL/kg/hr, lactate clearance, perfusion improving
* Caution: Avoid fluid overload

**Vasopressors:**
**Norepinephrine (first-line):**

* Dose: 0.05-0.5 mcg/kg/min (typical start 0.1)
* Mechanism: α₁ > β₁ agonist
* Effects: ↑SVR, ↑MAP, mild ↑CO
* Route: Central line preferred

**Vasopressin (second-line add-on):**

* Dose: 0.03-0.04 units/min (fixed)
* Mechanism: V₁ receptor agonist
* Effects: ↑SVR (non-adrenergic)
* Add when: NE >0.25-0.5 mcg/kg/min
* Benefit: Reduces NE requirements, possible renal protection

**Epinephrine:**

* Dose: 0.05-0.5 mcg/kg/min
* Mechanism: α + β agonist
* Use: Second-line if NE + vasopressin insufficient
* Risk: Tachycardia, arrhythmias, lactate elevation

**Phenylephrine:**

* Dose: 0.5-3 mcg/kg/min
* Mechanism: Pure α₁ agonist
* Use: Third-line (or if tachyarrhythmia)
* Effect: ↑SVR only, may ↓CO

**Inotropes (if low cardiac output):**

* Dobutamine: 2-20 mcg/kg/min (β₁ agonist)
* Consider if: ScvO₂ <70% despite MAP ≥65

**Antibiotics:**

* Timing: Within 1 hour of recognition
* Coverage: Broad-spectrum empiric
	* Common regimens:
		* Pip-tazo 4.5g q6h OR meropenem 1-2g q8h
		* PLUS vancomycin 15-20 mg/kg q8-12h (if MRSA risk)
		* Consider antifungal if immunocompromised
* Duration: 7-10 days (shorter if rapidly improving)
* De-escalate based on cultures

**Source Control:**

* Identify and drain abscesses
* Remove infected devices
* Debride necrotic tissue
* Within 6-12 hours if possible

**Adjunctive Therapies:**

* Hydrocortisone: 50 mg IV q6h or 200 mg/day continuous
	* Consider if: Shock refractory to fluids/vasopressors
	* Duration: Until vasopressors no longer needed
* Glucose control: Target 140-180 mg/dL
* VTE prophylaxis
* Stress ulcer prophylaxis (if mechanical ventilation)

### Cardiogenic Shock

**Definition:**

* Systolic BP <90 mmHg >30 min
* Cardiac index <2.2 L/min/m²
* PCWP >15 mmHg
* Signs of hypoperfusion

**Management:**

1. **Optimize preload:**
	* Cautious fluids if hypovolemic (250 mL boluses)
	* Diuresis if volume overloaded
2. **Inotropes:**
	* Dobutamine: 2-20 mcg/kg/min (first-line)
	* Milrinone: 0.25-0.75 mcg/kg/min (if β-blocked or refractory)
3. **Vasopressors (if hypotensive):**
	* Norepinephrine preferred
4. **Mechanical support:**
	* IABP (intra-aortic balloon pump)
	* Impella, ECMO (consider early)
5. **Treat underlying cause:**
	* Revascularization (PCI/CABG) if ACS
	* Treat arrhythmias
	* Address valvular disease

## 6\. SEPSIS & INFECTION

### Sepsis Definition & Diagnosis

See **Shock States** section above for detailed sepsis management.

### Common ICU Infections

**Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia (VAP):**

* Timing: >48 hours after intubation
* Clinical: New infiltrate + fever + leukocytosis + purulent secretions
* Diagnosis: Quantitative culture (BAL ≥10⁴ CFU/mL, protected specimen ≥10³)
* Empiric antibiotics:
	* Pip-tazo 4.5g q6h OR cefepime 2g q8h OR meropenem 1g q8h
	* PLUS vancomycin or linezolid (if MRSA risk)
	* Consider double Pseudomonas coverage in high-risk patients
* Prevention:
	* HOB elevation 30-45°
	* Daily sedation breaks
	* Oral care with chlorhexidine
	* Subglottic suctioning ETT
	* Avoid unnecessary reintubation

**Catheter-Related Bloodstream Infection (CRBSI):**

* Diagnosis: Positive blood culture from catheter + peripheral
* Quantitative difference (catheter:peripheral ≥3:1) or time difference (>2 hours)
* Management:
	* Remove catheter if possible
	* Antibiotics: Vancomycin ± gram-negative coverage
	* Duration: 7-14 days (longer if endocarditis, osteomyelitis)
* Prevention:
	* Sterile technique for insertion
	* Daily review of line necessity
	* Chlorhexidine site care

**Catheter-Associated UTI (CAUTI):**

* Diagnosis: Positive urine culture (≥10⁵ CFU/mL) + systemic signs
* Asymptomatic bacteriuria does NOT require treatment
* Antibiotics: Directed by culture (empiric: ceftriaxone or fluoroquinolone)
* Prevention:
	* Avoid unnecessary catheterization
	* Remove as soon as possible
	* Maintain closed drainage system

## 7\. CARDIAC EMERGENCIES

### Atrial Fibrillation with RVR

**Evaluation:**

* Hemodynamic stability
* Duration (<48h vs >48h)
* Underlying cause (sepsis, PE, MI, thyrotoxicosis, post-op)

**Unstable (hypotension, angina, severe heart failure):**

* Immediate synchronized cardioversion: 100-200J biphasic
* Sedation: Etomidate or propofol

**Stable - Rate Control (preferred in ICU):**
**β-blockers:**

* Metoprolol: 2.5-5 mg IV q5-10min (max 15 mg), then 25-100 mg PO q6-12h
* Esmolol: Load 500 mcg/kg over 1 min, then 50-200 mcg/kg/min infusion
* Avoid if: Hypotension, decompensated HF, bronchospasm

**Calcium channel blockers:**

* Diltiazem: Load 0.25 mg/kg (20 mg) over 2 min, repeat 0.35 mg/kg if needed, then 5-15 mg/hr infusion
* Better for rate control than metoprolol in most ICU studies
* Avoid if: Systolic HF, WPW, hypotension

**Amiodarone:**

* Load: 150 mg over 10 min, then 1 mg/min × 6h, then 0.5 mg/min × 18h
* Use if: β-blockers/CCB contraindicated, or rhythm control desired
* Rhythm conversion in ~25-30% by 24h

**Digoxin:**

* Load: 0.5 mg IV, then 0.25 mg q6h × 2 doses
* Maintenance: 0.125-0.25 mg daily
* Slow onset (hours); use for adjunctive rate control

**Target heart rate:** 80-110 bpm
**Anticoagulation:**

* Start heparin or DOAC if AF >48h or unknown duration (unless contraindicated)
* CHA₂DS₂-VASc score guides long-term anticoagulation

### Cardiac Arrest (ACLS)

**BLS:**

* High-quality CPR: 100-120/min, depth 2-2.4 inches, allow full recoil
* Minimize interruptions (<10 sec)
* Ratio 30:2 (if no advanced airway), continuous (if intubated)

**VF/Pulseless VT (Shockable):**

1. CPR immediately
2. Defibrillate: 120-200J biphasic (360J monophasic)
3. Resume CPR × 2 minutes
4. Check rhythm
5. If still VF/VT: Shock
6. CPR × 2 min
7. **Epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO q3-5min** (after 2nd shock)
8. CPR × 2 min + check rhythm
9. If still VF/VT: Shock
10. **Amiodarone 300 mg IV/IO** (or lidocaine 1-1.5 mg/kg)
11. Repeat CPR/shock cycles
12. Amiodarone 150 mg IV for recurrent VF/VT

**PEA/Asystole (Non-Shockable):**

1. CPR immediately
2. **Epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO q3-5min** (immediately)
3. CPR × 2 min
4. Check rhythm
5. If remains non-shockable: CPR × 2 min + epinephrine
6. **Reversible causes (H's and T's):**
	* Hypovolemia, Hypoxia, H⁺ (acidosis), Hypo/hyperkalemia, Hypothermia
	* Tension pneumothorax, Tamponade, Toxins, Thrombosis (MI/PE)

**Advanced Airway:**

* Intubate when feasible (do not delay CPR)
* Confirm with ETCO₂ (>10 mmHg indicates adequate CPR quality)
* Switch to continuous compressions (no pauses for breaths)

**Post-ROSC Care:**

* Target MAP >65 mmHg (vasopressors if needed)
* Targeted temperature management: 32-36°C × 24h (if comatose)
* PCI if STEMI suspected
* Avoid hyperoxia (SpO₂ 92-98%) and hypotension
* EEG monitoring for seizures
* Prognostication: Delayed ≥72h, multimodal

## 8\. ACUTE KIDNEY INJURY & RRT

### KDIGO Definition of AKI

**Stage 1:**

* SCr increase ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48h OR
* SCr ≥1.5-1.9× baseline OR
* UOP <0.5 mL/kg/h × 6-12h

**Stage 2:**

* SCr 2.0-2.9× baseline OR
* UOP <0.5 mL/kg/h × ≥12h

**Stage 3:**

* SCr ≥3× baseline OR SCr ≥4.0 mg/dL OR
* Initiation of RRT OR
* UOP <0.3 mL/kg/h × ≥24h OR anuria × ≥12h

### Etiology (Pre/Intra/Post-Renal)

**Pre-renal (hypovolemia/hypoperfusion):**

* FENa <1%, UNa <20, BUN:Cr >20:1
* Reversible with volume resuscitation

**Intrinsic (ATN, AIN, glomerulonephritis):**

* ATN: Muddy brown casts, FENa >2%
* AIN: Eosinophils in urine, rash, fever
* GN: RBC casts, proteinuria, hematuria

**Post-renal (obstruction):**

* Bladder scan, renal ultrasound
* Foley catheter placement
* Urology consult if upper tract obstruction

### Management of AKI

1. **Stop nephrotoxins:** NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, vancomycin (monitor levels), contrast
2. **Optimize hemodynamics:**
	* MAP ≥65 mmHg
	* Avoid hypotension
	* Consider dopamine/fenoldopam (limited evidence)
3. **Fluid management:**
	* Resuscitate if hypovolemic
	* Avoid fluid overload in oliguric AKI
4. **Adjust medication doses** for GFR
5. **Monitor:**
	* Daily creatinine, urine output
	* Electrolytes (especially K⁺)
	* Acid-base status
6. **Nutrition:**
	* Adequate protein (1.2-1.5 g/kg/day)
	* Restrict K⁺, PO₄³⁻ as needed

### Indications for RRT (Renal Replacement Therapy)

**Emergent indications (AEIOU):**

* **A**cidosis: Severe metabolic acidosis (pH <7.1) refractory to medical therapy
* **E**lectrolytes: Hyperkalemia >6.5 mEq/L or refractory to treatment
* **I**ngestions: Dialyzable toxins (lithium, salicylates, methanol, ethylene glycol)
* **O**verload: Volume overload/pulmonary edema unresponsive to diuretics
* **U**remia: Uremic complications (encephalopathy, pericarditis, bleeding)

**Additional indications:**

* Oliguria/anuria with volume overload
* Severe azotemia (BUN >100 mg/dL)
* Tumor lysis syndrome

### RRT Modalities

**Intermittent Hemodialysis (IHD):**

* Duration: 3-4 hours, 3-4×/week
* Advantages: Efficient solute clearance, rapid correction
* Disadvantages: Hemodynamic instability, rapid fluid shifts
* Use: Stable patients, chronic HD patients, emergent toxin removal

**Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy (CRRT):**

* Duration: 24h continuous
* Modes: CVVH, CVVHD, CVVHDF
* Advantages: Hemodynamic stability, gradual fluid removal, better for cerebral edema
* Disadvantages: Requires continuous anticoagulation, immobilization, resource-intensive
* Use: Hemodynamically unstable patients, high fluid removal needs

**CRRT anticoagulation:**

* Regional citrate (preferred): Citrate into circuit, calcium replacement
* Systemic heparin: Monitor aPTT, adjust dose
* No anticoagulation: If high bleeding risk (change circuit q12-24h)

## 9\. ELECTROLYTE EMERGENCIES

### Hyperkalemia

**ECG Changes (progressive):**

* Peaked T waves (earliest)
* Prolonged PR interval
* Loss of P waves
* QRS widening
* Sine wave pattern → VF/asystole

**Management:**
**Immediate (if ECG changes or K⁺ >6.5):**

1. **Cardiac stabilization:**
	* Calcium gluconate 10%: 10-20 mL (1-2g) IV over 2-5 min
	* Onset: 1-3 min, duration: 30-60 min
	* Repeat if ECG changes persist after 5 min
	* (Or calcium chloride 10%: 5-10 mL via central line)
2. **Shift K⁺ intracellularly:**
	* **Insulin + glucose:** Regular insulin 10 units IV + 25g dextrose (D50W 50 mL)
		* Onset: 15-30 min, duration: 4-6h
		* Check glucose q1h × 4h (risk of hypoglycemia)
	* **Albuterol:** 10-20 mg nebulized (or 8-10 puffs MDI)
		* Onset: 30 min, duration: 2-4h
	* **Sodium bicarbonate:** 50-150 mEq IV (if concurrent acidosis)
		* Onset: Hours, controversial efficacy
3. **Remove K⁺ from body:**
	* **Diuretics:** Furosemide 40-80 mg IV (if adequate renal function)
	* **Patiromer** or **sodium zirconium cyclosilicate** (PO)
	* **Hemodialysis:** Most effective for refractory or severe hyperkalemia (K >6.5 with ECG changes)

**Avoid:**

* Kayexalate (sodium polystyrene sulfonate): Risk of colonic necrosis, limited efficacy

### Hyponatremia

**Classification:**

* Hypovolemic: FENa <1% (GI losses, diuretics, adrenal insufficiency)
* Euvolemic: SIADH, hypothyroidism, adrenal insufficiency
* Hypervolemic: Heart failure, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome

**Management (depends on severity and chronicity):**
**Severe symptoms (seizures, coma):**

* 3% saline: 100 mL bolus over 10 min (repeat × 2 if needed)
* Goal: Increase Na by 4-6 mEq/L in first hours (stops symptoms)

**Moderate symptoms (confusion, nausea):**

* 3% saline: 0.5-2 mL/kg/h
* Goal: Increase Na 4-6 mEq/L over first 6h, then slower

**Asymptomatic/chronic:**

* Correct slowly: 6-8 mEq/L per 24h (max 10-12 mEq/L/24h)
* Risk: Osmotic demyelination syndrome if corrected too rapidly

**Specific treatments:**

* SIADH: Fluid restriction (<800 mL/day), salt tablets, tolvaptan (use cautiously)
* Hypovolemia: NS resuscitation
* Hypervolemia: Fluid restriction, diuresis

### Hypercalcemia

**Symptoms:**

* "Stones, bones, groans, thrones, psychiatric overtones"
* Severe: Ca >14 mg/dL → lethargy, coma, arrhythmias, shortened QT

**Management:**

1. **IV fluids:** NS 200-300 mL/h (rehydrate + increase calciuresis)
2. **Calcitonin:** 4 IU/kg SC/IM q12h (rapid onset, short duration)
3. **Bisphosphonates:**
	* Zoledronic acid: 4 mg IV over 15 min
	* Pamidronate: 60-90 mg IV over 2-4h
	* Onset: 2-4 days, lasts weeks
4. **Hemodialysis:** If refractory or Ca >18 mg/dL

## 10\. CNS ASSESSMENT & DELIRIUM

### Sedation Scales

**Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale (RASS):**

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Score | Term | Description |
| +4  | Combative | Violent, danger to staff |
| +3  | Very agitated | Pulls/removes tubes, aggressive |
| +2  | Agitated | Frequent non-purposeful movement |
| +1  | Restless | Anxious, apprehensive |
| 0   | Alert & calm |     |
| \-1 | Drowsy | Not fully alert, sustained awakening to voice (>10 sec) |
| \-2 | Light sedation | Briefly awakens to voice (<10 sec) |
| \-3 | Moderate sedation | Movement or eye opening to voice (no eye contact) |
| \-4 | Deep sedation | No response to voice, movement to physical stimulation |
| \-5 | Unarousable | No response to voice or physical stimulation |

**Target:** RASS 0 to -2 for most ICU patients

### Delirium Assessment (CAM-ICU)

**Confusion Assessment Method for ICU:**
Must have Features 1 AND 2 AND (3 OR 4):

1. **Acute change or fluctuating course**
2. **Inattention** (attention screening test)
3. **Altered level of consciousness** (RASS ≠0)
4. **Disorganized thinking** (yes/no questions, commands)

**Perform twice daily in all ICU patients with RASS ≥-3**
**Subtypes:**

* Hyperactive: Agitated, pulling at lines (easier to recognize)
* Hypoactive: Quiet, withdrawn (more common, worse prognosis)
* Mixed

### Delirium Prevention & Management

**Prevention (ABCDEF Bundle):**

* **A**ssess, prevent, and manage pain
* **B**oth SATs and SBTs (spontaneous awakening/breathing trials)
* **C**hoice of analgesia and sedation (avoid benzodiazepines)
* **D**elirium: Assess, prevent, manage
* **E**arly mobility and exercise
* **F**amily engagement and empowerment

**Non-pharmacologic (first-line):**

* Reorient frequently (clocks, calendars, windows)
* Minimize nighttime disruptions
* Hearing aids, glasses
* Early mobilization
* Family presence
* Reduce tethers (lines, catheters, restraints)

**Pharmacologic (if needed for safety):**

* **Haloperidol:** 0.5-2 mg IV/PO q6-8h PRN
	* No mortality benefit, may shorten duration
	* Caution: QT prolongation, extrapyramidal symptoms
* **Quetiapine:** 25-50 mg PO q12h
	* May reduce delirium duration
	* Preferred for Parkinson's/Lewy body dementia
* **Dexmedetomidine:** 0.2-0.7 mcg/kg/h infusion
	* α₂-agonist, less delirium than benzodiazepines
	* Expensive, bradycardia/hypotension

**Avoid:**

* Benzodiazepines (except alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal)

## 11\. SEDATION & ANALGESIA

### Analgesia-First Approach

* Treat pain first before sedation
* Reduces need for sedatives
* Improves patient comfort and outcomes

### Analgesics

**Fentanyl:**

* Dose: 25-100 mcg IV bolus; 25-200 mcg/h infusion
* Onset: 1-2 min, duration: 30-60 min
* No active metabolites (safe in renal failure)
* Context-sensitive half-life increases with prolonged infusion

**Morphine:**

* Dose: 2-5 mg IV bolus; 2-10 mg/h infusion
* Onset: 5-10 min, duration: 3-4h
* Active metabolite (M6G) accumulates in renal failure
* Histamine release (hypotension, itch)

**HYDROmorphone:**

* Dose: 0.2-0.6 mg IV bolus; 0.5-3 mg/h infusion
* Onset: 5 min, duration: 3-4h
* 5-7× more potent than morphine
* Preferred in renal failure (less accumulation than morphine)

### Sedatives

**Propofol:**

* Dose: 5-50 mcg/kg/min (typical 20-50)
* Onset: Seconds, offset: 5-10 min
* Advantages: Rapid on/off, anticonvulsant, antiemetic
* Disadvantages: Hypotension, propofol infusion syndrome (PRIS)
	* PRIS: Rhabdomyolysis, cardiac failure, metabolic acidosis, renal failure
	* Risk: >5 mg/kg/h × >48h (avoid if possible)
* Lipid emulsion: Monitor triglycerides

**Midazolam:**

* Dose: 1-2 mg IV bolus; 1-10 mg/h infusion
* Onset: 2-5 min, duration: 1-2h (longer with infusions)
* Advantages: Anxiolytic, anticonvulsant, anterograde amnesia
* Disadvantages: Accumulation (active metabolites), delirium, withdrawal

**Dexmedetomidine:**

* Dose: 0.2-1.5 mcg/kg/h (no bolus for ICU sedation)
* Onset: 15-30 min
* Advantages: No respiratory depression, less delirium, easier communication
* Disadvantages: Bradycardia, hypotension, expensive
* Use: Extubated patients, ventilator weaning, alcohol withdrawal

**Ketamine:**

* Dose: 0.5-2 mg/kg bolus; 0.05-0.4 mg/kg/h infusion
* Advantages: Maintains hemodynamics, bronchodilation, analgesia
* Use: Sedation for intubation, severe asthma, chronic pain
* Disadvantages: Emergence reactions, increased secretions

### Target Sedation

* Goal: RASS -2 to 0 for most patients
* Light sedation associated with:
	* Shorter ventilator duration
	* Shorter ICU stay
	* Lower mortality

### Daily Sedation Interruption (SAT)

* Stop sedation daily until patient awakens
* Reduces sedative exposure
* Coordinate with SBT (spontaneous breathing trial)
* Contraindications:
	* Active seizures
	* Alcohol withdrawal
	* Neuromuscular blockade
	* Requiring deep sedation (severe ARDS, ICP management)

## 12\. BRAIN DEATH

### Prerequisites for Brain Death Testing

1. **Established cause** of coma (irreversible brain injury)
2. **Exclusion of confounders:**
	* Core temp ≥36°C
	* Systolic BP ≥100 mmHg or MAP ≥75 mmHg
	* No CNS depressants (check drug levels if recent use)
	* No severe metabolic derangements (Na 115-160, no severe acidosis/alkalosis)
	* No neuromuscular blockade (TOF testing)
3. **Neuroimaging** consistent with catastrophic brain injury

### Clinical Examination

All must be absent:
**1\. Coma:** No response to noxious stimuli (spinal reflexes may persist)
**2\. Absent brainstem reflexes:**

* **Pupillary light reflex (CN II, III):** Pupils fixed, mid-size/dilated (4-9 mm)
* **Corneal reflex (CN V, VII):** No blink to touch
* **Oculocephalic reflex (CN III, VI, VIII):** No eye movement with head turning (Doll's eyes)
* **Oculovestibular reflex (CN III, VI, VIII):** Cold caloric test - instill 50 mL ice water in ear, no eye movement
* **Gag reflex (CN IX):** No response to posterior pharynx stimulation
* **Cough reflex (CN X):** No cough with deep tracheal suctioning

**3\. Apnea test:**
**Procedure:**

* Pre-oxygenate with 100% FiO₂ × 10 min
* Obtain baseline ABG (PaCO₂ 35-45 mmHg, pH 7.35-7.45)
* Disconnect ventilator, provide apneic oxygenation (O₂ 6 L/min via cannula through ETT)
* Observe for 8-10 minutes
* Obtain ABG

**Positive (confirms brain death):**

* No respiratory effort AND
* PaCO₂ ≥60 mmHg OR increase ≥20 mmHg from baseline AND
* pH <7.30

**Abort test if:**

* Systolic BP <90 mmHg or MAP <75 mmHg
* SpO₂ <85%
* Cardiac arrhythmia

### Ancillary Tests (if apnea test cannot be completed)

* Cerebral angiography (gold standard): No intracranial flow
* Transcranial Doppler: Reverberating flow or absent diastolic flow
* EEG: Electrocerebral silence
* Scintigraphy (nuclear scan): No uptake in brain

### Documentation

* Two separate examinations separated by ≥6 hours (varies by jurisdiction)
* Legal time of death: Completion of first examination (varies by jurisdiction)

## 13\. NUTRITION IN THE ICU

### Benefits of Enteral Nutrition

* Maintains gut barrier function
* Reduces bacterial translocation
* Decreases infectious complications
* Lower cost than parenteral nutrition

### Timing & Initiation

* **Start within 12-24 hours** of ICU admission (if hemodynamically stable)
* Delay if:
	* Uncontrolled shock (high-dose vasopressors)
	* Active GI bleeding
	* Bowel obstruction/ischemia
	* High-output fistula

### Route

* **Gastric feeding** (OGT/NGT): Preferred, easier, physiologic
* **Post-pyloric feeding** (nasoduodenal/nasojejunal): Consider if:
	* High aspiration risk (recurrent aspiration, severe GERD)
	* Gastroparesis
	* Gastric feeding intolerance

### Aspiration Prevention

* Head of bed elevated 30-45°
* Assess gastric residual volumes (GRV):
	* GRV <500 mL: Generally safe to continue
	* GRV >500 mL: Hold feeds, reassess, consider prokinetics
* Subglottic suctioning ETT

### Feeding Protocol

* **Calculate caloric needs:**
	* 25-30 kcal/kg/day (use actual body weight for BMI <30, adjusted weight for obesity)
	* Protein: 1.2-2 g/kg/day (higher in burns, trauma)
* **Start low, advance slowly:**
	* Initial rate: 10-20 mL/h
	* Advance by 10-20 mL/h q4-6h to goal
	* Monitor tolerance (abdominal distension, diarrhea, high GRV)
* **Trophic vs. full feeding:**
	* Early ARDS/sepsis: Trophic (10-20 mL/h) × 7 days may be equivalent to full feeding
	* After hemodynamic stability: Advance to full feeding

### Prokinetics (if feeding intolerance)

* **Metoclopramide:** 10 mg IV q6h
	* Dopamine antagonist, prokinetic
	* Caution: Extrapyramidal side effects, tardive dyskinesia
* **Erythromycin:** 200-250 mg IV q6-12h
	* Motilin agonist
	* Tachyphylaxis after 48-72h

### Parenteral Nutrition (PN)

* **Indications:**
	* Unable to use GI tract (obstruction, ischemia, short gut)
	* Severe feeding intolerance despite post-pyloric feeds
	* Malnutrition + EN contraindicated
* **Initiate:** After 7-10 days if EN unsuccessful
* **Avoid:** Early PN in first week (increased infections)

## 14\. TRANSFUSION THERAPY

### RBC Transfusion

**Restrictive Strategy (Recommended):**

* **Hemoglobin threshold: <7 g/dL** for most ICU patients
* Exceptions:
	* **Hgb <8 g/dL:** Acute coronary syndrome, severe cardiovascular disease
	* **Hgb <7.5 g/dL:** Cardiac surgery patients
* **Landmark trials:** TRICC, TRISS trials

**Dose:**

* 1 unit RBC = 10 points (1 g/dL) hemoglobin increase in average adult
* Transfuse 1 unit at a time, reassess

**Risks:**

* TRALI (transfusion-related acute lung injury)
* TACO (transfusion-associated circulatory overload)
* Infection transmission (rare)
* Alloimmunization
* Immunomodulation

### Platelet Transfusion

**Thresholds:**

* **<10,000/μL:** Prophylactic in stable patients
* **<20,000/μL:** Fever, infection, minor bleeding risk
* **<50,000/μL:** Active bleeding, pre-procedure (most)
* **<100,000/μL:** Neurosurgery, ophthalmologic surgery

**Dose:**

* 1 apheresis unit or 4-6 pooled units
* Expect increase of 30-60,000/μL

### FFP (Fresh Frozen Plasma)

**Indications:**

* Active bleeding with coagulopathy (INR >1.5-2)
* Pre-procedure correction if INR >1.5 (varies)
* Massive transfusion protocol

**Dose:**

* 10-15 mL/kg (typically 2-4 units)
* Reassess INR after transfusion

### Cryoprecipitate

**Indications:**

* Fibrinogen <100-150 mg/dL with bleeding or before procedure
* Massive transfusion protocol

**Dose:**

* 1 unit/10 kg (typically 10 units)
* Increases fibrinogen by ~50-100 mg/dL

### Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP)

**Definition:** ≥10 units RBC in 24h OR ≥4 units in 1h
**Protocol:**

* **Activate early** in hemorrhagic shock
* **Ratio 1:1:1** (RBC:FFP:platelets)
* **Tranexamic acid:** 1g IV over 10 min, then 1g over 8h (if within 3h of injury)
* **Calcium:** Replace (citrate toxicity from blood products)
* **Keep patient warm** (avoid hypothermia)
* **Monitor:** CBC, coags, fibrinogen, Ca²⁺, K⁺ q30-60min

## 15\. ANTICOAGULATION & DVT PROPHYLAXIS

### VTE Prophylaxis

**Indications:**

* All ICU patients unless contraindicated
* High risk: Surgery, trauma, immobility, malignancy, prior VTE

**Pharmacologic (preferred):**
**Enoxaparin:**

* Standard: 40 mg SC daily (or 30 mg SC q12h)
* High risk (obesity, trauma, cancer): 40 mg SC q12h
* Contraindications: CrCl <30, active bleeding, severe coagulopathy, epidural catheter (timing)

**Unfractionated heparin:**

* 5,000 units SC q8-12h
* Preferred if: Renal failure (CrCl <30), high bleeding risk (short half-life)

**Mechanical (if pharmacologic contraindicated):**

* Sequential compression devices (SCDs)
* Combine with pharmacologic when safe

### Therapeutic Anticoagulation

**Unfractionated Heparin:**

* **Loading dose:** 80 units/kg IV bolus (max 10,000 units)
* **Infusion:** 18 units/kg/h (typical start 1,000-1,300 units/h)
* **Monitoring:** aPTT q6h until therapeutic (goal aPTT 60-80 sec or 1.5-2.5× control)
* **Adjust:** Per institutional protocol (typically ±2-4 units/kg/h)
* **Reversal:** Protamine sulfate 1 mg per 100 units heparin (max 50 mg)

**Enoxaparin:**

* **VTE treatment:** 1 mg/kg SC q12h OR 1.5 mg/kg SC daily
* **ACS:** 1 mg/kg SC q12h
* **Monitoring:** Anti-Xa levels if renal impairment, obesity, pregnancy (goal 0.6-1 IU/mL 4h post-dose)

## 16\. STRESS ULCER PROPHYLAXIS

### Indications

**High risk (strongly recommend prophylaxis):**

* Mechanical ventilation >48 hours
* Coagulopathy (platelets <50,000, INR >1.5, aPTT >2× control)

**Moderate risk (consider prophylaxis):**

* ICU stay >1 week
* Occult GI bleeding ≥6 days
* High-dose corticosteroids (>250 mg hydrocortisone/day)
* History of GI ulcer/bleeding within 1 year

### Agents

**Proton Pump Inhibitors (preferred):**

* **Pantoprazole:** 40 mg IV/PO daily
* **Lansoprazole:** 30 mg PO/NG daily
* More effective than H2RAs in preventing clinically significant bleeding
* Risks: C. difficile, pneumonia (debated), drug interactions

**H2-Receptor Antagonists:**

* **Famotidine:** 20 mg IV/PO q12h
* Alternative if PPI contraindicated
* Tachyphylaxis develops (reduced effectiveness over time)

### Duration

* Continue while risk factors present
* Discontinue when:
	* Extubated
	* Tolerating enteral nutrition
	* Coagulopathy resolved
	* Transitioned out of ICU

### Recent Evidence

* **REVISE trial (2024):** Pantoprazole vs placebo in mechanically ventilated patients showed no difference in GI bleeding or mortality
* Trend toward more selective use (only in highest risk patients)

## 17\. GLYCEMIC CONTROL

### Target Glucose

* **ICU target: 140-180 mg/dL (7.8-10 mmol/L)**
* Avoid:
	* Intensive control (<110 mg/dL): Increased hypoglycemia and mortality (NICE-SUGAR trial)
	* Hyperglycemia >180 mg/dL: Increased infections, poor outcomes

### Insulin Protocol

**Initiate insulin infusion if:**

* Glucose persistently >180 mg/dL
* Hemodynamically unstable
* Highly variable glucose
* NPO or inconsistent nutrition

**IV Insulin Infusion:**

* Regular insulin: Start 0.5-2 units/h
* Check glucose q1-2h (hourly until stable)
* Titrate per protocol to maintain 140-180 mg/dL
* **Must have dextrose source** (D5W or D10W minimum 30-60 mL/h if NPO)

**Subcutaneous Insulin:**

* When stable, eating, predictable
* Basal-bolus regimen:
	* Basal: Glargine or detemir daily
	* Bolus: Rapid-acting (lispro, aspart) with meals
	* Correctional: Per sliding scale

### Hypoglycemia Management

* Glucose <70 mg/dL: Give 15-20g carbohydrate (D50W 25 mL IV or juice/glucose tabs PO)
* Glucose <54 mg/dL: D50W 50 mL IV or D10W 250 mL IV or glucagon 1 mg IM/SC
* Recheck q15min until >100 mg/dL
* Review insulin regimen, reduce doses

## 18\. VENTILATOR WEANING & EXTUBATION

### Readiness for Weaning

Daily screen (all must be present):

1. **Clinical improvement** of indication for intubation
2. **Adequate oxygenation:**
	* PaO₂/FiO₂ >150-200
	* FiO₂ ≤40-50%
	* PEEP ≤5-8 cmH₂O
3. **Hemodynamic stability:**
	* No/low-dose vasopressors
	* HR <140, no active ischemia
4. **Ability to initiate inspiratory effort**
5. **No planned procedures requiring intubation**

### Spontaneous Breathing Trial (SBT)

**Method:** T-piece OR PSV 5-7 cmH₂O + PEEP 5 cmH₂O
**Duration:** 30-120 minutes
**Monitor during SBT:**

* Respiratory rate, tidal volume, RR/TV ratio (RSBI)
* SpO₂, heart rate, blood pressure
* Level of consciousness, distress

**Pass SBT if:**

* SpO₂ ≥90%, RR <35/min, HR change <20%
* No respiratory distress (accessory muscle use, diaphoresis, anxiety)
* No sustained hypotension or arrhythmia

**Fail SBT if:**

* SpO₂ <90%, RR >35/min persistently
* Increased work of breathing, distress
* Altered mental status (agitation, decreased GCS)
* Hemodynamic instability

### Rapid Shallow Breathing Index (RSBI)

* RSBI = Respiratory Rate / Tidal Volume (L)
* **<105:** Predictive of successful extubation
* **\>105:** Higher risk of failure

### Pre-Extubation Assessment

1. **SBT passed**
2. **Adequate cough:** Assess strength with suctioning
3. **Airway patency:**
	* **Cuff leak test:** Deflate cuff, measure exhaled tidal volume
	* Leak <110 mL (or <12% of TV) → Risk of post-extubation stridor
	* Consider steroids if high risk: Methylprednisolone 40 mg IV q6h × 4 doses before extubation
4. **Secretion management:** Able to clear secretions
5. **Mental status:** Alert, cooperative (GCS ≥8, RASS ≥-2)

### Post-Extubation Care

* Head of bed elevated
* Supplemental oxygen (as needed)
* Incentive spirometry, coughing
* Early mobilization
* Monitor for stridor, distress (peak 4-6h post-extubation)
* Consider NIV (if high risk for failure)

### Extubation Failure

* **Definition:** Reintubation within 48-72 hours
* **Incidence:** 10-20%
* **Consequences:** Increased mortality, ICU length of stay
* **Risk factors:**
	* Age >65
	* Cardiac disease
	* Prolonged ventilation (>7 days)
	* Difficult secretion clearance
	* Weak cough

## 19\. END-OF-LIFE CARE

### Goals of Care Discussion

* Early and frequent communication with family
* Establish patient's values, preferences (advance directives)
* Assess decision-making capacity (or identify substitute decision-maker)
* Discuss prognosis realistically (avoid false hope, but don't take away all hope)
* Clarify goals: Curative, life-prolonging, comfort-focused

### Withdrawal of Life-Sustaining Therapy

1. **Consensus:** Team + family agreement
2. **Preparation:**
	* Explain process to family
	* Offer spiritual support, social work
	* Allow family presence
3. **Withdrawal process:**
	* Discontinue vasopressors, dialysis, antibiotics (as per goals)
	* Adjust ventilator: Lower FiO₂, PEEP, rate (or extubate)
	* Ensure comfort: Opioids, sedation, treat dyspnea
4. **Symptom management:**
	* **Dyspnea:** Morphine 2-5 mg IV q5-15min PRN
	* **Agitation:** Midazolam 1-2 mg IV q15min PRN
	* **Secretions:** Glycopyrrolate 0.2 mg IV or scopolamine patch

### Comfort Measures

* Reposition regularly
* Mouth care
* Music, quiet environment
* Family support and presence

### Organ Donation

* Refer all potential donors to organ procurement organization
* Discuss early if brain death suspected
* Separate discussion from goals of care

## 20\. LANDMARK TRIALS

### Mechanical Ventilation

**ARDSNet (ARMA) - 2000:**

* Low tidal volume (6 mL/kg PBW) vs. traditional (12 mL/kg)
* Outcome: 22% relative mortality reduction (39% → 31%)
* Established lung-protective ventilation as standard of care

**PROSEVA - 2013:**

* Prone positioning in severe ARDS (P/F <150)
* Duration: ≥16h/day
* Outcome: Reduced 28-day mortality (16% vs 32%)

### Fluid Management

**FACTT - 2006:**

* Conservative vs. liberal fluid strategy in ARDS
* Outcome: No mortality difference, but fewer ventilator days with conservative strategy

### Sepsis

**Rivers EGDT - 2001:**

* Early goal-directed therapy (CVP, MAP, ScvO₂ targets) in septic shock
* Outcome: Mortality reduced from 46.5% to 30.5%

**ProCESS, ARISE, ProMISe - 2014-2015:**

* EGDT protocol vs. usual care (less protocol-driven)
* Outcome: No difference in mortality; less invasive monitoring in usual care
* Conclusion: Early recognition and treatment more important than specific protocol

### Transfusion

**TRICC - 1999:**

* Restrictive (Hgb <7 g/dL) vs. liberal (Hgb <10 g/dL) transfusion
* Outcome: No difference in mortality; restrictive strategy safe

**TRISS - 2014:**

* Restrictive (Hgb <7 g/dL) vs. liberal (Hgb <9 g/dL) in septic shock
* Outcome: No difference in 90-day mortality or ischemic events

### Glycemic Control

**Leuven - 2001:**

* Intensive (80-110 mg/dL) vs. conventional (180-200 mg/dL) glycemic control
* Outcome: Reduced mortality and morbidity in surgical ICU

**NICE-SUGAR - 2009:**

* Intensive (81-108 mg/dL) vs. conventional (140-180 mg/dL) in mixed ICU
* Outcome: Increased mortality with intensive control (27.5% vs 24.9%)
* Conclusion: Target 140-180 mg/dL in ICU

### Steroids in Sepsis

**Annane CORTICUS - 2008:**

* Hydrocortisone in septic shock
* Outcome: No mortality benefit, faster shock reversal
* Current: Consider low-dose hydrocortisone if refractory shock
