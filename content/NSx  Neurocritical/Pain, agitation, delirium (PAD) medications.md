*Delirium strategy per PADIS: prioritize non-pharmacologic measures; antipsychotics are not recommended for routine treatment or prevention; dexmedetomidine may help agitated delirium to facilitate extubation. 

Guideline touchpoints (why/when to choose)
- Analgesia-first (treat pain before sedatives) and target light sedation with daily SAT/SBT when feasible. Prefer propofol or dexmedetomidine over benzodiazepines for routine ICU sedation.  
- Dexmedetomidine is useful for cooperative sedation and extubation; most labels list 0.2–0.7 µg/kg/h, though many ICUs safely titrate up to ~1–1.4 µg/kg/h with monitoring.  
- Propofol: keep within usual 5–50 µg/kg/min; monitor triglycerides; PRIS risk increases at >~65 µg/kg/min and >48 h.  
- Ketamine: low-dose (0.1–0.3 mg/kg/h) is an opioid-sparing adjunct; dissociative rates (≥0.5–2 mg/kg/h) are options in refractory agitation, especially with hypotension where other sedatives are limited.
