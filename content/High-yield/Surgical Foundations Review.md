## Royal College Surgical Foundations Study Guide

* * *

## 1\. PERIOPERATIVE CARE

### PREOPERATIVE ASSESSMENT

#### History & Risk Stratification

**Key Components:**

* **Medical History Focus Areas:**
	* Cardiac risk factors
	* Pulmonary conditions
	* Metabolic disorders
	* Current medications
	* Previous anesthetic complications

**Exercise Tolerance Assessment:**

* Measure in **METs** (Metabolic Equivalents)
* Apply risk indices (e.g., **Revised Cardiac Risk Index**)

**ASA Physical Status Classification:**

* **ASA I**: Healthy patient
* **ASA II**: Mild systemic disease
* **ASA III**: Severe systemic disease
* **ASA IV**: Life-threatening disease
* **ASA V**: Moribund patient

**Family History Alert:**

* Screen for **malignant hyperthermia** risk

#### Optimization of Comorbidities

**Cardiac Conditions**

* Manage coronary artery disease, heart failure, arrhythmias
* High-risk patients → cardiology evaluation/stress testing

**Pulmonary Risk Management**

* **Smoking cessation**: ≥8 weeks prior to surgery
* Optimize COPD/asthma with inhalers
* Consider incentive spirometry teaching

**Diabetes Management**

* Achieve reasonable glycemic control
* High blood sugars → impaired wound healing and immunity

**Nutritional Status**

* **Red Flag**: Albumin <3 g/dL
* Unintentional weight loss increases risk
* Correct deficiencies before surgery

**Anemia Workup**

* Investigate and treat causes
* Options: Iron/B12 supplementation, EPO
* Consider delaying surgery if severe

**Renal Function**

* Optimize blood pressure control
* **Day-of-surgery consideration**: Hold ACE inhibitors/ARBs to prevent refractory hypotension

#### Medication Management

**Continue These Medications:**

* **Beta-blockers** (if already in use)
* **Statins** (beneficial for vascular patients)
* Most cardiac medications

**Anticoagulation/Antiplatelet Management:**

* **Warfarin**: Stop ~5 days prior, bridge with heparin if high thrombotic risk
* **DOACs**: Hold 1-2 days (longer if renal impairment)
* **Aspirin**:
	* Continue if stents/high cardiac risk
	* Discontinue ~7 days prior for elective cases
* **Clopidogrel**: Stop ~7 days before elective surgery

**Steroid-Dependent Patients:**

* Give **stress-dose steroids**
* Example: Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV before induction, then q8h on surgery day

**Diabetes Medications:**

* Reduce long-acting insulin dose
* Use sliding scale on surgery day
* Hold metformin (risk of lactic acidosis)

**Hold These Medications:**

* MAOIs (interaction risk)
* Oral hypoglycemics
* Diuretics (morning of surgery)
* Herbal supplements (stop ~2 weeks prior):
	* Garlic, ginkgo, ginseng, St. John's Wort

#### Laboratory and Diagnostic Workup

**Order Tests Selectively Based On:**

* Patient factors
* Procedure magnitude

**Common Preoperative Tests:**

|     |     |
| --- | --- |
| Test | Indications |
| **CBC** | Check hemoglobin if blood loss expected |
| **Electrolytes/Creatinine** | Kidney disease, on diuretics, elderly |
| **Coagulation Studies** | On anticoagulants, liver disease, bleeding history |
| **ECG** | Men >45, women >55, cardiac risk factors |
| **CXR** | Known cardiopulmonary disease or new symptoms |
| **Type & Screen** | Major surgery requiring potential transfusion |

#### Informed Consent & Patient Safety

**Essential Elements of Consent:**
1. Diagnosis explanation
2. Planned procedure details
3. Alternative options
4. Risks (common and significant)
5. Prognosis with/without surgery

**Safety Measures:**
* **Site marking** for lateralized procedures
* Use surgical safety checklist
* Verify special needs (translator, religious considerations)

#### Fasting Guidelines and Prophylaxis

**NPO Guidelines:**
* **Solids**: NPO after midnight
* **Clear liquids**: May take up to 2 hours before anesthesia

**Aspiration Risk Reduction:**
* H2 blockers or non-particulate antacids
* Pro-motility agents (metoclopramide)

**VTE Prophylaxis:**
* **Pharmacologic**: Low-dose heparin or LMWH
	* Start 2 hours before or after surgery
* **Mechanical**: Compression devices
* Can reduce DVT incidence by up to **70%**

**Antibiotic Prophylaxis:**
* Give within **60 minutes** before incision
* Examples:
	* **Clean cases**: Cefazolin
	* **Colon surgery**: Add metronidazole for anaerobes
	* **MRSA/beta-lactam allergy**: Vancomycin
* Discontinue within **24 hours** post-op

### INTRAOPERATIVE MANAGEMENT

#### Anesthesia and Monitoring

**Standard ASA Monitors:**

* Continuous ECG
* Pulse oximetry
* Non-invasive BP (every 3-5 min)
* Capnography (for ventilated patients)
* Temperature probe

**Advanced Monitoring (When Indicated):**

* **Arterial line**: Beat-to-beat BP, blood gas sampling
* **Central venous pressure**
* **Urinary catheter**: Monitor output as perfusion proxy

#### Anesthetic Techniques

**Options:**

1. **General Anesthesia**
	* Endotracheal tube or LMA
	* Inhalational or IV agents
2. **Regional Anesthesia**
	* Spinal or epidural blocks
	* Peripheral nerve blocks
3. **Monitored Anesthesia Care**
	* Sedation for smaller procedures

**Common Anesthetic Agents & Effects:**

| Agent               | Effects                                                  |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Propofol**        | Vasodilation, hypotension                                |
| **Ketamine**        | ↑HR, ↑BP (useful in shock)                               |
| **Etomidate**       | Minimal hemodynamic change, may depress adrenal function |
| **Succinylcholine** | Fast-acting, ↑K+, malignant hyperthermia risk            |
| **Rocuronium**      | Longer action, reversed with sugammadex                  |

#### Intraoperative Considerations

**Temperature Management:**
* Prevent hypothermia (increases bleeding and infection risk)
* Use warming blankets and fluid warmers

**Patient Positioning:**
* Pad pressure points
* Avoid nerve injuries:
	* Arms <90° at shoulder (brachial plexus)
	* Protect ulnar nerve at elbow
	* Common peroneal nerve at fibular head (lithotomy)

**Surgical "Time-Out":**

* Verify: Patient identity, procedure, site
* Confirm: Antibiotics given, equipment available

#### Surgical Technique Principles (Halsted's)

1. **Gentle tissue handling**
2. **Meticulous hemostasis**
3. **Preservation of blood supply**
4. **Strict aseptic technique**
5. **Minimal tissue tension**
6. **Accurate tissue approximation**
7. **Obliteration of dead space**

#### Fluid Management

**Basic Guidelines:**
* Maintenance: ~1-2 mL/kg/h crystalloid
* Blood loss replacement:
	* **3:1** ratio with crystalloid
	* **1:1** with colloid/blood
* Urine output target: ≥0.5 mL/kg/h

**Transfusion Triggers:**
* Acute loss >500 mL in small adult
* Hemoglobin <7-8 g/dL (healthy patient)
* Hemoglobin <8-10 g/dL (cardiac disease)

#### Critical Events Preparedness
**Malignant Hyperthermia:**

* **Signs**: ↑CO₂, muscle rigidity, fever, arrhythmias
* **Treatment**:
	* Stop triggering agents
	* **Dantrolene** 2.5 mg/kg IV bolus
	* Mortality reduced from ~80% to <5% with early treatment

**Massive Hemorrhage:**

* Activate massive transfusion protocol
* Balanced ratio (1:1:1) RBCs:Plasma:Platelets

### POSTOPERATIVE CARE

#### Immediate Post-Anesthesia (PACU)

**Primary Concerns:**

* Airway patency
* Stable hemodynamics
* Adequate ventilation

**Common PACU Issues:**

|     |     |
| --- | --- |
| Issue | Management |
| **Shivering** | Warming devices, meperidine if severe |
| **PONV** | Ondansetron, dexamethasone, metoclopramide |
| **Pain** | IV opioids (PCA), multimodal analgesia |
| **Sedation** | Lowest effective doses, naloxone if needed |

#### Standard Postoperative Orders (POST)
**P** - Pain management **O** - Oxygen/Out of bed **S** - Sedation/Fluids **T** - Thromboprophylaxis
**O** - Observation/vitals **P** - Pathology (specimens) **E** - Elimination (catheter management) **R** - Respiratory care **A** - Activity **T** - Tests (labs, imaging) **I** - IV/Nutrition **V** - Ventilator care **E** - Education

#### Key Postoperative Management

**Vital Sign Monitoring:**
* PACU: q15min
* Ward (if stable): q4h

**Fluid Management:**
* Typical maintenance: D5/0.45% saline + 20 mEq KCl at 75-125 mL/h
* Early oral intake encouraged (ERAS protocols)

**Drain Management:**
* Monitor output volume and character
* Remove when output is low and serous

**Activity:**
* **Early mobilization** is crucial
* Reduces DVT/PE risk
* Improves pulmonary function
* Speeds bowel function recovery

#### Common Postoperative Complications
##### Fever (Classic Timeline - "5 W's")

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day | Cause | Action |
| **1-2** | **Wind** (atelectasis) | Pulmonary toilet |
| **3-5** | **Water** (UTI) | Check UA, antibiotics |
| **5-7** | **Wound** (infection) | Inspect incision |
| **7-10** | **Walk** (DVT/PE) | Doppler US or CTPA |
| **Any time** | **Wonder drugs** | Review medications |

##### Other Complications

**Bleeding:**

* Signs: Tachycardia, hypotension, distension
* Check hemoglobin, identify source
* May need reoperation or IR embolization

**Pulmonary Complications:**

* **Atelectasis**: Incentive spirometry
* **Pneumonia**: Antibiotics + respiratory therapy
* **PE**: Heparin + confirmatory imaging

**Cardiac Complications:**

* **MI**: Often day 2-5 post-op
* May not have chest pain if on analgesics
* Check ECG and cardiac enzymes

**Ileus:**

* Small bowel recovers in ~24h
* Stomach in 2-3 days
* Colon in 3-5 days (open surgery)
* Treatment: Bowel rest, minimize opioids

**Wound Issues:**

* **Dehiscence**: Usually day 5-10
* **Evisceration**: Surgical emergency
* Cover with saline gauze → OR

## 2\. SURGICAL CRITICAL CARE

### TYPES OF SHOCK

#### 1\. Hypovolemic Shock

**Cause:** Intravascular volume loss (hemorrhage, dehydration)
**Classification of Hemorrhagic Shock:**

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Class | Blood Loss | Signs |
| **I** | <15% | Minimal signs |
| **II** | 15-30% | HR >100, anxiety, normal BP |
| **III** | 30-40% | HR >120, ↓BP, confusion, oliguria |
| **IV** | \>40% | HR >140, profound hypotension, lethargy |

**Management:**

* ABCs
* Stop external bleeding (pressure, tourniquet)
* Rapid crystalloid infusion (1-2L)
* Massive transfusion protocol if needed (1:1:1 ratio)
* **Permissive hypotension** (SBP ~90) until surgical control

**Endpoints of Resuscitation:**

* MAP ≥65 mmHg
* Normal heart rate
* Improved mental status
* Urine output >0.5 mL/kg/hr
* Clearing lactate

#### 2\. Cardiogenic Shock

**Cause:** Pump failure (MI, cardiomyopathy)
**Signs:**

* Hypotension
* Pulmonary edema (crackles, hypoxia)
* Distended neck veins
* Cool extremities

**Management:**

* Ensure oxygenation/ventilation
* Treat arrhythmias
* **Inotropes**: Dobutamine, epinephrine
* **Avoid fluid overload**
* Vasopressors if hypotensive
* Consider mechanical support (IABP, ECMO)

#### 3\. Distributive Shock

**Types:**

* **Septic shock** (most common)
* **Neurogenic shock** (spinal cord injury)
* **Anaphylactic shock**

**Septic Shock Features:**

* Initially warm extremities
* Wide pulse pressure
* Hypotension despite high cardiac output

**Management by Type:**

* **Septic**: Fluids, antibiotics, pressors (norepinephrine first-line)
* **Neurogenic**: Fluids, pressors, atropine for bradycardia
* **Anaphylactic**: IM epinephrine, fluids, antihistamines, steroids

#### 4\. Obstructive Shock

**Causes:**

* Tension pneumothorax
* Cardiac tamponade
* Massive PE
* Abdominal compartment syndrome

**Specific Management:**

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Condition | Signs | Treatment |
| **Tension Pneumothorax** | Absent breath sounds, JVD, tracheal deviation | Needle decompression → chest tube |
| **Cardiac Tamponade** | Beck's triad (hypotension, JVD, muffled heart tones) | Pericardiocentesis |
| **Massive PE** | Right heart failure, JVD | Thrombolytics or embolectomy |

### SEPSIS AND SEPTIC SHOCK

#### Recognition

**Definition:** Life-threatening organ dysfunction from dysregulated response to infection
**Clinical Signs:**

* Fever or hypothermia
* Tachycardia, tachypnea
* Hypotension
* Altered mental status
* ↑Lactate (>2 mmol/L)
* Leukocytosis or leukopenia

#### Surviving Sepsis Campaign Bundle (First Hour)

1. **Obtain cultures** before antibiotics
2. **Check lactate**
3. **Give broad-spectrum antibiotics**
4. **Aggressive fluid resuscitation**
	* Initial bolus: 30 mL/kg crystalloid
	* For hypotension or lactate ≥4

#### Management Algorithm

**If Hypotension Persists After Fluids:**

1. Start **norepinephrine** (first-line)
2. Target MAP ≥65 mmHg
3. Add vasopressin or epinephrine if needed

**Source Control:**

* Critical component
* Identify and treat:
	* Abscesses (drainage)
	* Necrotic tissue (debridement)
	* Anastomotic leaks (surgery)

**Adjunct Therapies:**

* **Hydrocortisone** 50 mg q6h for refractory shock
* **Glycemic control** <180 mg/dL
* **DVT and stress ulcer prophylaxis**

### ACUTE RESPIRATORY DISTRESS SYNDROME (ARDS)

#### Berlin Definition (PaO₂/FiO₂ on PEEP ≥5)

* **Mild**: 201-300 mmHg
* **Moderate**: 101-200 mmHg
* **Severe**: ≤100 mmHg

#### Lung-Protective Ventilation Strategy

* **Low tidal volumes**: ~6 mL/kg predicted body weight
* **Plateau pressure**: <30 cm H₂O
* **Adequate PEEP**
* **Permissive hypercapnia** (tolerate if pH >7.2)

#### Additional Strategies for Severe ARDS

* **Prone positioning**
* **Neuromuscular blockade**
* **Consider ECMO** for refractory cases

#### Ventilator Weaning

**Daily Spontaneous Breathing Trial Criteria:**

* Underlying cause improving
* PEEP ≤5-8
* FiO₂ ≤40%
* Hemodynamically stable

**Rapid Shallow Breathing Index (RSBI):**

* RSBI = RR/TV(L)
* RSBI <100 favorable for extubation

### HEMODYNAMIC SUPPORT

#### Vasoactive Medications

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drug | Mechanism | Primary Use |
| **Norepinephrine** | α1 > β1 | First-line for septic shock |
| **Vasopressin** | V1 receptors | Add-on for refractory shock |
| **Epinephrine** | α1 + β1 | Anaphylaxis, second-line |
| **Phenylephrine** | Pure α1 | Neurogenic shock |
| **Dobutamine** | β1  | Cardiogenic shock |
| **Milrinone** | PDE-3 inhibitor | RV failure, pulm HTN |

#### Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

**RRT Indications ("AEIOU"):**

* **A**cidosis (refractory metabolic)
* **E**lectrolyte imbalance (K+ >6.5)
* **I**ngestions (dialyzable toxins)
* **O**verload (volume)
* **U**remic symptoms

### ICU MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES

#### Nutrition

* **Early enteral nutrition** (24-48h)
* Target: 25-30 kcal/kg/day
* Protein: 1.5-2 g/kg for surgical patients
* TPN only if gut not functional >5-7 days

#### Sedation Goals

* Light sedation (RASS 0 to -2)
* Daily sedation interruptions
* Minimize benzodiazepines
* Treat pain first

#### ICU Delirium Prevention

* Reorientation
* Day-night cycling
* Early mobilization
* Minimal restraints
* Family involvement

## 3\. SURGICAL ANATOMY AND PHYSIOLOGY

### CARDIOVASCULAR SYSTEM

#### Key Physiologic Principles

**Cardiac Output (CO) = HR × SV**

* Surgery/anesthesia affect both components
* Compensatory tachycardia with blood loss

**Blood Pressure = CO × SVR**

* Vasopressors ↑SVR
* Anesthetics often ↓SVR

**Starling's Law:**

* ↑Preload → ↑SV (to a point)
* Guide fluid management

#### Surgical Anatomy Points

**Coronary Arteries:**

* Originate from aortic root
* Important for valve surgery

**Central Line Anatomy:**

* Right IJ: Between SCM heads → brachiocephalic vein
* Femoral: NAVL (lateral to medial)
	* **N**erve
	* **A**rtery
	* **V**ein
	* **L**ymphatics

**Embryology Relevance:**

* PDA: Ductus arteriosus fails to close
* Coarctation: Often juxtaductal
* RLN path: Embryologic remnant
	* Right: Around subclavian
	* Left: Around ligamentum arteriosum

### RESPIRATORY SYSTEM

#### Physiology Basics

**Minute Ventilation = TV × RR**

* Controls CO₂ removal
* Anesthesia reduces respiratory drive

**Oxygenation Factors:**

* FiO₂
* V/Q matching
* Diffusion capacity

**Oxyhemoglobin Dissociation:**

* PaO₂ 60 = SaO₂ 90% (steep part begins)
* Right shift (easier O₂ release): Acidosis, fever
* Left shift: Alkalosis, hypothermia

#### Airway Anatomy

**Critical Landmarks:**

* Larynx: C3-C6 levels
* Cricothyroid membrane: Emergency airway site
* RLN innervation: All intrinsic muscles except cricothyroid

**Chest Tube Placement:**

* "Safe triangle":
	* Lateral pectoralis major
	* Anterior latissimus dorsi
	* Line above nipple (5th ICS)
* Insert above rib (neurovascular bundle runs below)

### RENAL & FLUID/ELECTROLYTE

#### Fluid Compartments

**Total Body Water = 60% body weight**

* 2/3 intracellular
* 1/3 extracellular
	* 25% intravascular
	* 75% interstitial

**Crystalloid Distribution:**

* Only ~25% stays intravascular
* Need large volumes for plasma expansion

#### Maintenance Fluids ("4-2-1 Rule")

* First 10 kg: 4 mL/kg/hr
* Next 10 kg: 2 mL/kg/hr
* Each kg >20: 1 mL/kg/hr
* Example: 70 kg = 110 mL/hr

#### Electrolyte Management

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Electrolyte | Normal | Key Points |
| **Sodium** | 135-145 | Correct slowly (8-10 mEq/24h) |
| **Potassium** | 3.5-5.0 | Deficit ~100-200 mEq per 1 mEq/L low |
| **Calcium** | 8.5-10.5 | Watch post-thyroid/parathyroid surgery |

#### Renal Anatomy

**Hilum Organization: "VAU"**

* **V**ein (anterior)
* **A**rtery (middle)
* **U**reter (posterior)

**Surgical Pearls:**

* "Water under the bridge": Ureter under uterine artery
* Upper ureter: Renal artery supply
* Lower ureter: Internal iliac supply

### GASTROINTESTINAL SYSTEM

#### GI Motility

**Post-op Ileus Recovery Timeline:**

* Small intestine: 24 hours
* Stomach: 2-3 days
* Colon: 3-5 days

#### Blood Supply

**Arterial Supply:**

* **Foregut**: Celiac trunk
* **Midgut**: SMA
* **Hindgut**: IMA

**Important Collaterals:**

* Pancreaticoduodenal (celiac-SMA)
* Marginal artery of Drummond (SMA-IMA)
* Internal iliac to IMA via rectal arteries

#### Embryologic Conditions

**Malrotation:**

* Incomplete 270° rotation around SMA
* Ladd's bands compress duodenum
* Risk of midgut volvulus

**Meckel's Diverticulum ("Rule of 2s"):**

* 2% prevalence
* 2 feet from ileocecal valve
* 2 inches long
* 2 types of ectopic tissue

### HIGH-YIELD ANATOMICAL STRUCTURES

#### Inguinal Canal

**Boundaries:**

* Floor: Inguinal ligament
* Anterior: External oblique
* Posterior: Transversalis fascia

**Hernia Types:**

* **Direct**: Medial to inferior epigastric vessels
* **Indirect**: Lateral to vessels (through deep ring)

#### Calot's Triangle (Cystohepatic)

**Borders:**

1. Cystic duct
2. Common hepatic duct
3. Inferior liver edge

**Contains:** Cystic artery (must identify for cholecystectomy)

#### Peritoneal Spaces

**Dependent Areas:**

* **Supine**: Morison's pouch (hepatorenal)
* **Female pelvis**: Pouch of Douglas
* Used for FAST exam fluid detection

#### Retroperitoneal Structures ("SAD PUCKER")

* **S**uprarenal glands
* **A**orta/IVC
* **D**uodenum (2-4)
* **P**ancreas (except tail)
* **U**reters
* **C**olon (ascending/descending)
* **K**idneys
* **E**sophagus (thoracic)
* **R**ectum

## 4\. SURGICAL PATHOLOGY AND MICROBIOLOGY

### GENERAL PATHOLOGY

#### Inflammation Types

**Acute Inflammation:**

* Vasodilation (redness, warmth)
* ↑Vascular permeability (swelling)
* Neutrophils first responders
* Pus = neutrophils + bacteria + liquefied tissue

**Chronic Inflammation:**

* Lymphocytes, plasma cells, macrophages
* Granulomas in certain diseases:
	* **Caseating**: TB
	* **Non-caseating**: Crohn's, sarcoid

#### Wound Healing Stages

1. **Inflammation** (0-3 days)
2. **Proliferation** (3-21 days)
	* Granulation tissue
	* Type III collagen
3. **Remodeling** (weeks to months)
	* Type III → Type I collagen
	* Final strength ~80% of original

#### Neoplasia

**Benign vs Malignant:**

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feature | Benign | Malignant |
| Growth | Slow | Rapid |
| Borders | Well-circumscribed | Invasive |
| Metastasis | No  | Yes |
| Differentiation | Well | Poor |

**TNM Staging:**

* **T**: Tumor size/extent
* **N**: Node involvement
* **M**: Metastases

**Resection Classification:**

* **R0**: Microscopically clear margins
* **R1**: Microscopic residual
* **R2**: Gross residual

### SURGICAL MICROBIOLOGY

#### Normal Flora by Site

|     |     |
| --- | --- |
| Location | Common Organisms |
| **Skin** | Staph epidermidis, S. aureus, Strep |
| **Colon** | Bacteroides, E. coli, Enterococcus |
| **Oropharynx** | Viridans strep, anaerobes |

#### Surgical Site Infections (SSI)

**Classification:**

* **Superficial incisional**: Skin/subcutaneous
* **Deep incisional**: Fascia/muscle
* **Organ-space**: Any manipulated anatomy

**Timing:** Within 30 days (90 days if implant)

#### Wound Classification & Infection Risk

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Class | Description | SSI Risk |
| **Clean** | No GI/GU/resp entry | ~2% |
| **Clean-Contaminated** | Controlled entry | 5-15% |
| **Contaminated** | Major spillage | 15-30% |
| **Dirty/Infected** | Existing infection | \>30% |

#### Common SSI Pathogens

**Clean Surgery:**

* #1: Staph aureus (including MRSA)
* Coagulase-negative staph (prosthetics)

**Abdominal Surgery:**

* Polymicrobial
* Gram-negatives (E. coli, Klebsiella)
* Anaerobes (Bacteroides)

#### Antibiotic Prophylaxis

**Timing:** Within 60 minutes of incision
**Duration:** Usually stop within 24 hours
**Common Regimens:**

* **Clean cases**: Cefazolin
* **Colorectal**: Cefazolin + metronidazole
* **MRSA risk**: Add vancomycin

#### Special Infections

##### Necrotizing Soft Tissue Infections

**Types:**

* Type I: Polymicrobial
* Type II: Group A Strep or Staph
* Type III: Clostridial (gas gangrene)

**Treatment:**

* **Emergent surgical debridement**
* Broad antibiotics:
	* Carbapenem or pip-tazo
	* Plus clindamycin (toxin inhibition)
	* Plus vancomycin (MRSA coverage)

##### Clostridial Infections

**Gas Gangrene Features:**

* Rapid progression
* Severe pain
* Crepitus
* Bronze skin discoloration
* "Dishwater" drainage

**Treatment:**

* Wide surgical excision
* High-dose penicillin + clindamycin
* Consider hyperbaric oxygen

#### Prevention Strategies

**Preoperative:**

* Chlorhexidine shower
* Clip (don't shave) hair
* Treat remote infections first

**Intraoperative:**

* Maintain normothermia
* Control glucose <180 mg/dL
* Minimize OR traffic
* Gentle tissue handling

**Postoperative:**

* Early line/catheter removal
* Hand hygiene
* Appropriate wound care

## 5\. PRINCIPLES OF SURGICAL ONCOLOGY

### TUMOR BIOLOGY

#### Hallmarks of Cancer

1. Uncontrolled proliferation
2. Evading growth suppressors
3. Invasion and metastasis
4. Limitless replicative potential
5. Sustained angiogenesis
6. Evading immune destruction

#### Metastatic Patterns

**Carcinomas:** Lymphatic → hematogenous spread
**Sarcomas:** Primarily hematogenous spread
**Common Sites:**

* Colon → Liver (portal drainage)
* Lung → Brain, adrenals
* Prostate → Bone

### SURGICAL ONCOLOGY PRINCIPLES

#### Margin Requirements by Cancer Type

|     |     |
| --- | --- |
| Cancer Type | Margin Requirement |
| **Breast (invasive)** | No ink on tumor |
| **Breast (DCIS)** | 2 mm |
| **Melanoma in situ** | 0.5 cm |
| **Melanoma <1mm** | 1 cm |
| **Melanoma 1-2mm** | 1-2 cm |
| **Melanoma >2mm** | 2 cm |
| **Sarcoma** | 1-2 cm or intact fascial plane |

#### Lymph Node Management

**Sentinel Node Biopsy Indications:**

* Clinically node-negative breast cancer
* Melanoma >1mm thickness
* Some head/neck cancers

**Adequate Nodal Sampling:**

* Colon cancer: ≥12 nodes for staging

#### Multimodal Therapy

**Adjuvant Therapy:**

* Given after surgery
* Treats microscopic disease
* Examples:
	* FOLFOX for Stage III colon cancer
	* Tamoxifen for ER+ breast cancer

**Neoadjuvant Therapy:**

* Given before surgery
* Benefits:
	* Shrinks tumors
	* Assesses treatment response
	* May allow less extensive surgery

### CANCER-SPECIFIC MANAGEMENT

#### Breast Cancer

**Surgical Options:**

* Breast-conserving therapy (BCT) + radiation
* Mastectomy ± reconstruction

**Adjuvant Treatment by Receptor:**

* **ER+**: Hormonal therapy
* **HER2+**: Trastuzumab
* **Triple-negative**: Chemotherapy

#### Colorectal Cancer

**Surgical Principles:**

* En bloc resection with lymph nodes
* Minimum 12 nodes for staging

**Adjuvant Therapy:**

* Stage III (node+): FOLFOX chemotherapy

#### Lung Cancer

**Resectable Disease:**

* Lobectomy + lymph node sampling
* Stage III often gets neoadjuvant therapy

#### Melanoma

**Biopsy:** Full-thickness excisional
**Management Based on Depth:**

* Wide local excision with appropriate margins
* Sentinel node biopsy if >1mm thick

#### Sarcoma

**Principles:**

* Wide excision with negative margins
* Often combined with radiation
* Limb-sparing surgery preferred

#### Pancreatic Cancer

**Resectable Disease:**

* Whipple procedure for head lesions
* Distal pancreatectomy for tail
* Nearly all get adjuvant chemotherapy

#### Tumor Markers

| --- | --- | --- |

| **CEA** | Colon | Surveillance |
| **CA 19-9** | Pancreas | Monitoring |

| **AFP/β-hCG** | Testicular | Diagnosis/monitoring |
| **PSA** | Prostate | Screening/surveillance |

#### Oncologic Emergencies

**Surgical:**

* Tumor bleeding
* Intestinal obstruction
* Spinal cord compression

**Medical:**

* SVC syndrome

**Indications:**

* Symptom relief in incurable disease

	* Bypass for obstruction

	* Metastasectomy for symptom control

## 6\. TRAUMA AND ATLS

### PRIMARY SURVEY - ABCDE

#### A - Airway with C-spine Protection

**Assessment:**

* Look for obstruction signs
* Talking = initially patent airway
* Assume C-spine injury in trauma

**Management:**

* Clear airway (suction, jaw thrust)
* Intubate with inline stabilization
* **Cricothyrotomy** if can't intubate/ventilate

#### B - Breathing and Ventilation

**Life-Threatening Chest Injuries:**

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Injury | Signs | Immediate Treatment |
| **Tension Pneumothorax** | Absent breath sounds, JVD, tracheal deviation, hypotension | Needle decompression → chest tube |
| **Open Pneumothorax** | Sucking chest wound | 3-sided occlusive dressing |
| **Flail Chest** | Paradoxical movement | Pain control, ventilation support |
| **Massive Hemothorax** | Shock + dull percussion | Chest tube, surgery if >1500mL or >200mL/hr |
| **Cardiac Tamponade** | Beck's triad | Pericardiocentesis or thoracotomy |

#### C - Circulation with Hemorrhage Control

**Shock Classification:** (See Critical Care section for details)
**Hemorrhage Sources ("On the floor and 4 more"):**

* External
* Chest
* Abdomen
* Retroperitoneum/pelvis
* Long bones

**Management:**

* 2 large-bore IVs
* Warm crystalloid initially
* Activate MTP early
* **1:1:1** ratio (RBC:FFP:Platelets)
* Permissive hypotension (SBP ~90)

**Pelvic Fracture:**

* Apply pelvic binder
* Consider IR embolization

#### D - Disability (Neurologic)

**Quick Assessment:**

* **AVPU** (Alert, Voice, Pain, Unresponsive)
* **GCS** (≤8 = intubate)
* Pupil exam
* Motor/sensory check

**Management:**

* Maintain C-spine immobilization
* If signs of herniation:
	* Elevate head 30°
	* Mannitol or hypertonic saline
	* Prevent hypotension/hypoxia

#### E - Exposure/Environment

**Actions:**

* Completely undress patient
* Log-roll to check back
* **Prevent hypothermia** (warm blankets, fluids)

### ADJUNCTS TO PRIMARY SURVEY

#### FAST Ultrasound Views

1. Pericardial
2. Perihepatic (Morison's pouch)
3. Perisplenic
4. Pelvic
5. Extended (eFAST): Check for pneumothorax

**Positive FAST:**

* Unstable → OR
* Stable → CT for details

#### Initial X-rays

* AP chest
* AP pelvis
* Lateral C-spine (or CT spine)

### SECONDARY SURVEY

**Systematic head-to-toe exam after primary survey complete**
**Key Points:**

* **AMPLE History**:
	* **A**llergies
	* **M**edications
	* **P**ast medical history/Pregnancy
	* **L**ast meal
	* **E**vents of injury

### SPECIFIC INJURY MANAGEMENT

#### Head Injuries

**Indications for Surgery:**

* Epidural hematoma with mass effect
* Acute subdural with midline shift
* Depressed skull fracture

**ICP Management:**

* Head elevation 30°
* Sedation
* Hyperosmolar therapy
* Target CO₂ 30-35 (temporary)

#### Abdominal Trauma

**Indications for Immediate Laparotomy:**

* Evisceration
* Peritonitis
* Hemodynamic instability with positive FAST

**Solid Organ Injuries:**

* Many managed non-operatively if stable
* Serial exams and H/H monitoring
* Angioembolization for active bleeding

#### Orthopedic Injuries

**Open Fractures:**

* IV antibiotics immediately
* Surgical debridement within 6-24h

**Compartment Syndrome (5 P's):**

1. **P**ain (out of proportion)
2. **P**allor
3. **P**ulselessness (late)
4. **P**aresthesias
5. **P**aralysis (late)

Treatment: **Emergent fasciotomy**

### DAMAGE CONTROL SURGERY

**Principles:**

1. Control hemorrhage
2. Control contamination
3. Temporary closure
4. ICU resuscitation
5. Definitive repair later

**Lethal Triad:**

1. Hypothermia
2. Acidosis
3. Coagulopathy

### SPECIAL POPULATIONS

#### Pediatric Trauma

* Compensate longer before hypotension
* Fluid bolus: 20 mL/kg
* Blood: 10 mL/kg
* Consider NAT if injury pattern suspicious

#### Geriatric Trauma

* "Normal" BP may be relative hypotension
* High risk of head bleeds on anticoagulants
* Lower threshold for imaging

## 7\. ETHICS AND PROFESSIONALISM

### FOUR PILLARS OF MEDICAL ETHICS

#### 1\. Autonomy

**Definition:** Respect for patient's right to make decisions
**Applications:**

* Informed consent
* Advance directives
* Treatment refusals
* Confidentiality

**Special Considerations:**

* Jehovah's Witnesses refusing blood
* Capacity assessment
* Surrogate decision-makers

#### 2\. Beneficence

**Definition:** Act in patient's best interest
**Applications:**

* Recommend beneficial treatments
* Advocate for patient welfare
* Provide skilled care

#### 3\. Non-maleficence

**Definition:** "First, do no harm"
**Applications:**

* Follow standards of care
* Disclose errors honestly
* Avoid unnecessary procedures

#### 4\. Justice

**Definition:** Fair distribution of resources
**Applications:**

* Treat patients equally
* Fair organ allocation
* EMTALA compliance

### INFORMED CONSENT

#### Essential Elements

1. **Capacity** to decide
2. **Voluntary** decision
3. **Adequate information**:
	* Diagnosis
	* Procedure details
	* Risks and benefits
	* Alternatives
	* Consequences of refusing

#### Special Situations

**Emergency Exception:**

* Unconscious patient
* No surrogate available
* Life-threatening condition
* Implied consent applies

**Minors:**

* Generally need parental consent
* Exceptions:
	* Emergencies
	* Emancipated minors
	* Specific conditions (STDs, contraception)

### CAPACITY AND SURROGATES

#### Assessing Capacity

Patient must be able to:

1. Understand information
2. Appreciate consequences
3. Reason through options
4. Communicate choice

#### Surrogate Decision-Making

**Hierarchy (if no designated proxy):**

1. Spouse
2. Adult children
3. Parents
4. Siblings
5. Other relatives

**Standards:**

* **Substituted judgment**: What patient would want
* **Best interests**: If wishes unknown

### PROFESSIONALISM

#### Key Components

**Competence:**

* Maintain skills
* Know limitations
* Seek help when needed

**Communication:**

* Respectful team interactions
* Clear patient communication
* Constructive conflict resolution

**Integrity:**

* Honest billing
* Transparent about conflicts of interest
* Report impaired colleagues

**Cultural Sensitivity:**

* Respect diverse values
* Use interpreters
* Accommodate religious practices

#### Error Disclosure

**Best Practices:**

1. Prompt disclosure
2. Private setting
3. Clear explanation
4. Sincere apology
5. Plan to prevent recurrence

### CHALLENGING SCENARIOS

#### End-of-Life Care

* Respect advance directives
* Discuss prognosis honestly
* Transition to comfort care when appropriate
* Don't abandon patient

#### Futile Care

* Can decline to offer non-beneficial treatment
* Communicate reasoning compassionately
* Offer alternatives
* Consider ethics consultation

#### Resource Allocation

* Use evidence-based guidelines
* Apply criteria consistently
* Document reasoning
* Avoid discrimination

## 8\. IMAGING AND DIAGNOSTIC STUDIES

### IMAGING MODALITIES COMPARISON

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Modality | Best For | Pros | Cons |
| **X-ray** | Bones, chest, foreign bodies | Fast, cheap, low radiation | Limited soft tissue detail |
| **Ultrasound** | Gallbladder, DVT, pregnancy | No radiation, real-time | Operator-dependent |
| **CT** | Trauma, acute abdomen, staging | Detailed, fast | High radiation, contrast risks |
| **MRI** | Brain/spine, soft tissue | Excellent contrast, no radiation | Slow, expensive, contraindications |
| **Nuclear** | Function, metastases | Whole body screening | Poor anatomic detail |

### X-RAY

#### Key Uses in Surgery

* **CXR**: Pneumothorax, line placement
* **AXR**: Obstruction, free air
* **Skeletal**: Fractures

#### Reading Tips

* Two views for localization
* Look for free air on upright films
* Check all edges and corners

### ULTRASOUND

#### FAST Exam Components

1. Pericardial fluid
2. Perihepatic (Morison's)
3. Perisplenic
4. Pelvis
5. Pneumothorax (eFAST)

#### Common Applications

**Abdominal:**

* **RUQ**: Gallstones, cholecystitis
* **Appendix**: If thin patient
* **AAA screening**: Men >65 with smoking

**Vascular:**

* **DVT**: Non-compressible vein
* **Carotid duplex**: Stenosis assessment

**Procedural:**

* Central line guidance
* Abscess localization

### CT SCAN

#### Contrast Considerations

**IV Contrast Risks:**

* Allergic reactions
* Contrast nephropathy
* Avoid if GFR <30

**Oral Contrast:**

* Not used in trauma
* Water-soluble if perforation suspected

#### Common Protocols

**Trauma Pan-Scan:**

* Head without contrast
* C-spine without contrast
* Chest/abdomen/pelvis with IV contrast

**Appendicitis CT:**

* IV contrast preferred
* Shows thickened appendix >6mm
* Fat stranding

**PE Protocol:**

* CT pulmonary angiography
* Timed for pulmonary arterial phase

### MRI

#### Contraindications

* Non-MRI compatible pacemakers
* Certain aneurysm clips
* Cochlear implants
* Metal in eyes

#### Key Surgical Uses

**Neuro:**

* Cord compression
* Brain lesions

**Musculoskeletal:**

* Soft tissue tumors
* Ligament injuries
* Osteomyelitis

**Abdominal:**

* MRCP for biliary tree
* Liver lesion characterization
* Rectal cancer staging

#### Gadolinium Precautions

* Risk of NSF if GFR <30
* Use lowest dose needed
* Consider alternatives

### NUCLEAR MEDICINE

#### Common Studies

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Study | Use | Finding |
| **HIDA** | Acute cholecystitis | Non-filling GB |
| **V/Q Scan** | PE (if CT contraindicated) | V/Q mismatch |
| **Bone Scan** | Metastases | Hot spots |
| **Sestamibi** | Parathyroid adenoma | Persistent uptake |
| **Tagged RBC** | GI bleeding | Accumulation at bleed site |

#### PET Scan

* Uses FDG (glucose analog)
* Shows metabolic activity
* Combined with CT for localization
* False positives: Inflammation

### ENDOSCOPY

#### Upper Endoscopy (EGD)

* Evaluates to 2nd portion duodenum
* Can treat bleeding
* Biopsy capability

#### Colonoscopy

* Screening and diagnostic
* Polypectomy
* Bleeding control

#### ERCP

* Therapeutic for bile duct stones
* Sphincterotomy
* Stent placement
* Risk: Pancreatitis

### CHOOSING THE RIGHT STUDY

#### Acute Abdomen

1. **RUQ pain**: US first
2. **Appendicitis**: CT (US if pediatric/pregnant)
3. **Obstruction**: CT with oral contrast
4. **Free air**: Upright CXR or CT

#### Trauma

1. **Unstable + FAST positive**: OR
2. **Stable**: CT pan-scan
3. **Isolated extremity**: X-ray first

#### Cancer Staging

* **Most solid tumors**: CT chest/abd/pelvis
* **Add PET**: For FDG-avid tumors
* **Add MRI**: Brain mets, liver detail

#### Vascular

* **DVT**: Ultrasound
* **PE**: CTPA
* **AAA**: US for screening, CT for planning

### RADIATION SAFETY

#### ALARA Principle

"As Low As Reasonably Achievable"

#### Relative Radiation Doses

* CXR: 0.1 mSv
* CT abdomen: 8-10 mSv
* CT trauma (whole body): ~20 mSv

#### Pregnancy Considerations

* Always ask if possibly pregnant
* Use US or MRI when possible
* Shield if must use X-ray
* First trimester most sensitive

## 9\. WOUND HEALING AND SURGICAL INFECTIONS

### WOUND HEALING PHASES

#### Phase 1: Hemostasis (Immediate)

* Vasoconstriction → dilation
* Platelet plug formation
* Coagulation cascade
* Fibrin clot scaffold

#### Phase 2: Inflammation (Days 0-4)

**Timeline:**

* 0-48h: Neutrophils dominate
* 48-72h: Macrophages take over

**Key Cells:**

* **Neutrophils**: Phagocytosis, kill bacteria
* **Macrophages**: Essential orchestrators
	* Release growth factors (VEGF, FGF, TGF-β)

#### Phase 3: Proliferation (Days 4-14)

**Components:**

* **Fibroplasia**: Type III collagen synthesis
* **Angiogenesis**: New vessel formation
* **Granulation tissue**: Red, granular appearance
* **Epithelialization**:
	* Primary intention: 24-48h
	* Secondary intention: ~1mm/day
* **Contraction**: Myofibroblasts

#### Phase 4: Remodeling (Weeks to 1+ year)

* Type III → Type I collagen
* Tensile strength timeline:
	* 3 weeks: 20%
	* 2 months: 50%
	* 6-12 months: 80% (maximum)

### FACTORS AFFECTING HEALING

#### Local Factors

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Factor | Effect | Management |
| **Infection** | Prolongs inflammation | Debride, antibiotics |
| **Poor blood supply** | Ischemia → poor healing | Revascularize |
| **Foreign bodies** | Chronic inflammation | Remove |
| **Mechanical stress** | Dehiscence risk | Immobilize, reduce tension |
| **Radiation** | Endarteritis, fibrosis | Consider HBO therapy |

#### Systemic Factors

**Nutrition:**

* **Protein**: Essential for collagen
* **Vitamin C**: Collagen hydroxylation
* **Zinc**: DNA/RNA polymerase cofactor
* **Vitamin A**: Counteracts steroids

**Medical Conditions:**

* **Diabetes**: ↓Neutrophil function
* **Smoking**: Vasoconstriction, ↓O₂
* **Steroids**: ↓Inflammation, ↓collagen

**Age:**

* Elderly heal slower
* Often have comorbidities

### WOUND MANAGEMENT

#### Closure Methods

**Primary Intention:**

* Immediate closure
* Clean wounds
* Minimal scarring

**Secondary Intention:**

* Left open
* Infected/contaminated wounds
* Broader scar

**Tertiary (Delayed Primary):**

* Initially open
* Close after 3-5 days if clean

#### Special Techniques

**Negative Pressure (Wound VAC):**

* Promotes granulation
* Reduces edema
* Decreases dressing changes

**Hyperbaric Oxygen:**

* Indications:
	* Diabetic foot ulcers
	* Radiation injury
	* Refractory osteomyelitis

**Skin Grafts:**

* **STSG**: Large defects, needs granulation
* **FTSG**: Small defects, better cosmesis

#### Chronic Wounds

**Pressure Ulcers:**

* Stages I-IV
* Prevention: Reposition q2h
* Offload pressure

**Diabetic Foot Ulcers:**

* Neuropathy + ischemia
* Offload (total contact cast)
* Revascularize if needed

**Venous Stasis Ulcers:**

* Medial ankle location
* Compression therapy
* Elevation

### SURGICAL SITE INFECTIONS (SSI)

#### Classification by Depth

1. **Superficial Incisional**
	* Skin/subcutaneous only
	* Open wound, local care
2. **Deep Incisional**
	* Fascia/muscle involved
	* May need debridement
3. **Organ/Space**
	* Any manipulated cavity
	* Often needs drainage

#### Common Pathogens by Surgery Type

|     |     |
| --- | --- |
| Surgery Type | Common Organisms |
| **Clean** | S. aureus, S. epidermidis |
| **Abdominal** | Polymicrobial: E. coli, Bacteroides |
| **Prosthetic** | Coag-neg staph (biofilm) |
| **Diabetic foot** | Polymicrobial + Pseudomonas |

#### SSI Prevention Bundle

**Preoperative:**

* Chlorhexidine shower
* Clip (don't shave) hair
* Prophylactic antibiotics (within 60 min)
* Treat remote infections

**Intraoperative:**

* Maintain normothermia
* Control glucose <180
* Minimize tissue trauma
* Change gloves before closure

**Postoperative:**

* Remove drains/catheters ASAP
* Appropriate wound care
* Monitor for signs of infection

### NECROTIZING INFECTIONS

#### Types and Treatment

**Type I (Polymicrobial):**

* Mixed aerobes/anaerobes
* Common in perineum (Fournier's)

**Type II (Monomicrobial):**

* Group A Strep or S. aureus
* "Flesh-eating disease"

**Type III (Gas Gangrene):**

* Clostridium perfringens
* Muscle necrosis
* Crepitus

**Universal Treatment:**

1. **Emergent surgical debridement**
2. Broad antibiotics:
	* Carbapenem/pip-tazo
	* Plus clindamycin (toxin inhibition)
	* Plus vancomycin (MRSA)
3. Serial debridements
4. Consider HBO for Clostridial

#### Clinical Pearls

**Warning Signs:**

* Pain out of proportion
* Rapid progression
* Systemic toxicity
* Crepitus (gas-forming)
* Skin color changes

**Management Principles:**

* Don't delay for imaging
* Debride to healthy bleeding tissue
* May require amputation
* High mortality even with treatment

### SPECIAL WOUND CONSIDERATIONS

#### Bite Wounds

**Dog/Cat Bites:**

* High infection risk
* **Pasteurella** common
* Prophylaxis: Amox-clav
* Don't close (except face)

**Human Bites:**

* **Eikenella** common
* Always treat if over joints
* Never close primarily

#### Contaminated Wounds

**Management:**

* Irrigate copiously
* Debride devitalized tissue
* Consider delayed closure
* Tetanus prophylaxis

#### Wound Complications

**Dehiscence:**

* Usually day 5-10
* Risk factors: Infection, ↑IAP, poor nutrition
* Evisceration = emergency

**Hypertrophic Scars/Keloids:**

* Excess collagen deposition
* Treatment:
	* Steroid injections
	* Silicone sheets
	* Pressure therapy

#### Key Healing Milestones

|     |     |
| --- | --- |
| Time | Achievement |
| 24-48h | Epithelialization (primary closure) |
| 5-7 days | Remove skin sutures (face) |
| 10-14 days | Remove sutures (trunk/extremities) |
| 3 weeks | 20% tensile strength |
| 6-8 weeks | Safe for heavy lifting (fascia ~70%) |
| 6-12 months | Maximum strength (~80%) |
