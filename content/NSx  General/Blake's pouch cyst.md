### Description
- is a cystic appearing structure
- represents posterior ballooning of the [inferior medullary velum](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/inferior-medullary-velum?lang=us) into the [cisterna magna](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/cisterna-magna?lang=us), below and posterior to the [vermis](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/vermis?lang=us) that communicates with an open [fourth ventricle](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/fourth-ventricle?lang=us).
- It is c<span style="background:#fff88f">aused by a failure of the regression of Blake's pouch secondary to the non-perforation of the </span>[foramen of Magendie](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/foramen-of-magendie-1?lang=us).
### Presentation
* in childhood or adulthood
* signs of hydrocephalus
	* headache
	* vomiting
	* blurred or double vision
* later in life or be even asymptomatic
	* p<span style="background:#fff88f">robably due to adequate CSF flow through</span> [lateral foramina of Luschka](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/lateral-apertures-of-luschka?lang=us)
### Pathology
- Blake's pouch, also known as the rudimental fourth ventricular [tela choroidea](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/tela-choroidea?lang=us) is a normal transient structure that regresses during embryological development, usually by 12 weeks gestation
- Regresses via development of fenestrations to form the [foramen of Magendie](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/foramen-of-magendie-1?lang=us) (which forms up to the fourth month of gestation).
- Persistent Blake's pouch cysts occur due to failed perforation of the [foramen of Magendie](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/foramen-of-magendie-1?lang=us).
	- As the foramina of Luschka open later than the foramen of Magendie during the embryologic development, this non-perforation of the foramen of Magendie causes enlargement of the [ventricular system](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/ventricular-system?lang=us) until the foramina of Luschka open and establishes a precarious compensation via [CSF](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/cerebrospinal-fluid-1?lang=us) flowing into the cisterns.
- Blake's pouch cysts used to be classified as part of the [Dandy-Walker continuum](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/dandy-walker-spectrum?lang=us), however, this entity is not related to cerebellar or fourth ventricle malformations.
