**PRES (Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome)** is a **neurologic disorder** characterized by acute or subacute onset of symptoms due to **vasogenic edema** in the posterior cerebral hemispheres. It is **potentially reversible** if recognized and managed early.
### **Key Clinical Features**
* **Neurological symptoms:**
	* Headache
	* Seizures (often generalized tonic-clonic)
	* Visual disturbances (e.g., cortical blindness, visual neglect)
	* Altered mental status (confusion, encephalopathy)
	* Focal deficits (less common)
### **Risk Factors / Triggers**
* **Hypertensive emergency**
* **Eclampsia/preeclampsia**
* **Immunosuppressive therapy** (e.g., cyclosporine, tacrolimus)
* **Renal failure**
* **Sepsis or autoimmune disease**
### **Pathophysiology**
* ==**Disrupted cerebral autoregulation**== → ==hyperperfusion==
* **Endothelial dysfunction** → blood-brain barrier breakdown
* **Vasogenic edema**, predominantly in the **posterior circulation** (parieto-occipital lobes)
### **Imaging Findings (MRI)**
* T2/FLAIR hyperintensities in:
	* **Parieto-occipital** lobes (most common)
	* Frontal lobes, cerebellum, brainstem in atypical cases
* **No diffusion restriction** (vs. infarct)

### **Diagnosis**
* **Clinical context + neuroimaging (MRI)**
* Rule out mimics: stroke, CNS infection, autoimmune encephalitis
### **Management**
* **Treat underlying cause** (e.g., control BP, stop offending drug)
* **Seizure management** if needed
* **Supportive care**
### **Prognosis**
* Usually reversible within days to weeks
* Delayed treatment may result in:
	* Cerebral infarction
	* Hemorrhage
	* Permanent neurologic deficits
