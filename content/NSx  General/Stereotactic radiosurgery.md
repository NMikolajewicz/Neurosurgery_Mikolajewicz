## 'Stereotactic'
* refers to the utilization of a system of 3-D coordinates to ensure that treatment is focussed in a highly precise
* allows smaller margins and higher doses per treatment.
##  Stereotactic radiosurgery
* involves a smaller number of treatments (typically just one) 
* compared to [conventional radiotherapy](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/conventional-radiation-therapy?lang=us) treatment schedules which require up to thirty treatments (a.k.a. fractions).
##  Methods
* [IMRT](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/intensity-modulated-radiation-therapy?lang=us) linear accelerators (requiring more advanced patient immobilization or image guidance)
* dedicated systems such as [Gamma Knife](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/gamma-knife-3?lang=us) or [CyberKnife](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/cyberknife-1?lang=us).
## Indications
* difficult **tumor location**
* **patient ill**
* patient preference for **less invasive treatment**
## Gamma Knife radiosurgery indications
### Brain tumor
* Radiosurgery can manage small noncancerous, also called benign, brain tumors.
* Radiosurgery also can manage cancerous, also called malignant, brain tumors.
### Arteriovenous malformation (AVM).
* AVMs are tangles of arteries and veins in the brain.
* These tangles are not typical.
* AVMs if not treated, may "steal" the typical flow of blood from the brain. This can cause a stroke or lead to bleeding in the brain.
	* Radiosurgery causes the AVMs to close off over time. This lowers the risk of bleeding.
### Trigeminal neuralgia
* After treatment, pain relief can happen within a few days to a few months.
* Acoustic neuroma

### Pituitary tumors
* Radiosurgery can be used to shrink the tumor and lessen the irregular secretion of pituitary hormones.

## Complications
* swelling
* hair loss
* fatigue
