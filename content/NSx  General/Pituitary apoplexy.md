## Definition

- Sudden expansion of a sellar mass (usually an adenoma) from hemorrhage/necrosis → abrupt neuro-endocrine decline .
- Typical presentation: 
	- thunderclap/paroxysmal headache ± visual loss and/or ophthalmoplegia; 
	- +/- acute hypopituitarism .

## Initial Management

- Give ==stress-dose glucocorticoids immediately== (do not wait for imaging/labs) .
- Protect airway/pressure/pain; 
- urgent neuro-ophthalmology + endocrinology 
- neurosurgery involvement.
## Imaging

- MRI sellar protocol when stable; CT can show acute sellar hemorrhage if MRI not immediately available (principle).

![[Pasted image 20251108140756.png|500]]

### Surgical Indications
- Visual compromise/ophthalmoplegia or deteriorating neuro status → transsphenoidal decompression.
- Timing: in most cases, ==operate within ~7 days== after stabilization and steroid coverage .

Xia, K., Peng, J., Zhou, Y., Liu, X., Chen, H., Xu, H., ... & Hu, J. (2025). Pituitary apoplexy: surgical or conservative? A meta-analytical insight. _Frontiers in Surgery_, _12_, 1579498.

- ==surgery associated with improvement in ophthalmoplegia==, ==but not visual acuity, VF recovery, or pituitary endocrine function.==
- surgical treatment significantly improved recovery from ocular muscle paralysis compared to conservative treatment (OR: 0.31; 95% CI 0.10–0.92; _p_ = 0.04). 
- However, no statistically significant differences were observed in postoperative recovery of visual acuity (OR: 1.15; 95% CI 0.54–2.44; _p_ = 0.72), visual field recovery (OR: 1.48; 95% CI 0.77–2.82; _p_ = 0.24), or pituitary endocrine function (OR: 0.67; 95% CI 0.27–1.67; _p_ = 0.38).

## Endocrine management pearls

- Everyone is functionally "at risk" for adrenal insufficiency peri-/post-op: 
	- continue steroids and taper thoughtfully; 
	- ==hydrocortisone== for mineralocorticoid effect, ==dexamethasone== for edema; 
	- taper slowly to avoid aseptic (chemical) meningitis .
- Diabetes insipidus can occur with pituitary apoplexy; 
	- ==monitor UOP, serum sodium, serum/urine osms== .
## DI/SIADH choreography

- Post-pituitary surgery water balance can be ==triphasic==: 
	- early DI (≈ days 1–4/5) → transient SIADH-like retention (≈ days 4–9) → possible recurrent or prolonged DI .
- First-line DI management: fluids; if needed, use short-acting vasopressin initially to avoid overshoot hyponatremia when the SIADH-like phase arrives .
## Visual system checks

- Baseline and follow-up formal fields in lesions with suprasellar extension; EOM palsies suggest cavernous sinus involvement .
    
## Pocket reminders

- Steroids first, then imaging.
- If vision or mental status is threatened: TSS within ~1 week.
- Watch the water balance: don't continue vasopressin into the SIADH window.
- Post-op: deliberate steroid taper; track Na/osms and fields.
