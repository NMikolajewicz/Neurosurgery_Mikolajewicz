## Antimicrobial coverage (Bug → Drug)

- **MSSA** → cefazolin or nafcillin/oxacillin; 
	- alt: ceftriaxone; severe β-lactam allergy: vancomycin
	
- **MRSA/MRSE** → vancomycin; (alts: linezolid; daptomycin _not for CNS/pneumonia_)

- **Streptococci (incl. pneumo)** → ceftriaxone or penicillin/ampicillin; 
	- add vanc if high-MIC pneumo suspected
	
- **Enterococcus faecalis** → ampicillin (± ceftriaxone synergy for endocarditis); 
	- alt: vancomycin
	
- **Enterococcus faecium/VRE** → linezolid or daptomycin* (_non-CNS_)

- **Listeria** → ampicillin (± gentamicin); 
	- alt if allergic: TMP-SMX)
	
- **Pseudomonas aeruginosa** → cefepime or ceftazidime or piperacillin-tazobactam or meropenem; (alts: ciprofloxacin/levofloxacin, aminoglycoside)

- **ESBL Enterobacterales** → carbapenem (e.g., meropenem)

- **AmpC producers (Enterobacter, Serratia, Citrobacter)** → cefepime or carbapenem

- **Stenotrophomonas** → TMP-SMX (alts: levofloxacin, minocycline)

- **Acinetobacter (MDR risk)** → high-dose ampicillin-sulbactam or carbapenem (variable); 
	- ==ID consult for MDR==
	
- **Anaerobes (B. fragilis, oral anaerobes)** → metronidazole; or piperacillin-tazobactam; or carbapenem (moxifloxacin less reliable vs B. fragilis)

- **Oral flora (dental/sinus)** → ampicillin-sulbactam or ceftriaxone + metronidazole 
	- require strep (amp/ctx) + anaerobe (flagyl) coverage 

## CNS scenarios (empiric, then narrow by cultures)

- **Post-op meningitis/ventriculitis/shunt** → vancomycin **+** cefepime/ceftazidime/**or** meropenem  
	- _Add metronidazole if sinus/ear/dental source or brain abscess._
	
- **Brain abscess (community; otogenic/sinogenic)** → ceftriaxone **+** ==metronidazole== 
	- ± vancomycin if prior healthcare/MRSA risk
	
- **Epidural abscess/vertebral osteo (no device)** → vancomycin **+** ceftriaxone 
	- healthcare or IVDA: swap ceftriaxone → cefepime
	
- **Hardware infection (cranial/spine)** → vancomycin (add gram-neg agent if hospital-acquired)
	- consider adding **rifampin** for staph **only with retained hardware and after bacteremia controlled—never monotherapy**

## Antibiotic coverage (Drug→Bug)

- **Cefazolin**: ==MSSA, streptococci==; no Enterococcus, no anaerobes, no Pseudomonas
- **Nafcillin/oxacillin**: MSSA, streptococci; nothing gram-negative
- **Ampicillin**: Enterococcus faecalis, Listeria, streptococci; (+ sulbactam) adds anaerobes/gram-neg (not Pseudomonas)
- **Piperacillin-tazobactam**: ==broad incl. Pseudomonas + anaerobes; no MRSA==; not first-line for meningitis - ==poor CNS penetration==

- **Ceftriaxone**: strep, many Enterobacterales, Neisseria; ==no Pseudomonas==
- **Ceftazidime**: ==Pseudomonas==; weak gram-positive
- **Cefepime**: Pseudomonas + better gram-positive; AmpC-stable
- **Cefoxitin/cefotetan (cephamycins)**: anaerobes incl. B. fragilis; not Pseudomonas

- **Meropenem**: very ==broad incl. Pseudomonas + anaerobes==; good CNS
- **Ertapenem**: broad but ==**no** Pseudomonas==/Acinetobacter
- **Aztreonam**: gram-neg only incl. Pseudomonas; no gram-pos/anaerobes

- **Vancomycin**: MRSA/MRSE, Enterococcus (not VRE); meningitis dosing needed
- **Linezolid**: MRSA/VRE; good CNS/lung; watch cytopenias/serotonergic meds
- **Daptomycin**: MRSA/VRE; **not** lungs; poor CNS

- **TMP-SMX**: CA-MRSA, Listeria, Nocardia, Stenotrophomonas; good CNS
- **Metronidazole**: anaerobes (incl. B. fragilis); excellent CNS; no aerobes
- **Clindamycin**: strep + mouth/skin anaerobes; unreliable vs B. fragilis; no gram-neg rods

- **Fluoroquinolones**:
    - **Cipro**: Pseudomonas strong; weak Strep
    - **Levo**: Strep + some Pseudomonas
    - **Moxi**: Strep + anaerobes; **no** Pseudomonas

- **Rifampin**: staph biofilm adjunct with hardware; never alone; many interactions

- **Aminoglycosides** (genta/tobra/amikacin): gram-neg incl. Pseudomonas; synergy for Enterococcus; poor CNS (consider intrathecal only with ID/NSGY guidance)
## CNS penetration (practical)

### **Good/used in CNS**
- ceftriaxone, cefotaxime, cefepime/ceftazidime, **meropenem**, vancomycin (meningitis dosing), **linezolid**, **TMP-SMX**, **metronidazole**, levo/moxi
### **Avoid/limited for meningitis** 
- piperacillin-tazobactam, clindamycin, **daptomycin**, macrolides, tigecycline

## Pathogens by location/source

- Sinus → Brain abscess: Strep anginosus grp (milleri), oral anaerobes (Prevotella, Fusobacterium, Peptostreptococcus), ± H. flu (kids) → think polymicrobial.  

- Dental/odontogenic → Brain abscess: Viridans strep, oral anaerobes, Actinomyces (chronic). 

- Otitis/mastoiditis → Temporal/cerebellar abscess: Strep pneumo, S. pyogenes, Staph aureus, Pseudomonas (chronic ears), anaerobes.  

- Penetrating cranial trauma: S. aureus, coag-neg staph, gram-negative rods (incl. Pseudomonas), anaerobes (if sinus breach).

- Post-neurosurgical (craniotomy/EVD/VP shunt): Coag-neg staph, S. aureus (incl. MRSA), Cutibacterium acnes, Pseudomonas/other GNRs. 

- CSF leak/ENT skull base: skin flora + oral anaerobes; chronic sinus disease skews to S. aureus + anaerobes. 

- Hematogenous brain abscess: S. aureus (IVDU), viridans/HACEK (endocarditis), Nocardia (immunocomp), Candida/Aspergillus (profoundly immunocomp).
### **Spine/epidural space**
- Vertebral osteomyelitis (hematogenous): S. aureus most common; ==GNRs (E. coli, Klebsiella) if urinary source==/older; Pseudomonas (IVDU).  
- Spinal epidural abscess: S. aureus, then streptococci, GNRs (healthcare/IVDU).  
- Post-op spine/hardware: S. aureus, MRSE, C. acnes (indolent), ± GNRs.
### **Hospital/ICU**  
- ==VAP/HAP:== Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, Enterobacterales, MRSA.  
- ==CLABSI==: Coag-neg staph, S. aureus, Enterococcus, Candida.  
- ==CAUTI==: E. coli, Klebsiella, Proteus, Enterococcus, Pseudomonas. (KEEPP)
### **Community respiratory**
- CAP: Strep pneumo, H. flu, atypicals (Mycoplasma/Chlamydia/Legionella); ==aspiration → oral anaerobes + streptococci.  ==
- ==Chronic aspiration/lung abscess: Anaerobes, S. anginosus grp==.
### **Skin/soft tissue**
- ==Cellulitis/abscess: S. aureus (incl. MRSA), S. pyogenes==.  
- Human bite/clenched-fist: Eikenella corrodens, streptococci, anaerobes.  
- Dog/cat bite: Pasteurella multocida, Capnocytophaga (asplenia risk), anaerobes.  
- Burns: early S. aureus, later Pseudomonas + GNRs.
### **Abdomen/pelvis**
- Intra-abdominal abscess: B. fragilis (anaerobes) + Enterobacterales.  
- Cholangitis: E. coli, Klebsiella, Enterococcus.  
- Female pelvic: Polymicrobial incl. anaerobes, enterics, Strep agalactiae.
### **GU**
- ==Uncomplicated UTI==: E. coli; Proteus (struvite/alkaline urine), Klebsiella; Enterococcus in catheterized/older.   (==KEEP; klebsiella, e.coli, enterococcus, proteus)==
- Prostatitis: Enterobacterales.
### **Environmental/exposure clues**  
- Freshwater wounds: Aeromonas.  
- Saltwater/shellfish: Vibrio vulnificus.  
- Soil/dust (steroids/T-cell down): Nocardia (brain abscess).  
- DKA/iron overload: Mucorales (rhinocerebral).  
- ==Pigeons/AIDs== (CD4 low): ==Cryptococcus== (meningoencephalitis).  
- Unpasteurized ==dairy==/pregnancy/elderly: ==Listeria== (meningoencephalitis).
### **Mnemonics that map sources → bugs**:  
- ESKAPE (ICU bad actors) = E. faecium, S. aureus, Klebsiella, Acinetobacter, Pseudomonas, Enterobacter.  
- ==HACEK== (endocarditis → brain seeds) = Haemophilus, Aggregatibacter, Cardiobacterium, Eikenella, Kingella.  
- SPACE (AmpC risk; devices/ICU) = Serratia, Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, Citrobacter, Enterobacter.  
- “Mouth = Strep + Anaerobes” (think anginosus + anaerobes for dental/sinus spread).  
- “Water Woes” = Fresh→Aeromonas, Salt→Vibrio.
### Pearls
- **Post-neurosurgical & shunt infections** are ==**skin flora first**== (coag-neg staph, **C. acnes**, S. aureus); **GNRs** rise with ICU/long LOS.  
- **Sinus/dental/ear sources** → assume **mixed aerobic–anaerobic**; pure “mono-bug” is the exception.  
- **IVDU** raises odds of **S. aureus**, **Pseudomonas**, and **Candida** seeding brain/spine.  
- **Indolent hardware infections** (cranial/spine) → think ==**C. acnes** when cultures are “slow/negative.”==  
- ==**Aspiration** patterns (dependent lobes) → **anaerobes + streptococci*==*; don’t forget oral source control (dentition, swallowing).
