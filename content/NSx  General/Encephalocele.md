### Risk for acquired encephalocele (in adults)
* trauma
* increased ICP
	* most common in locations with thin bone
		* temen tympani, middle cranial fossa pits
### Clinical features
* protuberant mass +/- pulsatile
* neurological deficits
	* correlated with extent of cortex herniation
* seizures
	* if malformed cortex
* associated with other intracranial abnormalities
	* hydrocephalus
### Prognosis
* best in sporadic frontal encephaloceles
* occipital encephaloceles more commonly associated with hydrocephalus, worse cognitive outcome
* temporal encephalocele are highly epileptogenic
* poor if encephaloceles contain large amount of neural tissue
### Management
* encephalocele contents
	* relocate into cranium
	* amputate
* watertight dural closure
* bone grafting to cover calvarial defect
* if CSF leak - urgent surgery
	* <12-24h to prevent meningitis
