## SMA Syndrome Overview

The Supplementary Motor Area (SMA) syndrome is a well-documented neurological condition that occurs following surgery in the dorsomedial prefrontal lobe. It's most commonly seen after resection of low-grade gliomas, which frequently develop in this region.
### Anatomy and Function of the SMA
* **Location**: Dorsomedial aspect of the superior frontal gyrus
* **Boundaries**: Limited by the primary motor cortex posteriorly, cingulate sulcus medially, and approximately 15mm from midline laterally
* **Subdivisions**: Pre-SMA (rostral) and SMA proper (caudal), divided by the vertical line crossing the anterior commissure (VCA-line)
### Key Functions
1. **Motor sequencing**: Determines the order and timing of motor actions
2. **Negative motor network**: Involved in inhibition of motor actions during execution
3. **Domain-general sequence processing**: Applied to motor, language, and music functions
4. **Somatotopic organization**: Lower limb representation at the caudal border, face representation at the rostral end
### White Matter Connectivity
The SMA has extensive connections via:
1. **Association fibers**:
	U-fibers to primary motor cortex, middle frontal gyrus, and cingulum Superior longitudinal fascicle (SLF I) to superior parietal lobule Frontal aslant tract (FAT) connecting to inferior frontal gyrus Frontostriatal tract (FST) to caudate nucleus
2. **Commissural fibers**:
	Strong interhemispheric connections via corpus callosum Crossed FAT connecting to contralateral premotor cortex
3. **Projection fibers**:
	An estimated 10-33% of corticospinal tract fibers originate in the SMA These modulate spinal motor neuron excitability and may influence inhibitory interneurons
## Clinical Aspects of SMA Syndrome
### Symptoms
* **Motor**: Global akinesia (reduced movement) of the contralateral body with mild hypotonia
* **Speech**: Reduced spontaneous speech or mutism in dominant hemisphere lesions
* **Preservation**: Normal comprehension, no paraphasia or dysnomia
### Recovery Pattern (Three Stages)
1. **Immediate postoperative**: Profound deficits
2. **Days to weeks**: Progressive recovery of strength and speech
3. **Weeks to months**: Near complete recovery for most patients
### Risk Factors for Developing SMA Syndrome
* Extent of SMA resection (>90% associated with higher incidence)
* Resection of SMA proper (posterior to VCA-line)
* Trespassing the medial part of SMA and adjacent cingulate gyrus
* Interruption of callosal fibers crucial for recovery
### Neuroplasticity Mechanisms in Recovery
Recovery appears to depend on:
1. Restoration of interhemispheric connectivity
2. The contralateral (healthy) SMA playing a crucial compensatory role
3. Development of connections between contralateral SMA and ipsilateral motor areas
### Prognosis
* Most patients make a full recovery
* A minority continue with mild motor and speech dysfunction
* Rarely, no recovery occurs (permanent deficit)
