> [!summary] **GN vs GP Bacteremia — Quick Hits** _(see also [[Neurosurgery/Neurosurgery_Mikolajewicz/content/NSx  General/Antibiotics]])_
> 
> - **Sources:** **GN = urine/abdomen/VAP/HAP**; **GP = ports/skin** (think **S. aureus**).
>     
> - **GN presentation:** abrupt fever/rigors, **early hypotension**; **↑PCT**, thrombocytopenia/DIC.
>     
> - **SAB hallmarks:** **persistent bacteremia**, **metastatic** until proven otherwise.
>     
> - **GN workup:** chase **source** (obstruction, biliary, devices); echo only if **Salmonella** or persistent positives.
>     
> - **SAB workup:** **repeat cultures to clearance + TEE**; search endocarditis, spine, psoas, emboli.
>     
> - **GN therapy:** ceftriaxone (cUTI) or **cefepime/pip-tazo/mero** if severe/HCA; early source control; **~7 days IV±PO** once stable.
>     
> - **SAB therapy:** empiric **vancomycin** if MRSA risk; **remove lines**; **≥14 days IV** (longer if complicated); avoid oral step-down.
>
## Gram-negative vs Gram-positive bacteremia 
### **Likely source (history/exam clues)**  

- **Gram-negative (GN)**: 
	- **Urinary** (dysuria, flank pain, obstruction)
	- **biliary/abdomen** (RUQ pain, jaundice, post-ERCP)
	- **VAP/HAP**
	- water/wound exposures.
- **Gram-positive (GP)**: 
	- **Skin/soft tissue** (cellulitis/abscess)
	- **catheters/ports/shunts**
	- **post-op wounds**
	- dental/skin breaches
	- **S. aureus** often with a **line or skin** portal.
### **Hemodynamics & presentation**  

- **GN**: 
	- Tends to present with **abrupt high fevers, rigors, early hypotension** (endotoxin/LPS effect), 
	- **DIC/thrombocytopenia** more common in severe cases.  
- **GP**: 
	- Variable sepsis; 
	- **S. aureus** often has **focal pain** pointing to metastatic seeding; 
	- **toxic shock** patterns with some staph/strep (fever, rash, shock) are toxin-mediated outliers.
### **Laboratory patterns (clues, not rules)**  

- **GN**: 
	- **Procalcitonin usually higher**; 
	- **thrombocytopenia/leukopenia** more frequent with fulminant endotoxemia; 
	- UA often positive if urinary source; cholestatic LFTs with biliary source.  
- **GP**: 
	- **Persistent bacteremia** more typical with **S. aureus**; 
	- inflammatory markers up but PCT can be lower than GN for a similar illness severity.

### **Complications to anticipate**  

- **GN**: **Septic shock**, **DIC**, organ hypoperfusion; **endovascular foci less common** (exceptions: **Salmonella** → endovascular).  
- **GP**: 
	- **Metastatic infection** hallmark of **S. aureus**
	- think **endocarditis, vertebral osteomyelitis, epidural/psoas abscess, septic emboli**.

### **Workup differences**  

- **GN**: 
	- Hunt the **source first**
		- renal ultrasound/CT for obstruction
		- RUQ/CT for biliary/abdomen
		- evaluate devices; **echo not routine**, except **Salmonella** or persistent positives. 
- **GP (especially S. aureus)**: 
	- **Always** repeat blood cultures to clearance
	- **echocardiography (TEE preferred)** for most SAB; aggressive search for metastatic sites.

### **Therapy & duration (uncomplicated cases)**  

- **GN**: 
	- Cover source 
		- often **ceftriaxone** for community UTI-source
		- **cefepime/pip-tazo/meropenem** if sick/healthcare exposure
	- **source control early**; **7 days total** (IV±PO) usually sufficient once stable and narrowed.  
- **GP**
	- **S. aureus** needs **at least 14 days IV** (longer if complicated)
	- **remove infected lines**
	- broaden early with **vancomycin** if MRSA risk while evaluating for deep foci.
### **Mnemonic anchors**  
- **"NEG = Nephro/Enteric/Gut"** → think **urine & abdomen**.  
- **"POS = Ports & Pus"** → think **lines & skin** (and **S. aureus** seeding).  
- **"7 if simple (GN), 14 if sticky (SAB)"** for typical durations.
### Pearls
- **GN shock** can spiral fast—don't miss **obstruction** or an undrained **biliary/abdominal** source.  
- **S. aureus bacteremia behaves like a metastatic disease** until proven otherwise—**repeat cultures + TEE**.  
- **Oral step-down** is often safe for GN once stable and susceptible; far less so for **SAB**.
