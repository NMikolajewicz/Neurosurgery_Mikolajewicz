### Etiology

* SAH
	* up to 80% radiographically, 55% clinically
* aneurysm rupture
* traumatic
* IVH

### Pathophysiology

* mechanical narrowing of arterial lumens
* decreased blood flow, ischemia, infarction
* Mechanism
	* nitrous oxide
	* endothelin
	* oxyhemoglobin
	* others...

### Predictors

* degree/thickness of SAH
* IVH

### Modified Fisher Scale

* method for radiological grading [subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH)](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/subarachnoid-haemorrhage?lang=us) secondary to [intracranial aneurysm rupture](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/ruptured-berry-aneurysm-1?lang=us), assessed on the first non-contrast CT.
* Grades
	* **grade 0 - 0% vasospasm incidence**
		* no SAH
		* ==no IVH==
	* **grade 1 - 24%**
		* ==focal/diffuse, thin SAH==
		* ==no IVH==
	* **grade 2 - 33%**
		* ==focal/diffuse, thin SAH==
		* ==IVH present==
	* **grade 3 - 33%**
		* ==thick SAH==
		* ==no IVH==
	* **grade 4 - 40%**
		* ==thick  SAH==
		* ==IVH present==

### Timeline

* typically 3 days after SAH
* peaks between days 6-10
* resolved by 14 days

### Management

* ==nimodipine==
	* calcium channel blocker
	* dilates vessels, especially leptomeningial collaterals
* ==triple H therapy== \- aim is to maintain cerebral perfusion pressure
	* hemodilution
	* hypertension
	* hypervolemia
* endovascular intervention
	* intra-arterial delivery of CCB (e.g., nimodipine or verapamil)
	* balloon angioplasty

### Prognosis

* severe and permanent neurological deficit or death in ==50%== of symptomatic patients
