### Epidemiology

	1/3 AVMs diagnosed due to hemorrhage are identified before the age of 20 years
	
	diagnosed at a mean age of 31 years
	
	4% population-wide incidence
		symptomatic in 12% of cases
		
	Multiple AVM suggest syndrome:
	* [hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/hereditary-haemorrhagic-telangiectasia?lang=us) (Osler-Weber-Rendu syndrome)
	* [Wyburn-Mason syndrome](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/wyburn-mason-syndrome?lang=us) ([craniofacial arteriovenous metameric syndrome)](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/cerebrofacial-arteriovenous-metameric-syndrome?lang=us)

### Pathology

* VEGF receptor-mediated endothelial proliferation 
* cytokine-mediated vessel remodeling

### Diferential diagnosis

* other [cerebral vascular malformations](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/cerebrovascular-malformations?lang=us)
	* [cerebral proliferative angiopathy](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/cerebral-proliferative-angiopathy?lang=us) 6,9
		* absence of early venous drainage
		* entire lobe or  hemisphere affected
		* feeder arteries tend to be of normal size
	* [craniofacial arteriovenous metameric syndrome (CAMS)](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/cerebrofacial-arteriovenous-metameric-syndrome?lang=us):
		* classic locations
		* association with facial AVM
	* [dural arteriovenous fistula (DAVF)](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/dural-arteriovenous-fistula?lang=us)
	* [developmental venous anomaly (DVA)](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/developmental-venous-anomaly?lang=us)
* vascular tumor
* [glioblastoma](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/glioblastoma-idh-wildtype?lang=us)

### Location

* supratentorial: ~85%
	* superficial (2/3)
	* deep (1/3)
* infratentorial: ~15%

### Incidence

* solitary AVMs (98%)
* multiple AVMs (2%)
	* often associated with syndromes

### AVM rupture risk

* annual rupture rate: 2-4%
* lifetime rupture rate
	* multiplicative law of probability
		* lifetime risk = 1 - (risk of no rupture)n
		* assume 3% annual rupture risk, 35 year old with 43 years of life remaining = 1 - (0.9743) = 73%
			* assumes constant annual risk
		* ==crude estimate = 105 - age==
			* e.g., 105 - 35 = 70% lifetime risk

### Flow-related aneurysms

* The feeding arteries are enlarged due to the low resistance (as blood bypasses the capillary beds)
* Low resistance leads to increased flow, which may lead to ==flow-related arterial aneurysms==

### Imaging

	CT
		The nidus is blood density and hyperdense compared to brain
		
		CTA shows feeding arteries, draining veins, and intervening nidus visible in the so-called "bag of worms" appearance.
		
	Angiography (DSA)
		==Cerebral angiography remains the gold standard==
		
		delineates the location and number of feeding vessels and the pattern of drainage
		

### Management

	Treatment options and rate of complications are dictated in part by the ==Spetzler-Martin grade.==
	
	In general, the three options available are:
	* ==microsurgical resection==
		* 96% obliteration rate
	* ==endovascular occlusion / embolization==
		* 13% obliteration rate
	* ==radiosurgery (SRS)==
		* 38% obliteration rate

### Prognosis

	Occasionally, arteriovenous malformations can spontaneously resolve
	
	The annual risk of hemorrhage for an untreated arteriovenous malformation is 2-3%
		resulting from a flow-related aneurysm, intra-nidal aneurysm, or venous thrombosis (rarely).
		
	Smaller arteriovenous malformations (≤3 cm) are at greater risk of hemorrhage due to the higher pressure of the feeding artery.
	
	Following hemorrhage, the risk of a further bleed in the next 12 months is up to 18%
	

### Spetzler-Martin grading

* allocates points for various angiographic features of [intracranial arteriovenous malformations](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/brain-arteriovenous-malformation?lang=us)
* predicts the morbidity/mortality risk of surgery
* 1 = best, 5 = worst
* Grading
	* **==size of nidus==** ==(1-3)==
		* small (<3 cm) = 1
		* medium (3–6 cm) = 2
		* large (>6 cm) = 3
	* **==eloquence of adjacent brain==** ==(0-1)==
		* non-eloquent = 0
		* eloquent = 1
			* following areas are considered [eloquent](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/eloquent-cortex?lang=us):
				* [sensory](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/postcentral-gyrus?lang=us), [motor](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/precentral-gyrus?lang=us), language, or [visual](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/primary-visual-cortex?lang=us) cortex
				* [hypothalamus](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/hypothalamus?lang=us) or [thalamus](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/thalamus?lang=us)
				* [internal capsule](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/internal-capsule?lang=us)
				* [brainstem](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/brainstem?lang=us)
				* cerebellar peduncles ([superior](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/superior-cerebellar-peduncle-1?lang=us), [middle](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/middle-cerebellar-peduncle-2?lang=us), or [inferior](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/inferior-cerebellar-peduncle-1?lang=us))
	* **==venous drainage==** ==(0-1)==
		* superficial veins only = 0
		* deep veins = 1
			* deep if drainage is to [deep cerebral veins](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/deep-cerebral-veins?lang=us) such as the [internal cerebral vein](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/internal-cerebral-vein?lang=us), [basal vein of Rosenthal](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/basal-vein-of-rosenthal-3?lang=us), or [precentral cerebellar vein](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/precentral-cerebellar-vein?lang=us).
* Managemet
	* ==A (grade I-II) - resection==
	* B (grade III) - multimodality treatment
	* C (grade IV-V) - no treatment

### Classification

* **compact (or glomerular) nidus**
	* abnormal vessels without any interposed normal brain tissue.
* **diffuse (or proliferative) nidus**
	* no well-formed nidus is present, with functional neuronal tissue interspersed amongst the anomalous vessels
	* when early venous drainage is absent, this is considered [cerebral proliferative angiopathy](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/cerebral-proliferative-angiopathy?lang=us)
