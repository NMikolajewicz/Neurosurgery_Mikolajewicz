## **Definition**  

Watershed infarct = ischemia at the periphery of two bordering arterial territories when flow in one/both drops.

## Locations
### **Cortical border zones (CBZ)** 
 - ACA↔MCA along parasagittal frontal/parietal convexity.
 - MCA↔PCA along posterior temporo-occipital convexity. 
### **Internal (deep) border zone** 
 - Between superficial cortical medullary arteries and deep perforators (e.g., MCA lenticulostriates) within centrum semiovale/corona radiata
 - classically "==man-in-the-barrel==" (proximal arm/leg weakness) when global flow slips.
### **Spinal cord "watershed"** 
 - ==Mid-thoracic cord (≈T4–T5)== has relatively tenuous supply—susceptible to hypotension, cross-clamp, TEVAR, etc. 
### **Neonatal brain (bonus)**  
 - ==Germinal matrix== is a watershed zone fed by Heubner + lateral striates + AChA—hypotension/hypoxemia tip it into infarct/hemorrhage (PIVH).
### **Central cord (concept)**  
- The centermost cervical cord behaves like a vascular watershed
- edema/ischemia here contributes to CCS pattern (UE > LE weakness).

## Triggers / risks
- Systemic ==hypotension/low-flow states== (sepsis, over-treated BP, post-arrest, cardiopulmonary bypass).
- Critical ==carotid disease== with impaired autoregulation/poor collaterals (internal watershed hits).
- SAH ==vasospasm==—monitoring is often aimed at ACA-MCA watershed when at risk.
- ==Aortic interventions/cross-clamp== → mid-thoracic cord.
## Clinical patterns
- **ACA↔MCA CBZ:** proximal arm > hand weakness, leg involvement, transcortical motor aphasia/abulia if dominant mesial frontal involved.
- **MCA↔PCA CBZ:** higher-order visual dysfunction (alexia without agraphia if splenial/left occipital hits), visuospatial deficits.
- **Internal border zone:** bilateral proximal > distal weakness ("==man-in-the-barrel=="), dysarthria-clumsy hand.
- **Spinal watershed (mid-T):** abrupt paraparesis ± dissociated pin-prick, sphincter changes after hypotension/TEVAR.
- **Neonatal GM:** apnea, bulging fontanel, altered tone in preterm with hemodynamic instability.
## Imaging cues (fast reads)
- **CBZ:** wedge-shaped cortical/subcortical lesions abutting the interhemispheric fissure (ACA↔MCA) or posterior convexity (MCA↔PCA); DWI bright, cortical ribboning sparing deep gray. 
- **Internal border zone:** linear/chain-like DWI lesions in deep white matter along corona radiata/centrum semiovale.
- **Cord:** T2 hyperintensity anterior two-thirds (ASA) around T4–T5; look for perioperative hypotension history.

## Management pearls 
- **Immediate goals:** correct hypotension and hypovolemia; avoid aggressive BP drops in acute ischemia (hemodynamic failure worsens watershed). 
- **Carotid disease:** treat culprit stenosis per guideline timing; until then, maintain adequate MAP, optimize Hct/O2, antiplatelet as indicated.
- **SAH vasospasm risk:** consider brain oxygen probe targeting ACA-MCA watershed zone (≈3 cm lateral to midline) in selected cases; treat vasospasm per protocol.
- **Spinal cord risk (aortic/TEVAR):** protect cord with MAP targets, CSF drainage protocols, limit clamp time; know Adamkiewicz level variability.
- **Neonates:** meticulous cardiorespiratory management to prevent GM hypoperfusion.
## Pitfalls & look-alikes
- Don't confuse **lacunes** (small, deep perforator disease) with internal watershed chains—different mechanism & secondary prevention.
- Symmetric periventricular **leukoaraiosis** can reflect chronic small-vessel disease or watershed-type hypoperfusion—correlate clinically.
