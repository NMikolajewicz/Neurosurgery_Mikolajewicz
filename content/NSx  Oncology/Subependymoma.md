## Background
* slow-growing, benign tumor
* 4th ventricle most common
* grows into ventricle
* **WHO grade:** I (benign, slow-growing glial tumor).
## **Epidemiology:** 
* Middle-aged/older adults
* slight ♂ predominance
* often incidental.
## Location
* ==Fourth ventricle== > lateral ventricle
* classic site is **floor of the 4th ventricle near the obex** .
## Clinical presentation 
* Obstructive HCP (headache, gait/imbalance, diplopia); 
* cognitive/attention complaints if large LV lesion; 
* many are asymptomatic.
## **Imaging** 
* Well-circumscribed intraventricular mass; 
* ==T1 iso/hypo, T2 hyperintense; **minimal or patchy enhancement**==; 
* often large with little edema; 
* look for attachment to ventricular wall (LV: septum pellucidum/foramen of Monro corridor).

## Pathology 
* Low cellularity; 
* clusters of bland nuclei in dense fibrillary matrix; 
* microcysts; 
* ==GFAP==+; EMA usually negative/rare dot-like; 
* ==**no perivascular pseudorosettes**== (helps distinguish from ependymoma).
## **Workup** 
* MRI brain w/wo; assess HCP; 
* no routine CSF cytology for classic cases; 
* meds: steroids only if symptomatic.
## **Management**
### Cranial
* **Indication:** Symptomatic HCP, growth, or diagnostic uncertainty.
* **Goal:** GTR if safe; internal debulk → gentle capsule mobilization; **avoid violating the floor of the 4th ventricle**; use telovelar route for 4th ventricle lesions; leave adherent slivers if risky.
* **Adjuvant:** None after GTR; observe STR with serial MRI; consider RT only for progressive residual (rare).
### Spine
* Eccentric intramedullary cervical/thoracic nodules; 
* attempt GTR if clear plane; STR + surveillance acceptable; RT only if progressive.
## Prognosis
* Excellent; long PFS/OS with GTR; 
* recurrence uncommon; CSF seeding/malignant change exceedingly rare.
## Pearls/Pitfalls
    
* If LV lesion near foramen of Monro, plan corridor (transcallosal vs transcortical) based on ventricle size/trajectory.
* Don't overcall enhancement—subependymoma can be very subtle; correlation with symptoms/HCP drives surgery more than imaging drama.
* ==Distinguish from classic ependymoma ==(enhancing, pseudorosettes, more infiltrative) and ==central neurocytoma== (calcified, bubbly, neuronal markers).
* Large tumors may be surprisingly avascular/soft—prioritize CSF release and methodical debulk.
