## Overview

- Mixed glioneuronal tumor
- usually WHO 1 (anaplastic = WHO 3; rare)
- Pediatric/young adult
- strong epilepsy association (temporal > others).
- Common differentials in lesional epilepsy: DNET, PXA, low-grade glioma, focal cortical dysplasia.
## Imaging (brain)

- Cortical-based, often ==temporal==.
- Cyst with mural nodule or mixed solid-cystic mass (45% cases); variable enhancement; calcification uncommon to occasional.
- Surrounding cortical dysplasia is common; long-standing epilepsy may show secondary changes.

## Pathology

- D==ysplastic/neuronal (ganglion) cells + neoplastic glial ==component (often pilocytic-like).
- CD34 frequently positive; low Ki-67 except in anaplastic areas.
- Molecular: 
	- MAPK pathway activation common; 
	- ==BRAF V600E== in a sizeable subset
## Clinical

- ==Drug-resistant focal seizures== (often decades-long).
- Headache or focal deficits if outside temporal lobe; hydrocephalus rare unless large/posterior fossa.
## Management (brain)

- Goal: 
	- maximal safe gross-total resection (GTR). 
	- Seizure outcomes correlate with extent of resection
- If mesial temporal involvement, plan lesionectomy ± tailored amygdalohippocampectomy (dominant-side memory counseling).
- Subtotal resection (STR): observe if stable; re-op if seizure-limiting residual or growth.
- Adjuvant therapy: Consider focal RT ± chemo (e.g., temozolomide) for anaplastic subtypes, progressive residual, or recurrence.
- Targeted therapy: 	- BRAFi (± MEK) for refractory disease
## Spine ganglioglioma (rarer)

- Intramedullary, often cervical/thoracic; cysts/syrinx common.
- Try GTR if well-circumscribed; STR with surveillance if planes are poor. RT reserved for progression/anaplasia.\
## Prognosis

- Excellent with GTR (long PFS/OS). Recurrence risk rises with STR, atypia/anaplasia.
- Malignant transformation: rare but reported—watch for new enhancement, rising Ki-67, accelerating growth.

## Pearls & pitfalls

- Always distinguish from DNET and PXA (both common epilepsy tumors) and address adjacent cortical dysplasia if present.
- Pre-op neuropsych (dominant temporal). Use intraop mapping for lesions abutting eloquence.
- Don't reflexively irradiate a subtotally resected WHO 1—observe first unless clinical/radiographic momentum.
