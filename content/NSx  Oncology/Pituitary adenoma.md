## Summary
- Hyposecretion, most common presentation:
	- Orthoststic hypotension
	- Fatigue
- Hardy system : Anatomical classification of pituitary adenomas
- MEN association: pituitary, pancreatic, parathyroid
- 1 year prognosis: 66%
- Hormones most commonly affected: prolactin, ACTH
## Tumor size
* Macroadenoma > 1 cm
* Larger tumours: non-functional or prolactinomas
* Smaller tumours: ACTH secreting
## Anatomy
* Frequently compressed structures
	* Optic chiasm
	* 3rd ventricle
		* Obstructive hydrocephalus
	* CN III, IV, V1, V2, VI
		* Ptosis, diplopia, facial pain

## Management by subtype
- #prolactinoma (first-line medical): 
	- dopamine agonist (cabergoline preferred). 
	- valvulopathy signal at Parkinson’s doses; at pituitary doses <2 mg/week, don’t stop solely for this concern . 
	- Surgery/rad tx if DA failure/intolerance, apoplexy, or discordant diagnosis (rare) .
    
- #acromegaly (GH): 
	- surgery first for most; 
	- if persistent disease → somatostatin analogs (short-acting SQ then LAR IM with titration), DA adjunct in some, or pegvisomant (normalizes IGF-1 in ~97%; does not shrink tumor) . 
	- Biochemical cure targets: normal IGF-1 + GH nadir <1 ng/mL on OGTT .
    
- #cushing’s (ACTH): 
	- transsphenoidal resection is primary; 
	- stringent post-op cortisol targets predict remission; 
	- watch for Nelson’s after adrenalectomy (aggressive corticotroph progression) .
    
- #TSH adenoma: 
	- surgery ± octreotide (TSH and T4/T3 normalize in most; one third shrinkage) .
    
- #nonfunctioning-macroadenoma: 
	- operate for field loss, chiasmal elevation, or acute neurovisualdecline; 
	- otherwise consider surveillance/radiosurgery depending on growth and invasion .

## Peri/Post-operative management
- ==Everyone is “hypoadrenal” post-TSS==: 
	- give hydrocortisone ± dexamethasone and taper; 
	- check 8 AM cortisol during the taper .
    
- DI/SIADH choreography: 
	- ==expect early DI==; best initial management is fluids; if needed, use short-acting vasopressin first to avoid renal shutdown if a SIADH-like phase appears. 
	- ==DI patterns: transient (resolves ~12–36 h), prolonged, or triphasic (DI → SIADH → DI)== .
    
- DDAVP dosing option: 
	- intranasal 10–40 µg BID PRN; 
	- track renal panel/osms q6h; 
	- remove nasal packs POD 3–6 .
    
- Visual fields: 
	- obtain pre-op and at follow-up in lesions with suprasellar extension (baseline for comparison) .

## Associated Syndromes
### Cushing's syndrome
(Hint: steroids)
* striae
* thin skin
* ecchymosis
* reduced libido
* obesity
* impotence, increased BP
* diabetes
* skin hyperpigmentation
### Pituitary apoplexy
- see also [[Pituitary apoplexy]]
- **Typical**: thunderclap/worst H/A ± ophthalmoplegia/vision loss, acute hypopituitarism .
- **Immediate steps**: 
	- stress-dose steroids now; 
	- urgent endocrine + neurosurgery eval. 
	- ==Decompress transsphenoidally within ~7 days== in most cases .
		- TSS improves ophthalmoplegia, but not VF/VA/endocrinopathies
### Nelson Syndrome
- Follows ==bilateral **adrenalectomy**== in 10-30% of cases. 
- Classic triad includes 
	* hyperpigmentation
	* increased ACTH 
	* enlargement of the pituitary tumor. 
* Usually occurs 1-4 years after adrenalectomy
### Acromegaly
* \> 95% cases of excess GH result from pituitary somatotrophadenoma. 
* Ectopic GH secretion uncommonly occur with: 
	* **carcinoid tumor**
	* lymphoma
	* pancreatic islet-cell tumor
* 25% of acromegalics have thyromegaly with normal thyroid studies
* Hypothalamus produces GHRH which causes the pituitary to make GH
* ==Excess GH induces IGF-1== secretion from the liver, also known as somatomedin-C
* What medication can suppress GH release? somatostatin
* Mortality rates 2-3x higher than expected due to: 
	* cancer
	* cardiomyopathy
	* diabetes
	* hypertension
	* infection
	* neural entrapment
## Tumor of the neurophypophysis and infundibulum
* The most common tumor in the posterior pituitary is **metastatic**
* The most common primary tumor is the granular cell tumor (GCT) with a predilection for the stalk
* If this tumor is suspected, operative approach is transcranial preferred over transphenoidal
