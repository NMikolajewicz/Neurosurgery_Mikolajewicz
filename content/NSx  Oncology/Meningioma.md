## Definition
- Slow-growing, usually benign, extra-axial tumors that arise from ==arachnoid cap cells== (not dura).
- Classically show a broad dural attachment, often a "dural tail," dense contrast enhancement, and can induce hyperostosis of adjacent bone. 
- Many incidentally found lesions never grow; 
- surgery is typically for documented growth and/or symptoms not controlled medically .
## Epidemiology
- ==0.9% incidence==
- 32% of primary brain tumors (39% asymptomatic)
- intraventricular: >5% of all meningioma
- **Intracranial:** Meningiomas are the most common primary intracranial tumors;
- **Spinal:** 
	- Peak age 40–70; 
	- female:male ~4:1 overall (≈1:1 in lumbar); 
	- location distribution ≈ thoracic 82% > cervical 15% > lumbar 2%; 
	- 90% intradural (5% extradural, 5% "dumbbell") .
## Location-specific symptoms
-  parasagittal near motor strip
	- early foot drop
- olfactory groove
	- anosmia, frontal behavioral changes, ==Foster-Kennedy==
- parasellar/tuberculum sellae
	- homogeneous enhancement, suprasellar epicenter, dural tail; endocrine disturbance is uncommon .
- Spinal meningioma
	- progressive myelopathy is typical; 
	- series show frequent pyramidal signs pre-op .
## Principles of meningioma surgery (4D)
- Devascularize
	- Early interruption of blood supply (dural base/periosteal feeders)
- Debulk
	- Internal debulking (e.g., ultrasonic aspirator)
- Dedress/dissect
	- Extra-arachnoid circumferential dissection while infolding the capsule; cut/coagulate arachnoid and small vascular adhesions; keep brain retraction minimal
- Detachment
	- Address dura + involved bone (resect when possible; otherwise widely coagulate dural base) .

	- Grade 2 ~ 20% 
	- Grade 3 ~ 30%
	- Grade 4 ~ 40%

### WHO Grades
#### **Grade I (benign, low risk):** 
- meningothelial, fibrous (fibroblastic), transitional, ==psammomatous==, angiomatous, microcystic, secretory, lymphoplasmacyte-rich. 
#### **Grade II (higher recurrence/aggressiveness):** 
- atypical; chordoid; clear cell (intracranial). 
#### **Grade III (malignant):** 
- ==anaplastic== (malignant), papillary, rhabdoid. 
- Rhabdoid and malignant variants behave aggressively; frequent mitoses/cortical invasion correlate with rapid recurrence and rare metastases .

## Prognosis
- Overall 5-year survival ≈ **91.3%**. 
- Extent of resection is the strongest recurrence predictor (Simpson): 
	- after gross total resection, recurrence ≈ **11–15%**; 
	- **~29%** when resection is incomplete (follow-up not specified). 
- Four histological prognostic variables: 
	- grade
	- histologic subtype
	- proliferation index (e.g., Ki-67/MIB-1)
	- brain invasion .
