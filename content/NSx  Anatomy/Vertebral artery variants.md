## Origin and Extracranial Course

	The VA most commonly originates from the subclavian artery, but can also arise from the aortic arch (left VA, ~4.8%), brachiocephalic trunk, or other vessels.
	
	The usual entrance into the transverse foramen is at C6 in ~92% of cases, but variants may enter as high as C3 or as low as C7, which may impact anterior cervical or esophageal surgeries
	

## High-Riding Vertebral Artery (HRVA)

	Definition: HRVA is defined by:
		C2 isthmus height (C2IsH) ≤ 5 mm
		
		C2 internal height (C2InH) ≤ 2 mm (measured 3 mm lateral to the spinal canal)
		
	Clinical Impact: HRVA significantly narrows the safe corridor for screw placement during C2 instrumentation, increasing the risk of VA injury.
	

## Classification (Recent)

	Type 1 (Isthmic): Only C2IsH reduced, normal C2InH
	
	Type 2 (Internal): Only C2InH reduced, C2IsH normal
	
	Type 3 (Isthmo-internal): Both C2IsH and C2InH reduced
	
	Prevalence: Type 1 (78.2%), Type 2 (8.8%), Type 3 (12.9%) among HRVA cases.
	
	Ipsilateral narrow pedicle frequently coexists, especially in type 3 HRVA.
