## Gpi

### Background
* Where dystonia or dyskinesia are prominent features, GPi is favoured by some as it improves dystonic postures and eases painful cramping
* GPi stimulation usually requires higher amplitudes than STN stimulation, but it may be linked to a lower risk of side effects.
### Coordinates
* Schaltenbrand atlas for GPi \[with respect to midcommissural point (MCP)\]:
	* 22 mm lateral (x)
	* 2 mm anterior (y)
	* 4 mm ventral (z)
* The directly targeted coordinates for GPi were
	* x: \[22.3 ± 2.0 (17.8 - 26.1)\]
	* y: \[-0.2 ± 2.2 (-4.5 -3.4)\]
	* z: \[-4.3 ± 0.8 (-6.2 – -2.3)\].
	* The mean value for the GPi was 2.2 mm (p < 0.001) more posterior and 0.3 mm (p < 0.01) more ventral than the atlas-based coordinates.
### Anatomy
* The GPi region includes the
	* GPe: dorsally and laterally
	* optic tract: ventrally
	* internal capsule: medially and posteriorly

## STN
### Background
* effective for motor symptoms, especially bradykinesia, rigidity and tremor, and reduces the use of dopaminergic medication
* more likely than GPi stimulation to produce stimulation-induced side effects such as speech and swallowing problems, double vision and psychiatric symptoms
### Coordinates
* Schaltenbrand atlas for STN \[with respect to midcommissural point (MCP)\]:
	* 12 mm lateral (x)
	* 2 mm posterior (y) 
	* 4 mm ventral (z)
* The directly targeted coordinates (mean, SD, range) for STN were
	* _x_: \[9.9 ± 1.1 (7.1 − 13.2)\]
	* _y_: \[−0.8 ± 1.1 (−4.2 − 2)\]
	* _z_: \[−4.7 ± 0.53 (−5.9 – −3.2)\]
* The mean value for the direct STN coordinates was 2.1 mm more medial (_p_ < 0.0001), 1.2 mm more anterior (_p_ < 0.0001) and 0.7 mm more ventral (_p_ < 0.0001) than the atlas target.
