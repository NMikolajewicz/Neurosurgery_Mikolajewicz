### Background

* The Leksell frame, a stereotactic headframe, is a tool used in neurosurgery for precise targeting of brain areas, offering high accuracy and stability, particularly for procedures like biopsies and radiosurgery.
* it can also present challenges like reduced flexibility and potential for distortion, especially when targeting the posterior fossa. 

### Advantages

* High Accuracy and Stability
* Versatile Applications: stereotactic biopsies, radiosurgery (Gamma Knife), and electrode placement for stereoelectroencephalography
* Established Technology:  developed by Lars Leksell in 1949

### Disadvantages and Considerations

* Posterior Fossa Access: The rigid design of the Leksell frame can make access to the posterior fossa more challenging
* Reduced Flexibility: The rigid, contoured design of the Leksell frame offers less flexibility than other frame
* Potential for Distortion: Magnetic susceptibility and other distortions can appear greater at the periphery of the magnetic field, potentially affecting image quality when using the Leksell frame with MRI. 
* Airway Management: In emergency situations, removing the entire frame can be time-consuming, so removing only the frontal bar might be easier and quicker for airway management. 

The evolutionary Leksell stereotactic system satisfies all of the following:
(1) Is it a complete system?
(2) Is it simple?
(3) Is it dependable?
(4) Is it versatile?
(5) Is it accurate?
(6) Is it compatible with multiple current imaging modalities?
(7) Is it both computer-compatible and independent?
(8) Is its development keeping pace with developments in other related technologies?
