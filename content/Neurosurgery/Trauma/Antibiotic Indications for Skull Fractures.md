The use of prophylactic antibiotics in skull fractures varies significantly based on fracture type, contamination status, and associated complications. Current evidence-based guidelines provide specific recommendations for different clinical scenarios.

## Open Skull Fractures

Open skull fractures represent a **clear indication** for antibiotic prophylaxis due to direct communication between the external environment and intracranial contents. [vumc+2](https://www.vumc.org/trauma-and-scc/sites/default/files/public_files/Protocols/Antibiotics%20in%20Craniofacial%20Trauma%202022.pdf)

### Recommended Antibiotic Regimens

**Clean Open Fractures (minimal contamination):**

* **Cefazolin 2g IV every 8 hours** for 3 days[surgicalcriticalcare+1](https://www.surgicalcriticalcare.net/Guidelines/Antibiotics%20in%20Facial%20Fractures%202021.pdf)
* Alternative: **Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV every 6 hours** for 24 hours[vumc](https://www.vumc.org/trauma-and-scc/sites/default/files/public_files/Protocols/Antibiotics%20in%20Craniofacial%20Trauma%202022.pdf)

**Contaminated Open Fractures:**

* **Ceftriaxone 2g IV every 12 hours** + **Metronidazole 500mg IV every 8 hours** for 3 days[med.uth+1](https://med.uth.edu/surgery/antibiotic-therapy/)
* Alternative: **Vancomycin** + **Ceftriaxone** at meningitic doses for 24-48 hours[idmp.ucsf](https://idmp.ucsf.edu/sites/g/files/tkssra4251/f/ZSFG%20Penetrating%20Brain%20Injury%20Antibiotic%20Prophylaxis%20Guidance%202022_0.pdf)

**Penetrating Skull Injuries:**

* **Ceftriaxone 2g IV twice daily** with or without **Metronidazole 500mg IV every 8 hours**[nhstaysideadtc.scot+1](https://www.nhstaysideadtc.scot.nhs.uk/Antibiotic%20site/pdf%20docs/Penetrating%20Injuries%20Policy.pdf)
* Duration: **2 weeks** if foreign body removed, **up to 6 weeks** if foreign body retained[nhstaysideadtc.scot](https://www.nhstaysideadtc.scot.nhs.uk/Antibiotic%20site/pdf%20docs/Penetrating%20Injuries%20Policy.pdf)
* Add metronidazole if missile tract crosses air sinuses[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9922212/)

## Closed (Simple) Skull Fractures

### Non-operative Management

**No prophylactic antibiotics** are recommended for closed, non-operative skull fractures. This includes simple linear fractures and non-displaced fractures without complications.[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11149135/)

### Operative Management

For closed skull fractures requiring surgical intervention:

* **Single dose of cefazolin** 30 minutes before surgery is sufficient [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11149135/)
* **No postoperative antibiotics** beyond 24 hours are recommended [surgicalcriticalcare](https://www.surgicalcriticalcare.net/Guidelines/Antibiotics%20in%20Facial%20Fractures%202021.pdf)

## Basilar Skull Fractures

### Evidence Against Prophylaxis

Multiple high-quality studies and meta-analyses demonstrate that **prophylactic antibiotics are not indicated** for basilar skull fractures, even with CSF leak. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih+3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10554555/)
Key findings include:

* **No reduction in meningitis risk** with antibiotic prophylaxis [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40857668/)
* Possible **increased susceptibility** to more pathogenic organisms[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10554555/)
* **Cochrane meta-analysis** found no benefit of prophylactic antibiotics[tsaco.bmj](https://tsaco.bmj.com/content/9/1/e001304)

### Alternative Interventions

Instead of antibiotics, patients with basilar skull fractures and CSF leak should receive:

* **Pneumococcal vaccination**[vumc+1](https://www.vumc.org/trauma-and-scc/sites/default/files/public_files/Antibiotics%20in%20Craniofacial%20Trauma%202024.pdf)
	* **13-valent conjugate vaccine** (Prevnar13) during hospitalization
	* **23-valent polysaccharide vaccine** (Pneumovax) at least 8 weeks later

## Frontal Sinus Fractures

### Antibiotic Indications

Frontal sinus fractures warrant antibiotic prophylaxis when:[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11149135/)

* **Posterior table involvement** exists
* **Significant contamination** is present
* **Open fractures** communicate with the sinus

### Recommended Regimen

* **Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV every 6 hours** for 24 hours postoperatively[vumc](https://www.vumc.org/trauma-and-scc/sites/default/files/public_files/Protocols/Antibiotics%20in%20Craniofacial%20Trauma%202022.pdf)
* For non-operative sinus fractures: **Amoxicillin/clavulanic acid** for 3-5 days[surgicalcriticalcare](https://www.surgicalcriticalcare.net/Guidelines/Antibiotics%20in%20Facial%20Fractures%202021.pdf)

## Intracranial Monitoring

### ICP Monitors and Ventriculostomies

Current evidence suggests **limited antibiotic prophylaxis** for intracranial monitoring devices:[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9685777/)
**Antibiotic-impregnated catheters:**

* **Single dose of cefazolin 2g IV** 30 minutes before insertion[vumc](https://www.vumc.org/trauma-and-scc/sites/default/files/public_files/Antibiotics%20in%20Craniofacial%20Trauma%202024.pdf)

**Non-antibiotic impregnated catheters:**

* **Cefazolin 2g IV every 8 hours** for 24 hours (first dose 30 minutes before insertion)[vumc](https://www.vumc.org/trauma-and-scc/sites/default/files/public_files/Antibiotics%20in%20Craniofacial%20Trauma%202024.pdf)

### Evidence Against Prolonged Prophylaxis

Multiple studies demonstrate that **prolonged antibiotic prophylaxis** for ICP monitors:

* **Does not reduce CNS infection rates**[liebertpub+1](https://www.liebertpub.com/doi/10.1089/sur.2007.032)
* **Increases risk of multidrug-resistant infections**[liebertpub+1](https://www.liebertpub.com/doi/10.1089/sur.2006.7.409)
* **Associated with higher septic morbidity**[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9685777/)

## Pediatric Considerations

Antibiotic indications in pediatric skull fractures follow similar principles to adults:[cerebralpalsyguidance+1](https://cerebralpalsyguidance.com/birth-injury/infant-skull-fracture/treatment/)

* **Open fractures** require the same prophylactic regimens
* **Closed fractures** typically do not require antibiotics
* **Dosing adjustments** based on weight and age are essential

## Timing and Duration

### Critical Timing Factors

* **Antibiotics should be initiated within 1 hour** of injury for open fractures[sunnybrook+1](https://sunnybrook.ca/content/?page=antimicrobial-stewardship-prophylaxis-guidelines)
* **Preoperative dosing** should occur 30 minutes before incision[vumc](https://www.vumc.org/trauma-and-scc/sites/default/files/public_files/Protocols/Antibiotics%20in%20Craniofacial%20Trauma%202022.pdf)

### Duration Guidelines

* **Open skull fractures**: 24-72 hours maximum[surgicalcriticalcare+1](https://www.surgicalcriticalcare.net/Guidelines/Antibiotics%20in%20Facial%20Fractures%202021.pdf)
* **Penetrating injuries**: 2-6 weeks depending on contamination and foreign body presence[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9922212/)
* **Operative cases**: Discontinue within 24 hours of soft tissue closure[sunnybrook](https://sunnybrook.ca/content/?page=antimicrobial-stewardship-prophylaxis-guidelines)

## Contraindications and Cautions

### When NOT to Use Antibiotics

* **Closed basilar skull fractures** with or without CSF leak[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih+2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40857668/)
* **Simple linear skull fractures** managed non-operatively[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11149135/)
* **Prolonged prophylaxis** for ICP monitoring beyond single perioperative dose[liebertpub+1](https://www.liebertpub.com/doi/10.1089/sur.2007.032)

### Risk-Benefit Considerations

Unnecessary antibiotic use in skull fractures can lead to:

* **Development of resistant organisms**[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10554555/)
* **Increased healthcare-associated infections**[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9685777/)
* **C. difficile colitis** and other antibiotic-related complications[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11149135/)

## Summary of Key Recommendations

The evidence strongly supports a **selective approach** to antibiotic prophylaxis in skull fractures, with clear indications limited to open fractures and penetrating injuries. The routine use of antibiotics for closed fractures, including basilar skull fractures with CSF leak, is not supported by current evidence and may be harmful. When antibiotics are indicated, short courses with appropriate spectrum coverage should be used, with careful attention to timing and duration to minimize resistance development.
