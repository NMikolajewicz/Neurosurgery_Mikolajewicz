
Prognostic features in cranial gunshot wounds (CGSW) span clinical, radiological, and injury-related domains, and together they guide triage, surgical decision-making, and goals-of-care discussions. Overall mortality is approximately 41% in surgically managed cohorts, with roughly 62% of survivors achieving favorable functional outcome.[1][2]

## Clinical Predictors

**GCS on admission** is the single most powerful predictor of outcome across virtually every study. Survival stratifies dramatically by GCS band:[3][4]

- GCS 3–5 → ~3% survival
- GCS 6–8 → ~52% survival
- GCS 9–12 → ~92% survival
- GCS 13–15 → near-complete survival[5]

**Pupillary reactivity** is the second dominant predictor; fixed/non-reactive pupils independently predict mortality and, combined with GCS motor subscore, form the core of the SPIN score (Surviving Penetrating Injury to the Brain). The SPIN score (comprising motor GCS, pupils, self-inflicted status, transfer, sex, ISS, and INR) achieves AUC of 0.962 for survival prediction.[4][6]

## Radiological & Anatomical Features

CT findings carry strong independent prognostic weight:[2][1]

- **Bihemispheric injury** — crosses midline, associated with dramatically higher mortality
- **Intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH)** — independent predictor of death
- **Posterior fossa involvement** — associated with non-survival
- **Cerebral herniation** — markedly worsens prognosis
- **Rotterdam CT Score** — higher scores significantly predict mortality[1]
- **Perforating (through-and-through) vs. penetrating injury** — perforating trajectory correlates with increased mortality[7][2]

## Injury-Related Factors

| Feature | Favorable | Unfavorable |
|---|---|---|
| Bullet trajectory | Unihemispheric, non-midline | Bihemispheric, midline crossing |
| Wound type | Penetrating (lodged bullet) | Perforating (entry + exit) |
| Projectile velocity | Low-velocity | High-velocity |
| IVH | Absent | Present |
| Herniation | Absent | Present |
| Posterior fossa | Not involved | Involved |

[2][7]

## Patient-Level Factors

Age >30 years, male sex, lower ISS, and elevated INR are all independently associated with outcome. Self-inflicted GSW is an independent negative predictor of survival — likely reflecting proximity effect and more lethal trajectories — and is explicitly included in the SPIN score.[6][4][1]

## Decision-Making Algorithm

A validated decision-tree framework (Thieme, 2020) suggests:[8][7]

1. **GCS >8 + unihemispheric penetrating injury** → aggressive surgical management
2. **GCS ≤8 but normal pupils + absent bihemispheric injury, posterior fossa involvement, herniation, and IVH** → consider surgery
3. **GCS ≤8 + fixed pupils + any of the adverse radiological features** → comfort-focused care should be discussed

## Surgical Intervention as Prognostic Modifier

Importantly, surgical treatment itself is an independent factor associated with reduced mortality — suggesting that triage decisions directly shape outcomes. Conservative management remains appropriate and yields favorable long-term outcomes in neurologically stable patients, but withholding surgery in operable patients worsens prognosis. Aggressive early resuscitation (hemostatic + hyperosmolar therapy) has also been shown to improve survival even at low GCS, challenging older thresholds for futility.[9][7][2]
