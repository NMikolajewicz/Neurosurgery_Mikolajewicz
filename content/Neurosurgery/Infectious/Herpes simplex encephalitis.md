### **Background**

* 90% of cases of herpes encephalitis are caused by herpes simplex virus-1 (HSV-1).
* most common cause of fatal sporadic fulminant necrotizing viral encephalitis

### **Diagnosis**

	Brain CT scan (with/without contrast).
	
	Lumbar puncture.
	
	Brain MRI—Increased T2 signal intensity in the frontotemporal region → **viral (HSV) encephalitis**.
	

### **Differential Diagnosis**

* Other viruses can cause similar symptoms of encephalitis, though usually milder
	* HSV6, varicella zoster, EBV, CMV, coxsackie virus

### **Prognosis**

	**Without treatment:**
		Rapid death in 70% of cases.
		
		Survivors suffer severe neurological damage.
		
	**With treatment:**
		Fatal in 1/3 of cases.
		
		Long-term neurological damage in 1/2 of survivors.
		
		20% of treated patients recover with minor damage.
		2.5% regain completely normal brain function.
		
	Many amnesic cases in the scientific literature involve **herpes simplex encephalitis (HSE)**.
	

### **Complications**

	[anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-NMDA_receptor_encephalitis) (30% of cases).
	

### **Management**

* Medical: high-dose intravenous [acyclovir](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acyclovir), infused at 10 mg/kg (adult) over 1 hour to avoid [kidney failure](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_failure).           
* Surgical: Decompressive craniotomy if increased ICP.
