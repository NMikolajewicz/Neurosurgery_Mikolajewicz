Kernig’s Sign

  

  

- How to elicit: With the patient supine and the hip flexed to 90°, attempt to extend the knee.
- Positive sign: Resistance to knee extension or pain in the lower back/posterior thigh.
- Mechanism: Stretching the inflamed meninges and nerve roots causes discomfort.

  

  

  

  

  

Brudzinski’s Sign

  

  

- How to elicit: While the patient is lying supine, passively flex the neck toward the chest.
- Positive sign: Involuntary flexion of the hips and knees.
- Mechanism: Neck flexion stretches the spinal cord and meninges, provoking a protective response.
