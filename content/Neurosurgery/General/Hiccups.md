### Core concepts

- **Hiccups** = myoclonic, repetitive contractions of **diaphragm + accessory muscles** via **phrenic + intercostal nerves**.
- Triggered by activation of a **"hiccup center"** from **CNS or peripheral** lesions.
- The "**hic**" sound = reflex **glottic closure** via the **recurrent laryngeal nerve**.
- In neurocritical care, persistent hiccups can signal **structural irritation** (central or peripheral) and may complicate ventilation/sedation.
### Anatomy / Physiology (hiccup reflex)

- **Afferent/trigger inputs**: CNS/peripheral lesions → activate "hiccup center."
- **Efferent limb**: **phrenic + intercostal nerves** → diaphragm/accessory muscle contractions.
- **Sound production**: **recurrent laryngeal nerve** → reflex **glottic closure**.

### Pathophysiology / Pathology

- Lesions (central or peripheral) **activate the hiccup center** → stereotyped motor output (diaphragm/accessory muscle myoclonus) + glottic closure.
