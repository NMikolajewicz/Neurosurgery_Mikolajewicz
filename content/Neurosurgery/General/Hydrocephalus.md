### Axes of classification
* Archaic/obsolete: communicating vs non-ccommunicating
* 1) absorptive vs. obstructive
* 2) compensated vs decompensated (require tx)
* 3) acute vs chronic
* 4) congenital vs acquired
### Pathophysiology / Etiology
* **Increased CSF production**
	* rarest cause
	* choroid plexus papilloma
* **obstruction of CSF circulation**
	* most common
	* ventricles dilated proximal to obstruction
	* experimental monkey models
		* 4th ventricular obstruction results in immediate ventricular enlargment with gross evidence at 3 hours
		* after 3 weeks, damage is irreversible
	* 2 types
		* intra-ventricular, proximal to formina of luschka/magendie = **non-communicating (obstructive)**
			* tumors
			* hemorrhage
			* congenital malformation
				* colloid cyst (3rd ventricle)
				* chiari II
				* dandy-walker
				* achondroplasia (narrow foramen magnum)
			* infection
				* aquaductal gliosis - due to neonatal meningitis or SAH
		* extra-ventricular, distal to foramina of luschka/magendia = **communicating**
			* hemorrhages - SAH, IVH
				* any event resulting in RBC in CSF may result in communicating hydro
				* CSF > 500 mg/dl may interfere with CSF absorption
			* tumors
			* infections
			* tonsillar elongation/prolapse
* **Decreased CSF absorption**
	* rare
	* venous drainage insufficiency
	* causes
		* raised cerebral venous sinus pressure
			* sinus thrombosis
			* otitic hydrocephalus - due to lateral sinus thrombosis in children after chronic otitis media / mastoiditis
			* superior vena cava syndrome
		* congenital absence of arachnoid granulations
* increased CSF viscosity
	* cause
		* high protein content (e.g., spinal neurofibromas)
### External hydrocephalus
* CSF accumulation in subarachnoid spaces with macrocrania and normal +/- mildly dilated ventricular system
* cause: immature CSF absorption system (at level of arachnoid villi)
	* self-resolving
### Hydrocephalus ex vacuo
* replacement  of lost cerebral tissue with CSF
* ICP is normal
* examples - Alzheimer's disease
### Classification
* not all forms can be distinctly classified
* tracer study
	* inject tracer dye into lateral ventricle
	* dye in lumbar CSF:
		* present = communicating hydrocephalus
		* absent = non-communicating hydrocephalus
* Etiologies
	* congenital hydrocephalus
		* etiology unknown
		* incidence: 0.5-5/1000 births
		* <2% inherited
			* X-linked aqueductal stenosis
	* acquired hydrocephalus
### Presentation
* dilation of cerebral ventricles
	* at expense of periventricular WM - relative preservation of grey matter
	* temporal horns - dilate > 2 mm
		* young children - occipital horns
	* transependymal flow phenomenon
	* Evan's index > 0.3
	* suture status
		* open sutures
			* \+ pressure increase = +++ ventricle expansion (enormous)
			* surrounding brain appears compressed
		* fixed-volume skull (closed sutures)
			* +++ pressure increase = + ventricle expansion (moderate)
* raised ICP
	* active CSF secretion despite ICP increase
	* ICP normal in chronic hydrocephalus (e.g., NPH)
* bulging fontanelles
	* "setting sun" sign
	* increased head circumference
		* with cranial suture diastasis
	* "beaten-silver" appearance + non papilledema (infants)
* CN6 palsy
	* long intracranial course
* empty sella
* seizures (infants)
### Workup
* CT brain
* lumbar puncture
	* only after imaging r/o obstructive hydrocephalus
	* RBC in CSF may indicate communicating hydrocephalus
	* CSF protein > 500 mg/dl may interfere with CSF absorption
### Shunting
* parietal catheters directed into frontal horn
	* aiming towards ipsilateral medial eye canthus
	* endoscopic: can guide catheter anterior to foramen of Monro
* ventricular loculations
	* use multiple ventricular catheters connected to single-valve system
		* used to equalize pressures in various compartments and avoid dangerous brain shifts
* Torkildsen (internal) shunt
	* straight tubes that communicate ventricles to CSF spaces without valve
		* e.g., occipital horn to cisterna magma
* Assessment
	* shunt
		* palpation
		* CT (U/S for kids)
		* x-ray (shunt series)
		* tap
	* ETV or Torkildsen shunt
		* MRI flow studies
* Complications
	* 90-day shunt complication leads to surgery in 16.9% of cases
		* 35% of cases are preventable
	* Infection
		* organisms: staph. epidermidis/aureus
		* transient bacteremia not associated with shunt infection
		* time frame:
			* 70% < 1 month of surgery
			* 90% < 6 months of surgery
			* 0% > 6-9 months
		* workup
			* tap shunt
				* only if other causes of fever exluded
				* 25G butterfly noncoring needle
					* angle of puncture: 20-30 degrees
