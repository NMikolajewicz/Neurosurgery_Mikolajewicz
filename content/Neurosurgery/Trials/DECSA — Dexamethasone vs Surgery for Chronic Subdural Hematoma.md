> [!abstract] Bottom line (one line) Primary **dexamethasone monotherapy was not noninferior to burr-hole drainage** for 3-month functional outcome in symptomatic [[Chronic Subdural Hematoma|cSDH]] — the CI actually favoured **surgery** — with more complications and a high rate of crossover to surgery. Trial **stopped early** for safety.

## PICO

- **P** — Symptomatic [[Chronic Subdural Hematoma|cSDH]], [[Markwalder Grading Scale|Markwalder]] grade 1–3 (headache → focal deficit/hemiparesis)
- **I** — 19-day tapering course of **dexamethasone** (start ~8 mg BID), _no_ initial surgery
- **C** — **Burr-hole drainage** (current standard)
- **O** — Functional outcome at **3 months** via [[Modified Rankin Scale|mRS]] (0–6)

## Design

- Multicenter, open-label, controlled, **noninferiority** RCT; 1:1 randomization
- **n = 252** (dexamethasone 127, surgery 125) — enrolment halted early
- **Noninferiority margin:** lower bound of 95% CI of the OR for a _better_ outcome with dexamethasone ≥ **0.9**
- Secondary: [[Markwalder Grading Scale]], Extended Glasgow Outcome Scale (GOSE)

## Results

|Outcome|Dexamethasone|Surgery|
|---|---|---|
|Adjusted common OR for better mRS (dex vs surgery)|**0.55 (95% CI 0.34–0.90)** — fails noninferiority|ref|
|Complications|**59%**|32%|
|Additional (rescue) surgery|**55%**|6%|

- Markwalder & GOSE secondary endpoints **supported** the primary result.
- Trial **terminated early** due to safety concerns.

> [!warning] Read the confidence interval carefully The **entire** 95% CI (0.34–0.90) sits **below 1.0**, so this is not merely "noninferiority not shown" — it is statistical evidence that **surgery was superior** on the primary functional endpoint. The cautious published wording ("not noninferior") understates this.

## Interpretation & critical appraisal

- [!note] **Monotherapy frequently failed.** A **55% crossover to surgery** means primary dexamethasone often _delayed_ rather than _replaced_ the operation it was meant to avoid — eroding any "less invasive" rationale.
- **Open-label design** → outcome ascertainment / management bias possible (no blinding, subjective mRS at the margins).
- **Selection critique (commentary, Williamson; others):** non-operative management was applied to patients who arguably needed surgery up front (Markwalder ≥2, hemiparesis). This may bias _against_ steroids and limits generalization to milder, surgery-deferrable disease.
- **Steroid harms** (hyperglycaemia, infection, psychiatric, etc.) are well-recognized and plausibly drove the excess complications.
- Underlying pathophysiology (trauma → chronic inflammatory neomembrane) still leaves anti-inflammatory therapy _theoretically_ plausible as an **adjunct**, just not as a **primary alternative** to evacuation.

## How it fits with the prior literature

> [!info] Companion trial — Dex-CSDH (Hutchinson et al.) N Engl J Med 2020;383(27):2616–2627 — DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2020473. Dexamethasone **vs placebo as an adjunct** (n = 748; most patients _had_ surgery). Steroids → **fewer favourable outcomes + more adverse events**, but **fewer reoperations**.

- **Convergent message:** across both trials, dexamethasone **does not improve functional outcomes and adds harm**.
- **Key divergence on reoperation:** Dex-CSDH (steroid as _adjunct to surgery_) showed **fewer** reoperations with steroids; DECSA (steroid as _alternative to surgery_) showed **more** subsequent surgery — because monotherapy fails. The role of the drug (adjunct vs substitute) explains the opposite direction.

## Practice implications

- **Surgery (burr-hole drainage) remains first-line** for symptomatic cSDH.
- **Primary dexamethasone monotherapy is not supported** as an equivalent alternative for operative-grade disease.
- A steroid _adjunct_ role is not established by these data and carries net harm signals; if used at all, weigh against complication risk.
- Caveat: trials enrolled operative-grade patients — they do **not** answer what to do in very mild/asymptomatic cSDH or in poor surgical candidates.

## Open questions / follow-up

- Does an anti-inflammatory adjunct help in a _defined_ subgroup (e.g., post-evacuation recurrence prevention)?
- Where does this leave **[[Middle Meningeal Artery Embolization|MMA embolization]]** in the algorithm vs steroids vs repeat burr-hole? (Newer EMBOLISE / MAGIC-MT / STEM data — worth a separate note.)
- Role of [[Markwalder Grading Scale|Markwalder grade]] and radiographic membrane architecture as a treatment-selection biomarker.
