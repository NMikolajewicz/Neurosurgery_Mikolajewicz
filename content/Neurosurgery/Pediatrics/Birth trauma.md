> [!summary] Birth trauma (neonate) 
> 
> - **Risk**: primipara, **large infant**, **preterm**, difficult/operative delivery (vacuum/forceps/C-section), breech/rapid birth → ↑ skull fx/ICH.
> 
> - **Deformational forces** → **tentorial tears** (± falx) → ==**subdural hematoma**==.
> 
> - **Forceps**: **"ping-pong" skull fracture** → elevate mainly for **cosmesis**.
> 
> - **ICH by GA**: **term** → supratentorial **SDH** (difficult delivery/large baby) or venous **SAH** (focal seizures, usually benign); **<32 w** → **periventricular hemorrhage**.
> 
> - **Dx**: **CT** (not U/S for SDH/SAH); **SAH prognosis good**, **SDH guarded**.
> 
> - **Tx**: **vitamin K**; **daily subdural taps** if symptomatic SDH; **surgical drainage** if posterior fossa hematoma.
> 
> - **Scalp lesions**: **caput** (above periosteum, crosses sutures, <2 w); **cephalohematoma** (subperiosteal, **no suture crossing**, appears <24 h, may calcify); **subgaleal** (diffuse/fluctuant, crosses sutures, **do NOT drain**).
> 
> - **Nerve injuries**: **facial nerve palsy** most common → resolves **2–3 mo**; **brachial plexus** (shoulder dystocia, C5–6) → support + passive ROM; **~90% resolve**, reassess ~3 mo (MRI/repair 3–9 mo if persistent).
>

## **Head trauma**

### Risk
- primiparas
- large infants
- preterm delivery
- difficult delivery
	- operative delivery
		- vaccum extraction, forceps, cesarean section
	- rapid birth
		- breech presentation
		- rapid head moudling during final moments of birth
	- increased risk of skull fractures, intracranial hemorrhage
### Slow deformational forces
- tears in tentorium
	- less commonly falx
- complication: ==subdural hematoma==
### Forceps delivery
- ==ping-pong skull fracture==
- requires elevation for cosmetic reasons

### Intracranial hemorrhage
- site of ICH is GA-dependent
	- supratentorial SDH
		- exclusively full-term
		- large infants with difficult deliveries
	- supratentorial SAH
		- venous origin
		- full term newborns with focal seizures and benign clinical course
	- periventricular hemorrhage
		- premature < 32w GA
- Diagnosis
	- ==CT==
	- not U/S
- prognosis
	- SAH: good
	- SDH: guarded
- management
	- ==vitamin K==
	- ==daily subdural taps==
		- if symptomatic subdural hematomas
	- ==surgical drainage==
		- if posterior fossa hematoma

### Vaginal delivery complications

#### ==molding==
- bone overlapping at sutures
- resolves < 2 weeks
#### ==caput succedaneum==
- swelling in presenting portion of scalp
- above periosteum
- secondary to compression by cervix
- resolves < 2 weeks
#### ==subgaleal hematoma==
- subgaleal blood accumulation
- soft fluctuant swelling over entire scalp
- not limited by periosteal insertions
- self-limiting - do not needle/incisionally drain d/t infection risk
#### ==cephalhematoma==
- subperiosteal blood accumulation
- after instrumental delivery
- no presenta at birth, appears within 24 h
- fluid-blood collection limited by periosteal insertion at suture lines
	- does not extend across suture
- resorbs spontaneously within few weeks
	- may calcify and form bony protrusion

## **Cranial nerve injury**

### Pathophysiology
- ==facial nerve== most common
- mechanism of injury
	- pressure on nerve in utero
	- forceps pressure
- injury typically at or distal to exit from stylomastoid foramen
### Presentation
- facial asymmetry
### Workup
- clinical diagnosis
- no further testing required
### Management
- ==typically resolves by 2-3 months==

## **Brachial plexus injuries**

### Pathophysiology
- stretching by ==shoulder dystocia==
- upper plexus injury more common
	- Lower lesions (C7-T1) are rare (2% cases)
### Associated injuries
- fractures of clavicle/humerus
- subluxation of should or cervical spine
### Presentation
- asymmetric moro reflex
- entire brachial plexus
	- flair arm + horner syndrome
	- 0% recovery, extremity growth may be impaired
### Management
- ==hand support==
- ==passive ROM exercises==
- ==self-resolving (90% cases)==
	- especially C5-C6 (most common injury)
- 3 month observation
	- MRI to determine extent of injury
	- surgical exploration / repair (3-9 months age)
