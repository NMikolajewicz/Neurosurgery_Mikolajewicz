a.k.a PVH-IVH
## Pathophysiology
* ==hemorrhage into germinal matrix== exclusively seen in preterm infants after asphyxia
* IVH in term infants from choroid plexus (or venous sinus thrombosis)
* hemorrhage occuring in subependymal friable, richly vascular germinal matrix
	* lies on lateral wall of lateral ventricles between thalamus and caudate, near foramen of monro
## Germinal matrix
* site of ==neuronal proliferation== as neuroblasts divide and migrate into cerebral parenchyma
	* present only in <32
* neuronal proliferation is ==completed by 20w GA==
* metabolically-active differentiating cells of germinal matrix are ==rich in mitochondria==
	* sensitive to ischemia
	* blood supply
		* primitive and fragile retelike capillary network
		* arterial supply
			* recurrent artery of Heubner and lateral striate arteries
		* venous supply thalamostriate veins
		* vessels tend to rupture with episodes of hypoxemia (immature lungs) and fluctuations in cerebral perfusion

## Neurogeneisis in adults
* olfactory bulb
* hippocampus
## Workup
* ultrasound
	* blood is echogenic material
* all infants < 30w GA must be screened
	* cranial U/S at 7-14 days postnatal life
## Classification
* Grade I: confined to germinal matrix (i.e., subependymal)
	* asymptomatic
	* good prognosis
* Grade II: +blood in ventricles
	* small volume (<50% ventricular volume); no ventricular enlargement
	* non-specific irritability/lethargy
	* good prognosis
* Grade III: +++blood in ventricles
	* ventricular dilation
	* > 50% ventricular volume
	* posthemorrhagic ==hydrocephalus==
	* cerebral palsy / mental retardation (30-40%)
	* mortality (< 10%)
* Grade IV: ==periventricular hemorrhagic venous infarction==
	* additional ==hemorrhage into parenchyma==
	* involves periventricular motor tracts
	* secondary to Grade I-III hemorrhage
		* congestion in periventricular white matter -> venous infarction -> secondary hemorrhage
	* mortality (27-80%)
	* cerebral palsy / mental retardation (90%)

## Presentation
* destruction of periventricular cerebral parenchyma
	* cerebral palsy
	* mental retardation
	* seizures
* post-hemorrhagic hydrocephalus
* other complications
	* global hypoxic-ischemic injury
	* periventricular leukomalacia
		* non-hemorrhagic ischemic necrosis

![[Neurosurgery/Pediatrics/_resources/Periventricular__intraventricular_hemorrhage.resources/image.6.png|500]]

## Management
* prophylaxis
	* ==indomethacin==
		* accelerates maturation of germinal matrix vasculature
	* r==educe systemic blood pressure fluctuations==
		* greatest risk <72 h of life
			* 50% hemorrhages on 1st day
* correct anemia, acidosis, hypotension
* provide ventilatory support
* daily head circumference
* weekly head U/S
* Grade I-II
	* no treatment
* Grade III-IV
	* hydrocephalus typically resolves within weeks
	* daily ==ventricular punctures== to drain large CSF volumes to prevent hydrocephalus
		* e.g., 10 mL CSF/kg
	* ==ventriculosubgaleal (VSG) shunt==
		* if head growth double normal rate over 2 weeks
		* if elevated ICP persists
