> [!summary] Benign Enlargement of the Subarachnoid Spaces in Infancy (BESSI / BEH)
> 
> - **Most common cause of macrocephaly** (♂>♀), often **+family history**; typically **no trauma** and **neuro exam normal**.
> 
> - Clinical: **macrocephaly + frontal bossing**, ± enlarged anterior fontanelle; development usually **normal**.
> 
> - Imaging: **widened bifrontal/anterior CSF spaces** that **follow gyral contours** with **no gyrus flattening**; posterior sulci usually normal.
> 
> - Ventricles: **normal caliber** (helps separate from true hydrocephalus).
> 
> - Key DDx: **SDH/subdural fluid** vs BESSI → **cortical vein sign**: **BESSI veins adjacent to inner table**; **SDH veins displaced inward** ± **mass effect**.
> 
> - DDx: **cerebral atrophy** (often **small head circumference**, diffuse sulcal prominence).
> 
> - Course: **spontaneous resolution by ~2 years**; head size may stay large but **prognosis is good**.
>

## Background
- also known as benign external hydrocephalus (BEH)
## Clinical characteristics
- macrocephaly
- frontal bossing
- neurologically intact
- no history of trauma
## Epidemiology
- most common cause of macrocephaly
- males > females
- family history is common
## Associations
- scaphocephaly
## Radiographic findings
- widening of bifrontal/anterior CSF spaces
- no flattening of adjacent gyri
- CSF space follows gyral contours
- usually normal sulci posteriorly
- anterior fontanelle frequently enlarged
- normal ventricular calibre
## Differential diagnosis
- subdural fluid collection
	- distinguish with cortical vein sign
	- BESSI - cortical veins adjacent to inner table
	- SDH
		- cortical veins displaced away from inner table
		- collection may have mass effect
- cerebral atrophy
	- associated with small head circumference
## Treatment and prognosis
- resolves spontaneously by 2 years age
- macrocephaly may persist
- good developmental prognosis
