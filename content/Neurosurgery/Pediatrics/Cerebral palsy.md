> [!summary] Cerebral palsy (CP) 
> 
> - **Non-progressive motor disorder** from **static injury** to developing brain (cerebrum/cerebellum); **skills not lost once acquired**.
>     
> - Phenotypes: ==**spastic**== (mono/hemi/di/quad) most common; **dyskinetic** (==dystonia==/choreoathetosis), **ataxic**, **mixed**.
>     
> - Big etiologies: **prematurity/PVL** and **term HIE**; impaired control of posture/movement.
>     
> - Tx ladder: **PT/OT + stretching/orthoses/casting → meds** (==baclofen==, tizanidine, diazepam; **dantrolene** refractory—watch liver) → **botox/phenol**.
>     
> - **SDR**: best for **ambulatory spastic diplegia** (GMFCS **II–III**), good selective motor control, **PVL pattern**, **minimal dystonia** → durable tone ↓, **gait improves**.
>     
> - **ITB pump**: best for **severe generalized spasticity/dystonia** (often GMFCS **IV–V**) when oral meds fail/SDR unsuitable.
>     
> - ITB emergencies: **withdrawal = urgent re-establish intrathecal baclofen**; **overdose** → airway support + **empty reservoir** ± **physostigmine**.
>     
> - **GMFCS**: I runs/jumps; II walks w/ limits; III walks w/device; IV wheelchair; V dependent mobility.
>     
> - Quick consult: define **pattern + distribution**, **GMFCS + goals**, prior response, **contractures/deformity** (ortho/PM&R), and ITB **dose/refill/alarms**.
>

### Pathophysiology
* non-progressive motor disorder due to early postnatal non-progressive (static) injury to developing brain
	* cerebrum or cerebellum
	* i.e., due to non-progressive (static) encephalopathy
	* patients do not lose skills once acquired
* Most common patterns: 
	* ==spastic== (mono/hemi/di/quad)
	* dyskinetic (==dystonia==/choreoathetosis)
	* ataxic
	* mixed
* Etiologies: Prematurity/PVL and term HIE dominate etiologies.
* abnormal control of movements/posture

## Spasticity/dystonnia management ladder

1. **Conservative**: ==PT/OT==, stretching, orthoses, serial casting.
2. **Medications**: ==baclofen==, tizanidine, diazepam; dantrolene for refractory spasticity (watch liver).
3. **Focal procedures**: ==botulinum toxin==, phenol neurolysis (historical but still used selectively)
4. **Neurosurgical** (choose by pattern/GMFCS/phenotype):
    - ==**Selective dorsal rhizotomy (SDR)**== – best for ambulatory kids with spastic diplegia (GMFCS II–III), good selective motor control, PVL pattern, minimal dystonia. Intra-op EMG-guided sectioning of sensory rootlets to break the reflex arc; tone reduction durable ≥ ~5 yrs; improves gait in ambulatory CP; little effect on α-spasticity; non-ambulators may ease care but usually remain non-ambulatory .
	    - ==best for ambulatory spastic diplegia; improves gait; durable tone reduction; not for dystonia .==
    - ==**Intrathecal baclofen (ITB) pump**== – best for severe, generalized spasticity/dystonia (often GMFCS IV–V) when oral meds fail or SDR not suitable. 
	    - ==best for generalized spasticity/dystonia; avoid withdrawal (re-establish intrathecal drug fast), treat overdose with airway + reservoir empty + physostigmine .==
    - **Ablative/other** (rare today): thalamotomy/dentatotomy can help unilateral dystonia but bilateral lesions threaten speech; reserve for select cases .
### Gross motor function classification system (GMFCS)
- The gross motor skills (e.g. sitting and walking) of children and young people with cerebral palsy can be categorised into 5 different levels using a tool called the Gross Motor Function Classification System (GMFCS) developed by CanChild in Canada.
- GMFCS I–V
	- I runs/jumps
	- II walks limits
	- III walks with device
	- IV power/manual wheelchair
	- V dependent for mobility

# Quick consult checklist
- Pattern? (spastic vs dystonic; distribution)
- GMFCS? Goals? (function vs care/comfort vs pain)
- Prior treatments tried? Response?
- Contractures/deformity that will limit gains post-procedure? (coordinate with ortho/PM&R)
- For ITB patients: last refill, dose, alarms; watch for **withdrawal** vs **overdose**; have the overdose kit plan (reservoir empty + physostigmine) and withdrawal rescue plan ready .
