> [!summary] Dandy–Walker malformation (DWM) — exam essentials
> 
> - **Most common posterior fossa malformation**; often detected **prenatally/infancy**.
>     
> - **Triad**: ==vermian hypoplasia== + **cephalad rotation**, ==cystic dilatation of 4th ventricle== (extends posteriorly), ==enlarged posterior fossa== with **high tentorium/torcular–lambdoid inversion**.
>     
> - **Epi**: ~**1/30,000** live births; ~**7.5%** (4–12%) of **infantile hydrocephalus**.
>     
> - Presents via **CSF/ICP physiology**: macrocephaly/↑OFC, vomiting/irritability, bulging fontanelle; variable developmental/motor issues.
>     
> - **Workup**: **MRI brain** (± CINE) to define vermis/4th ventricle communication, posterior fossa size + torcula position, and **hydrocephalus**.
>     
> - **Key DDx**: **mega cisterna magna** (normal vermis + 4th ventricle, no hydro); **Blake pouch cyst** (normal vermis + torcula, tetraventricular hydro).
>     
> - **Arachnoid cyst**: anteriorly displaces cerebellum/4th ventricle; often no hydro; **epidermoid** restricts diffusion.
>     
> - **Tx**: treat **hydrocephalus/ICP** (VP shunt vs ETV ± cyst diversion); outcome depends on **associated anomalies**.
>

## Definition

**Dandy-Walker malformation (DWM)** = the **most common posterior fossa malformation**, classically defined by a triad:

- ==**Vermis hypoplasia**== with **cephalad rotation** of the vermian remnant
- ==**Cystic dilatation of the 4th ventricle**== extending posteriorly
- ==**Enlarged posterior fossa**== with **torcular–lambdoid inversion** (high tentorium; torcula above lambdoid level)

> [!important] Exam anchor  
> If you see **vermian hypoplasia + enlarged posterior fossa + 4th ventricle cystic expansion**, think **DWM** (not just "large cisterna magna").

---
## Epidemiology

- Prevalence: **~1 in 30,000 live births**
- Accounts for **~7.5%** of infantile **[[Hydrocephalus]]** (range **4–12%**)

---

## Clinical features (targeted supplementation)

- Often detected **prenatally** or in infancy (US/MRI).
- Common presenting issues are driven by **hydrocephalus/posterior fossa crowding**:
    - macrocephaly / rapidly increasing OFC, irritability/vomiting, bulging fontanelle
    - developmental delay or motor coordination issues (variable)
- Prognosis is strongly influenced by **associated CNS/extracranial anomalies** (look for them).

> [!tip] What to look for on your consult  
> Symptoms of **raised ICP**, OFC trend, developmental milestones, and any syndromic features; review prenatal imaging if available.

---

## Workup

- **MRI brain** (± spine depending on associated findings) to define:
    - vermis development/rotation
    - 4th ventricle communication
    - posterior fossa size + tentorium/torcula position
    - presence/degree of **hydrocephalus**
- In prenatal/neonatal settings: correlate with **fetal/neonatal US** and multidisciplinary counseling (MFM + genetics + neurosurgery).

---

## Differential diagnosis (key discriminators)

### Mega cisterna magna

- **Normal 4th ventricle**
- **Normal vermis**
- **No hydrocephalus**

### Retrocerebellar arachnoid cyst

- **Anterior displacement** of cerebellum and 4th ventricle
- Often **no hydrocephalus** (as provided)

### Blake pouch cyst

- **Normal vermis (not hypoplastic)**
- **Normal torcular position**
- **Tetraventricular hydrocephalus**

### Epidermoid cyst

- Mimics CSF spaces but typically shows **diffusion restriction** (practical MRI clue)

### Joubert anomaly

- Can have **vermian hypoplasia** (distinguish by additional brainstem/cerebellar features)
### Isolated 4th ventricle

- Consider especially in post-shunt or complex hydrocephalus contexts

> [!warning] Common pitfall  
> Labeling every "large retrocerebellar CSF space" as DWM. **Posterior fossa size** and **torcular/tentorium position** are major separators.

## Management

- Treat **hydrocephalus/ICP physiology** if present:
    - CSF diversion options commonly include **VP shunt** or **ETV** (caseand anatomy-dependent).
    - Some patients require **cyst-related diversion** strategies (decision typically imaging-driven).
- Long-term: neurodevelopmental surveillance + therapies; ophthalmology if ICP concerns.

> [!important] Practical principle  
> Management is usually **about CSF dynamics** and associated anomalies—not "fixing" the malformation itself.
