> [!summary] One-liner  
> **Growing skull fracture (GSF)** = **progressively enlarging pediatric skull fracture** after trauma due to **dural tear** and **CSF/brain pulsations** → **fracture margin erosion + non-union** with **extracranial herniation/encephalomalacia extension** (the "cyst" is a **misnomer**). Often appears **months post-injury**.

## Definition

- **Leptomeningeal cyst / growing skull fracture**: enlarging skull fracture near post-traumatic **encephalomalacia**
- "Cyst" is not a true cyst → **extension of encephalomalacia**
- Typically recognized **a few months after trauma**

## Epidemiology

- **0.05%–1%** of childhood skull fractures
- Majority occur in children **< 3 years**

> [!tip] High-yield association  
> Think GSF in a **young child** with a **prior linear/diastatic fracture** and a **progressively enlarging scalp mass** weeks–months later.

## Presentation

- **Enlarging scalp mass**
- **Seizures**
- **Focal neurological deficit**
- **Headache**

## Pathogenesis

- **CSF pulsations erode fracture margins** → gradual expansion + **non-union**
- Common accompanying findings (imaging/clinical):
    - **Extracranial brain herniation**
    - **Hydrocephalus**
    - **Unilateral ventricular dilatation**
    - **Porencephalic cyst**

> [!warning] Key risk factor 
> **Dural tear** is the major driver of GSF. Even **post-craniotomy** skull defects can "grow" if the **dura is not closed watertight**.

## Complications

- In neonates/young infants, **brain extrusion** may occur soon after a **diastatic linear fracture**
    - Can cause **focal dilation of the lateral ventricle** adjacent to the fracture
    - This **may be reversible** and can normalize after surgical repair
- Risk of **neurological deterioration**
- Development of **seizure disorders**

## Workup & Imaging (targeted supplement)

- **CT head (bone windows):** defines fracture widening/diastasis and bone loss over time
- **MRI brain:** evaluates **dural defect**, **herniated brain**, **encephalomalacia**, and associated **porencephaly/hydrocephalus**
- Track change over time if presentation is delayed (helps confirm "growing" nature)

## Management

- **Craniectomy and repair**
    - Core principles (targeted supplement):
        - **Repair dural laceration watertight**
        - Reduce/excise nonviable herniated tissue as needed
        - **Cranioplasty** to reconstruct calvarial defect (technique varies by age/defect)

## Rahman Classification

- **Type I:** GSF with **leptomeningeal cyst**
- **Type II:** GSF with underlying **gliotic brain**
- **Type III:** GSF with **porencephalic cyst**
    - _Porencephaly:_ CSF-filled cavity within brain

> [!note] Differentials 
> Consider **cephalohematoma/subgaleal hematoma**, **encephalocele**, or **post-traumatic scalp swelling**—GSF is suggested by **progressive enlargement** + imaging evidence of **fracture widening/dural defect**.
