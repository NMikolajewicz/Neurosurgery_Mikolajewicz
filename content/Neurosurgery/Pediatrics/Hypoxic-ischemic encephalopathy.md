> [!summary] Key points
> 
> - **HIE** = (sub)acute encephalopathy from **systemic hypoxemia** and/or **cerebral ischemia** (perinatal asphyxia).
>     
> - Severity depends on **GA/brain maturity** + **duration/pattern** of ischemia (profound/acute vs partial/prolonged).
>     
> - ==Multi-organ dysfunction== (renal/hepatic/cardiac/coagulopathy) is a hallmark and tracks severity.
>     
> - Typical progression: **depression → hyperalertness/hyperreflexia → coma**.
>     
> - Lesion pattern by GA: **preterm** → germinal matrix/PVL; **≥36 wks** → cortex (_ulegyria_), basal ganglia (_status marmoratus_), brainstem, Purkinje cells.
>     
> - Workup is **clinical**; supportive: US/CT for edema/infarct, Doppler **luxury perfusion**; **EEG/aEEG** for occult seizures.
>     
> - Seizures: **phenobarbital 1st-line**, then **lorazepam**, then **IV phenytoin**.
>     
> - Major sequelae: **[[Cerebral palsy]]**, epilepsy, cognitive/motor deficits (prognosis tied to encephalopathy + EEG/imaging pattern).

## Pathophysiology

- (Sub)acute brain lesions due to **systemic hypoxemia** or **cerebral ischemia**
- Encephalopathy from **asphyxia**
- Severity dependent on:
    - **Brain maturity** (preterm vs term)
    - **Duration** + **pattern** (profound/total vs partial/prolonged)
- ==Involvement of multiple organs besides brain is hallmark of HIE==
    - clinical cue: kidney, liver, myocardium, lungs, GI, coagulopathy often track severity and resuscitation burden.

---

## Typical course / presentation

1. **Depression**
2. **Hyperalertness / hyperreflexia**
3. **Coma**

> [!note] Severity progression
> The "depression → hyperalertness → coma" progression roughly maps to evolving encephalopathy severity. In practice, clinicians often communicate severity as **mild / moderate / severe** with parallel EEG/aEEG patterns and seizure burden.

### Quick severity scaffold (high-yield bedside)

|Feature|Mild|Moderate|Severe|
|---|---|---|---|
|Consciousness|irritable / hyperalert|lethargic|stupor/coma|
|Tone|normal ↑|hypotonia|flaccid|
|Reflexes|hyperreflexia|↓|absent/weak|
|Seizures|uncommon|common|variable (often refractory)|
|EEG/aEEG|near-normal|abnormal background|severely suppressed/burst-suppression|

## Duration/pattern of ischemia → lesion pattern

### <10 min **(profound) asphyxia**

- ==No permanent brain damage==
### >20–25 min **(total) asphyxia**

- ==Diffuse lesions==
- Rarely compatible with life
### **Partial asphyxia** (minutes to hours)

- Predominantly **supratentorial** lesions
#### Preterm infants

- Damage occurs at **germinal matrix** area
    - Periventricular hemorrhage, leukomalacia
#### Post–36 week GA

Lesions involve:

- **Cerebral cortex**
    - _Ulegyria_ = laminar neuronal necrosis in depths of sulci
- **Basal ganglia**
    - _Status marmoratus_
- **Brain stem**
- **Cerebellar Purkinje cells**
    - Cerebellar atrophy

### Minor intrauterine anoxic episodes (duration uncertain)

- Diffuse neuronal involvement
- Preferentially affects **hippocampal** areas

> [!tip] Pattern recognition  
> A useful mental model is **"profound/acute"** (deep gray structures, brainstem risk) vs **"partial/prolonged"** (watershed/cortical–subcortical injury).

## Workup

- Clinical diagnosis
- **Ultrasound**
    - Cerebral edema
    - Ischemic lesions: diffuse/localized echodensities
- **Pulsed Doppler**
    - Increased diastolic velocities with reduced resistive index
        - Sign of **luxury perfusion**
- **CT**
    - Cerebral edema
    - Infarction

> [!note] Common practice
> 
> - **MRI (DWI)** is often the most sensitive for early ischemic injury patterns and prognostication (timing-dependent).
> 
> - **EEG/aEEG** helps detect subclinical seizures and background suppression.
>  

---
## Management

- Supportive
- +/- ==Brain hypothermia==
    - Potentially useful for mild-to-moderate cases
### Seizures

- ==Phenobarbital== (1st line)
- Lorazepam (2nd line)
- Phenytoin IV (3rd line)

> [!warning] seizure pearl  
> Neonatal seizures are frequently **subtle or electrographic-only**. If exam and course don't match, prioritize **EEG/aEEG correlation**.
## Outcomes

- Major long-term sequelae: **[[Cerebral palsy]]**, epilepsy, cognitive/behavioral impairment, motor deficits, vision/hearing issues
- Prognosis correlates with: encephalopathy severity trajectory, seizure burden, and injury pattern on imaging/EEG
