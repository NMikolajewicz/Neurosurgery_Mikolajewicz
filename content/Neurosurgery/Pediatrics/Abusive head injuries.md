> [!summary] Abusive Head Trauma (AHT) 
> 
> - Suspect in **<2 yrs** (≈ **1/3** head injuries) or **>2 yrs** with **unexplained AMS**; diagnosis is **pattern-based** (history + exam + imaging + labs).
>     
> - Mechanism: **angular acceleration** → **bridging vein SDH** (often **multi-compartment/bilateral**) + **diffuse HII**; **impact may be occult**.
>     
> - Exam: **bulging fontanelle**, **splayed sutures**, **engorged scalp veins**; eyes: **retinal hemorrhage**, **setting-sun**, **CN VI palsy**, ± **dilated pupils**.
>     
> - Development: **hand preference <1 yr is abnormal** (suggests focal deficit).
>     
> - Workup: **fundoscopy**, **CT/MRI brain + cervical spine**, **skeletal survey**, **CBC + INR/PTT** (rule out coagulopathy/Vit K deficiency).
>     
> - Imaging clues: **falx/tentorium hemorrhage**, **effaced cisterns**, scalp edema, skull fractures; **don’t skip spine MRI**.
>     
> - Tx: resuscitate/ICP care + seizure control; **burr holes/craniotomy** ± **subdural/peritoneal shunt** if indicated.
>     
> - Pearl: **“Triad” (SDH + RH + encephalopathy) ≠ pathognomonic**, but **highly concerning** in context; avoid over-aging blood on MRI.
>

## Abusive Head Trauma (Non-Accidental Injury)

### Overview

- ==non-accident injury==
- ~ **1/3 head injuries in kids < 2 years**
- **Screen** in children **> 2 years** with **altered mental status of unknown etiology**
- **Epidemiology**
    - ==neglect (57%)==
    - physical abuse (19%)
    - sexual abuse (10%)
    - others

> [!important] Clinical framing  
> AHT is a **pattern-based diagnosis**: integrate history plausibility + exam + imaging + labs + multidisciplinary input.

---

### Mechanisms / Pathophysiology

- ==**Angular acceleration**== → **bridging vein injury** (**multi-compartment hemorrhage**), **diffuse hypoxic–ischemic injury (HII)**, **cervicomedullary ligamentous damage**
- **Impact may be occult**

**Clinical implications**

- Expect **intracranial + cervical/spinal** findings more often than appreciated.
- Neurologic injury often reflects **secondary hypoxia/ischemia** and **diffuse injury**, not just mass effect.

### Presentation & Physical Exam

#### Head/Fontanelle/Scalp

- splayed sutures
- bulging fontanelles
- scalp veins engorged

#### Eyes / Cranial Nerves

- dilated eyes
- setting-sun
- CN VI palsy
- **retinal hemorrhage**

#### Developmental clue

- **handedness**
    - hand preference only appears **> 1 year** children

---
### Differential Diagnosis

- acute hydrocephalus
- subdural empyema/abscess

**common mimics**

- **Coagulopathy/bleeding diathesis** (incl. vitamin K deficiency)
- **Benign extra-axial fluid collections** of infancy (± superimposed hemorrhage)
- **Metabolic disorders** (e.g., glutaric aciduria type I) when history/exam suggests
- Accidental trauma with **corroborated mechanism** / witnessed event

---

### Workup 

- physical exam
- fundoscopy
- **CT/MRI brain + spine**
- skeletal survey
- **CBC + clotting studies (INR, PTT)**
    - rule out **vitamin K deficiency**, **secondary anemia**

**Targeted supplementation (often paired with the above in suspected abuse)**

- Consider **LFTs + lipase/amylase ± UA** to screen for occult abdominal injury (institution-dependent protocols).
- **Repeat skeletal survey** in ~10–14 days is commonly used when initial survey is negative but suspicion persists (per child protection protocols).

> [!warning] Safety / systems  
> In suspected AHT, coordinate early with **pediatrics/child protection**, **ophthalmology**, **radiology**, and social services; ensure **chain-of-custody documentation** per local policy.

---

### Imaging Findings 

- **subdural hemorrhage** (+/- bilateral)
- **Non-patent subarachnoid cisterns**
- hemorrhages along **falx/tentorium**
- **scalp edema** (+/- bilateral)
- skull fractures
- splayed sutures

**Targeted supplementation (high-yield adds)**

- MRI sequences to lean on: **DWI** (HII), **SWI/GRE** (microhemorrhage), and **cervical spine MRI** for ligamentous injury/spinal SDH.

---

### Management

#### Acute priorities 

- Stabilize **ABCs**, treat seizures, manage temperature/glucose, and apply **ICP-directed care** as indicated (neurocritical care pathway).
- Engage multidisciplinary team early (care + documentation quality).

#### Neurosurgical interventions

- **craniotomy vs. burr hole drainage** of SDH
- +/- **subdural or peritoneal shunt**

> [!danger] Do not anchor on "surgical fix"  
> Neurologic outcome is often driven by **diffuse HII** and secondary injury—optimize physiologic parameters early.

---

### Long-term Outcome

- possible chronic neurological deficits
    - developmental delay
    - cerebral palsy
    - epilepsy

---

### Pearls & Pitfalls

- **The =="triad" (SDH, RH, encephalopathy) ≠ pathognomonic==**, but in context is highly concerning—synthesize the full picture.
- **Spine matters** — Cervical cord/ligamentous injury and spinal SDH are frequent; don't skip spine MRI.
- **Avoid over-precision in hemorrhage "age"** on MRI; stick to relative acute/subacute/chronic descriptors.
- **Screen for polytrauma** — Skeletal survey and abdominal injury labs can be decisive.
- **Early multidisciplinary coordination** improves care quality and documentation.
