### Pathophysiology
* premature fusion (in utero) of cranial suture
* skull growth
	* restricted perpendicular to affected suture
	* enhanced parallel to affected suture
* increased ICP only when >1 suture affected
	* adverse effects on development
### Normal development
* occipital frontal circumference (OFC) routinely assessed in all children < 2 years
* growth
	* 90% of adult head size achieved by 1 year
	* 95% by 6 years
	* growth ceases by 7 years
* skull is **unilaminar at birth**
	* diploe appears by 4th year
		* reaches maximum by 35 years
* fontanelles
	* anterior closes by 2.5 years
	* posterior/sphenoid/mastoid close by 2-3 months
* sutures
	* serve as bone deposition sites in growing calvarium
	* skull grows perpendicular to suture
	* maintained open by ongoing brain growth
	* closure by 12 years
### Types
* single suture synostosis
	* sagittal suture (55% cases)
		* scaphocephaly
		* no neurological deficits
	* coronal suture (20-30% cases)
		* brachycephaly
		* increased rate of neurological complications
			* optic atrophy
		* harlequin eye sign
			* supraorbital margin higher than normal
		* orbit rotates resulting in amblyopia
	* metopic suture
		* trigonocephally
		* mild
		* no surgical intervention
* unilateral (asymmetric) synostosis
	* lambdoid suture
		* ==posterior plagiocephaly==
		* enlargement of ipsilateral mastoid process
			* pathognomonic for lambdoid synostosis
	* coronal suture
		* ==anterior plagiocephaly==
* combined - suggests craniofacial syndrome
	* coronal + sagittal sutures
		* oxycephaly
		* high, conical head
		* sharp bossing of anterior fontanelle
	* coronal + sagittal + lambdoid sutures
		* triphyllocephaly
		* ==most severe== craniosynostosis
		* vision/hearing loss
		* mental deficiency rare
		* urgent surgical repair

### Syndromic craniosynostoses (10-20% cases)
* FGFR mutations
	* FGFR2 autosomal mutation
### Presentation
* worsening facial/cranial deformity in first few months of life
* visible/palpable riding of closed suture
* increased ICP
* airway problems
* vision loss
### Workup
* CT with 3D reconstruction
* clinical diagnosis
* technetium bone scan
	* increased activity if prematurely closing sutures
	* no uptake in completely closed sutures
	* normally, little isotope uptake in first weeks of life
### Management - see extended reading for details
* surgical
	* indications
		* cosmetic
		* elevated ICP
			* maximal constrictive effect occurs at birth
		* progressive exophthalmos
			* threatens eyes
	* timing
		* early surgery
			* soon after birth
			* best cosmetic results
			* risk of recurrent deformity
		* late surgery (12 months old)
	* principles
		* single suture involvement
			* strip suturectomy
			* overcorrect to accomodate growing head
	* postoperative
		* ICU for 24 h
		* 6 month follow up
		* reoperate if recurrence

## Sheila's recommended reading

### **Etiology_**

* premature closure of single or multiple cranial sutures - idiopathic or secondary
* **Idiopathic**
	* simple
		* nonsyndromic: sagittal > coronal > metopic > lambdoid
	* compound
		* nonsyndromic: bicoronal
		* syndromic: Crouzon’s, Apert’s, Pfeiffer’s, Saethre-Chotzen
* **Secondary:**
	* metabolic
		* rickets
		* hyperthyroidism
	* mucopolysaccharidosis
		* Hurlers
		* Morquio
	* hematologic
		* PCV (polycythemia vera)
		* SCD (sickle cell)
		* thalassemia
	* pharmacologic
		* MTX
		* valproate
		* dilantin
	* structural
		* shunted hydrocephalus
		* microcephaly, holoprosencephaly, encephalocele

### **_Epidemiology_**

* 10 per 10,000 live births
* can be familial (usually AD)
	* 10% of coronal synostoses are familial
	* ~2% of the other synostoses are familial
* strong male predominance for: sagittal, metopic, lambdoid
* female preponderace for coronal
* proportion of synostoses:
	* sagittal        55%
	* coronal        30%
	* metopic       13%
	* lambdoid     2%
		

### **_Pathogenesis_**

* ectomeninx (forming eventual dura) formed by neural crest cells at 5-6 wks
* ossification centres develop in center of ectomeninx - bone grows outward (membranous growth)
* coalesce and new bone growth occurs at the sutures (induced by brain growth)
	* brain doubles in size by 6 months, then again by 2 years
* Virchow’s law:
	* arrest of forward growth along the suture line is accompanied by increased compensatory growth parallel to the fused suture
* asymmetric growth occurs in adjacent open sutures to compensate - bossing
* abnormality also involves sutures of skull base
* ==mechanism mostly undetermined==. Proposed theories:
	* mechanical
		* ==intrauterine constraint== (twins, oligohydramnios)
	* molecular/syndromic
		* defect in ==FGFR== (i.e. Aperts, Crouzons)
		* ? aberrant “on” signalling of osteoblasts - excess micro-bone formation at suture
		* other potential protein mutations: shh, BMP

### **_Clinical_**

* age of presentation varies:
	* some born with misshapen head
	* some develop develop misshapen head in first few months
	* if secondary cause is present, misshapen head may not be evident for 2-3 years
* history:
	* perinatal history (incl. maternal illness & drug use)
	* ==family history== of craniosynostosis
* physical:
	* head shape/circumference, feel for ==palpable ridge==
	* neurological exam, ==dev. delay, papilledema== (important: some deformities assoc. w/­ICP)
	* facial/skeletal/limb deformities
	* torticollis
* ==ICP==:
	* more common with multiple suture involvement
	* ~ 11% with single suture involvement

### **_Imaging_**
**Plain XR**
* fused suture = lack of lucency, sclerosis along margin      

**CT**        
* more sensitive
* better at differentiating positional plagio vs. lambdoid
* may see widened SAS in areas of increased skull growth
**Bone scan**
* no longer used, but,
	* normal  = mild uptake
	* closing prematurely = increased uptake
	* closed = no uptake

### **_General Surgical Principles_**
* aim to perform ==corrective surgery ~ 4-6 months==, balancing:
	* child grown enough - slightly better blood loss tolerance

				   _versus_
* surgery >6 mths =  higher failure, more extensive procedure (usually)
* general risks:
	* ==blood loss==
	* ==seizures==
	* ==stroke==
* prepare for likely ==blood transfusion==: offer parents directed-donation
	* requires minimum 5-7 day preparation period
	* associated with ­risk of graft-versus-host disease (GVHD), affecting:
		* liver - jaundice, liver failure
		* skin - rashes, blistering
		* gut - diarrhoea, belly pain
		* if serious - immunosuppressants
* blood-saving maneuvers:
	* blood in room before starting case
	* infiltrate scalp with LA + ==1:400,000 epinephrine==
	* ==Raney clips==
	* preserve pericranium – dissect it only in regions necessary for bone cuts
	* apply good ==head-dressing== to prevent hematoma formation
	* ==NO drain==
	* check Hb post-op and daily for 3-4 days while in hospital

### **_Sagittal Synostosis_** 
* ==80% of all synostosis==
* males 2:1
* usually apparent at birth
* 2% familial (AD with 40% penetrance)
* characteristic shape = **==scaphocephaly==**
	* long narrow head (boat-shaped) with narrow BPD
	* palpable midline ridge (“keel”)
	* ant. suture fusion  -  frontal bossing (compensatory growth at coronal suture)
	* post. suture fusion - occipital bossing (compensatory growth at lambdoid suture)
	* can get temporal area “pinching” – depression along axis of coronal suture
#### Treatment
* ==best if done early (<6mo)==
* prone position, head extended - must protect eyes and face from pressure
* various forms:
	* strip craniectomy (about **4 cm wide**) from coronal suture to lambdoid suture
	* notes: width of strip important, skin incision long. or transverse, endoscopic described…
	* risks: dural laceration, SSS injury
	* can add:
		* barrel-staving of parietal bone
		* re-shaping of frontal or occipital regions
		* p-procedure – 2 paramedian strips plus resection of coronal suture
		* reverse p-procedure – resection of the lambdoid suture                            
		* postop, consider use of helmet (used routinely by Drake/Dirks at HSC with claimed good results)

### **_Unilateral Coronal Synostosis_**
* ==30% of synostoses==
* females slightly more common
* characteristic shape = **==anterior plagiocephaly==**
	* fusion effects entire “coronal ring” (concept of Bertelsen, 1958)
	* therefore, face also affected
		* ipsilateral flat forehead, contralateral frontal bossing
		* ipsilateral eyebrow is recessed and drawn up = Harlequin deformity on XR, see below
		* ipsilateral eye may be proptotic
		* contralateral eye may be inferior and lateral  can cause diplopia & head tilt
		* palpable ridge with flattened zygoma
		* tragus displaced anteriorly & inferiorly
* Humphrey’s pearls on uni-coronal synostosis:
	* MUCH more common on right side
	* if left side affected, almost all are girls
	* the nose tip will point subtly away from affected side, but nasal root points toward lesion
#### Treatment
* best 6-12 mths
* surgery should address sphenoethmoidal and other skull base sutures
* ==bifrontal craniotomy==
	* care to recognize thickened pterion and raised sphenoid ridge
	* \+ advancement of supraorbital bar
	* variations described
#### Outcome
* 80-90% good outcome
* 10% need further surgery

### **_Metopic Synostosis_**
* characteristic shape = **==trigonocephaly==**
	* keel-shaped, ==triangular forehead==
	* recessed supraorbital rims
	* flattening of the frontal bones
	* temporal narrowing
	* biparietal bossing (flaring)
	* hypotelorism with oval-shaped orbits = “surprised racoon” appearance
* highest incidence of assoc. brain anomalies       
	* agenesis of CC
	* holoprosencephaly
	* 40% have learning disabilities
#### Treatment
* can be mild and not require treatment  - only 20% require surgery
* perform at 3-6 months
* ==bifrontal craniotomy with supraorbital advancement==
#### Outcome
* 80-90% good outcome

![[Neurosurgery/Pediatrics/_resources/Craniosynostosis.resources/image.6.png]]

### **_Lambdoid synostosis_**
* male:female = 4:1
* 70% right side
* rare: ==must distinguish from the far, far more common positional plagiocephaly==
* chacteristic shape = **posterior plagiocephaly**
	* flattened ipsilateral parieto-occiput
#### Treatment
* perform at 3-6 mths
* ==bilateral occipitoparietal craniotomy and reshaping==
#### Outcome
* roughly similar to other synostoses

### **_Positional (deformational) plagiocephaly_**
* 100x more common than true lambdoid synostosis
* d/t ==persistent positioning on one side==
* above can be due to:
	* decreased mobility: CP, mental retardation, premie, chronic illness
	* abnormal posture: congenital torticollis or c-spine probs
	* intrauterine crowding, etc
	* intentional positioning to avoid SIDS ® increased incidence since 1992 (when AAP recommended supine sleeping to prevent SIDS)
* can be differentiated clinically from true lambdoid synostosis (see above)
* usually no imaging in classical positional plagiocephaly, but:
	* XR - open suture with varying perisutural sclerosis
	* **CT -  on 3-D recon, check sagittal alignment of post fossa vs true sagittal midline**
		* **positional plagiocephaly: nearly parallel**
		* **true lambdoid synostosis: deviated towards lesion**
#### Treatment
* first step: ==active repositioning==
	* move location of toys, mobiles
	* use pillows
	* change location of crib relative to room entrance
	* physical therapy if torticollis/tight SCM
	* most will respond to this alone
* if no response to positioning/PT: ==headband/helmet==
	* best for those <6 mths, but works also for 7-12 mths
	* keep on for 20-23 hours per day with daily washing and check for pressure sores
	* try for 3-6 months
* VERY rarely:
	* if persistent severe deformity >12 mths old, might consider surgery (~2-3%)

### **_SYNDROMIC CRANIOSYNOSTOSIS_**
Crouzon's and Alpert's syndromes are most common syndromic craniosynostoses
#### **_Crouzon's Syndrome_**
* 1 in 25,000 births - more common than Aperts
* AD, 50% are sporadic
* assoc. with:
	* increased paternal age
	* mutations ==Chr. 10 and FGFR2==
	* short final height
	* hydrocephalus (less common than Aperts)
* craniosynostosis rarely present at birth
	* ==evolves during infancy==
	* variable clinical expression: from no craniosynostosis - severe clover-leaf
* most common:
	* ==brachycephaly = bilateral coronal (95%)==                
* less common:
	* scaphocephaly = sagittal
	* Kleeblatschadel = bilat coronal, lambdoid, and metopic
* other:
	* closed coronal sutures and of the basal sphenofrontal and sphenoethmoidal sutures
* features:
	* shallow orbits - ==ocular proptosis==
	* ==hypertelorism==
	* parrot-beak nose
	* maxillary hypoplasia
* can have excellent intellectual function if craniosynostosis and hydrocephalus are treated appropriately.

#### **_Apert's Syndrome_**
* AD but 95% of cases sporadic since few grow to adulthood and have children
* 1:160,000 births
* assoc. with:
	* increased paternal age
	* ==syndactyly==
	* ==more sutures closed - hydro== (more common than Crouzon’s, ? 2° ­venous pressures from closed skull)
	* deformity obvious at birth (versus evolves during infancy in Crouzon’s)
	* mental retardation
* features:
	* acrobrachycephaly
	* hypertelorism & ocular proptosis
	* maxillary hypoplasia
	* beaked nose
	* large, bulging anterior fontanelle that closes late
	* low-set ears

#### **_Saethre-Chotzen syndrome_**
* AD, no sporadic cases
* 1 in 50, 000 births
* similar to Apert's, but coronal synostosis is asymmetrical, causing plagiocephaly
* normal mental development
#### **_Pfeiffer’s syndrome_**
* AD with occasional sporadic cases
* 1 in 200,000
* turribrchycephaly (?!) – bilateral coronal synostoses, max. hypoplasia, mandibular prognathism
* “classic” form  -> good outcome
* other forms -> very poor outcome:
	* severe craniofacial anomalies
	* hydrocephalus
	* upper airway difficulties

## Miscillaneous

**CRANIOSYNOSTOSIS**:

Def: Premature closure of the cranial suture(s).

Classification:
            Sagittal- most common.
            Coronal
            Lambdoid- least common
            metopic
            multiple suture synostosis

Morphological:

* Scaphocephaly
	* elongated in AP & narrow in biparietal dimension with a posterior "Keel" and a frontal "boss".
* Trigonocephaly
	* pointed forehead & triangular skull.
* Plagiocephaly
	* Anterior - anterior flattening of one portion of skull
	* Posterior - posterior flattening of one portion of skull
* Brachycephaly
	* Broadening of skull with short AP diameter, recessed lower forehead and supraorbital boss.
* Oxycephaly
	* Pointed skull
* Turicephaly
	* Very high pointed skull with vertical forehead.

Epidemiology:
0.4/1000 live births. Most cases are sporadic. Familial incidence in 2% of saggital & 8% of coronal synostosis.

Etiology: multiple etiologies can be invoked:

* Congenital:
	* metabolic conditions affecting bone formation       
	* skull base abnormality with transmission of force by dural bands
	* intrauterine events, genetic determinants, syndromes (e.g., Cruzon's, Apert's).
	* Teratogens (phenytoin, valproate, aminopterin, vit A)
* Acquired:
	* bone deformity from overshunting resulting in premature suture closure.

Pathology:
**pathogenesis:**

* Normal brain growth: 50% increase in weight by 2 mos., 100% by 6 mos., and 200% by 10 mos.
* Brain growth is virtually complete by the 2nd year.
* Fusion of the sutures is complete by 6-8 years.
* Cranial deformity occurs when 1 or more of the cranial sutures closes prematurely.
	* This causes compensatory enlargement of the cranium from the remaining sutures.
	* The compensatory growth occurs primarily in sutures adjoining the closed one.
* **Virchow's law**: compensatory skull growth occurs in a direction perpendicular to a fused suture.

**Gross:** SEE BELOW.

Clinical: Usually skull deformity as above, raised ICP in multiple synostoses.

Natural history of uncorrected craniosynostosis:

* in one series of 171 patients:
	* 26  papilledema
	* 33 optic atrophy
	* 85 headaches
	* 39 epilepsy
	* 42 with below-average IQ.

Up to 33% of patients may have elevated ICP.
In single suture synostosis, increased ICP is in 13%.
Almost invariably present in clover-leaf deformity (kleeblattschadel).
Associated CNS anomalies are least common with saggital synostosis, AND may include septo-optic dysplasia, ventriculomegaly, and others.

Ophthalmologic problems may occur in patients, either due to increased ICP, or due to bony abnormalities of the orbit. Strabismus most common.

Hydrocephalus may accompany multiple caraniosynostoses, clover leaf deformity, Apert's & Crouzon's.

Physical exam:
            inspect skull, sutures, fontanelles
            Measure head circumference
            Assess suture: gap, mobility, ridge.
            Fundi for papilledema
            Neuroophthalmologic assessment.

Rule of thumb: Child with normal head size & shape does not have craniosynostosis.

Laboratory

* Plain radiographs
* CT scan (3D)
* bone scan: increased activity during active phase of union, decreased once union has occurred.

Treatment & Results:
Parental counselling about:
            nature of deformity
            difficulty growing up as "cone head"
            associated neurology

Surgery is usually for cosmesis, except in cases of elevated ICP.

Technical issues:
            positioning for extensive calvarial exposure
            maintain integrity of dura

Timing of Surgery:

* earlier the better.
* Best age 1-3 months.
* \>6 months, surgical correction more difficult.

**Sagittal**

* 50% of craniosynostoses in most series
* 80% are male
* scaphocephalic skull
* Surgery
	* usually craniectomy for excision of the saggital suture, or bilateral strip craniectomies with wrapping of the bone edges
	* The midline craniectomy is about 6-8 cm wide.
	* More extensive surgery is required >1 yr of age.
* Outcomes    
	* virtually all patients, regardless of the exact type of surgery, benefit from it.

**Coronal**

* Bilateral
	* will produce Brachycephalic skull
		* narrow AP dimension, buldging forehead, wide cranium
		* If untreated, becomes Oxycephaly or Turricephaly (cone head).
* Unilateral:         
	* will produce Anterior plagyicephaly
		* ipsilateral frontal bone and orbital flattening, harlequin eye deformity (elevated lateral canthus), contralateral frontal bossing.
* Surgery:                        
	* Unilateral require strip craniectomies with canthal advancement.
	* Bilateral may have more involved craniofacial anomalies (Apert's, Cruzon's), and require significant reconstruction.
	* Before 6 mos may be treated with only bilateral canthal advancement.

**Lambdoid**

* Unilateral:         
	* will produce posterior Plagiocephaly-
		* Unilateral occipital flattening, frontal bossing is Ipsilateral, with ipsilateral forward displacement of ear.
	* MUST DISTINGUISH from positional molding (abnormality not present at birth!)
* Surgery:           
	* Suturectomy in the very young may be acceptable (1-3 mos).
	* May require more extensive crniofacial surgery.
	* Since defect is covered by hair, consider NO treatment.

**Metopic**

* Produces trigonocephaly. Mozart had this!
* Surgery:
	* Suturectomy is no good.
	* Need to remove the frontal bone, bilateral canthal advancements, replace the bone with contoured split cranial graft from parietal bone ("Floating forehead repair").

**Multiple suture synostosis:**
Requires major reconstructive surgery. High incidence of raised ICP.

**CRANIOFACIAL ANOMALIES.**

Def: A group of conditions that have in common an abnormal configuration of the cranium and face.

Epidemiology: Incidence is between 1:5000 & 1:20,000.

Etiology: Multiple. Syndromic, chromosomal.

Pathology:
**pathogenesis:** Inherent pathologica defect is in the basal skull sutures, including the frontoethmoidal and fronto sphenoidal. These, together with the coronal sutures, form a ring around the anterior aspect of the skull. With their closure, the anterior fossa is foreshortened so that the middle fossa frequently extends anterior to the anterior fossa. The midportion of the face is attached to the frontal bone, and thus remains behind along with it. This retropositioning of the frontal bone results in shallow orbits and hypoplastic maxillae.

_Crouzon's Syndrome:_
            - Autosomal dominant, 25% are sporadic.
				\- closed coronal sutures and of the basal frontosphenoidal and frontoethmoidal sutures. Sometimes saggital and metopic sutures also closed. Shallow orbits with proptotic eyes. Maxillary hypoplasia. Nose resembles a parrot's beak.
				\- Can have excellent intellectual function if craniosynostosis and hydrocephalus are treated appropriately.

_Apert's Syndrome (acrocephalysyndactily):_
				\- Autosomal Dominant, 1:160,000 births. 95% of cases sporadic, since few used to grow to adulthood and have children.
				\- Facies are similar to Crouzon's, except that all of the sutures are closed. Hydrocephalus is thus more common (? due to high venus pressures caused by the closed cranium).
				\- There is osseus and soft tissue syndactily of digits 2,3,4 in the hands and feet. The thumb is small & hypoplastic. Upper extremities are shortened.

_Saethre-Chotzen syndrome:_
            - Autosomal dominant, no sporadic cases
				\- Similar to Apert's, but coronal synostosis is asymmetrical, causing plagycephaly.

_Kleeblattschadel (clover-leaf skull)_:
				\- Synostosis of the coronal, basal skull, lambdoid and metopic sutures. Hydro is common. May occur in Crouzon's or Apert's.
