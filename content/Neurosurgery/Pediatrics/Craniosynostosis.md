## Definition

Premature (often in utero) fusion of one or more cranial sutures → characteristic **head shape deformity** from altered calvarial growth.

> [!important] Virchow's law 
> Skull growth is **restricted perpendicular** to the fused suture and **compensatorily increased parallel** to it → predictable deformational patterns + bossing at adjacent open sutures.

---

## Pathophysiology

- Premature fusion of cranial suture(s)
- Calvarial growth pattern:
    - **restricted perpendicular** to affected suture
    - **enhanced parallel** to affected suture
- **Increased ICP**: classically when **>1 suture** affected (but can occur in single-suture cases too)
    - adverse effects on development

---

## Normal skull development 

### Head growth / OFC

- **OFC** routinely assessed in all children **<2 years**
- Head size milestones:
    - **90%** adult head size by **1 year**
    - **95%** by **6 years**
    - growth ceases by **~7 years**

### Skull tables / diploë

- Skull is **unilaminar at birth**
- Diploë appears by **~4th year**
    - reaches maximum by **~35 years**

### Fontanelles

- Anterior closes by **~2.5 years**
- Posterior/sphenoid/mastoid close by **~2–3 months**

### Sutures

- Sutures are **bone deposition sites** in the growing calvarium
- Skull grows **perpendicular** to suture
- Maintained open by ongoing brain growth
- Closure: cited as **~12 years** in one section; elsewhere **~6–8 years** (practical takeaway: pediatric skull remains suture-dependent through childhood)

## Classification

### By number/pattern of sutures

- **Single-suture synostosis**
- **Unilateral (asymmetric) synostosis**
- **Combined/multiple sutures** → suggests craniofacial syndrome; higher ICP risk; more urgent

### By etiology

- **Idiopathic**
    - simple nonsyndromic: **sagittal > coronal > metopic > lambdoid**
    - compound: bicoronal
    - syndromic: **Crouzon, Apert, Pfeiffer, Saethre–Chotzen**
- **Secondary**
    - metabolic: rickets, hyperthyroidism
    - mucopolysaccharidosis: Hurler, Morquio
    - hematologic: polycythemia vera, sickle cell, thalassemia
    - pharmacologic/teratogens: MTX, valproate, phenytoin (dilantin), aminopterin, vitamin A
    - structural: shunted hydrocephalus (overshunting), microcephaly, HPE, encephalocele

## Epidemiology 

- Incidence reported as **~10 per 10,000** live births (also cited as **0.4/1000**)
- Familial (usually AD):
    - ~**10%** coronal synostoses familial
    - ~**2%** other synostoses familial
- Sex tendencies:
    - male predominance: sagittal, metopic, lambdoid
    - female preponderance: coronal
- Proportion of sutures involved (commonly cited):
    - sagittal **55%**
    - coronal **30%**
    - metopic **13%**
    - lambdoid **2%**

---

## Clinical presentation

- Worsening cranial/facial deformity in first months of life
- Visible/palpable **riding** of closed suture; **palpable ridge**
- Symptoms/signs of increased ICP:
    - headache (older child), irritability/vomiting
    - developmental delay
    - papilledema → optic atrophy → vision loss
- Syndromic clues:
    - airway problems, proptosis, limb anomalies, midface hypoplasia, multi-suture patterns

> [!tip] Rule of thumb  
> Child with **normal head size + normal head shape** is unlikely to have craniosynostosis.

---

## Increased ICP 

- More common with **multiple sutures**
- Reported frequency:
    - ~**11–13%** in single-suture synostosis
    - up to **33%** overall in some series
    - nearly universal in **cloverleaf (kleeblattschädel)** patterns

## Workup

### Diagnosis (often clinical + imaging confirmation)

- **CT with 3D reconstruction** (most informative; differentiates positional plagiocephaly vs lambdoid synostosis)
- Plain XR: fused suture = loss of lucency/sclerosis along margins (less sensitive)
- Technetium bone scan (historical/rare now):
    - normally little isotope uptake in first weeks of life
    - increased uptake if actively closing prematurely
    - no uptake if completely closed

### History/physical essentials

- Perinatal history (incl maternal illness/drug use)
- **Family history**
- Head shape + OFC trajectory
- Palpate sutures/fontanelles for **ridge/gap/mobility**
- Neuro exam + development
- Fundoscopy for papilledema; neuro-ophthalmologic assessment
- Look for craniofacial/limb anomalies, torticollis

## Morphologic patterns and hallmark findings

|Fused suture/pattern|Head shape|Key clinical/imaging features|
|---|---|---|
|**Sagittal** (most common)|**Scaphocephaly**|Long narrow "boat-shaped" head; midline "keel" ridge; frontal and/or occipital bossing; usually **no neuro deficits**|
|**Coronal (bilateral)**|**Brachycephaly** → may progress to **oxycephaly/turricephaly** if untreated|Higher neuro complication rate; optic issues; shallow orbits|
|**Unilateral coronal**|**Anterior plagiocephaly**|Ipsilateral forehead flattening + contralateral bossing; orbital dystopia; **Harlequin eye sign** (elevated supraorbital margin/lateral canthus); possible amblyopia/diplopia/head tilt|
|**Metopic**|**Trigonocephaly**|Triangular/keel forehead, hypotelorism, temporal narrowing; often mild (many do not need surgery); higher assoc brain anomalies cited|
|**Lambdoid** (rare)|**Posterior plagiocephaly**|Ipsilateral occipital flattening; **ipsilateral mastoid enlargement** (pathognomonic); must distinguish from positional plagiocephaly|
|**Coronal + sagittal**|**Oxycephaly**|High conical head; sharp bossing at anterior fontanelle|
|**Coronal + sagittal + lambdoid**|**Triphyllocephaly** (most severe)|Vision/hearing loss; mental deficiency rare; **urgent repair**|

## Positional (deformational) plagiocephaly

- **~100×** more common than true lambdoid synostosis
- Due to persistent positioning (↑ since "Back to Sleep" era), torticollis, prematurity, decreased mobility, intrauterine crowding
- Imaging usually not needed if classic; if uncertain:
    - XR: open suture (± perisutural sclerosis)
    - CT 3D: check **posterior fossa alignment**
        - positional: nearly parallel
        - true lambdoid: deviated toward lesion

### Management (positional)

- First-line: **active repositioning** + PT for SCM/torticollis
- If persistent: **helmet/headband** (best <6 mo; works 7–12 mo)
    - wear 20–23 h/day; daily skin checks; trial 3–6 months
- Surgery: very rare (persistent severe deformity >12 mo; ~2–3%)

## Syndromic craniosynostosis (10–20%)

- Often **FGFR mutations** (FGFR2 highlighted)
- Common syndromes: **Crouzon, Apert** (plus Pfeiffer, Saethre–Chotzen)
- Red flags: multi-suture synostosis, midface hypoplasia, proptosis, airway compromise, limb anomalies (e.g., syndactyly)

### Crouzon syndrome

- AD; many sporadic; associated with ↑ paternal age
- Mutations: **Chr 10 / FGFR2**
- Often evolves during infancy (not always at birth)
- Common: **brachycephaly (bilateral coronal ~95%)**
- Features: shallow orbits → **proptosis**, hypertelorism, parrot-beak nose, maxillary hypoplasia
- Can have excellent intellectual function if treated appropriately

### Apert syndrome

- AD; **~95% sporadic**; ↑ paternal age
- **Syndactyly**; more sutures closed; hydrocephalus more common than Crouzon
- Deformity often obvious at birth; may have cognitive impairment
- Features: acrobrachycephaly, proptosis/hypertelorism, maxillary hypoplasia, late-closing large anterior fontanelle, low-set ears

## Management

### Indications for surgery

- **Cosmesis** (most common indication)
- **Elevated ICP**
    - "maximal constrictive effect" occurs at birth (noted in provided content)
- Progressive **exophthalmos** threatening eyes
- (Practical add) Functional issues: airway compromise (syndromic), significant orbital dystopia/strabismus

### Timing

- Early surgery (soon after birth)
    - best cosmetic results
    - risk of recurrent deformity
- Late surgery (~12 months)
- General principle from reading: target **~4–6 months** (balance growth vs blood loss tolerance)
    - > 6 months: higher failure, more extensive procedures often required

### Surgical concepts (single-suture)

- **Strip suturectomy** ± overcorrection to accommodate growth
- Sagittal synostosis (examples listed):
    - strip craniectomy (width emphasized; e.g., ~4 cm in one section)
    - ± barrel-staving; frontal/occipital reshaping; "P-procedure" variants
    - postop helmet sometimes used (center-dependent)

### Post-op

- ICU for **24 h**
- Follow-up at **6 months**
- Reoperate if recurrence
- Monitor Hb daily (3–4 days in hospital noted in reading)

> [!warning] Peri-op risks and prep  
> Major themes: **blood loss**, seizures, stroke. Prepare for likely transfusion; consider blood-sparing maneuvers (scalp LA + dilute epi, Raney clips, preserve pericranium, head dressing, typically **no drain**).

## Procedural considerations

### Sagittal synostosis (scaphocephaly)

- Best done **early (<6 mo)**
- Risks: dural laceration, SSS injury
- Positioning: prone with head extended; protect eyes/face

### Unilateral coronal synostosis (anterior plagiocephaly)

- Often treated **6–12 months**
- **Bifrontal craniotomy** + supraorbital bar advancement; address skull base sutures
- 80–90% good outcome; 10% need further surgery

### Metopic synostosis (trigonocephaly)

- Many mild cases: no treatment (only ~20% require surgery noted)
- If operating: **3–6 months**, bifrontal craniotomy + supraorbital advancement
- Assoc brain anomalies more common than other types (as cited)

### Lambdoid synostosis (posterior plagiocephaly)

- Rare; distinguish from positional
- Surgery: **3–6 months**, bilateral occipitoparietal craniotomy + reshaping
- Since hair covers defect, some cases may be observed (noted in misc section)

---

## Quick differentiation: true lambdoid vs positional plagiocephaly

- **True lambdoid synostosis**
    - posterior plagiocephaly + **ipsilateral mastoid enlargement** (pathognomonic)
    - CT 3D: posterior fossa alignment deviates toward lesion
- **Positional plagiocephaly**
    - history of positioning/torticollis; open suture
    - CT 3D: alignment nearly parallel
    - responds to repositioning/helmet

---

## Complications / natural history (uncorrected; cited series)

- Papilledema, optic atrophy, headaches, epilepsy, below-average IQ reported
- Ophthalmologic: strabismus (common), vision loss from ICP or orbital bony issues
- Hydrocephalus may accompany multi-suture, cloverleaf, Apert, Crouzon

---

## Practical clinic checklist

- Pattern: which suture(s), symmetric vs asymmetric
- OFC trend + head shape evolution (birth vs develops over months)
- Palpable ridge / suture mobility
- Neurodevelopment; symptoms of ICP
- Ophthalmology: papilledema/strabismus/proptosis
- Syndromic screen: airway, limbs, midface, family history
- Imaging: CT 3D if uncertain or for operative planning; avoid unnecessary imaging in classic positional plagiocephaly
