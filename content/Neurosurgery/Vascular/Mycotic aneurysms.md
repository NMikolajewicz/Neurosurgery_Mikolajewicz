## Cerebral Mycotic Aneurysm

Cerebral mycotic aneurysms, also known as infectious intracranial aneurysms (IIAs), represent a rare but serious cerebrovascular complication arising from bacterial or, less commonly, fungal infection of the intracranial arterial wall. Despite the name "mycotic" coined by William Osler in 1885 based on gross pathological appearance, these aneurysms are predominantly bacterial in origin, with _Streptococcus_ (especially viridans group) and _Staphylococcus aureus_ accounting for up to 90% of cases.\[1\]\[2\]\[3\]

### Epidemiology and Pathophysiology

Mycotic aneurysms occur in approximately 2-5% of patients with infective endocarditis, which accounts for nearly 70% of all cerebral mycotic aneurysm cases. Up to 25% of affected patients harbor multiple intracranial aneurysms. The pathogenesis involves hematogenous spread of septic emboli to either the arterial wall itself or its vasa vasorum, leading to bacterial invasion, arteritis, degradation of the elastic lamina, and subsequent pseudoaneurysm formation through hydrostatic pulsations.\[3\]\[4\]\[5\]\[1\]

### Anatomic Distribution and Morphology

Unlike berry aneurysms located at major circle of Willis branch points, bacterial cerebral mycotic aneurysms characteristically occur at **peripheral branch points**, particularly in the distal middle cerebral artery (M2 and beyond). However, up to one-third arise proximally, especially with direct extravascular invasion from adjacent infections like meningitis or abscess. Fungal mycotic aneurysms tend to involve long segments of proximal larger circle of Willis vessels, often not involving branching points at all.\[6\]\[7\]\[1\]
Morphologically, these aneurysms are typically small (often <3-5 mm), though giant variants are rarely described. They exhibit fusiform (more common), saccular, or sacculofusiform configurations with frequently wide or absent necks.\[4\]\[8\]\[1\]\[6\]

### Diagnostic Imaging Modalities

### Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA)

DSA remains the **gold standard** for detecting cerebral mycotic aneurysms, particularly for small aneurysms <3 mm in size. The sensitivity of DSA is superior to cross-sectional imaging, with one study showing CT angiography detecting only 42.9% (3/7) and MR angiography detecting 33.3% (2/6) of aneurysms confirmed by DSA. The angiographic characteristics include focal vascular dilatation, saclike outpouching from the vessel wall, and the critical finding of **rapid morphologic changes** on serial imaging—aneurysms may disappear, regress, persist, enlarge, or rupture, and new aneurysms may appear during follow-up. One case documented mycotic aneurysm development within just 5 days on serial DSA.\[9\]\[10\]\[11\]\[1\]\[6\]

### Computed Tomography

Non-contrast CT is often the first-line imaging modality given its accessibility and serves to identify complications rather than the aneurysm itself. Common findings include:\[1\]\[6\]

* **Subarachnoid hemorrhage** (often convexal rather than basal cistern location)\[12\]\[13\]\[1\]
* **Intracerebral hemorrhage** (often lobar)\[1\]
* Associated abscess or ischemic stroke from septic emboli\[1\]

**CT angiography (CTA)** demonstrates moderate sensitivity for mycotic aneurysms. A meta-analysis reported pooled sensitivity of 71% (95% CI, 0.54–0.85) and specificity of 90% (95% CI, 0.83–0.95) for cerebral mycotic aneurysms. CTA sensitivity decreases dramatically for aneurysms <3 mm. Characteristic CT features when the aneurysm is visualized include peripheral location, lobulated or fusiform morphology, and irregular arterial wall.\[14\]\[15\]\[16\]\[17\]\[6\]

### Magnetic Resonance Imaging

MRI offers similar overall diagnostic performance to CT with pooled sensitivity of 79% (95% CI, 0.61–0.91) and specificity of 89% (95% CI, 0.81–0.95) for all mycotic aneurysms. However, MRI provides unique advantages:\[15\]\[14\]
**Susceptibility-Weighted Imaging (SWI)** has emerged as particularly valuable. Key findings associated with infectious intracranial aneurysms include:\[18\]\[19\]\[20\]\[21\]

* **Sulcal SWI lesions** with or without contrast enhancement (odds ratio 69, 95% CI 7.8-610, p<0.001)\[19\]\[20\]
* **Cerebral microbleeds** >5 mm\[21\]\[18\]\[19\]
* **Contrast enhancement within SWI lesions** (odds ratio 16.5, 95% CI 2.3-121, p=0.007)\[20\]\[19\]

In one study, 90% of patients with infectious intracranial aneurysms had cerebral microbleeds >5 mm or sulcal SWI lesions (9/10 patients). The absence of any MRI hemorrhage may have a high negative predictive value, potentially obviating the need for DSA in some cases.\[19\]\[20\]\[21\]
**Diffusion-Weighted Imaging (DWI)** reveals subclinical or asymptomatic ischemic strokes from septic emboli not visible on CT.\[1\]
**Conventional MRI sequences** may demonstrate:

* High T2 signal and low T1 signal in periarterial soft tissues\[22\]
* Post-contrast enhancement of the periarterial inflammatory tissues\[4\]
* Aneurysm wall enhancement\[23\]

**Vessel Wall Imaging (VWI)** using high-resolution black-blood contrast-enhanced T1-weighted sequences can detect arterial wall thickening and enhancement indicative of infectious vasculitis. This technique detected vascular involvement in cases where time-of-flight MR angiography failed to show abnormalities.\[24\]\[4\]

### Imaging Characteristics Favoring Mycotic Aneurysms

Several radiologic features help distinguish infectious aneurysms from berry aneurysms:\[1\]\[6\]\[4\]

1. **Peripheral/distal location** (especially M2-M4 segments)
2. **Multiplicity** (present in ~25% of cases)
3. **Rapidly changing morphology** on serial imaging
4. **Wide or absent neck**
5. **Fusiform or irregular morphology**
6. **Associated findings**: concurrent acute multifocal ischemic lesions, cerebral microbleeds, meningeal enhancement, abscess formation\[4\]\[12\]
7. **Perivascular inflammatory changes**: edema, soft tissue stranding, enhancement\[6\]\[4\]

### Hemorrhagic Complications

Ruptured mycotic aneurysms demonstrate distinctive hemorrhage patterns. While berry aneurysms typically cause basal cisternal subarachnoid hemorrhage, mycotic aneurysms more commonly produce:

* **Convexal (non-aneurysmal pattern) subarachnoid hemorrhage**\[12\]\[13\]\[25\]\[1\]
* **Lobar intracerebral hemorrhage**\[1\]
* Combination patterns with both subarachnoid and intraparenchymal components\[26\]

Rupture occurs in approximately 45-63% of cerebral mycotic aneurysms, with intracranial location carrying significantly higher rupture risk than extracranial aneurysms (63.0% vs 25.6%, p<0.001). Among initially unruptured aneurysms, 16.4% subsequently rupture, most commonly within the first 3-7 days after diagnosis. Ruptured aneurysms carry 43.6% in-hospital mortality compared to 21.7% for unruptured lesions.\[5\]\[27\]

### Clinical Imaging Algorithms

Given the varying sensitivities of imaging modalities, a rational diagnostic approach includes:

1. **Initial evaluation**: Non-contrast CT to detect hemorrhage; MRI with DWI and SWI sequences to identify ischemic lesions, microbleeds, and sulcal SWI lesions
2. **Aneurysm detection**: CTA or MRA as initial vascular imaging in patients with infective endocarditis and neurological symptoms or hemorrhage
3. **Gold standard confirmation**: DSA when clinical suspicion is high despite negative or equivocal CTA/MRA, particularly for small aneurysms\[10\]\[4\]\[1\]
4. **Serial imaging**: Repeat angiography to monitor aneurysm evolution during antibiotic therapy, as morphology can change rapidly (within days)\[9\]\[11\]\[4\]

The presence of intracranial hemorrhage on initial imaging in patients with infective endocarditis should prompt vascular imaging, preferably with DSA. Conversely, the absence of any MRI hemorrhage may carry high negative predictive value (0.977).\[17\]\[21\]\[9\]

### Imaging Follow-Up

Mycotic aneurysms managed conservatively with antibiotics require close angiographic surveillance, as they demonstrate unpredictable behavior—they may disappear, regress, persist, enlarge, or rupture despite appropriate antimicrobial therapy. Serial imaging at short intervals (days to weeks) is recommended to detect interval changes that may warrant intervention.\[11\]\[9\]\[4\]
