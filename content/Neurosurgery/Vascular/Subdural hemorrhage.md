### Epidemiology

* at-risk population
	* infants: [non-accidental injury](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/suspected-physical-abuse-1?lang=us)
	* young adults: motor vehicle accidents, vascular lesions
	* elderly: falls (although a definite history of trauma may be lacking)
* SDH present in ~15% of all head traumas
	* occur in up to 30% of fatal injuries.

### Presentation

	in the setting of head trauma
	
	depressed conscious state  (65-80%)
	
	pupillary abnormalities (~40%)
	
	+/- underlying bleeding disorder
		e.g. anticoagulation medication, thrombocytopenia)
		
	+/- structural abnormality
		e.g. [dural arteriovenous fistula](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/dural-arteriovenous-fistula?lang=us)
		

### Pathophysiology

	due to stretching and ==tearing of bridging cortical veins== as they cross the subdural space to drain into adjacent dural sinus.
		These veins rupture d/t shearing forces
			sudden change in the velocity of the head.
			
			The arachnoid may also be torn
				mixture of blood and CSF in the subdural space.
				
	10-30% of chronic SDH show evidence of ==repeated hemorrhage==.
		Rebleeding occurs from
			rupture of stretched cortical veins
			
			vascularized neomembrane
			
	not limited by sutures
		but is l==imited by dural reflections==
			e.g., [falx cerebri](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/falx-cerebri?lang=us), [tentorium](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/tentorium-cerebelli?lang=us), and [falx cerebelli](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/falx-cerebelli?lang=us)
			

### Imaging

	Acute
		crescent-shaped homogeneously hyperdense extra-axial collection.  
		
		As clot retracts, the ==density increases typically to >50-60 HU and is thus hyperdense== relative to the cortex 4.
		
		<40% SDHs have mixed hyper/hypodense areas
			reflects unclotted blood, serum extruded during clot retraction, or CSF due to an arachnoid laceration.
			
		Rarely, acute SDH is _isodense_ with the adjacent cerebral cortex.
			occurs with ==anticoagulation, coagulopathies, or severe anemia==
			
			when the hemoglobin concentration drops to 8 to 10 g/dL.
			
	Subacute
		As clot ages and ==protein degradation== occurs, SDH density decreases. 
			between 3 and 21 days (typically 10-14 days), the density will drop to ~ 35-40 HU 
				isodense to the adjacent cortex
				
				identification is tricky
					especially if subdural collections are bilateral.
					
					C==ontrast-enhanced CT== is often useful in this instance if MRI is unavailable. 
					
					The key to identification is visualizing a number of indirect signs, including:
					* CSF-filled sulci do not reach the skull but rather fade out into the subdural
					* mass-effect including [sulcal effacement (distortion)](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/sulcal-effacement?lang=us) and [midline shift](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/midline-shift?lang=us)
					* apparent thickening of the cortex
	Chronic
		at least ==3 weeks== old.
		
		SDH becomes ==hypodense== to the adjacent cortex 
			can reach ~0 HU
			
			isodense to CSF
			
		A crescentic shape may change to a biconvex one.
		
		Rarely, periphery of the SDH may calcify
			see [calcified chronic subdural hematoma](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/calcified-chronic-subdural-haematoma?lang=us) for details
			

### Acute on Chronic SDH

	refers to a ==second episode== of acute hemorrhage into a pre-existing chronic subdural hematoma.
	
	It typically appears as a hypodense collection with a hematocrit level (located posteriorly).
	
	similarly seen in patients with
		clotting disorders o
		
		anticoagulants
		

### Acute SDH Management

	indications for surgery
		\>10 mm thickness or MLS > 5 mm
			regardless of GCS
			
		<10 mm thickness and MLS
		
		clinical herniation
		
		GCS < 8
		
		GCS declines >1 due to mass effect
		
		regractory ICP
		
	timing
		emergent
		
		reduced mortality if <2-4 from injury to OR
		
	surgical technique
		==craniotomy==
			elderly
			
			no cerebral edema
			
			following commands prior to surgery
			
		==craniectomy==
			young patients with cerebral edema
			
			any patient with malignant cerebral edema
			

### Other treatment notes

	treatment depends on size of SDH
		amount of mass-effect 
		
		neurological impairment
		
	Small collections – so-called 'smear subdurals'
		chronic, asymptomatic
		
		observe with ==serial CT scans.==
		
	Symptomatic SDH
		acute
			==surgically evacuation==
			
			perform rapidly (< 4 hours)
			
			requires ==craniotomy==
				clot is not easily evacuated via burr holes.
				
		Subacute/chronic
			one or more ==burr holes==
				blood clot has liquefied
					can be washed out
					
	if cerebral edema
		dismal prognosis
		
		requires aggressive treatment against cerebral swelling
		

### Prognosis

	mortality in acute subdural hematomas requiring surgery is very high (50-90%)
		particularly if anticoagulated - only 20% fully recover
