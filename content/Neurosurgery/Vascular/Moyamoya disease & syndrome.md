## TL;DR

- **Moyamoya disease (MMD)** = idiopathic, progressive steno-occlusion of the terminal ICA ± proximal ACA/MCA with a fragile basal collateral network ("puff of smoke"); **moyamoya syndrome / quasi-MMD** = identical angiographic pattern WITH an associated condition (NF1, sickle cell, Down syndrome, radiation, autoimmune). Children present mainly with **ischemia**; adults with ischemia OR **hemorrhage** ("about one half of those who develop adult-onset MMD experience intracranial hemorrhage," JAM Trial, _Stroke_ 2014).
- **Surgical revascularization is the cornerstone**: indirect bypass (EDAS/pial synangiosis) favored in children; direct STA-MCA or combined favored in adults. The **JAM Trial (Stroke 2014)** is the only RCT — direct bypass cut rebleeding in hemorrhagic MMD (2.7%/yr vs 7.6%/yr, P=0.042, HR 0.355).
- High-yield: **RNF213 p.R4810K** founder variant (East Asians), **Suzuki staging** (I–VI), **ivy sign** (FLAIR), avoid **hyperventilation** (pediatric precipitant) and maintain **normocapnia/normotension/euvolemia** perioperatively, beware **cerebral hyperperfusion syndrome** after direct bypass.

## Key Findings

- MMD is a non-atherosclerotic, non-inflammatory intimal proliferative arteriopathy; bilaterality is no longer required for diagnosis (unilateral disease IS moyamoya disease per Japanese criteria).
- East Asian predominance; per Baba et al. (Hokkaido all-inclusive survey 2002–2006) the annual detection rate was 0.94/100,000 and prevalence 10.5/100,000; the 2003 Japanese nationwide survey found ~7,700 patients, 0.54/100,000/yr new cases, and family history in 12.1%. North American incidence is far lower (~0.086/100,000). Female:male ratio 1.8 (2003 nationwide survey) to 2.18 (Hokkaido); bimodal age peaks (~5–9 yr and 45–49 yr).
- DSA is the gold standard; cerebrovascular reserve (acetazolamide challenge) guides surgical indication.
- Surgery is low-risk in experienced hands and superior to conservative management for stroke prevention: per Guzman et al. (_J Neurosurg_ 2009; 264 patients, 450 procedures, mean follow-up 4.9 yr) surgical morbidity was 3.5% and mortality 0.7% per treated hemisphere, 5-yr stroke/death 5.5%, and 91.8% remained TIA-free at ≥1 yr.

## Details

### 1. Definition & Nomenclature

- **"Moyamoya"** = Japanese for "puff of smoke" — angiographic appearance of the basal collateral network. 
	- First described by Suzuki & Takaku (Arch Neurol 1969).
- **MMD (idiopathic)**: progressive stenosis/occlusion of the terminal ICA and proximal ACA/MCA with abnormal moyamoya collaterals, no associated condition.
- **Moyamoya syndrome / quasi-MMD**: same angiographic findings + an associated disease.
- **Diagnostic criteria** (Research Committee on Moyamoya Disease, Japan; **2021 Revised Version**, Kuroda et al., Neurol Med Chir 2022;62:307–312):
    - DSA: 
	    - (1) stenosis/occlusion at terminal intracranial ICA/A1/M1; 
	    - (2) abnormal vascular network (moyamoya vessels) in arterial phase. 
	      **Both bilateral AND unilateral cases can be diagnosed as MMD.**
    - The **"definite" vs "probable" distinction was abolished in the 2015 revision** (NOT 2021); the 2021 version added an explicit clarifying note that unilateral disease qualifies. **RNF213 testing is NOT a formal diagnostic criterion** (cited only as scientific rationale).
    - **MRI/MRA (≥1.5T, 3.0T preferred) can substitute for DSA** only if all 3 present: terminal ICA stenosis/occlusion; **bilateral decrease in OUTER diameter** of terminal ICA + horizontal MCA on heavy-T2 (a 2021 addition — distinguishes from atherosclerosis, where the lumen narrows but outer diameter is preserved); abnormal networks on MRA. DSA remains mandatory for unilateral or atherosclerosis-complicated cases.
    - **Quasi-MMD associated conditions (2021)**: autoimmune disease (SLE, antiphospholipid syndrome, polyarteritis nodosa, Sjögren), meningitis, brain tumors, Down syndrome, NF1, post-cranial-irradiation lesions. **Atherosclerosis, hyperthyroidism/Graves, and head trauma were REMOVED in 2021** — MMD + hyperthyroidism is now diagnosed as MMD.


### 2. Etiology & Genetics

- **RNF213** (chromosome 17q25.3) = major susceptibility gene in East Asians; founder variant **p.R4810K (c.14429G>A; rs112735431)**.
- Strongly associated: in a Japanese cohort the variant was found in **95.1% of familial MMD, 79.2% of sporadic MMD, vs 1.8% of controls (OR ~259)**; the original East Asian GWAS/founder studies reported ORs in the ~110–339 range (Liu et al. 2011; Kamada et al. 2011; Liu/Hitomi 2012 — Japanese OR ~339). Present in ~20% of Chinese sporadic cases; virtually absent in Caucasians (who carry other rare RNF213 variants).
- Homozygous p.R4810K → earlier onset and more severe phenotype.
- **Familial MMD ~10–15%** in Japan (12.1% family history in the nationwide survey); relatives have ~30–40× higher risk.
- **Moyamoya syndrome associations**: sickle cell disease, NF1, Down syndrome (trisomy 21), cranial irradiation (e.g., optic glioma, craniopharyngioma), Graves/thyroid, autoimmune (SLE, APS), prior meningitis. ACTA2 mutation linked to a familial smooth-muscle phenotype.

### 3. Pathophysiology

- Progressive stenosis/occlusion of **terminal ICA → proximal ACA/MCA**; posterior circulation (PCA) involved later/advanced disease.
- **Histopathology**: 
	- concentric **fibrocellular intimal hyperplasia** with smooth-muscle cell proliferation and ECM accumulation → luminal stenosis; **wavy/duplicated/disrupted internal elastic lamina**; **medial thinning (attenuation)**. 
	- **NO inflammation, NO atherosclerosis, NO lipid deposits** (lipids only occasionally). Both luminal AND outer arterial diameter shrink.
- **Moyamoya vessels** = dilated lenticulostriate/thalamoperforator collaterals; fragile, prone to microaneurysms → hemorrhage. Also transdural collaterals ("vault moyamoya"), leptomeningeal collaterals (→ ivy sign), ethmoidal collaterals.
- **Ischemic mechanism**: hemodynamic insufficiency in watershed/borderzone territories as ICA flow drops. **Hemorrhagic mechanism**: rupture of fragile moyamoya/periventricular collaterals (esp. **choroidal anastomosis**) or associated microaneurysms → IVH/IPH.

### 4. Epidemiology

- Highest in **East Asia** (Japan, Korea, China). Japan: prevalence ~6–10.5/100,000 (rising with detection); incidence ~0.35–0.94/100,000/yr. Korea prevalence rising (~9/100,000 by 2008). North American incidence ~0.086/100,000.
- **Bimodal age**: pediatric peak first decade (~5–9 yr); adult peak fourth–fifth decade (Hokkaido highest 45–49 yr).
- **F:M 1.8–2.18:1** (1.8 in 2003 nationwide survey; 2.18 Hokkaido).
- **Pediatric = predominantly ischemic** (TIA/infarct); **adult = higher proportion hemorrhagic** — about half of adult-onset cases in Asia are hemorrhagic. ICH represents ~20–50% of adult presentations; rare in children (~2–3%).

### 5. Clinical Presentation

- **Pediatric**: recurrent TIA/ischemic stroke (often **precipitated by hyperventilation** — crying, blowing hot food, playing wind instruments, balloon inflation → hypocapnia → cerebral vasoconstriction → ischemia), seizures, migraine-like headache, movement disorders (**chorea, limb-shaking TIA**), progressive cognitive decline. Watershed infarcts.
- **Adult**: ischemic AND hemorrhagic (IVH, IPH); headache; cognitive impairment. Hemorrhage prognosis poor.
	- hemorrhagic causes: dilated fragile vessels, rupture of associated aneurysms
- ==86% progression to stroke wihtin 5 years after initial TIA==
- risk of rebleeding within the next 20 years: 30-65%

### 6. Imaging & Diagnosis

- **DSA = GOLD STANDARD**. **Suzuki staging (I–VI)**:
    - I: narrowing of carotid fork (ICA bifurcation).
    - II: initiation of moyamoya — dilated ACA/MCA, basal moyamoya vessels appear.
    - III: intensification — most prominent basal moyamoya, ACA/MCA filling reduced (peak "puff of smoke").
    - IV: minimization — moyamoya vessels diminish, ECA transdural collaterals develop, PCA may be involved.
    - V: reduction — further decrease of basal moyamoya, increasing ECA collaterals.
    - VI: disappearance — moyamoya vessels gone; brain supplied entirely by ECA + vertebrobasilar collaterals ("burnt out").
- **MRI/MRA**: **ivy sign** (FLAIR/post-contrast leptomeningeal hyperintensity = slow flow in pial/leptomeningeal collaterals); flow voids in basal ganglia/thalamus (T2); **brush sign** (prominent hypointense deep medullary veins on SWI = oxygen-extraction/hypoperfusion); infarcts.
	- important to rule out watershed infarcts
- **CT/CTA**: hemorrhage, infarct, vascular anatomy.
- **Hemodynamic/CVR assessment**: SPECT, PET (↑OEF = "misery perfusion"), CT/MR perfusion, **acetazolamide (Diamox) challenge** to assess cerebrovascular reserve; 
	- "**steal phenomenon**" = paradoxical CBF decrease on challenge.
- **High-resolution vessel-wall MRI**: MMD shows concentric, homogeneous wall thickening with little/no enhancement and outer-diameter shrinkage — vs eccentric enhancing atherosclerotic plaque or concentric enhancing vasculitis.

### 7. Natural History & Prognosis

- Progressive; untreated symptomatic disease carries high recurrent-stroke risk.
- **Ischemic** (AHA/ASA 2023, Gonzalez et al., Stroke 2023): in US symptomatic patients, conservative management carries a "5-year risk of recurrent ischemic events reported as high as 65% in patients with unilateral disease and 82% in those with bilateral involvement." US cohort: ~10.3%/yr recurrent stroke; recurrent stroke ~18% in first year.
- **Hemorrhagic**: per the AHA/ASA 2023 statement, "recurrent hemorrhage has been reported in Japanese populations to occur in ≈30% to 60% of patients over long-term follow-up." A conservatively-managed cohort showed 4.5%/yr annual rebleed; cumulative rebleed 7.8% (5yr), 22.6% (10yr), 35.9% (15yr), with ~19% mortality per rebleed.
- **Choroidal anastomosis / posterior hemorrhage** predicts rebleeding (JAM substudy).
- Surgery reduces ischemic events and (for direct bypass in hemorrhagic MMD) rebleeding.
- Berlin Classification: can stratify onset and predict outcomes

### 8. Management

**Medical:**

- **Antiplatelet (aspirin)** for ischemic type — weak evidence; "reasonable" per AHA/ASA 2023 but caution re: hemorrhage. Cilostazol may benefit CBF/cognition (needs validation). No role for stenting/angioplasty (not advisable for ischemic MMD).
- **Avoid hyperventilation, hypotension, dehydration**. **Perioperative**: maintain **normocapnia (EtCO2 35–45 mmHg)**, normotension, **euvolemia to mild hypervolemia**; avoid mannitol; control pain/crying in children.
- **Calcium channel blockers** for headache/migrainous symptoms.
- Treat underlying condition in moyamoya syndrome.

**Surgical revascularization (cornerstone):**

- **Direct bypass — STA-MCA anastomosis**: immediate revascularization; technically demanding (small fragile recipient vessels); risk of **cerebral hyperperfusion syndrome (CHS)**.
- **Indirect bypass**: EDAS (encephaloduroarteriosynangiosis), EMS (encephalomyosynangiosis), EDAMS, **pial synangiosis**, omental transposition, multiple burr holes — relies on **neoangiogenesis over weeks–months**; favored in **young children** (small vessel caliber makes direct anastomosis difficult).
- **Combined direct + indirect** — increasingly used in adults.
- **Indications**: symptomatic ischemia, impaired cerebrovascular reserve, hemorrhagic presentation (direct bypass).
- **Pediatric**: indirect preferred (pial synangiosis). **Adult**: direct/combined preferred.
- **Complications**: **CHS** (incidence ~15–30% after direct STA-MCA, with symptomatic/transient neurologic deterioration as high as ~40% in some adult series; ~2.5% present with hemorrhage), perioperative stroke/TIA, watershed shift, wound/scalp ischemia (STA harvested), bypass occlusion, seizures.
- **Outcomes**: Stanford 450-procedure series (Guzman 2009): morbidity 3.5%, mortality 0.7% per hemisphere, 5-yr stroke/death 5.5%, 91.8% TIA-free at ≥1 yr. Boston pial synangiosis (Scott, >20-yr follow-up, 59 children): durable stroke protection, mRS stable/improved in most (6 deaths, 4 with prior cranial radiation).

### 9. Key Trials & Studies

- **JAM Trial (Japan Adult Moyamoya Trial)** — Miyamoto et al., _Stroke_ 2014;45:1415–21. Multicenter RCT, 22 institutes, 80 adults with **hemorrhagic** MMD (surgical 42 / nonsurgical 38), bilateral direct EC-IC bypass vs conservative, 5-yr follow-up. **Rebleeding 2.7%/yr (surgical) vs 7.6%/yr (nonsurgical), P=0.042, HR 0.355 (95% CI 0.125–1.009)**; all adverse events 3.2%/yr vs 8.2%/yr (P=0.048, HR 0.391, 95% CI 0.148–1.029). First RCT evidence that direct bypass reduces rebleeding (statistically marginal). Substudy: posterior/choroidal hemorrhage = high rebleed risk; bypass benefit greatest there.
- **Pediatric pial synangiosis** — Scott & Smith (Boston Children's): durable stroke prevention; the **Scott & Smith NEJM review (N Engl J Med 2009;360:1226–37)** is the classic reference.
- **Direct vs indirect**: no RCT in ischemic MMD; meta-analyses (e.g., Jeon, World Neurosurg 2016; Stroke 2023 systematic review) show direct/combined yields **superior angiographic revascularization** and better late stroke/hemorrhage outcomes, with comparable perioperative complications; combined favored over indirect for favorable outcomes (low-quality evidence per GRADE).
- **Guidelines**: AHA/ASA Scientific Statement on adult MMD/MMS (Gonzalez et al., _Stroke_ 2023;54:e465–e479); Japanese 2021 management guidelines (Research Committee + Japan Stroke Society); ESO 2023 guidelines.
- **Bypass context (atherosclerotic, NOT moyamoya)**: **COSS** (Carotid Occlusion Surgery Study, _JAMA_ 2011, Powers) — STA-MCA bypass + medical vs medical alone for symptomatic atherosclerotic ICA occlusion with ↑OEF; **negative** (no benefit; ~15% 30-day surgical stroke/death). **JET** (Japanese EC-IC Bypass Trial, 196 patients) — selected hemodynamic-failure patients (↓CBF + ↓CVR), suggested benefit and 0% 30-day morbidity; final English peer-reviewed results not published. These do NOT apply to MMD but provide EC-IC bypass evidence context — bypass benefit depends on strict hemodynamic selection.
- **Emerging**: RNF213-related vasculopathy spectrum (includes intracranial atherosclerosis); RNF213 as biomarker/risk-stratifier; cilostazol; refinement of hemorrhage-risk markers (choroidal anastomosis); novel/hybrid indirect techniques (e.g., pial pericranial dural revascularization).

## Recommendations

1. **Suspect MMD** in any child with hyperventilation-triggered TIA/stroke or any young adult with ICH (esp. IVH) or recurrent ischemia, particularly of East Asian ancestry. Get MRI/MRA first; **confirm with DSA** (gold standard — also stages disease and plans surgery).
2. **Assess cerebrovascular reserve** (SPECT/PET/perfusion ± acetazolamide) — impaired CVR/misery perfusion is a key surgical trigger.
3. **Offer revascularization to symptomatic patients** (ischemic with impaired reserve, or hemorrhagic):
    - **Children → indirect** (pial synangiosis/EDAS).
    - **Adults → direct STA-MCA or combined**; for **hemorrhagic adult MMD, direct bypass** (JAM evidence).
4. **Perioperative discipline**: normocapnia (EtCO2 35–45), normotension, euvolemia; avoid hyperventilation/hypotension/dehydration; monitor for CHS (worsening deficit/headache/seizure days after direct bypass → strict BP control).
5. **Medical**: consider aspirin for ischemic type (weak evidence, watch hemorrhage); avoid stenting; treat associated conditions in syndrome.
6. **Thresholds that change management**: progression to bilateral disease or contralateral CVR impairment → operate the contralateral side; new ("de novo") ivy sign post-op may indicate hyperperfusion; recurrent hemorrhage/choroidal anastomosis → favor direct bypass.

## High-Yield Exam Pearls

- **"Moyamoya" = "puff of smoke"** (basal collaterals); Suzuki & Takaku 1969.
- **MMD = idiopathic; moyamoya syndrome/quasi-MMD = + associated condition** (NF1, sickle cell, Down syndrome, radiation, autoimmune). **Unilateral disease now qualifies as MMD** (definite/probable distinction abolished 2015).
- **RNF213 p.R4810K** = East Asian founder susceptibility variant (chr 17q25.3); ~95% familial / ~80% sporadic Japanese MMD vs ~2% controls; absent in Caucasians. Familial ~10–15%.
- **Histology**: intimal fibrocellular hyperplasia + smooth-muscle proliferation, wavy internal elastic lamina, medial thinning; **NO inflammation/atherosclerosis/lipid**.
- **Kids = ischemic, hyperventilation-precipitated** (crying/blowing/wind instruments → hypocapnia → vasoconstriction); chorea/limb-shaking TIA. **Adults = often hemorrhagic** (≈half of adult-onset; IVH from choroidal/periventricular collaterals).
- **Suzuki I–VI** (I narrowing → III peak moyamoya → VI burnt-out, ECA-supplied).
- **Ivy sign** (FLAIR leptomeningeal = slow collateral flow); **brush sign** (SWI deep medullary veins); **flow voids** in basal ganglia.
- **DSA = gold standard**; acetazolamide challenge for cerebrovascular reserve; ↑OEF on PET = misery perfusion.
- **JAM Trial (Stroke 2014)** = only RCT: direct bypass reduces rebleeding in hemorrhagic adult MMD (2.7 vs 7.6%/yr, HR 0.355) — marginal significance.
- **Children → indirect (pial synangiosis); adults → direct/combined.** Direct gives better angiographic revascularization.
- **Cerebral hyperperfusion syndrome** = key complication after direct bypass (headache/deficit/seizure, ~days post-op) → tight BP control.
- **Perioperative: normocapnia, normotension, euvolemia; avoid hyperventilation/hypotension/dehydration/mannitol.**
- **COSS = negative; JET = positive in selected hemodynamic-failure patients** — both for ATHEROSCLEROTIC occlusion, NOT moyamoya.

## Caveats

- JAM Trial benefit was **statistically marginal** (HR CIs cross 1.0) and applies to hemorrhagic adult MMD treated with **direct** bypass only.
- No RCT exists for ischemic MMD or for direct-vs-indirect comparison; those recommendations rest on observational/meta-analytic (low-quality) evidence.
- Epidemiology figures are heavily East Asian; Western incidence is far lower and the phenotype somewhat different (less hemorrhage).
- Suzuki staging is angiographic with interobserver variability; correlates with collaterals in children better than adults.
- COSS/JET are atherosclerotic-occlusion trials — do NOT extrapolate directly to MMD.
- Antiplatelet use in MMD remains controversial given coexisting hemorrhage risk; cilostazol data are preliminary.

**Moyamoya disease (MMD)** is a progressive cerebrovascular disorder marked by bilateral stenosis or occlusion of the distal internal carotid arteries (ICAs), with development of a fragile, compensatory collateral network at the base of the brain. This network has a characteristic angiographic appearance likened to a "puff of smoke" ("moyamoya" in Japanese).
Moyamoya disease if no etiology. Moyamoya syndrome if secondary to other condition, e.g., downs syndrome.

### **Diagnostic criteria**

- stenosis of terminal portion of ICA, prox A1 and M1
- abnormal vascular networks (collaterals)
- bilateral involvement

### **Epidemiology**

- Higher incidence in Japan (0.35/100,000/year) with a **bimodal age distribution**: pediatric (<10 years, mean ~3) and adult (3rd–4th decade).
- Female predominance (F:M ~1.8:1).
- Genetics: _RNF213_ risk gene
- A **familial form** exists, often with autosomal dominant inheritance and low penetrance.
- higher incidence among asian countries (0.9-2.3 per 1e5) compared to non-asian (0.005-0.009 per 1e5)

### **Pathophysiology**

- Primary pathology: progressive narrowing of the ==ICA termini== → compensatory collateralization via lenticulostriate and transdural arteries (e.g., STA, middle meningeal).
- Histology:
	- intimal thickening without inflammation.
	- fibromuscular thickening of intima and attenuation of media
- In later stages, the basal moyamoya vessels may regress as extracranial-intracranial collaterals increase.

### **Angiographic Staging (Suzuki's Stages I–VI)**

- Starts with supraclinoid ICA stenosis and progresses to complete disappearance of the moyamoya network, replaced by transdural collaterals.

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stage** | **Descriptor** | **Radiographic Features** |
| I   | "Narrowing of the carotid fork" | Narrowed ICA bifurcation |
| II  | "Initiation of the moyamoya" | Dilated ACA and MCA; narrowed ICA bifurcation with early moyamoya changes |
| III | "Intensification of the moyamoya" | Increased moyamoya changes at ICA bifurcation; narrowing of ACA and MCA |
| IV  | "Minimization of the moyamoya" | Decreasing moyamoya vessels; ICA occlusive changes; tenuous ACA and MCA |
| V   | "Reduction of the moyamoya" | Further regression of moyamoya vessels; occlusion of ICA, ACA, and MCA |
| VI  | "Disappearance of the moyamoya" | ICA essentially absent; cerebral perfusion via external carotid artery (ECA) collaterals |

### **Clinical Presentation**

- **Children**: typically present with ischemic symptoms—TIAs, strokes, or alternating hemiparesis. Events often triggered by crying or hyperventilation.
- **Adults**: ischemia is still common, but hemorrhagic presentation (e.g., intraventricular or basal ganglia hemorrhage) becomes more likely in advanced stages.

### **Diagnosis**

- Requires **bilateral stenosis/occlusion** of distal ICAs and presence of abnormal basal collaterals.
- Unilateral findings are considered questionable and may evolve into bilateral disease.
- Diagnostic modalities:
	- cerebral angiography (gold standard)
	- CTA
	- MRI/MRA
	- perfusion studies.
- hemodynamic studies
	- CVR (cerebrovascular reactivity)
		- MRI with blood O2 levels
		- Controlled CO2 to modulate vessel calibre
	- SPECT following acetazolamide challenge
		- dilates vessels

### **Management**

- **Medical**:
	- Acute stroke phase
		- tPA contraindicated
		- ASA, heparin and ozagrel (level evidence III)
		- supportive symptomatic treatment
		- hemorrhagic MMD (anti-HTN, vitmain K, plasma)
			- maintain BP +/- 15% of baseline
	- Preventing recurrence or treating asymptomatic MDD
		- ASA or plavix (level III evidence
- **Surgical**:
	- Indications: hemorrhagic or ischemic MMD (misery perfusion on PET)
	- Revascularization (direct and/or indirect) is the mainstay. Shown to reduce stroke/TIA rates. Options include:
		- _Direct_:
			- Immediate flow augmentation
			- Approaches:
				- STA–MCA (M4 or M3)
				- OA-MCA
				- OA-PCA
				- STA-ACA-MCA bypass.
		- _Indirect_:
			- delayed flow augmentation
			- Approaches:
				- encephalo-duro-arterio-synangiosis (EDAS)
				- encephalo-myo-synangiosis (EMS)
				- omental-cerebral transposition
				- multiple burrholes
				- dural inversion techniques.
		- _Combination_

### **Surgical complications**

- postoperative stroke: 7% (adults), 2% (pediatric)
- hyperperfusion syndrome: 3-5%
- wound healing problems: 1.7-5.1% (direct bypass), 4.4-14% (combined)

### **Prognosis**

- Poor in untreated patients—up to **73% risk of major deficit or death in children** within 2 years.
- Females and younger patients may have higher progression risk.
- Risk of rebleeding within 20 years: 30-65%

### **Asymptomatic MMD**

- unknown prognosis
- disease progresses with age
- risk of 3.2% per year
- pediatric cases with PCA stenoss - progression 26-43%
