## Endovascular Interventions for Intracranial Aneurysms

#neurosurgery #neurointervention #vascular #aneurysm #board-review #RCPSC

> [!abstract] Scope Board-level (RCPSC neurosurgery) condensed reference covering endovascular techniques, landmark trials with levels of evidence, open-surgical alternatives, and decision-making for intracranial aneurysms. Levels of evidence follow conventional grading (Level I = RCT; II = prospective cohort/lower-quality RCT; III = registry/retrospective).

---

### HIGH-YIELD SUMMARY

> [!tip] One-line trial facts
> 
> - **[[ISAT]]** (Molyneux, _Lancet_ 2002/2005/2009; **Level I RCT**): 2143 ruptured aneurysms → **190/801 (23.7%) dead or dependent at 1 yr with coiling vs 243/793 (30.6%) with clipping (p=0.0019); RRR 22.6% (95% CI 8.9–34.2), ARR 6.9% (2.5–11.3)**. Coiling rebleed slightly higher (2.6% vs ~1%); durability/retreatment worse; survival benefit persists long-term (lower death at 5 yr).
> - **[[BRAT]]** (Spetzler, _J Neurosurg_ 2015; **Level I RCT**, 471 pts): anterior circulation = no significant difference at 6 yr; **posterior circulation outcomes continued to favor coiling**. **64/178 (36%) overall crossover coil→clip; 42% (70/168) for anterior circulation.** Clipping better obliteration / less retreatment; no coil rebleeds at 6 yr.
> - **[[ISUIA]]** (**Level II prospective cohort**): 5-yr rupture risk rises with size + posterior location; anterior <7 mm ≈ 0%/yr (no prior SAH).
> - **[[PUFS]]** (flow diverter, **Level II single-arm**): complete occlusion 73.6% (180 d) → 86.8% (1 yr) → 95.2% (5 yr) for large/giant ICA aneurysms; mRS ≤2 in 96.3% at 5 yr.
> - **[[WEB-IT]]** (intrasaccular, **Level II**): adequate occlusion 84.6% at 1 yr; only one (0.7%) safety event — exceptionally safe.
> - **[[CARAT]]**: rebleed risk tracks occlusion completeness — **1.1% (complete) → 2.9% (91–99%) → 5.9% (70–90%) → 17.6% (<70%)**.
> - **[[HELPS]]/[[Cerecyte]]/[[MAPS]]**: coated/bioactive coils ≈ bare platinum (no robust durable benefit).

> [!warning] Must-know complications
> 
> - **Coiling**: thromboembolism (most common — 7.1% per procedure in ATENA), intraprocedural rupture (~2.6%), coil herniation/migration, recanalization (~20%, up to 33%), retreatment ~10%.
> - **Stents/flow diverters**: **mandatory DUAL antiplatelet therapy (ASA + clopidogrel)**; in-stent stenosis; thromboembolism; **delayed aneurysm rupture & delayed ipsilateral parenchymal hemorrhage** (flow diverters, esp. giant/fusiform).
> - **Clipping**: intraoperative rupture, perforator/branch occlusion, retraction injury.

> [!note] Classic clip-vs-coil decision points
> 
> - **Favor COIL**: posterior circulation (esp. basilar tip), elderly, poor-grade/medical comorbidity, narrow neck, deep/eloquent location.
> - **Favor CLIP**: MCA bifurcation (wide neck, branch incorporation), space-occupying hematoma needing evacuation, very young patients (durability), wide-neck/complex morphology, recurrence after coiling.

> [!info] Antiplatelet quick reference
> 
> - Elective stent/flow diverter: **ASA 81–325 mg + clopidogrel 75 mg, started 5–10 days pre-op**; VerifyNow **P2Y12 target ~60/70–200 PRU**; continue DAPT ≥3–6 months, ASA often lifelong.
> - **Clopidogrel hyporesponse (PRU ≥208–220)** → switch to **ticagrelor or prasugrel** (significantly lower thromboembolism, e.g., 2.7% vs 24.4% in non-responders switched to ticagrelor).
> - Ruptured + need for stent/FD: relative problem (DAPT + EVD bleeding risk) — favor surface-modified devices/SAPT or a non-stent strategy.

### 1. Aneurysmal SAH Foundations (treatment-relevant)

#### Clinical grading

|Scale|Basis|Grades|
|---|---|---|
|**Hunt–Hess**|Clinical (operative risk)|I asymptomatic/mild HA + slight nuchal rigidity; II mod–severe HA + nuchal rigidity ± CN palsy; III drowsy/confused, mild focal deficit; IV stupor, mod–severe hemiparesis; V coma/decerebrate/moribund (+1 grade for serious systemic disease)|
|**WFNS**|GCS + motor deficit|I GCS 15 no deficit; II GCS 13–14 no deficit; III GCS 13–14 + deficit; IV GCS 7–12 ± deficit; V GCS 3–6 ± deficit|
|**Modified Fisher**|CT cisternal blood + IVH (vasospasm risk)|0 none; 1 thin/focal SAH no IVH (~6%); 2 thin SAH + IVH (~15%); 3 thick SAH no IVH (~35%); 4 thick SAH + IVH (~34%)|

> [!important] Timing & system of care (AHA/ASA 2023, _Stroke_)
> 
> - Treat ruptured aneurysm (clip or coil) **as early as feasible, preferably within 24 h** to reduce rebleeding. **Goal = complete obliteration.**
> - Manage at **high-volume comprehensive centers** with dedicated neurocritical care and multidisciplinary teams → lower mortality (Class 1).
> - WFNS I–III (or Hunt-Hess I–III) = "good grade"; IV–V = "poor grade" but still treated, as securing the aneurysm and aggressive care improve outcomes.
> - Modified Fisher (Frontera et al., _Neurosurgery_ 2006; derived from 1355-pt placebo arm, 33% developed symptomatic vasospasm) predicts symptomatic vasospasm better than original Fisher.

#### Hydrocephalus / EVD

- Acute hydrocephalus common (~20%+ acute obstructive); shunt-dependent chronic ~8.9–48%. Risk factors: older age, IVH, antifibrinolytics, seizures.
- **AHA/ASA 2023: urgent CSF diversion (EVD ± lumbar drain) — Class 1, LOE B-NR.** LUMAS/EARLYDRAIN support lumbar drainage adjunct in absence of obstructive hydrocephalus.

#### Vasospasm / Delayed Cerebral Ischemia (DCI)

- Window **post-bleed days 4–14** (peak 7–10). Monitor with TCD, CTA/CT-perfusion, cEEG (AHA 2023 "reasonable").
- **Oral/enteral nimodipine 60 mg q4h × 21 days — Class 1, LOE A.** Landmark Allen et al. (_NEJM_ 1983, 125-pt RCT): persistent severe/fatal spasm-related deficit in **8/60 placebo vs 1/56 nimodipine (P=0.03)**. Improves outcome but **does not reduce angiographic vasospasm** (neuroprotective). IV calcium antagonists not recommended routinely.
- Maintain **euvolemia + induced hypertension** for symptomatic DCI; **hypervolemia NOT recommended**; statins/Mg not routine.
- Refractory spasm: **endovascular rescue — balloon angioplasty (proximal vessels only) ± intra-arterial vasodilators (verapamil, nicardipine, milrinone)**. AHA/ASA 2023 = **Class 2b** (downgraded from 2012's IIa). Caution: **IMCVS RCT was interrupted for higher mortality** in the interventional arm; angiographic response not reliably linked to clinical outcome.

### 2. Endovascular Coiling (GDC / detachable coils)

> [!note] What it is Guglielmi detachable coils (GDC, 1991): soft platinum coils delivered via microcatheter into the sac and detached electrolytically/mechanically. Induce intra-aneurysmal thrombosis and exclude the sac from circulation. Bare-platinum is standard; "bioactive"/coated coils (HydroCoil, Matrix, Cerecyte) intended to improve healing/packing density.

- **Indications**: ruptured & unruptured saccular aneurysms; ideal = narrow neck (<4 mm), dome-to-neck ratio ≥2; posterior circulation (basilar tip, PICA), PComm, AComm. First-line for most ruptured aneurysms (per ISAT).
- **Contraindications/limits**: wide neck (coil herniation), very small aneurysms (<2–3 mm, rupture risk), fusiform/blister morphology, branch incorporation, giant aneurysms (poor durability).
- **Complications**: thromboembolism (most common, 7.1% per procedure — ATENA), intraprocedural rupture (~2.6%), coil herniation/migration, recanalization/recurrence (~20%, up to 33%), coil compaction. No routine DAPT for simple coiling (intra-procedure heparin only).

#### Coil trials

|Trial|Comparison|Result|LoE|
|---|---|---|---|
|**HELPS**|HydroCoil vs bare platinum|Major angiographic recurrence 24% vs 34% (P=0.049); retreatment 3% both|I RCT|
|**Cerecyte**|Cerecyte vs bare platinum|No significant difference (success ~59% vs 54%)|I RCT|
|**MAPS**|Matrix2 vs bare platinum|Target aneurysm recurrence 13.3% vs 14.6% (NS)|I RCT|
|**CARAT**|Occlusion vs rerupture|Rerupture: 1.1% (complete) → 2.9% → 5.9% → 17.6% (<70%); median time to rerupture 3 days, 58% fatal|II cohort|

> [!tip] Pearl: Coated coils confer no consistent benefit over bare platinum → bare platinum remains standard (cost-effective). Per CARAT, **degree of occlusion is the strongest modifiable predictor of rebleeding** — justifies pursuing complete obliteration.

### 3. Balloon-Assisted Coiling (Remodeling Technique)

> [!note] What it is Temporary non-detachable compliant balloon inflated across the neck during coil deployment (Moret remodeling technique) to prevent coil prolapse into the parent vessel; deflated after the coil mass is stable. No permanent implant → **no mandatory DAPT** (advantage in ruptured aneurysms).

- **Indications**: wide-neck aneurysms, bifurcation aneurysms, ruptured wide-neck (avoids DAPT); intraprocedural rupture control (inflate balloon to tamponade).
- **Limitations**: transient parent-artery occlusion (ischemia risk); historically debated higher thromboembolic/rupture risk.
- **Evidence**: ATENA/CLARITY found balloon remodeling **as safe as standard coiling**; used in **37.3% of ATENA procedures**.

### 4. Stent-Assisted Coiling (SAC)

> [!note] What it is Self-expanding intracranial stent across the neck acts as a scaffold to retain coils in wide-neck aneurysms; also provides mild flow diversion and a surface for neck endothelialization. Permanent implant → **DAPT mandatory**.

- **Indications**: wide-neck (dome:neck <2, neck >4 mm) **unruptured** aneurysms; recurrent aneurysms; sidewall aneurysms. Generally avoided in acutely ruptured cases (DAPT bleeding risk) unless necessary.
- **Complications**: higher procedure-related complication rate than non-stented coiling (AJNR series: **9.4% vs 5.6%, RR 1.5, 95% CI 1.1–2.7**); thromboembolism, in-stent stenosis, perforation.
- **Antiplatelet**: ASA + clopidogrel pre-load with P2Y12 testing.

#### Stent types

|Stent|Design|Cell|Notes|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Neuroform / Atlas**|Laser-cut nitinol|Open-cell|Good wall apposition; coil-prolapse risk in tortuous curves; not resheathable|
|**Enterprise / Enterprise 2**|Laser-cut nitinol|Closed-cell|Resheathable/recapturable; lowest metal coverage (~5%); can ovalize/kink in tight curves|
|**LVIS / LVIS Jr**|Braided nitinol|Closed-cell|High metal coverage (~23%), flow-diverting effect; shortens markedly on deployment; lower recanalization in some series|

> [!tip] Open-cell (Neuroform) = better conformability but cell-opening/coil-prolapse in tortuous vessels. Closed-cell/braided (Enterprise, LVIS) = recapturable, higher coverage. **Braided stents shorten substantially on deployment** — anticipate landing-zone change.

### 5. Flow Diverters (PED, Surpass, FRED, p64, Silk)

> [!note] What it is High-metal-coverage braided stent placed in the **parent artery across the neck** (not in the sac). Redirects flow → intra-aneurysmal stasis/thrombosis → scaffold for neointimal/endothelial reconstruction. **Mandatory DAPT.**

- **Devices**: Pipeline Embolization Device (PED, Medtronic), Surpass (Stryker), FRED (MicroVention), p64 (phenox), Silk (Balt).
- **Indications**: large/giant wide-neck **ICA aneurysms** (cavernous → paraophthalmic/superior hypophyseal), fusiform/dissecting, blister aneurysms, recurrent/uncoilable; increasingly small (<12 mm) ICA aneurysms (PREMIER).
- **Contraindications/limits**: acutely ruptured (DAPT + delayed occlusion), bifurcation aneurysms with branches arising from dome/neck, distal small-vessel locations (higher stroke risk), inability to tolerate DAPT.
- **Complications**: thromboembolic/ischemic stroke (IntrePED morbidity 7.4%; **ischemic stroke after PED is uncommon but ~100% major morbidity/mortality**), **delayed aneurysm rupture** (esp. giant — thrombus autolysis/intra-aneurysmal pressure mechanisms; ~76% within 1 month, often fatal), **delayed ipsilateral parenchymal hemorrhage** (~1%, days–weeks; postulated flow-reversal/Windkessel mechanism), perforator infarction, in-stent stenosis. **Fusiform aneurysms = highest stroke risk** (only independent risk factor in IntrePED).
- **Antiplatelet**: ASA + clopidogrel 5–10 days pre-op + P2Y12 testing (target ~80–200 PRU per single-center data); DAPT ≥6 months then ASA.

#### Flow diverter trials

|Trial|Population|Result|LoE|
|---|---|---|---|
|**PUFS**|Large/giant ICA (n=107)|Complete occ 73.6% (180 d) → 86.8% (1 y) → 93.4% (3 y) → 95.2% (5 y); mRS ≤2 96.3% at 5 y; no recanalization|II single-arm|
|**PREMIER**|Small/medium wide-neck ≤12 mm|Complete occ 76.7% (1 y) → 83.3% (3 y); primary safety event 2.2%|II single-arm|
|**IntrePED**|Real-world registry (n=793)|Neuro morbidity 7.4%, mortality 3.8%, combined 8.4%; small ICA aneurysms safest (4.1%)|III registry|
|**ASPIRe**|Prospective registry|Supportive safety/occlusion data|III registry|

#### Recent developments (2022–2026)

- **Surface-modified flow diverters** — phosphorylcholine (Pipeline Shield/sPED), hydrophilic polymer (p48/p64 HPC), poly-MEA (FRED X), Derivo 2Heal — reduce thrombogenicity → enable **single antiplatelet therapy (SAPT)**, particularly attractive in ruptured/SAH where DAPT bleeding risk is high.
- **Theme: "the drug matters."** In pooled SAPT meta-analyses (Gawlitza 2022/23; Senol _Interv Neuroradiol_ 2023; Ma 2023), thromboembolic complications occurred **predominantly under aspirin monotherapy** (e.g., 20.8% ischemic with ASA vs lower with P2Y12 inhibitors); ticagrelor/prasugrel monotherapy performs better. Surface-modified FD pooled complete occlusion ~72% at ≥6 months.
- Evidence base is **retrospective, heterogeneous, small-n, high risk of bias**. **COATING (p64 MW HPC)** is the first RCT of a coated flow diverter — verify final results.

### 6. Flow Disruptors / Intrasaccular Devices (WEB)

> [!note] What it is Woven EndoBridge (WEB): self-expanding braided nitinol mesh deployed **inside the sac**, disrupting neck inflow → intra-aneurysmal thrombosis. **No DAPT required** (major advantage for ruptured wide-neck bifurcation aneurysms).

- **Indications**: **wide-neck bifurcation aneurysms (WNBA)** — MCA bifurcation, basilar tip, AComm, ICA terminus; ruptured & unruptured.
- **Limitations**: device sizing is critical (oversizing improves occlusion); not for very small or sidewall aneurysms; aneurysm/neck remnant relatively common.
- **Complications**: low; thromboembolic events; some recanalization/retreatment.

#### WEB trials

|Trial|Result|LoE|
|---|---|---|
|**WEB-IT** (US pivotal)|Adequate occlusion **84.6% (121/143)** at 1 yr; complete 53.8%; **only one (0.7%) safety event** (delayed ipsilateral parenchymal hemorrhage POD 22); FDA PMA approval Dec 31, 2018; 5-yr adequate occlusion ~87%|II single-arm|
|**WEBCAST/WEBCAST-2** (5-yr)|Adequate occlusion 77.9%; retreatment 11.6%|II prospective|
|**WEBCAST/WEBCAST-2** (3-yr)|Adequate occlusion 83.6%; retreatment 11.4%|II prospective|
|**CLARYS**|WEB in ruptured WNBA — effective protection against rebleeding, good safety|II prospective|

- **Other intrasaccular devices**: Contour (CERUS study; CE Mark 2020), SEAL (PRE-SEAL IT first-in-human data — preliminary, n=33, complete occlusion 76.9%), Medina, Nautilus, Trenza (2024). **Only WEB has FDA approval.**

### 7. Intracranial Stents (laser-cut vs braided)

See §4 table. Key board distinctions:

- **Laser-cut**: Neuroform (open-cell), Enterprise (closed-cell), Solitaire. Higher vascular compliance, fewer collateral-vessel thromboembolic events; lengths stable on deployment.
- **Braided**: LVIS/LVIS Jr, LEO Baby. Higher pore density + metal coverage (flow-diversion effect); shorten substantially on deployment; lower recanalization in some series. Highest metal coverage = LEO (~14%); lowest = Enterprise (~5%).
- All require **DAPT**.

### 8. Liquid Embolic Agents (Onyx, nBCA glue)

> [!note] Relevance to aneurysms Liquid embolics (Onyx — ethylene-vinyl-alcohol copolymer in DMSO; nBCA — n-butyl cyanoacrylate glue) are primarily AVM/dural-fistula agents. In aneurysms: **Onyx HD-500** was historically used for select wide-neck/giant sidewall aneurysms with balloon protection (largely supplanted by flow diverters/WEB). Glue/Onyx are also used for **parent-vessel sacrifice** and for **distal/mycotic aneurysm** embolization where the parent branch is expendable.

- **Limitations**: technical complexity, DMSO-related toxicity/vasospasm, nontarget embolization, recanalization. Niche role today.

### 9. Parent Vessel Occlusion / Sacrifice & Balloon Test Occlusion

> [!note] What it is Deliberate occlusion of the parent artery (endovascular coils/plugs or surgical ligation/trapping) for giant, fusiform, dissecting, blister, or mycotic aneurysms not amenable to reconstruction. Requires assessment of collateral tolerance first.

#### Balloon Test Occlusion (BTO)

- Temporary balloon inflation in ICA/VA with **clinical exam + adjuncts** (venous-phase delay, stump-pressure ratio, hypotensive challenge, SPECT/Xe-CT/CT-perfusion) to predict tolerance of permanent occlusion.
- **Abrupt ICA occlusion without BTO ≈ 26% ischemia risk; with BTO ≈ 13%** (Linskey).
- Up to ~20% may not tolerate sacrifice despite symmetric BTO scans; quantitative CBF (Xe-CT) lowers false-negatives to 3–10%.
- **BTO fail → EC-IC bypass (STA-MCA or high-flow graft) before sacrifice.**

### 10. Open Surgical Alternatives

|Technique|Use|
|---|---|
|**Microsurgical clipping (pterional/other)**|Definitive durable obliteration; MCA bifurcation, wide-neck, branch incorporation, hematoma evacuation; temporary clipping for proximal control; intraoperative rupture managed by suction + temporary clip|
|**Bypass (STA-MCA low-flow; high-flow EC-IC with radial/saphenous graft)**|Giant/fusiform/dissecting aneurysms requiring parent-vessel sacrifice; BTO-fail cases|
|**Wrapping / clip-wrapping (Gore-Tex, cotton/cottonoid, Sundt clip-graft)**|Blister and unclippable/dolichoectatic aneurysms|
|**Trapping ± proximal occlusion**|Fusiform, dissecting, giant aneurysms; combined with bypass|

#### Clip vs Coil decision framework

- **Coil**: posterior circulation, elderly, poor grade/comorbidity, narrow neck, deep/eloquent location (ISAT; BRAT posterior-circulation advantage).
- **Clip**: MCA aneurysms (better occlusion/durability; clipping superior obliteration in multiple series), space-occupying hematoma requiring evacuation (**clip + evacuate in one sitting → better functional outcome than coil-then-evacuate**, per French multicenter cohort), very young patients (durability), wide-neck/branch incorporation, post-coiling recurrence.
- Coiling shows **fewer intraoperative ruptures, less ischemic infarction, fewer seizures** but **lower complete-occlusion and higher incomplete-occlusion rates** than clipping in anterior-circulation meta-analyses.
- **Multidisciplinary neurovascular team** decision essential (AHA/ASA 2023 Class 1 — treat at experienced, high-volume centers).

### 11. Challenging Aneurysm Types

|Type|Preferred strategy|
|---|---|
|**Blister (dorsal/anterior supraclinoid ICA)**|Pseudoaneurysm (absent media/IEL, adventitia only); fragile, high rebleed/recurrence; **intraop rupture up to 57%**; clip-wrap (mortality/disability historically up to 55%). Modern: **flow diverter ± coiling** (good outcomes, watch ischemia/vasospasm — ~50% severe vasospasm in some series); Sundt clip-graft; trapping + bypass|
|**Dissecting (esp. vertebral/PICA)**|Reconstruction (stent/FD) if branch-preserving; deconstruction (PVO ± bypass) if sacrificeable|
|**Mycotic/infectious**|Often distal/peripheral; antibiotics first; endovascular glue/coil occlusion of the parent branch (branch BTO) or surgical resection|
|**Giant (>25 mm)**|Flow diverter (high long-term occlusion) or PVO ± bypass; clipping ± bypass. **High delayed-rupture risk with FD**|
|**Fusiform**|FD/stent reconstruction or trapping + bypass; **highest FD stroke risk**|
|**Pediatric**|Higher proportion giant/fusiform/dissecting/posterior; tailored; reconstruction favored to preserve growth|

### 12. Imaging & Follow-up

- **DSA = gold standard** for diagnosis and follow-up occlusion assessment.
- **CTA**: first-line in acute SAH; **MRA (TOF)**: noninvasive follow-up of coiled aneurysms.
- **Raymond-Roy Occlusion Classification (RROC)**: **Class I** complete occlusion; **Class II** residual neck; **Class III** residual aneurysm. **Modified RROC**: **IIIa** contrast within coil interstices (83% improve to I/II); **IIIb** contrast along aneurysm wall (worst prognosis — 85% remain incompletely occluded, highest recurrence/shortest recurrence-free survival). RROC class + aneurysm diameter (13–24 mm) are independent predictors of recurrence; most class change occurs in the first year.

---

### 13. Procedural Access & Anatomy

- **Transfemoral** (traditional) vs **transradial** (growing — fewer access-site complications, patient preference).
- Guide catheter in cervical ICA/VA → intermediate/distal access catheter → microcatheter + microwire into sac under biplane DSA roadmap.
- Intraprocedural heparinization with ACT monitoring; protamine reversal available for intraprocedural rupture.

> [!quote] Bottom line Endovascular therapy is **first-line for most ruptured aneurysms** (ISAT/BRAT), with a clear advantage in the **posterior circulation, elderly, and poor-grade patients**; **clipping retains the edge for MCA aneurysms, lesions with mass-effect hematoma, and durability in the young**. Across all modalities, **complete occlusion is the strongest predictor of freedom from rebleeding** (CARAT). Flow diverters revolutionized large/giant/fusiform ICA aneurysm care (PUFS), and the WEB device made wide-neck bifurcation aneurysms tractable without stents or DAPT (WEB-IT). Stents and flow diverters demand **meticulous dual-antiplatelet management with P2Y12 testing**, and flow diverters carry the unique risks of **delayed aneurysm rupture and delayed parenchymal hemorrhage**. Decisions belong to a **multidisciplinary neurovascular team** at a high-volume center.
