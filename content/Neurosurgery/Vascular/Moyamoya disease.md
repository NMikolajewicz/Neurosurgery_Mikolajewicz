**Moyamoya disease (MMD)** is a progressive cerebrovascular disorder marked by bilateral stenosis or occlusion of the distal internal carotid arteries (ICAs), with development of a fragile, compensatory collateral network at the base of the brain. This network has a characteristic angiographic appearance likened to a “puff of smoke” (“moyamoya” in Japanese).
Moyamoya disease if no etiology. Moyamoya syndrome if secondary to other condition, e.g., downs syndrome.

#### **Diagnostic criteria**

* stenosis of terminal portion of ICA, prox A1 and M1
* abnormal vascular networks (collaterals)
* bilateral involvement

#### **Epidemiology**

* Higher incidence in Japan (0.35/100,000/year) with a **bimodal age distribution**: pediatric (<10 years, mean ~3) and adult (3rd–4th decade).
* Female predominance (F:M ~1.8:1).
* Genetics: _RNF213_ risk gene
* A **familial form** exists, often with autosomal dominant inheritance and low penetrance.
* higher incidence among asian countries (0.9-2.3 per 1e5) compared to non-asian (0.005-0.009 per 1e5)

#### **Pathophysiology**

* Primary pathology: progressive narrowing of the ==ICA termini== → compensatory collateralization via lenticulostriate and transdural arteries (e.g., STA, middle meningeal).
* Histology:
	* intimal thickening without inflammation.
	* fibromuscular thickening of intima and attenuation of media
* In later stages, the basal moyamoya vessels may regress as extracranial-intracranial collaterals increase.

#### **Angiographic Staging (Suzuki’s Stages I–VI)**

* Starts with supraclinoid ICA stenosis and progresses to complete disappearance of the moyamoya network, replaced by transdural collaterals.

|     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stage** | **Descriptor** | **Radiographic Features** |
| I   | "Narrowing of the carotid fork" | Narrowed ICA bifurcation |
| II  | "Initiation of the moyamoya" | Dilated ACA and MCA; narrowed ICA bifurcation with early moyamoya changes |
| III | "Intensification of the moyamoya" | Increased moyamoya changes at ICA bifurcation; narrowing of ACA and MCA |
| IV  | "Minimization of the moyamoya" | Decreasing moyamoya vessels; ICA occlusive changes; tenuous ACA and MCA |
| V   | "Reduction of the moyamoya" | Further regression of moyamoya vessels; occlusion of ICA, ACA, and MCA |
| VI  | "Disappearance of the moyamoya" | ICA essentially absent; cerebral perfusion via external carotid artery (ECA) collaterals |

#### **Clinical Presentation**

* **Children**: typically present with ischemic symptoms—TIAs, strokes, or alternating hemiparesis. Events often triggered by crying or hyperventilation.
* **Adults**: ischemia is still common, but hemorrhagic presentation (e.g., intraventricular or basal ganglia hemorrhage) becomes more likely in advanced stages.

#### **Diagnosis**

* Requires **bilateral stenosis/occlusion** of distal ICAs and presence of abnormal basal collaterals.
* Unilateral findings are considered questionable and may evolve into bilateral disease.
* Diagnostic modalities:
	* cerebral angiography (gold standard)
	* CTA
	* MRI/MRA
	* perfusion studies.
* hemodynamic studies
	* CVR (cerebrovascular reactivity)
		* MRI with blood O2 levels
		* Controlled CO2 to modulate vessel calibre
	* SPECT following acetazolamide challenge
		* dilates vessels

#### **Management**

* **Medical**:
	* Acute stroke phase
		* tPA contraindicated
		* ASA, heparin and ozagrel (level evidence III)
		* supportive symptomatic treatment
		* hemorrhagic MMD (anti-HTN, vitmain K, plasma)
			* maintain BP +/- 15% of baseline
	* Preventing recurrence or treating asymptomatic MDD
		* ASA or plavix (level III evidence
* **Surgical**:
	* Indications: hemorrhagic or ischemic MMD (misery perfusion on PET)
	* Revascularization (direct and/or indirect) is the mainstay. Shown to reduce stroke/TIA rates. Options include:
		* _Direct_:
			* Immediate flow augmentation
			* Approaches:
				* STA–MCA (M4 or M3)
				* OA-MCA
				* OA-PCA
				* STA-ACA-MCA bypass.
		* _Indirect_:
			* delayed flow augmentation
			* Approaches:
				* encephalo-duro-arterio-synangiosis (EDAS)
				* encephalo-myo-synangiosis (EMS)
				* omental-cerebral transposition
				* multiple burrholes
				* dural inversion techniques.
		* _Combination_

#### **Surgical complications**

* postoperative stroke: 7% (adults), 2% (pediatric)
* hyperperfusion syndrome: 3-5%
* wound healing problems: 1.7-5.1% (direct bypass), 4.4-14% (combined)

#### **Prognosis**

* Poor in untreated patients—up to **73% risk of major deficit or death in children** within 2 years.
* Females and younger patients may have higher progression risk.
* Risk of rebleeding within 20 years: 30-65%

#### **Asymptomatic MMD**

* unknown prognosis
* disease progresses with age
* risk of 3.2% per year
* pediatric cases with PCA stenoss - progression 26-43%
