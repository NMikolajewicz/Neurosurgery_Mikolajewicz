🧠 RCVS – Reversible Cerebral Vasoconstriction Syndrome

  

  

Source: Erhart et al., RCVS Review, J Neurol. 2023

  

  

  

  

🔍 Definition

  

  

RCVS is a clinical–radiological syndrome marked by recurrent thunderclap headaches (TCH) and reversible multifocal cerebral vasoconstriction.

Can occur idiopathically or secondary to triggers (e.g. drugs, postpartum state).

  

  

  

  

🧩 Pathophysiology

  

  

Two primary mechanisms:

  

1. Sympathetic overactivity
2. Endothelial dysfunction  
    These lead to transient vasospasm in medium-to-large cerebral arteries.

  

  

  

  

  

📈 Epidemiology

  

  

- Mean age: 42–47 years
- Strong female predominance (up to 8:1 in adults)
- Often occurs in postpartum women or those using vasoactive substances
- Pediatric RCVS: up to 85% male

  

  

  

  

  

⚠️ Risk Factors & Triggers

  

  

  

Drugs:

  

  

- SSRIs/SNRIs, triptans, ergotamines, pseudoephedrine, cocaine, amphetamines, ecstasy, cannabis

  

  

  

Physiologic:

  

  

- Postpartum period, eclampsia, migraine, tumors (pheochromocytoma)

  

  

  

Activities:

  

  

- Valsalva, bathing, sexual activity, coughing, exercise

  

  

  

  

  

🧠 Clinical Presentation

  

  

- Thunderclap headache (TCH) – abrupt, maximal intensity in seconds
- Focal deficits: hemiparesis, aphasia, seizures, visual loss
- Complications:  
    

- SAH (esp. convexity), ICH, infarcts, PRES

-   
    

  

  

  

  

  

🧪 Diagnosis

  

  

  

Clinical + Imaging criteria (Calabrese 2007) 

  

:

  

  

1. Acute severe headache ± neuro deficits
2. Segmental vasoconstriction on CTA/MRA (“string-of-beads”)
3. No aneurysmal SAH
4. Normal or near-normal CSF
5. Reversibility of vasoconstriction within 3 months

  

  

🧠 CT/MRI → CTA/MRA → consider DSA for unclear cases

  

  

  

  

💊 Treatment

  

  

- Supportive + symptom control
- Nimodipine – oral; intra-arterial in fulminant cases
- Avoid corticosteroids (can worsen outcome)
- Seizure or stroke management as per standard protocols

  

  

  

  

  

📈 Prognosis

  

  

- Excellent with >90% functionally independent at discharge
- Vasoconstriction typically resolves within 3 months
- Recurrence is rare but possible

  

  

  

  

  

🚨 Royal College Quick Review (RCVS)

  

  

[!note] 🧠 High-Yield Pearls – Royal College Prep

  

- TCH + segmental vasospasm + reversibility = RCVS
- Rule out PACNS: CSF in RCVS is typically normal; biopsy in PACNS
- PRES and RCVS often coexist, especially postpartum
- Avoid corticosteroids (harmful in RCVS, helpful in PACNS)
- Key DDX: PACNS, PRES, cSAH, migraine, SAH (aneurysmal)
- Imaging: MRA “string of beads”, follow-up imaging for reversibility
- Complications include SAH (non-aneurysmal), infarct, hemorrhage

  

  

  

  

  

🧠 Additional Pearls for the Neurosurgeon

  

  

- Up to 43% present with neurological deficits
- Neurosurgical relevance: Convexity SAH often misattributed to aneurysms
- Important DDX in SAH without aneurysm on angiography
- Be cautious of iatrogenic triggers: e.g. serotonergic medications in hospitalized patients
- Consider RCVS in postpartum seizures not fitting eclampsia criteria
