* Cause of hypernatremia
* inverse of SIADH.
* inadequate amount (not an inappropriate excess) of ADH.
* Water is lost and the serum gets concentrated, causing a hypovolemic hypernatremia.
* The patient's urine output increases primarily due to nonreabsorption of water, causing the specific gravity and urine osmolality to drop, while the serum sodium and osmolality increases.
* ==Loss of dilute urine from DI can result in dangerous volume depletion.== 
### Workup
* trend  serum sodium/osmolality
* Trend urine-specific gravity, urine osmolality
* hourly urine output.
### Management
* For central DI, ==1:1 fluid replacement== is acceptable for transient cases.
* If the patient is awake and able to detect thirst, they are instructed to ==drink only when thirsty.==
* If they are unable to keep up with volume losses through oral intake, ==intravenous infusions== are employed.
* If the patient is unable to drink or fluids are unable to be matched due to very high outputs, ==DDAVP== may be used to decrease urine output.
	* DDAVP is a potent long-acting agent that binds to V2 receptors at collecting ducts of the nephron, recruiting aquaporin channels, and facilitating fluid reabsorption back into body.
