> - Seen after **closed head injury** or **hydrocephalus** with **paroxysmal/nonsustained hyperpyrexia** + **tachycardia, tachypnea, ↑BP, diaphoresis**, ± **↑extensor tone** and **pupillary dilatation**.

## Central Fever / Paroxysmal Hyperthermic Autonomic Dysregulation

### Clinical pattern

- Often with **closed head injury, hydrocephalus**.
- **Nonsustained**: **hyperpyrexia, tachycardia, tachypnea, ↑BP, ↑extensor tone, pupil dilatation, diaphoresis**.
### Pathophysiology

- **Hypothalamic injury**; possible neuroimmunologic drivers:
    - cytokines (**IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α, IFN-γ**) → **COX-2** → **PGE**
    - **heat shock proteins**
    - brainstem dysregulation → **glutamate/NO** release
### Rule out (must exclude)

- **Infection**, **epileptic disorders**, **pheochromocytoma**, **NMS**, **↑ICP**, **hydrocephalus**, **Cushing's syndrome**, **thyrotoxicosis**, **DVT**
### Treatment (reported/used agents)

- **Propranolol, opioid, clonidine, bromocriptine, chlorpromazine, dantrolene**
- **Propranolol 20–30 mg q6h**
- **Stereotactic surgery** sometimes considered if refractory
#### Bromocriptine

- Median **7.5 mg/day** (2.5–40), duration **13 days** (5–160)
- Median starting dose **10 mg/day** (5.0–32.5)
- Temp reduction: nadir by **72 h** ≈ **–0.7°C**
- No dosing guidance; monitor **48–72 h** before titration
