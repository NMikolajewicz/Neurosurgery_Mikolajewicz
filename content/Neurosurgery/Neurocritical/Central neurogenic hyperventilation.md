> [!summary] Central Neurogenic Hyperventilation 
> 
> - **Sustained rapid + deep breathing** with **no metabolic/pulmonary cause** → **central** origin.
>     
> - Localizes to **midbrain/upper pons** (parabrachial–Kölliker-Fuse / **periaqueductal gray** region).
>     
> - Mechanism: **loss of inhibitory input** → **disinhibited medullary drive** → **↓PaCO₂**.
>     
> - ABG: **hypocapnic respiratory alkalosis** (can be severe).
>     
> - ICU clue: persistent hyperventilation in **sedated/intubated** patient = **image brainstem**; can signal **herniation**.
>     
> - Tx: **treat underlying lesion + supportive ventilation**; avoid worsening hypocapnia (**target ~PaCO₂ 35**).
>

## Core concepts

- **CNH** = sustained **rapid + deep** breathing **without metabolic/pulmonary cause**, due to **brainstem respiratory center disruption**.
- Classically associated with **midbrain/upper pontine** lesions (near **parabrachial–Kölliker-Fuse complex** / **periaqueductal gray**).
- Mechanism: **loss of inhibitory cortical input** / **disinhibition** → **unchecked medullary inspiratory drive** → **hypocapnic respiratory alkalosis** (can be severe).
- ICU relevance: may occur in **comatose patients** with midbrain pathology and can be a **red flag for herniation/structural compromise**.
- Management is **supportive + treat underlying lesion**; no single therapy reliably "turns off" CNH.

## Pathophysiology / Pathology

- Lesion location: **midbrain or upper pons**, disrupting modulatory pathways controlling respiratory drive.
- Physiologic consequence: persistent **↓PaCO₂** → **respiratory alkalosis**.

## Etiologies

- **Brainstem tumors** (esp. **gliomas**)
- **Trauma**
- **Pontine hemorrhage**
- **Brainstem infarcts**
- **Large midline diencephalic lesions**
- Rare: **postictal states**, **encephalitis**

## Clinical presentation

- **Persistent hyperventilation** not explained by pain/anxiety/metabolic derangement.
- **Not responsive to volitional control** (helps distinguish from anxiety-driven hyperventilation).
- Often in **altered LOC/coma**; may accompany other signs of **brainstem dysfunction**.

> [!question] Common presentations
> 
> - "Intubated + sedated patient still hyperventilating" → think **CNH** → **image the brainstem**.
> 
> - Expected ABG: **respiratory alkalosis** with **low PaCO₂**.
>

## Workup / Imaging

- **ABG**: confirm **hypocapnia/alkalosis**; track severity/trends.
- Exclude non-neuro causes: pulmonary pathology, sepsis, metabolic acidosis, pain/anxiety, ventilator over-assist.
- **Urgent neuroimaging (CT/MRI brain/brainstem)** to localize **structural lesion** (tumor/hemorrhage/infarct/herniation).

## Management

### 1) Identify + treat underlying cause

- **Neuroimaging essential** to localize lesion and guide therapy.
- Etiology-directed treatment (examples from note):
    - **Tumor**: debulking/biopsy/radiation
    - **Hemorrhage**: supportive vs surgical (case-dependent)
    - **Edema/herniation**: **hyperosmolar therapy**, **decompressive craniectomy** (when indicated)

### 2) Ventilatory strategy (avoid iatrogenic harm)

- Mechanical ventilation may be required for **excess drive** and/or neurologic deterioration.
- Avoid pushing an already low PaCO₂ lower:
    - If **PaCO₂ < 30 mmHg**, **avoid aggressive minute ventilation** (worsens alkalosis → **↓cerebral perfusion**).
- Practical target: aim **low-normal PaCO₂ (~35 mmHg)** to reduce cerebral vasoconstriction risk.
    - Uncertain / institution-specific: exact PaCO₂ target may vary with ICP/CPP goals and neuromonitoring.

### 3) Pharmacologic support (limited/variable efficacy)

- **Sedation** (e.g., **propofol**, **midazolam**) may blunt drive (variable response).
- **Opioids** (e.g., **morphine**, **fentanyl**) reported in case reports to suppress drive (weak evidence).
- **Neuromuscular blockade**: generally **not typical** unless ventilation is severely dysregulated and other measures fail.

## Complications (monitor + correct)

- **Respiratory alkalosis** → frequent **ABGs**.
- Electrolyte shifts from alkalosis:
    - **Hypokalemia**
    - **Hypocalcemia**
- Severe alkalosis may **lower seizure threshold** → consider prophylaxis only if clinically indicated.

> [!warning] Pitfalls / complications
> 
> - Over-ventilating CNH (especially **PaCO₂ < 30**) can worsen **cerebral vasoconstriction** and perfusion.
> 
> - Treating as "anxiety hyperventilation" risks missing **brainstem lesion/herniation**.
>  

## Prognosis

- Often **poor**, largely determined by **underlying brainstem insult**.
- Potentially better if due to a **reversible compressive lesion** and corrected promptly.

## Distinction from therapeutic hyperventilation for ICP (context)

- CNH is a **pathologic brainstem pattern** with **diagnostic/prognostic significance**, distinct from deliberate **therapeutic hyperventilation** used transiently in **ICP crises**.

> [!note] Supplement (Greenberg)
> 
> - **Hyperventilation lowers ICP** via **↓PaCO₂ → ↓CBV → ↓ICP**, but also **↓CBF**.
> 
> - **Avoid prophylactic hyperventilation**; **PaCO₂ ≤ 25 mmHg** is **not recommended**.
> 
> - Typical "moderate" target when used: **PaCO₂ 30–35 mmHg**; **avoid** in the **first 24 h after TBI** when CBF may be low.
> 
> - Effect kinetics: dropping PaCO₂ **35 → 29 mmHg** lowers ICP **~25–30%**, onset **≤30 s**, peak **~8 min**, duration sometimes **15–20 min**; prolonged use may blunt effect and requires **slow wean** to avoid rebound ICP.
> 
> - If hyperventilation is used, consider monitoring brain oxygen delivery (e.g., **SjVO₂ / PbrO₂**).
