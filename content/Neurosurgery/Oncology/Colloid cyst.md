Colloid cysts originate from the abnormal folding of the primitive neuroepithelium (the [paraphysis elements](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/paraphysis-elements?lang=us)) 2,3. They contain mucin, old blood (hemosiderin), cholesterol, and various ions, accounting for a wide range of imaging appearance.
## Colloid cyst risk score

The **colloid cyst risk score** (CCRS) is a validated tool for the prediction of symptomatic status and risk of [hydrocephalus](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/hydrocephalus?lang=us "Hydrocephalus") in patients with [colloid cyst of the third ventricle](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/colloid-cyst-of-the-third-ventricle?lang=us "Colloid cyst of the third ventricle").

One point is assigned for each of the following five categories:
- age <65 years
- [headache](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/headache-overview?lang=us "Headache (overview)") present
- colloid cyst maximal axial diameter ≥7 mm
- cyst hyperintense on FLAIR imaging
- cyst lies is an anatomic risk zone (zone I or zone III)

Scores can range from a minimum of 0 to a maximum of 5 points. The higher the score, the greater the risk of [hydrocephalus](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/hydrocephalus?lang=us "Hydrocephalus") and symptomatic status 1,2.

Zone I: anterior to a line drawn tangentially between the anterior aspect of the [massa intermedia](https://radiopaedia.org/cases/colloid-cyst-risk-zones#annotation-71144 "massa intermedia") and the [mamillary bodies](https://radiopaedia.org/cases/colloid-cyst-risk-zones#annotation-71145 "mamillary bodies")
Zone II: between zone I and zone III
Zone III: posterior to a line drawn parallel to zone I at the [inlet of the cerebral aqueduct](https://radiopaedia.org/cases/colloid-cyst-risk-zones#annotation-71146 "inlet of the cerebral aqueduct")
