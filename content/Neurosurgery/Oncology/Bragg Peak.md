## Bragg Peak in Neuro-Radiotherapy

> [!summary] Key takeaways
> 
> - **Bragg peak** = sharp depth–dose maximum of charged particles (protons, heavier ions) with rapid distal fall-off.
> 
> - Exploited in **proton / heavy-ion therapy** to deliver high intracranial dose while sparing proximal and distal normal brain.
> 
> - In neuro-oncology, it is most relevant for **peds**, **skull base & spinal axis**, and **re-irradiation** strategies.
> 
> - Planning is dominated by **range uncertainty** and **variable RBE/LET at the distal edge** rather than by geometric penumbra alone.

---

### 1. Context in Greenberg / neurosurgical practice

Greenberg treats stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) and radiotherapy (SRT) as dose-escalation tools built on precise localization, deliverable by LINAC, Gamma Knife, and **less commonly protons and heavy charged particles**.

Within the SRS literature, **Bragg-peak proton and heavy charged particle therapy** appears in classic series for intracranial AVMs (Kjellberg, Steinberg).  
Greenberg also notes proton-beam radiation as a useful adjunct for **radioresistant chordoma**, highlighting its role for high-dose delivery near critical structures.

> [!note] Where Bragg peak actually matters for us
> 
> - When we want **dose-escalation into tumor** while respecting **tight OAR constraints** (brainstem, optic apparatus, cochlea, hippocampi).
> 
> - When we care about **integral dose**, especially in **children** and young adults.
> 
> - When we need **complex fields** (e.g. craniospinal axis) and are trying to avoid exit dose into heart/lungs/bowel.

### 2. Physics refresher – what is the Bragg peak?

#### 2.1 Basic definition

- **Charged particles** (protons, helium, carbon) lose energy as they traverse tissue mainly via **Coulomb interactions** with electrons.
- The **linear energy transfer (LET)** is relatively modest in the entrance region, then **rises steeply near the end of range** – this sharp maximum is the **Bragg peak**.
- Beyond that peak, the dose falls off over a few mm to **essentially zero** – there is **no significant exit dose**.

Contrast with photons (XRT/SRS):

- Photons show **exponential dose fall-off** with significant **exit dose** beyond the target.
- With protons, the depth–dose curve has:
    - **Entrance region** (plateau)
    - **Bragg peak** at planned depth
    - **Abrupt distal fall-off** (2–3 mm order of magnitude)

> [!info] Practical numbers (rule-of-thumb, not protocol)
> 
> - **Proton RBE** is typically modeled as **1.1** vs photons, but local RBE/LET may be higher at the distal edge.
> 
> - Range in water ≈ **1 cm per ~100 MeV** (very rough; actual is more granular and machine-specific).
> 
> - **Range uncertainty** is commonly handled as **a few mm + % of range** (e.g. ~3.5% + 1–2 mm), driving robust margins.

#### 2.2 Spread-Out Bragg Peak (SOBP)

A pristine Bragg peak is razor-thin and clinically useless by itself. To cover a target volume:

- Combine many Bragg peaks of **different energies** → **Spread-Out Bragg Peak (SOBP)**.
- Techniques:
    - Passive scattering (range modulators, ridge filters).
    - Pencil-beam scanning (PBS) / IMPT: energy-layer scanning and lateral steering.

Result: a **uniform dose "plateau"** across the tumor depth, with **sharp distal fall-off** beyond.

### 3. Modalities using the Bragg peak in neuro

#### 3.1 Proton radiosurgery / radiotherapy

Greenberg notes **heavy charged particle radiosurgery** (protons, helium) generated by a cyclotron as one of the SRS platforms.

Clinically, in neuro:

- **SRS-like proton therapy**:
    - Historically: small AVMs and selected benign lesions (e.g., older Kjellberg AVM series).
    - Currently more niche vs Gamma Knife/linac, but still used in some centers.
- **Fractionated / hypofractionated proton RT**:
    - Skull base chordoma/chondrosarcoma, pituitary, clival lesions.
    - Pediatric ependymoma, medulloblastoma (especially craniospinal).
    - Selected low-grade and high-grade gliomas near OARs.
    - Re-irradiation scenarios.

#### 3.2 Heavy ions (He, C, etc.)

- Same Bragg-peak physics, but **higher LET and RBE**, particularly at and just beyond the peak.
- Potential **radiobiologic advantage** in hypoxic / radioresistant tumors, at the cost of:
    - Greater uncertainty about **late toxicity**.
    - Limited availability and less neurosurgical experience vs proton.

### 4. Neuro-specific advantages of the Bragg peak

> [!summary] Why a neurosurgeon should care  
> The Bragg peak is a **geometry + biology tool**: it lets you push dose into tumor while reducing "dose bath" to normal brain, especially critical structures and developing CNS.

#### 4.1 Dose shaping around critical structures

Classic neuro indications where Bragg peak helps:

- **Skull base & clival tumors**
    - Chordoma, chondrosarcoma abutting:
        - Brainstem
        - Optic chiasm and nerves
        - Cavernous ICA
    - Protons allow **dose escalation** above what would be safe with photon IMRT alone.
- **Pediatric posterior fossa tumors**
    - Ependymoma, medulloblastoma:
        - Goal: maintain control while reducing dose to **cochlea, brainstem, temporal lobes**, and cervical cord.
- **Craniospinal irradiation (CSI)**
    - For medulloblastoma, PNET, etc.
    - Protons markedly reduce integral dose to:
        - Heart, lungs, bowel, gonads.
        - Thyroid and vertebral growth plates.
- **Optic apparatus / peri-optic lesions**
    - Pituitary adenoma, craniopharyngioma, optic nerve sheath meningioma:
        - Bragg peak distal fall-off used to spare **chiasm/optic nerves** while covering suprasellar/parasellar disease.
- **AVMs (historical and niche)**
    - Bragg-peak proton and heavy-charged-particle radiosurgery used in select intracranial AVMs series.

#### 4.2 Lower integral dose

Especially relevant in:

- **Children and YA**:
    - Reduced risk (theoretical and observed) of:
        - Secondary malignancy
        - Endocrine dysfunction
        - Neurocognitive decline
- **Long-survivors** with benign disease:
    - Vestibular schwannoma, meningioma, pituitary disease, etc.

### 5. Planning considerations tied to the Bragg peak

#### 5.1 Range uncertainty

Unlike photons, where the main spatial concerns are **isocenter accuracy and penumbra**, for protons the critical question is **"where does the Bragg peak actually stop?"**

Key sources of uncertainty:

- CT Hounsfield → stopping power conversion.
- Setup error and motion (including breathing for skull base/upper C-spine fields).
- Anatomical changes:
    - Weight loss
    - Sinus aeration changes
    - Ventricular size, post-op cavity evolution.

**Practical consequences:**

- **Robust planning**:
    - Add **proximal and distal margins** around CTV to create robust target (sometimes instead of or in addition to conventional PTV).
    - Consider **beam arrangement** so that **distal fall-off does NOT sit directly on a critical structure** if avoidable:
        - e.g., don't stop range right at brainstem when alternatives exist.

> [!warning] Clinical pitfall – distal edge on brainstem  
> The **highest LET / RBE zone** can be near the **distal edge** of the Bragg peak. Parking that edge in the brainstem or optic apparatus can risk **unexpected toxicity** even if nominal dose looks acceptable.

#### 5.2 RBE / LET issues

- Protons are often planned with **uniform RBE = 1.1**, but in reality:
    - RBE likely **increases near the distal edge** as LET rises.
- Potential manifestations:
    - Brainstem necrosis / cranial neuropathies in posterior fossa cases despite "safe" physical doses.
    - More pronounced late effects where distal edge overlaps eloquent structures.

**Practical neurosurgical translation:**

- When reviewing plans, don't just ask "What is the physical dose?"  
    Ask:
    
    - **Where are distal edges located?**
    - **What margin for range uncertainty is assumed?**
    - **Are institutional constraints adjusted for proton RBE/LET experience?**

#### 5.3 Delivery technique: passive vs PBS

- **Passive scattering**:
    - Uses range modulators, scatterers, collimators.
    - Simpler, more homogeneous SOBP, but more neutron scatter and less conformal in 3D.
- **Pencil Beam Scanning / IMPT**:
    - Scans narrow proton "pencil" through tumor layer by layer.
    - Allows **IMRT-like modulation** with proton Bragg physics.
    - Introduces **interplay effects** (motion vs scanning) – more of an issue in body, but can matter for e.g. CSI or near skull base with swallowing.

### 6. Clinical scenarios & neurosurgical decision points

#### 6.1 When to push for a Bragg-peak modality

> [!tip] Situations where proton/Bragg-based therapy is worth actively advocating
> 
> - **Pediatric cranial or craniospinal RT** where survival expectation is long.
> 
> - **Skull base chordoma/chondrosarcoma** where photon dose constraints would compromise control.
> 
> - **Lesions hugging brainstem/optic apparatus** where photon dose distributions just barely fail organ constraints.
> 
> - **Re-irradiation** where you are "spending the last few Gy" of tolerance on critical structures.

#### 6.2 When protons may not add much

- Small, **well-separated intracranial targets** already well served by:
    - Gamma Knife
    - LINAC SRS/SRT
- Scenarios where:
    - **Photon IMRT** already respects all OAR constraints.
    - Logistics, access, or cost of proton therapy are prohibitive and the dosimetric advantage is marginal.

### 7. Complications & late effects with Bragg-peak therapies

Most of the **complication spectrum** overlaps with conventional XRT/SRS and is discussed in Greenberg's radiation injury and necrosis section.

Specific concerns with Bragg-peak proton / heavy-ion use:

- **Brainstem and cranial nerve toxicity**:
    - Often associated with distal edge placement and possible higher RBE.
- **Radiation necrosis**:
    - Still occurs; not abolished by using protons.
- **Endocrine / neurocognitive effects**:
    - Likely reduced in magnitude due to lower integral dose but not zero.
- **Secondary malignancies**:
    - Lower volume of normal tissue in high dose.
    - Counterbalanced by neutron scatter (especially in passive systems); net effect depends on age, field, and technique.

> [!warning] Interpret proton plans with the same skepticism as photon plans
> 
> - The presence of a Bragg peak **does not automatically guarantee safety**.
> 
> - Check:
> 
>     - Dose/volume to critical structures
> 
>     - Distal edge placement
> 
>     - Robustness / uncertainty analysis
> 
>     - Past XRT exposure and cumulative biologically effective dose

### 8. Quick checklist for reviewing a "Bragg-peak" neuro plan

> [!check] As a neurosurgeon signing off on proton / heavy-ion plans
> 
> - **Target**
> 
>     - GTV/CTV defined appropriately?
> 
>     - Post-op cavity changes accounted for?
> 
> - **Edges**
> 
>     - Where do **distal Bragg edges** fall relative to brainstem, optic apparatus, cord, cochlea, hippocampi?
> 
>     - Are these edges **deliberately placed** rather than incidental?
> 
> - **Uncertainty**
> 
>     - Has range uncertainty been modeled (robustness, multiple scenarios)?
> 
>     - Is there a plan for **re-simulation** if anatomy changes (sinus aeration, fluid, shunt adjustments)?
> 
> - **Cumulative dose**
> 
>     - Prior XRT accounted for with biological summation?
> 
> - **Pediatrics**
> 
>     - Endocrine follow-up and neurocognitive monitoring arranged?
