## I. Anterior Circulation

The anterior circulation originates from the **Internal Carotid Artery (ICA)**.

1. ### Internal Carotid Artery (ICA)
	
	* **Origin**: Bifurcates from the Common Carotid Artery (CCA) at the level of the thyroid cartilage.
	* **Segments**: Cervical, Petrous, Cavernous, Clinoidal, and Supraclinoid (or intradural).
	* **Main Branches**:
		* **Ophthalmic Artery (OA)**: Arises from the anterior ICA, usually medial to the anterior clinoid process; supplies the globe and orbital contents.
		* **Posterior Communicating Artery (PComA)**: Arises from the posteromedial ICA, connects to the Posterior Cerebral Artery (PCA). Gives off perforators to the thalamus, hypothalamus, and internal capsule.
		* **Anterior Choroidal Artery (AChA)**: Arises from the lateral ICA, often distal to the PComA. Supplies the optic tract, cerebral peduncle, lateral geniculate body, globus pallidus, midbrain, thalamus, and posterior limb of the internal capsule.
		* **Terminal Bifurcation**: Into the Anterior Cerebral Artery (ACA) and Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA).

1. ### Anterior Cerebral Artery (ACA)
	
	* **Origin**: Terminal branch of ICA.
	* **Segments** (Fischer's classification):
		* **A1 (Precommunicating)**: From ICA to Anterior Communicating Artery (ACommA).
			* **Branches**: Medial lenticulostriate arteries (to anterior basal ganglion, anterior commissure, anterior hypothalamus, septum pellucidum, optic nerve/chiasm).
			* **Recurrent Artery of Heubner**: Often from proximal A2, supplies caudate nucleus, lentiform nucleus, and internal capsule.
		* **A2 (Postcommunicating/Pericallosal proximal)**: From ACommA to genu of corpus callosum.
			* **Branches**: Orbitofrontal artery, Frontopolar artery.
		* **A3 (Pericallosal distal/Callosomarginal)**: Follows corpus callosum posteriorly.
		* **A4/A5**: Distal branches beyond the genu to the splenium, sometimes grouped with A3 as cortical branches.
	* **Anterior Communicating Artery (ACommA)**: Connects the two ACAs.
		* **Branches**: Perforating branches (subcallosal, hypothalamic, chiasmatic).
2. ### Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA):
	
	* **Origin**: Terminal branch of ICA.
	* **Segments**:
		* **M1 (Sphenoidal/Horizontal)**: From ICA bifurcation to limen insulae.
			* **Branches**: Lateral lenticulostriate arteries (to basal ganglia, internal capsule). Early temporal/frontal branches may arise.
		* **M2 (Insular)**: From limen insulae, runs in insular compartment of sylvian fissure. Supplies insular cortex.
		* **M3 (Opercular)**: From circular sulcus to superficial aspect of sylvian fissure.
		* **M4 (Cortical)**: Emerge from sylvian fissure to cerebral hemisphere.
			* **Branches**: Orbitofrontal, Prefrontal, Precentral, Central, Anterior Parietal, Posterior Parietal, Angular (terminal, largest), Temporo-occipital, Posterior Temporal, Middle Temporal, Anterior Temporal, Temporopolar.

## II. Posterior Circulation

The posterior circulation is composed of the **Vertebral Arteries (VAs)** and the **Basilar Artery (BA)**.

1. ### Vertebral Artery (VA)
	
	* **Origin**: Posterosuperior wall of the subclavian artery.
	* **Segments**:
		* **V1**: From subclavian origin to C6 transverse foramen.
		* **V2**: Through C6 to C1 transverse foramina.
		* **V3**: From C1 transverse foramen to dura.
		* **V4 (Intradural)**: Pierces atlanto-occipital membrane and dura, enters foramen magnum to pontomedullary junction.
	* **Main Intracranial Branches**:
		* **Posterior Spinal Arteries**: Arise from VAs or PICAs, supply posterior and lateral spinal cord.
		* **Anterior Spinal Artery (ASA)**: Formed by bilateral VA branches, runs caudally in anteromedian sulcus; supplies anterior 75% of spinal cord and medial medulla.
		* **Posterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery (PICA)**.
2. ### Basilar Artery (BA)
	
	* **Origin**: Formed by the confluence of the two VAs at the pontomedullary junction.
	* **Course**: Ascends anterior to the pons, terminates near pontomesencephalic junction.
	* **Main Branches**:
		* **Anterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery (AICA)**
		* **Basilar Perforators**: Small branches supplying brainstem structures (corticospinal tracts, pontine nuclei, lemnisci, fasciculi, motor nuclei of midbrain and pons).
		* **Superior Cerebellar Artery (SCA)**.
		* **Posterior Cerebral Artery (PCA)**.
3. ### Posterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery (PICA)
	
	* **Origin**: Typically from the intradural VA.
	* **Segments** (ALPS-C mnemonic):
		* **Anterior Medullary**: From origin to inferior olivary prominence, related to hypoglossal nerve.
		* **Lateral Medullary**: Around inferior olive to CN IX, X, XI origins.
		* **Posterior Medullary (Tonsillomedullary)**: Loops below cerebellar tonsil (caudal loop).
		* **Telovelotonsillar (Supratonsillar)**: Ascends alongside tonsil (cranial loop); apex is choroidal point.
		* **Cortical**: Emerges onto cerebellar hemisphere, divides into medial and lateral trunks.
	* **Supply**: Posteroinferior cerebellar hemispheres, vermis, lateral and posterior medulla oblongata, choroid plexus of fourth ventricle.
	* **Clinical Relevance**: Occlusion of medullary branches causes Wallenberg syndrome.
4. **Anterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery (AICA)**
	* **Origin**: Basilar artery, typically mid-portion.
	* **Segments**: Premeatal, Meatal (related to internal auditory meatus and CN VII/VIII bundle), Postmeatal.
	* **Supply**: Brachium pontis, lateral pontine tegmentum, flocculus, anteroinferior cerebellum, choroid plexus of lateral recess of fourth ventricle.
	* **Branches**: Internal auditory artery (labyrinthine artery) (to inner ear).
5. **Superior Cerebellar Artery (SCA)**:
	* **Origin**: Basilar artery, immediately before basilar bifurcation.
	* **Course**: Posterolaterally around brainstem, inferior to CN III/IV, superior to CN V.
	* **Segments**: Anterior pontomesencephalic, Lateral pontomesencephalic (ambient), Cerebellomesencephalic (quadrigeminal), Cortical.
	* **Supply**: Superior vermis, superior/lateral cerebellar hemisphere, superior cerebellar peduncle, dentate nucleus, part of brachium pontis, tectum of mesencephalon.
	* **Clinical Relevance**: Common target for microvascular decompression in trigeminal neuralgia.
6. ### Posterior Cerebral Artery (PCA)
	
	* **Origin**: Terminal bifurcation of the basilar artery.
	* **Segments**:
		* **P1 (Precommunicating/Mesencephalic)**: From basilar bifurcation to PComA junction.
			* **Branches**: Posterior thalamoperforating arteries (to thalamus, brainstem, posterior internal capsule), Circumflex arteries (short/long, to geniculate bodies, superior colliculi, tectum, tegmentum, cerebral peduncle).
		* **P2 (Postcommunicating)**: From PComA to posterior midbrain. Divided into P2A (crural) and P2P (ambient).
			* **Branches**: Thalamogeniculate arteries (to lateral thalamus, posterior internal capsule, optic tract), Medial posterior choroidal artery (to choroid plexus of third ventricle, midbrain, pineal gland, thalamus), Lateral posterior choroidal artery (to choroid plexus of lateral ventricle, thalamus, fornix), Hippocampal artery, Inferior temporal arteries (anterior, middle, posterior).
		* **P3**: From posterior midbrain to calcarine fissure.
		* **P4**: Terminal cortical branches distal to calcarine fissure.
			* **Branches**: Calcarine artery (visual cortex), Splenial artery (posterior pericallosal artery, anastomoses with pericallosal artery).
	* **Supply**: Midbrain, thalamus, medial temporal and occipital lobes.
