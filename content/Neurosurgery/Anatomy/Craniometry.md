## Asterion

![[Neurosurgery/Anatomy/_resources/Craniometry.resources/IMG_7467.PNG]]![[Neurosurgery/Anatomy/_resources/Craniometry.resources/IMG_7467.PNG]]

* _Mercedes point_ is an alternative term
* junction of the lambdoid, occipitomastoid, and parietomastoid sutures
* Asterion types
	* Type 1: island of woven bone (39%)
	* Type 2: no woven bone (61%)
* external reference point for the transverse–sigmoid sinus junction (TSSJ)
	* superficial to TSSJ: 87%
	* Inferior to TSSJ: 11%
	* superior to TSSJ: 2%.
* Landmarks
	* ~4 cm posterior and 12 mm superior to the center of the EAC
	* Distance to root of zygoma: 5.8 cm
	* Distance to mastoid tip: 5.1-5.5 cm
	* Distance to inion: 6.3 cm
	* Distance to frankfult plane: 1.6 cm

## Stephanion

stephanion provides an important topographic anatomical key point. It can approximate the junction between the inferior frontal sulcus and precentral sulcus.

important burr hole site for many procedures, such as some chronic subdural hematoma evacuation

stephanion is a craniometric point, where the coronal suture meets the superior temporal line.

## Frankfurt plane

The Frankfurt plane (or Frankfurt horizontal) is a craniometric reference plane used to standardize head positioning. It is defined by a line passing through:

* the inferior margin of the orbit (orbitale), and
* the superior margin of the external auditory meatus (porion).

## Superior rolandic point (SRP)

* superior point of the central sulcus
* Multiple landmarks:
	* ~ 2 cm posterior to the 50% mark between the Nasion and Inion. 
	* Distance from coronal suture to SRP is 4.7 cm and 5.9 cm (~5 cm)

## Central sulcus

## Central sulcus

* Five historical topographical methods (Rhoton, Taylor-Haughton, Broca, Krönlein, Chipault) for localizing the central sulcus
* Methods based on the nasion-inion line (Rhoton, Taylor-Haughton, Chipault) generally provided the most accurate approximations
* Rhoton and Taylor-Haughton’s methods are the simplest and most accurate, with Taylor-Haughton being the closest to the true central sulcus.

### Tailor-Haughton line

![[Neurosurgery/Anatomy/_resources/Craniometry.resources/Image 20250928 120226.jpeg]]
The Taylor-Haughton line is drawn from a point 2 cm behind the midpoint of the nasion-inion line (SRP) to the intersection of the sylvian fissure and the condylar line (IRP), using the Frankfurt plane and specific surface cranial landmarks for reference.

1. Measure the distance from the nasion to the inion (the midline points at the front and back of the skull). 
2. Mark the 50% and 75% points along this ==nasion-inion line.== 
3. Draw the ==Frankfurt plane==: a line from the bottom of the orbit (eye socket) through the external auditory meatus (ear canal). 
4. Draw the ==posterior ear line==: starting 2 cm behind the 50% mark on the nasion-inion line, draw a line perpendicular to the Frankfurt plane, passing through the mastoid process. 
5. Draw the ==condylar line==: a line perpendicular to the Frankfurt plane from the mandibular condyle (jaw joint). 
6. Define the ==SRP/IRP==:
	* The superior rolandic point (==SRP==) is at 2 cm behind the 50% mark on the nasion-inion line.
	* The inferior rolandic point (==IRP==) is at the intersection of the sylvian fissure (drawn from the middle of the orbit to the 75% mark) and the condylar line. 
7. The ==Taylor-Haughton line== (central sulcus) is drawn from the SRP to the IRP.
