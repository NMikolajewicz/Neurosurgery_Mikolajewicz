**Role:** principal **parasympathetic** outflow to thoracoabdominal viscera + **motor** control of **larynx/pharynx** (phonation + swallowing).  
**Central origin:** medulla; long extracranial course → vulnerable from **posterior fossa → neck → thorax**.

---

## High-yield map

- **Exit skull:** **[[Neurosurgery/Anatomy/Jugular foramen]]** with **CN IX** and **CN XI**
- **Neck:** descends in the **[[carotid sheath]]** (classically between carotid artery and IJV)
- **Thorax:** key branch is **[[Recurrent laryngeal nerve]] (RLN)** (voice/airway protection)
- **Unilateral lesion:** hoarseness ± dysphagia, palatal weakness, ↓ gag
- **Bilateral lesion:** incompatible with life (airway + autonomic failure)

---

## Surgical anatomy and course

### Cranial course and exit

- Emerges from **medulla** as **rootlets lateral to the olive**
- Traverses the **jugular foramen** (often described via an intrajugular/pars nervosa compartment in skull-base frameworks)
- Forms 2 sensory ganglia at/near the foramen:
    - **Superior (jugular) ganglion**
    - **Inferior (nodose) ganglion**
- **CN XI (cranial root)** is functionally inseparable from CN X and joins it after jugular foramen transit

### Cervical course (carotid sheath)

- Descends in the carotid sheath; Greenberg notes the **ICA travels in the carotid sheath with the IJV and vagal nerve**.

> [!surgical-pearl]  
> In anterior cervical exposure, remember the vagus is _in the sheath_, not "on the side." Excess traction/retraction can produce vagal reflexes (bradycardia) and contribute to laryngeal symptoms.

### Thoracic branches (recurrent laryngeal nerves)

- **Left RLN:** loops under **aortic arch** (6th arch derivative)
- **Right RLN:** loops under **subclavian artery** (4th arch derivative)
- **Motor:** RLN supplies **all intrinsic laryngeal muscles except cricothyroid** (cricothyroid = **external branch of superior laryngeal nerve**)

## Neurovascular + intraoperative relevance

### Vascular relationships

- Posterior fossa: vascular loops (e.g., PICA) may threaten rootlets at medullary exit
- Jugular foramen vascular supply: often described via ascending pharyngeal contributions (handy for skull-base dissection planning)

### Reflex bradycardia/syncope risk

- Manipulation/stretch can trigger **profound vagal responses** → pause, irrigate, deepen anesthesia, treat as needed (e.g., anticholinergic) depending on context.

## Clinical localization

### Core deficits (CN X)

- **Hoarseness** (vocal cord paresis/paralysis)
- **Dysphagia**, aspiration risk
- **Palatal weakness** (uvula typically deviates **away** from weak side)
- **↓ gag reflex** (often more reliable as "palate elevates?" than as a pure IX vs X discriminator)

> [!note]  
> Greenberg highlights that lower medullary syndromes can include **dysphagia, diminished gag, hoarseness** from involvement of **IX & X** fibers/nuclei.

### Jugular foramen syndromes (skull base)

Greenberg tabulates cranial nerve patterns across classic jugular foramen–related syndromes:

- **Vernet**: IX, X, XI
- **Collet-Sicard**: IX, X, XI, XII
- **Villaret**: IX, X, XI, XII + sympathetics (Horner)
- (Others listed: Tapia, Jackson, Schmidt, Sicard)

**Etiologies to keep on your radar:** condylar/Jefferson fractures, ICA dissection, primary/metastatic tumors, Lyme disease, fibromuscular dysplasia.

> [!danger]  
> Jugular bulb thrombosis can compress nerves in the **jugular foramen pars nervosa** and present with **hoarseness/aphonia + dysphagia + breathlessness** (think Vernet-pattern symptoms).

## Vagal nuclei and functional components (medulla)

|Nucleus|Fiber type|Function / targets|Clinical hooks|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Nucleus ambiguus**|**SVE**|Motor to striated muscles of **pharynx/larynx/soft palate**|Bulbar dysphagia, dysphonia|
|**Dorsal motor nucleus of vagus**|**GVE**|Preganglionic parasympathetic to **heart, lungs, GI**|Autonomic instability; implicated in lateral medullary patterns|
|**Nucleus of the solitary tract (NTS)**|**GVA/SVA**|Visceral sensation (pharynx/larynx, cardiorespiratory & GI reflexes); taste from epiglottis|Afferent limb of visceral reflex arcs|
|**Spinal trigeminal nucleus**|**GSA**|External ear canal/TM + posterior fossa dura (via Arnold's nerve contributions)|Ear-cough/vagal reflex phenomena|

## Vagus nerve stimulation (VNS)

- **Technique concept:** cervical electrodes wrapped around vagus → connected to implanted programmable generator
- **FDA indication (per Greenberg):** adjunctive therapy for patients **>12** with **partial-onset seizures refractory** to medical therapy
- **Key surgical complication:** transient or permanent **vocal cord paralysis**

**Side selection (practical convention):** typically **left cervical vagus trunk** (often chosen to reduce theoretical cardiac conduction risk because the right vagus is commonly considered more influential on SA node activity).

## Practical surgical pearls

> [!tip] Preventing postoperative voice issues (generalizable)  
> RLN or vagal injury can present as **hoarseness, breathiness, cough, aspiration, dysphagia, vocal fatigue**; Greenberg notes vocal cord paresis can occur from **RLN or vagus injury**, with reported temporary/permanent rates in an anterior cervical context. Minimize sharp paratracheal dissection and avoid prolonged/retractive pressure; ET tube cuff management may help in select scenarios.

**Branch-level localization (quick bedside heuristic)**

- **Isolated RLN palsy:** hoarseness ± aspiration; palate often spared
- **High vagal lesion (above SLN/RLN takeoff):** hoarseness + dysphagia + palate weakness
- **Jugular foramen pattern:** add **IX** (posterior tongue/pharyngeal sensory) ± **XI** (SCM/trap)
