### Internal carotid artery

The term "carotid siphon" is sometimes used to refer to the cavernous plus clinoid or paraclinoid internal carotid artery segments

7 segments
C1 - cervical
C2 - petrous 
C3 - lacerum
C4 - cavernous
C5 - clinoid
C6 - ophthalmic
C7 - communicating 

- C1: cervical segment
    
- C2: petrous (horizontal) segment
    
- C3: lacerum segment
    
- C4: cavernous segment
    
- C5: clinoid segment
    
- C6: ophthalmic (supraclinoid) segment
    
- C7: communicating (terminal) segment

- **C1:** cervical segment (starts at [carotid bifurcation](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/carotid-bifurcation?embed_domain=external.radpair.com%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D&lang=us))
- **C2:** petrous segment (starts at [carotid canal](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/carotid-canal?embed_domain=external.radpair.com%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D&lang=us))
- **C3:** lacerum segment (starts at posterior edge of [foramen lacerum](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/foramen-lacerum?embed_domain=external.radpair.com%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D&lang=us))
- **C4:** cavernous segment (starts at the superior margin of [petrolingual ligament](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/missing?article%5Btitle%5D=petrolingual-ligament&embed_domain=external.radpair.com%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D&lang=us))
- **C5:** clinoid segment (starts at [proximal dural ring](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/missing?article%5Btitle%5D=proximal-dural-ring&embed_domain=external.radpair.com%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D&lang=us) / [anterior clinoid process](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/missing?article%5Btitle%5D=anterior-clinoid-process&embed_domain=external.radpair.com%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D&lang=us))
- **C6:** ophthalmic (supraclinoid) segment (starts at [distal dural ring](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/distal-dural-ring?embed_domain=external.radpair.com%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D&lang=us) / [cavernous sinus](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/cavernous-sinus?embed_domain=external.radpair.com%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D&lang=us)roof)
- **C7:** communicating (terminal) segment (starts just proximal to the origin of [posterior communicating artery](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/posterior-communicating-artery?embed_domain=external.radpair.com%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D&lang=us), ends at bifurcation into [middle](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/middle-cerebral-artery?embed_domain=external.radpair.com%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D&lang=us) and [anterior cerebral](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/anterior-cerebral-artery?embed_domain=external.radpair.com%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D%252525252527%25252525255B0%25252525255D&lang=us)arteries)



Except for the terminal segment (C7), the odd-numbered segments usually have no branches. ==The even-numbered segments (C2, C4, C6) often have branches==, although they are inconstant and usually small, and are therefore often not visualized even on high-resolution [digital subtraction angiography](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/digital-subtraction-angiography?lang=us). The exception is the [ophthalmic artery](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/ophthalmic-artery?lang=us "Ophthalmic artery"), which is seen in nearly all cases 3.

- C1: cervical segment
	- Branches: none
- C2: petrous (horizontal) segment
	- Branches:
		- [caroticotympanic artery](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/caroticotympanic-artery-2?lang=us) (often absent)
		- [Vidian artery](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/vidian-artery-2?lang=us)
		- periosteal branch 
- C3: lacerum segment
	- Branches: none

	    - [capsular arteries (of McConnell)](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/mcconnells-capsular-arteries?lang=us) (variable)
- C5: clinoid segment
	- Branches: none
- C6: ophthalmic (supraclinoid) segment
	- Branches
	    - [ophthalmic artery](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/ophthalmic-artery?lang=us)
	    - [superior hypophyseal artery](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/superior-hypophyseal-artery?lang=us)
- C7: communicating segment
	- Branches
		- [posterior communicating artery](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/posterior-communicating-artery?lang=us)
	    - [anterior choroidal artery](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/anterior-choroidal-artery?lang=us)
	    - [anterior cerebral artery](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/anterior-cerebral-artery?lang=us)
	    - [middle cerebral artery](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/middle-cerebral-artery?lang=us)

### Anterior choroidal artery

* * *

anterior choroidal artery 

* preterminal branch of the communicating segment (C7) of the internal carotid artery
	* ==originates from the posterior wall of the [internal carotid artery (ICA)](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/internal-carotid-artery-1?lang=us)==
	* between the origin of the [posterior communicating artery](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/posterior-communicating-artery?lang=us) (PCOM) (which is 2-5 mm proximal to the AChA) and the internal carotid termination (which is 2-5 mm distal to the AChA). 
	* measures ~1 mm in diameter.
* supplies several ==subcortical structures==
	* limbic system
	* basal ganglia
	* diencephalon
	* midbrain
	* temporal lobe 
	* visual pathway.

Occlusion

* Hemiparesis
* hemianesthesia
* hemianopsia

### Posterior inferior cerebellar artery (PICA)

* * *

*   1/3 arterial vessels that supply [cerebellum](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/cerebellum?lang=us).
*   Most variable and tortuous cerebellar artery.
* **Paired artery**
* **Origin:**[vertebral artery](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/vertebral-artery?lang=us) V4 segment.
	* **Highly variable origin**
		* ~20% extracranially, inferior to [foramen magnum](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/foramen-magnum?lang=us)
		* 10% from the basilar
		* 2% bilaterally absent
* **Supply**
	* Variable territory depending on the size of the [AICA](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/anterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery?lang=us) ([AICA-PICA dominance](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/aica-pica-dominance-1?lang=us)).
	* Typically supplies: 
		* ==Posteroinferior [cerebellar](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/cerebellum?lang=us)== hemispheres (up to the great horizontal fissure) 
			* Cerebellar tonsils: 85% of the time
			* Biventral lobule: 80%
			* Nucleus gracilis: 85%
			* Superior semilunar lobule: 50%
		* Inferior portion of the vermis
		* Lower part of the medulla: 50%
		* [inferior cerebellar peduncles](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/inferior-cerebellar-peduncle-1?lang=us)
* Lateral medullary syndrome
	* A.k.a. wallenberg syndrome
	* commonly due to occlusion of the vertebral artery on the ipsilateral side.
	* results from infarct in region supplied by PICA (branch of vertebral artery)
* highest ==risk of injury in a Chiari decompression== surgery

### Internal capsule

* * *

derived from several sources.

* The lateral lenticulostriate branches from the middle cerebral artery (MCA)
* the medial striate artery from the ACA
* direct branches from the ICA.
* The anterior choroidal artery comes off the ICA
