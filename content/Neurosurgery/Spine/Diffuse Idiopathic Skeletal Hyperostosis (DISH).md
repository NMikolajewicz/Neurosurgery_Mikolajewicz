> [!summary] DISH Review
> 
> - **Dx:** **Flowing anterolateral ossification** spanning **≥4 contiguous vertebrae** + **preserved disc height** + **no facet/SI ankylosis**
> 
> - **Best test:** AP/lateral radiographs (confirmation); **CT** for **occult fracture** when trauma/concern after **low-energy** mechanism
> 
> - **Key imaging:** **Nonmarginal syndesmophytes** ("**flowing candle wax**"); thoracic often **right-sided**, commonly **T7–T11**
> 
> - **Presentation:** often incidental; thoracolumbar **AM stiffness** ± mild chronic pain; cervical osteophytes → **dysphagia/stridor/OSA**
> 
> - **Risk factors:** **gout**, **hyperlipidemia**, **diabetes**
> 
> - **Tx:** most cases → activity modification + PT + brace/NSAIDs + **bisphosphonate therapy**
> 
> - **Fracture:** ankylosed segments → unstable patterns with minor trauma (hyperextension common) → **long spinal fusion**; traction **with caution**
> 
> - **Complication:** cervical trauma mortality **15% (operative)** vs **67% (nonoperative)**
>  

## Definitions 

- Definition: DISH = common disorder of unknown etiology characterized by ==enthesopathy of the spine and extremities==
	- Also known as: Forestier disease
- Core concept: spinal ankylosis + large anterior osteophytes → stiff spine + high-risk trauma mechanics + cervical airway/swallowing sequelae

## Overview

- Epidemiology: overall incidence 6–12%; uncommon before 50 years
    - Prevalence: 
	    - 50 y: 25% males / 15% females
	    - 80 y: 28% males / 26% females
- Distribution: 
	- ==thoracic== (often right-sided) > cervical > lumbar
	- thoracic often isolated (T7–T11)
- Risk factors: 
	- gout
	- hyperlipidemia
	- diabetes
- Symptoms: often asymptomatic; thoracolumbar mild chronic pain + stiffness (AM worse; cold aggravates)
- ==Cervical sequelae==: dysphagia/stridor; hoarseness; sleep apnea; difficult intubation; cervical myelopathy
- Trauma: minor/low-energy mechanisms can cause unstable fractures (hyperextension common) due to ==long lever arms==
- Imaging: 
	- AP/lateral radiographs
	- CT when trauma/concern for occult fracture
- Tx: 
	- conservative for most
	- fractures → long spinal fusion
	- decompression/stabilization for stenosis/myelopathy/deformity

## Diagnosis & Workup

### Diagnosis criteria

| Diagnostic criterion | Requirement                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Flowing ossification | Flowing ==ossification== along anterolateral aspect of ==≥4 contiguous vertebrae==                                                                      |
| Disc space           | Preservation of disk height in involved segment; <br>relative absence of significant degenerative changes (e.g., marginal sclerosis, vacuum phenomenon) |
| Joints               | Absence of facet-joint ankylosis; <br>absence of SI joint erosion, sclerosis, or intraarticular osseous fusion                                          |
### Clinical suspicion

- **Dx:** decreased spinal ROM + stiffness (often **AM worse**) ± mild chronic pain
	- consider when large cervical anterior osteophytes with dysphagia/stridor/OSA
- **Red flag:** pain after seemingly minor trauma in an ankylosed spine → treat as **possible unstable fracture** until excluded

### Imaging

- Best test: 
	- AP/lateral xray of involved region (cervical/thoracic commonly used to establish diagnosis)
	- CT when concern for fracture after low-energy trauma or when DISH + neck pain + trauma (evaluate for occult fracture)
- Key imaging: 
	- nonmarginal syndesmophytes at three successive levels (4 contiguous vertebrae)
	- thoracic often right-sided; cervical anterior bone formation with preserved disc space (lateral view); lumbar may be symmetrical
- Pitfall: 
	- technetium bone scan shows increased uptake at involved areas and may be confused with metastases

| Modality                 | **Indication:**                                                            | **Key imaging:** / notes                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Radiographs (AP/lateral) | Confirm diagnosis; evaluate involved region                                | **Nonmarginal syndesmophytes** at 3 successive levels (**4 contiguous vertebrae**); thoracic often **right-sided**; cervical anterior bone formation with **preserved disc space** (best on lateral) |

### Differential diagnosis

- **Rule-out:** 
	- ankylosing spondylitis 
		- **SI joint involvement**, **marginal** syndesmophytes, **osteopenia**, or HLA-B27 association pattern
	- metastases 
		- bone scan uptake pattern is discordant with DISH distribution (correlate with radiographs/CT)

### DISH vs. ankylosing spondylitis

| Feature              | DISH                                                                 | Ankylosing spondylitis                                                    |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pathophysiology      | ==Excess bone formed==                                               | ==Soft tissue converted to bone==                                         |
| Syndesmophytes       | Nonmarginal                                                          | Marginal                                                                  |
| Radiographs          | "==Flowing candle wax=="                                             | "==Bamboo spine==", vertebral squaring, Romanus lesions ("shiny corners") |
| Disc space           | Preservation of disc space                                           | Cervical AS: disc space ossification (fusion between vertebral bodies)    |
| Osteopenia           | No osteopenia (may have increased radiodensity)                      | Osteopenia present                                                        |
| HLA                  | No HLA‑B27 association; <br>+/- HLA‑B8 association (DISH + diabetes) | Strong association with ==HLA‑B27==                                       |
| Age group            | Older / middle-aged                                                  | Younger                                                                   |
| SI joint involvement | No involvement (SI abnormality generally excludes DISH)              | Bilateral sacroiliitis                                                    |
| Diabetes             | Yes                                                                  | No                                                                        |

## Management

### Nonoperative

- Activity modification + physical therapy + brace wear + NSAIDs + **bisphosphonate therapy**
    - **Indication:** most cases

### Operative 

- Spinal decompression + stabilization
    - **Indication:** specific sequelae (e.g., lumbar stenosis, cervical myelopathy, adult spinal deformity)
- Cervical traction
    - **Indication:** cervical spine fracture
    - **Pitfall:** **use with caution**; traction may cause **excessive distraction** due to lack of ligamentous structures
- Long spinal fusion (long-segment stabilization)
    - **Indication:** associated spine fractures / instability in DISH

> [!checklist] Imaging + Tx pathway (DISH)
> 
> - **Suspected DISH** → AP/lateral radiographs of involved region
> 
> - **Trauma or concern for fracture (even low-energy)** → **CT** to evaluate for **occult fracture**
> 
> - **Cervical fracture** → traction only **with caution** (risk of excessive distraction)
> 
> - **Fracture/instability** → **long spinal fusion** (stabilization)
> 
> - **Sequelae (stenosis/myelopathy/deformity)** → decompression + stabilization

## Pitfalls & Red Flags

> [!warning] Pitfalls & Red Flags
> 
> - **Red flag:** 
> 	- DISH + pain after **low-energy trauma** → **occult unstable fracture** risk → **CT** recommended
> 	- dysphagia, stridor, hoarseness, sleep apnea, or **difficult intubation** (cervical anterior osteophytes)
> 	- new neurologic deficits (myelopathy/stenosis symptoms)
> 
> - **Pitfall:** 
> 	- dismissing "minor mechanism" injuries; ankylosed segments create **long lever arms** → displacement/instability
> 	- cervical traction without caution → **excessive distraction** (lack of ligamentous structures)
> 	- technetium bone scan uptake misread as **metastases** without correlation to radiographs/CT
