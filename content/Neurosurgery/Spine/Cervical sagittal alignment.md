> [!summary] Cervical sagittal alignment (CSM/DCM ± OPLL) 
> 
> - **Sagittal reserve:** C2–7 Cobb (lordosis vs kyphosis); ==preop kyphosis (≤0–10°)== → ↑ myelopathy severity + ↑ postop malalignment risk
>     
> - **Global balance:** ==C2–7 SVA ≥30–42 mm== (abnl >40 mm) → worse mJOA/disability, higher compensation demand
>     
> - **T1 slope:** high **T1S** = higher **lordosis requirement**; high T1S + limited lordosis reserve → postop sagittal problems
>     
> - **Dynamics/instability:** spondylolisthesis, high flex ROM, ==gap ROM >30°== → ↑ malalignment risk
>     
> - **OPLL “drift” feasibility:** **K-line** (+) → posterior cord drift likely; ==K-line (−)== / flex K-line (−) + high occupation ratio → poor drift
>     
> - **Approach selection:** CSM **without kyphosis** → ADF vs laminoplasty vs PDF similar; **severe kyphosis** or **K-line (−) OPLL** → favor **ADF ± PDF** (avoid posterior-only)
>

Cervical sagittal alignment influences **myelopathy severity, functional outcomes, and risk of postoperative malalignment**—especially in **CSM/DCM** and **OPLL**.

> [!tip] Practical takeaway  
> 
> 1. **Baseline alignment reserve** (lordosis/kyphosis + global balance)
> 
> 2. **Can the spinal cord drift posteriorly after decompression?** (esp. OPLL → **K-line**)
>

## Clinical Relevance
### Postoperative misalignment risk factors (CSM/OPLL/DCM)
In patients with CSM/OPLL/DCM, the risk factors for postoperative misalignment:
- ==preoperative kyphosis (C2–7 angle ≤ 0–10°)==
- ==SVA ≥ 30–42 mm==
- ==greater T1 slope==
- ==spondylolisthesis==
- ==K-line (−)==
- flex K-line (−)
- greater flex ROM
- gap ROM exceeding 30°
- age over 75
- large OPLL occupation ratio

> [!warning] Pattern recognition  
> These cluster into:  
> **(1) poor preop sagittal reserve** (kyphosis/large SVA/high T1 slope) 
> **(2) dynamic instability** (high flex ROM / large gap ROM / spondylolisthesis)
> **(3) "insufficient posterior drift" anatomy** (K-line − / large OPLL).

---
### Surgical approaches (selection pearls)
- In CSM patients ==without kyphosis==, there are **no significant differences** between **ADF**, **laminoplasty only**, and **PDF** overall.
- In patients ==with severe local kyphosis==, **ADF** was the better surgery for **maintenance of cervical alignment**.
- **K-line** can determine appropriate surgical procedures for patients with **C-OPLL**.
- **ADF and/or PDF** are suitable surgical treatments for **K-line (–) OPLL** with **local kyphosis**, **large OPLL**, and/or **cervical kyphosis**.

> [!note] Abbreviations (as used here)
> 
> - **CSM/DCM**: cervical spondylotic myelopathy / degenerative cervical myelopathy
> 
> - **OPLL**: ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament
> 
> - **ADF**: anterior decompression (± discectomy/corpectomy) and fusion
> 
> - **PDF**: posterior decompression and fusion
>

## Metrics

![[Neurosurgery/Spine/_resources/Cervical_sagittal_alignment.resources/image.4.png]]
### Cobb angle (C2–C7)
- Most common metric used for cervical sagittal alignment (CSA)
    - measure of **lordosis/kyphosis** in the cervical spine

**Definition**
- The Cobb angle is obtained by drawing parallel lines extending from:
    - the **lower endplate** of the most superior vertebral level (**C2**) and
    - the **lower endplate** of the most inferior vertebral level (**C7**).
- Perpendicular lines are then drawn from the parallel lines, and the angle of their intersection is the **Cobb angle**.

**Population characteristics**
- Average lordosis in the C2–C7 region: ==13.9 ± 12.3 degrees==
- Associations:
    - male and female lordosis is similar
    - upward trend in lordosis with age

**Thresholds (as listed)**
- Abnormal > 10 degrees
- anterior approach if > 13 degrees

![[Neurosurgery/Spine/_resources/Cervical_sagittal_alignment.resources/image.1.png|200]]

> [!tip] Measurement hygiene  
> Use **standing lateral** radiographs when possible (alignment is posture-dependent). Be consistent with:
> 
> - C2 and C7 endplate selection
> 
> - neutral head position
> 
> - whether you're measuring **local** vs **global** alignment
>

### Cervical SVA (C2–C7 SVA)
**Definition**
- Horizontal distance between the **posterosuperior corner of C7** and a **plumb line** drawn from the **centroid of C2**
    - "plumb line from C2 to C7"
    
**Population characteristics**
- C2–C7 SVA in standing asymptomatic volunteers: ==16.8 ± 11.2 mm==
- ==Abnormal > 40 mm==
    - associated with worse myelopathy and disability score
    
**Associations**
- C2–C7 SVA is significantly correlated with **mJOA** in patients with cervical spondylotic myelopathy

![[Neurosurgery/Spine/_resources/Cervical_sagittal_alignment.resources/image.png|400]]

> [!note] Clinical interpretation  
> **SVA** is a "how far forward is the head?" proxy.  
> Larger SVA → higher **compensatory demand**, often worse patient-reported outcomes and myelopathy scores.

---

### K-line (OPLL-focused)

**Definition**
- A straight line connecting the **midpoints of the spinal canal at C2 and C7** on a plain lateral radiograph

**What it captures**
- A single index incorporating **alignment + OPLL size**:
    - **K-line (+)**: OPLL does **not** exceed the K-line
    - **K-line (−)**: OPLL **does** exceed the K-line

**Population characteristics**
- Mean K-line tilt is ==11.28 ± 8.31°==.

**Associations**
- Prognosis of laminoplasty (LP) for CSM: **yes**, K-line predicts outcome
    - recovery of **K-line (−)** group (19%) was worse than **K-line (+)** group (51%, _p<0.05_)
- K-line tilt correlation:
    - with C2–C7 SVA (r = 0.81, P = 0.000)
    - with T1S-CL (r = 0.32, P = 0.026)

> [!warning] Surgical implication (core concept)  
> **K-line (−)** suggests posterior-only decompression is less likely to allow adequate **posterior cord drift** (especially with kyphosis/large OPLL), pushing you toward **ADF ± PDF**.

> [!tip] "Flex K-line (−)" (dynamic K-line)  
> If the K-line becomes negative in **flexion**, it flags **dynamic encroachment** and reduced drift reserve—often aligning with the "risk factor" bucket (greater flex ROM / gap ROM).

---

### T1-slope (T1S)

**Definition**
- Angle between a **horizontal line** and the **upper endplate of T1**

**Concept**
- T1 slope is analogous to **sacral slope**:
    - Greater pelvic incidence → needs greater lumbar lordosis to compensate
    - Greater **T1 slope** → needs greater **cervical lordosis** to balance the head over the thoracic inlet

**Associations**
- Cobb angle C2–7 increases as T1 slope increases
    - higher lordosis required to compensate for higher T1 slope
- SVA C2–7 decreases as T1 slope increases

> [!note] How to "use" T1 slope in planning  
> T1S helps estimate the **lordosis demand** required to keep the head balanced—high T1S with limited cervical lordosis reserve is a common setup for postoperative sagittal issues.

## Synthesis: a planning scaffold (CSM/DCM ± OPLL)

> [!summary] Preop checklist (fast mental model)
> 
> - **Lordosis/kyphosis**: is there ==preoperative kyphosis (C2–7 angle ≤ 0–10°)==?
> 
> - **Global balance**: is SVA trending high (==≥ 30–42 mm== risk; >40 mm "abnormal" here)?
> 
> - **Compensation demand**: is **T1 slope** high?
> 
> - **Dynamics**: flex ROM high / gap ROM >30° / spondylolisthesis?
> 
> - **OPLL drift feasibility**: **K-line ±** (and **flex K-line ±**) + occupation ratio
>  

> [!summary] Approach 
> 
> - **CSM without kyphosis** → ADF vs laminoplasty vs PDF: **no major overall difference**
> 
> - **Severe local kyphosis** → **ADF** best for maintaining alignment
> 
> - **OPLL with K-line (−)** (esp. kyphosis/large OPLL) → **ADF and/or PDF** favored over posterior-only decompression
>
