## **Cervical Spine Clearance — CCR & NEXUS**

> [!summary] **CCR & NEXUS Review**  
> • Two major clearance tools: **Canadian C-Spine Rule (CCR)** and **NEXUS**.  
> • **CCR is superior** (higher specificity, **100% NPV**) and preferred when patient is alert/sober/stable.  
> • CCR: image if **age ≥65**, **dangerous mechanism**, or **paresthesias**; otherwise check low-risk factors and **45° neck rotation**.  
> • NEXUS: no imaging if **all** negative - midline tenderness, intoxication, AMS, neuro deficit, distracting injury.  
> • **Elderly limitation:** NEXUS may miss up to **20%** of fractures ≥65 yrs; CCR performs better.  
> • Distracting injuries → invalidate **NEXUS**, not CCR.  
> • If unable to rotate neck or clinical doubt persists → **CT cervical spine**.

## **1. Canadian C-Spine Rule (CCR)**

![[Neurosurgery/Spine/_resources/Canadian_C-Spine_Rule.resources/image.png|700]]
### **Stepwise Logic**
1. **High-risk factors** → **Image**
    - Age ≥65
    - Dangerous mechanism (axial load, high-speed MVC, fall >3 ft/5 stairs, etc.)
    - Paresthesias in extremities
2. **Low-risk factors** → allow safe assessment of ROM
    - Simple rear-end MVC
    - Sitting position in ED
    - Ambulatory at any time
    - Delayed onset of neck pain
    - No midline cervical tenderness
3. **Active neck rotation**
    - Rotate 45° left + right → If able → **No imaging needed**

> [!tip] **Pearl:** CCR is **not validated in non-alert patients (GCS <15), intoxication, or unstable vitals**.

## **2. NEXUS Criteria**

![[Neurosurgery/Spine/_resources/Canadian_C-Spine_Rule.resources/image.1.png|500]]
### **C-Spine Imaging NOT Needed if _all_ are negative**
- **No posterior midline tenderness**
- **No intoxication**
- **Normal alertness (GCS 15)**
- **No focal neurologic deficit**
- **No painful distracting injury**

> [!warning] **Limitations:** Lower specificity compared to CCR; may over-image.

> [!summary] **Why NEXUS Underperforms in Older Adults**  
> • NEXUS relies on a **reliable exam**, but older adults often have baseline cognitive impairment, altered pain perception, or communication limitations.  
> • **Midline tenderness** is unreliable—many elderly have chronic cervical pain or, conversely, underreport pain.  
> • **Distracting injuries** (ribs, hips, extremity fractures) are common and invalidate NEXUS by definition.  
> • **Polypharmacy** (opioids, benzos, gabapentinoids) complicates the “no intoxication” criterion.  
> • **Degenerative disease** and higher fracture risk even with low-energy mechanisms make NEXUS less sensitive.  
> • Result: NEXUS may **miss up to 20%** of fractures ≥65 yrs → **CCR preferred** for geriatric trauma.
---

==Note that the 20% miss rate using NEXUS for 65y individuals is likely an over-estimate. Real miss rate is closer to 5-11%. ==
## **3. Diagnostic Performance**

> [!summary] **Key Findings**  
> • **CCR:** 100% NPV; higher specificity than NEXUS.  
> • **NEXUS:** High sensitivity but lower specificity → more imaging.  
> • Meta-analyses consistently show **CCR reduces unnecessary CT imaging**.

## **4. Quick Comparison Table**

|Feature|**CCR**|**NEXUS**|
|---|---|---|
|Sensitivity|Very high (≈99–100%)|Very high (≈99%)|
|Specificity|**Higher**|Lower|
|NPV|**100%**|Lower|
|Complexity|More steps|Simpler|
|Best For|Alert, stable trauma patients|Broad, rapid screening|
|Limitations|Not validated in intoxicated or GCS<15|Over-imaging due to low specificity|

---
## **5. Pearls**

> [!tip] **Key Points**  
> • **CCR > NEXUS** for ruling out significant c-spine injury (better specificity + same sensitivity).  
> • CCR requires **GCS 15**, stable vitals, and **neck rotation assessment**.  
> • NEXUS fails in elderly—misses up to **20%** of fractures in age ≥65.  
> • CCR: **Age ≥65, dangerous mechanism, paresthesias → automatic CT**.  
> • Distracting injuries invalidate NEXUS, **not** CCR.  
> • If in doubt or patient cannot rotate their neck → **CT**.
