structural spinal disorder characterized by excessive, rigid kyphosis (forward rounding) of the thoracic or thoracolumbar spine due to wedge-shaped vertebral bodies in adolescents.

### Definition and Epidemiology

* Criteria
	* ==\>3 adjacent vertebral with > 5 degrees of anterior wedging==
	* ==\> 40 degrees kyphosis (hyperkyphosis)==
* common during adolescence (ages 12–17), males > females 

### Etiology and Pathophysiology

* Etiology NYD
* hereditary component and abnormal mineralization/growth of vertebral endplates during puberty.
* This process leads to irregular growth, anterior vertebral wedging, and rigid roundback deformity

### Clinical Features

* Visible, rigid hunchback or roundback that cannot be corrected by posture.
* About half of patients experience back pain, often worsened by activity or prolonged sitting/standing.
* In severe cases, there can be cosmetic concerns, decreased lung function, and rarely, neurological deficits.

### Associated Features and Complications

* ==Schmorl's nodes== (herniations of disc material into vertebral bodies)
* Scoliosis (lateral curvature) in one third of cases.
* Complications include progressive deformity, possible chronic pain, and rarely respiratory/neurological compromise in severe cases.

### Management

* Conservative treatment (physical therapy, bracing, NSAIDs) is usually effective for mild to moderate cases or kyphosis less than 60–80 degrees.
* Braces can slow progression in skeletally immature patients
* Surgical spinal fusion is considered for severe fixed kyphosis (>75 degrees), refractory pain, or neurological compromise.

### Prognosis

* Most cases respond well to conservative care, with pain and progression stabilizing after skeletal maturity.
* Some adults may have persistent back pain or reduced quality of life compared to the general population.\[1\]
