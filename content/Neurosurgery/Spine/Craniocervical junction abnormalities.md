## Core concept

Craniocervical junction (CCJ) pathology often converges on two problems:
1. **Foramen magnum / posterior fossa crowding** (ventral brainstem/upper cord compression)
2. **Instability** (dynamic compression, myelopathy risk)

> [!warning] Clinical red flags  
> Progressive myelopathy, lower cranial nerve symptoms, drop attacks/syncope, severe cervico-occipital pain, or dynamic symptoms with flexion/extension → treat as **high-risk CCJ** until proven otherwise.

---

## 1) Basilar impression, basilar invagination, platybasia, convexobasia

**Shared phenotype**
- Floor of posterior fossa **bulges upward**
- Results in **narrowing of the foramen magnum**
### Types (definitions)

- **Basilar invagination**
    - Congenital upward displacement of dens into a _normal_ foramen magnum with **normal bone**
    - Presentation: may be **asymptomatic**
- **Basilar impression**
    - Upward displacement of dens due to **acquired softening of skull base bones**
    - Examples: **Paget disease**, **osteomalacia/rickets**, **hyperparathyroidism**, **RA**
- **Platybasia**
    - Abnormal **flattening** of the skull base
- **Convexobasia**
    - More extreme form
    - **Cupular** occipital shape
    - Often associated with **occipitalization of atlas**

### Management 

- **Preoperative halo traction** (5–25 lbs) × 2–5 days
    - reduces cervical instability
- **Surgical decompression** at foramen magnum ± **C2–C3 laminectomy** with **cervico-occipital fusion**

> [!note] Common radiographic "screeners"  
> Often quantified with skull base lines/angles (choose per institutional preference):
> 
> - **Chamberlain / McGregor** lines (dens migration)
> 
> - **McRae** line (basion–opisthion; "dens above FM" concept)
> 
> - **Wackenheim clivus line** (clivus–dens relationship)  
> These help objectify "basilar invagination/impression" and guide whether the problem is primarily **reducible vs irreducible** (traction response is clinically useful).
>

## 2) Atlantoaxial instability (AAI)

### Definition

- Anterior arch of atlas–dens interval (**ADI**) > **3 mm**

### Clinical risk
- Can cause **acute** (rare catastrophic deterioration) or **chronic spinal cord compression**
### Causes
- Weakness of structures maintaining stability
- Examples: **RA**, **mucopolysaccharidoses**, **Down syndrome**

### Workup
- **Dynamic X-ray** (flexion/extension)
### Management
- If **myelopathy**:
    - **Surgical arthrodesis**
        - **Posterior atlanto-axial fusion**

> [!tip] Practical framing  
> AAI is often a **dynamic disease**—a neutral MRI can understate severity. If symptoms are position-dependent, push for **dynamic evaluation**.

---
## 3) Occipitalization of atlas

- **Assimilation of atlas** (C1 fused to occiput)

> [!note] Occipitalization can reduce the "buffer" motion at O–C1 and shift stress to C1–2 → watch for coexisting **atlantoaxial instability** or basilar invagination patterns.

---

## 4) Dens hypoplasia, os odontoideum

### Core feature
- Abnormal mobility of dens with respect to C2
### Workup
- **Dynamic X-ray**
- **Kinematic MRI**
### Management
- If **myelopathy**:
    - **Surgical arthrodesis**
        - **Posterior atlanto-axial fusion**

> [!warning] Surgical pearl  
> Treat os odontoideum/dens hypoplasia as an **instability diagnosis** first; symptoms often track with **motion**, not just static stenosis.

---

## 5) Klippel–Feil anomaly

- Congenital fusion of cervical vertebrae
- Most complications arise from **co-existent craniocervical instability**

> [!tip] Targeted supplementation  
> "Fused segments" can create **hypermobile adjacent segments**—screen for CCJ instability and counsel on trauma risk.

---

## Quick "workup → decision" scaffold

> [!summary]
> 
> - Suspect **crowding/ventral compression** → CT (bony anatomy) + MRI (cord/brainstem) ± traction assessment
> 
> - Suspect **instability** (AAI, os odontoideum, syndromic) → **dynamic X-rays** ± kinematic MRI
> 
> - **Myelopathy or high-risk instability** → favor **stabilization (fusion)** ± decompression based on reducibility and compression pattern
>
