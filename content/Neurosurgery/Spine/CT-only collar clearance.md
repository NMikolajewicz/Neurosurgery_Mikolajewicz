> [!summary] CT-only collar clearance (obtunded adult blunt trauma)
> 
> - Use **high-quality CT** (≤3 mm slices, **multiplanar reconstructions/64-slice**) as the screening study.
>     
> - **EAST 2015 (GRADE SR):** _conditional_ recommendation to **remove collar after negative high‑quality CT alone**; **0% unstable injuries** reported after neg CT.
>     
> - **WTA 2019:** with normal high‑res CT, **NPV for unstable injury “approaches 100%”** → discontinue spine motion restriction.
>     
> - **Meta-analyses:** Panczykowski 2011 **Se/Sp >99.9%, NPV 100%** (unstable); Raza 2013 **NPV ~99.7%** (clinically significant).
>     
> - **Prospective data (Como 2011):** safe collar removal after neg CT when **gross motor movement all extremities**; **no routine MRI** needed.
>     
> - **MRI yield after normal CT:** mostly minor ligament/disk findings; **unstable injury essentially excluded** (Hogan 2005).
>     
> - **Do NOT “CT-only clear”** if **focal neuro deficit**, **abnormal CT**, or **inadequate CT quality** → **MRI/spine consult** (Inaba/WTA 2016 false-negatives tied to focal deficits).
>

## Evidence supporting CT-only collar clearance in the obtunded adult

### 1) Guideline-level evidence

**EAST Practice Management Guideline (2015; systematic review using GRADE)**

- **PICO question**: whether cervical collar removal in the obtunded adult blunt trauma patient should be done after negative high‑quality CT alone vs CT + adjunct imaging. 
- **Study design**: Systematic review; level 3 evidence
- **Eligibility criteria**: High-quality CT; axial slice thickness <3 mm.
- **Findings**: 0% incidence of unstable injuries after a negative high‑quality CT
- **Recommendation**: conditionally ==recommend collar removal after negative high‑quality CT alone==. 
- **Source**: Patel et al. 2015 ([East.org](https://www.east.org/Content/documents/practicemanagementguidelines/spinal_clearance_obtunded_pt_Patel_Feb_2015_JoT.pdf "TA142775 430..441")); 

**Western Trauma Association (WTA) Critical Decisions Algorithm (2019)**

- States high‑resolution CT (64-slice, <3 mm thickness, multiplanar reconstructions) is the standard radiographic evaluation.
- For the obtunded patient with a normal screening neck CT, WTA summarizes the evidence (including EAST) that the ==NPV for excluding an unstable injury "approaches 100%"== so spine motion restriction can be discontinued; MRI is reserved for specific indications (e.g., persistent neuro deficit).

---
### 2) Meta-analyses (large numbers; directly relevant)

**Panczykowski et al., J Neurosurg 2011 (meta-analysis; 17 studies, 14,327 patients)**

- ==CT sensitivity and specificity >99.9% and NPV 100% for unstable injury==
- CT alone is sufficient to detect unstable cervical spine injuries and adjuvant imaging is unnecessary when CT is negative. 
- Source: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21619408/ "Comparative effectiveness of using computed tomography alone to exclude cervical spine injuries in obtunded or intubated patients: meta-analysis of 14,327 patients with blunt trauma - PubMed")

**Raza et al., Injury 2013 (meta-analysis; 10 studies, 1,850 obtunded patients with normal CT)**

- Reported cumulative ==NPV 99.7% for clinically significant injury after negative CT==. PPV was 93.7%. 
- concluded normal CT is conclusive to rule out clinically significant injury and that additional imaging (MRI) should be case-by-case, not routine. 
- Source: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23856632/ "Safe cervical spine clearance in adult obtunded blunt trauma patients on the basis of a normal multidetector CT scan--a meta-analysis and cohort study - PubMed")

### 3) Prospective cohort evidence (obtunded patients)

**Como et al., J Trauma 2011 (prospective protocol study)**

- Evaluated a protocol to discontinue the collar in **obtunded blunt trauma** patients (with **gross movement of all extremities**) after **negative CT**.
- Conclusion: ==removal of spine precautions was safe/efficacious if gross movement in all extremities and CT was negative;== **supplemental MRI was not needed** in that patient population. 
	- translates to i) faster clearance time, ii) reduced pressures ulcers, iii) fewer MRIs and iv) reduced length of stay
- Source: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21307733/ "Computed tomography alone may clear the cervical spine in obtunded blunt trauma patients: a prospective evaluation of a revised protocol - PubMed")

### 4) MRI-after-normal-CT incremental yield for _unstable_ injury is very low

**Hogan et al., Radiology 2005** (366 obtunded/unreliable patients with _normal_ multidetector CT who then had MRI)

- **Question**: Is MR imaging required when CT findings are normal?
- **Finding**: CT had NPV of 98.9% (362 of 366 patients) for ligament injury and 100% (366 of 366 patients) for unstable cervical spine injury.
	- MRI found a small number of cord/ligament/disk findings, but **unstable injuries were excluded by CT**. 
- Source: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16183927/ "Exclusion of unstable cervical spine injury in obtunded patients with blunt trauma: is MR imaging needed when multi-detector row CT findings are normal? - PubMed")

---

### 5) Large multicenter CT accuracy trial

**Inaba et al., WTA multi-institutional prospective trial (2016; 10,276 enrolled)**

- For clinically significant injuries requiring surgery/halo/CTO, CT sensitivity **98.5%** and **NPV 99.97%**.
- The **false-negative CTs** that missed clinically significant injury all occurred in patients with **focal neurologic abnormalities** (central cord–type exam). ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5121083/ "Cervical Spinal Clearance: A Prospective Western Trauma Association Multi-Institutional Trial - PMC"))

---

### 6) Cost / harm considerations (why routine MRI is often avoided)

**JAMA Surgery 2018 cost-effectiveness analysis**

- MRI follow-up after a normal CT in obtunded blunt trauma had **higher cost** and **lower health benefit (QALYs)** than no follow-up, arguing against routine MRI solely for clearance after normal CT. ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamasurgery/fullarticle/2674878 "Cost-effectiveness of Magnetic Resonance Imaging in Cervical Clearance of Obtunded Blunt Trauma After a Normal Computed Tomographic Finding | Neurology | JAMA Surgery | JAMA Network"))

## Exceptions where MRI is  recommended 

Most CT-only clearance pathways still advise MRI (or spine consult) when:

- **Persistent focal neurologic deficit** with normal CT (possible SCI/central cord).
- **Abnormal CT** (malalignment, concerning soft tissue findings) or **inadequate CT quality** (motion, incomplete visualization).

Conclusion: **CT is sufficient for clearance in the obtunded adult blunt trauma patient when it is high quality and negative**, and MRI is **not required solely for clearance**, but remains **indicated when the clinical picture demands it**.
