> [!summary] Cervical facet dislocation review
> 
> - **Unilateral** facet dislocation: flexion-rotation; ~**25%** anterior translation; rotation signs common.
> 
> - **Bilateral** facet dislocation: flexion-distraction; ~**50%** translation; **highly unstable**; high **SCI** risk.
> 
> - **Best test:** CT C-spine (alignment + fractures); MRI for **disc/cord/ligaments**.
> 
> - Initial reduction attempt often **closed traction reduction** (e.g., Gardner-Wells tongs) with serial neuro checks/neuromonitoring.
> 
> - SMH pathway: **MRI en route → closed reduction in OR (light sedation)** → definitive **fusion**.
> 
> - **Failed** closed reduction: **disc herniation/ventral compression → anterior discectomy + reduction + fusion (ACDF)**.
> 
> - **Failed** closed reduction without ventral disc issue: **posterior decompression/reduction/fusion**.
> 
> - During posterior reduction: **ventral compression on intra-op U/S** or **neuromonitoring change** → **immediate ACDF**.
>  

## Definitions & Scope

- **Dx:** traumatic displacement due to **Facet joint|facet subluxation/dislocation** ("perched/locked/jumped facets"), usually with **PLC** disruption ± fracture.
- **Scope:** unilateral vs bilateral dislocation; fracture-dislocation variants; neurologically intact → complete [[Spinal cord injury (traumatic)]].
- **Core principle:** reduction restores alignment, but **instability persists** → definitive **arthrodesis/fusion** is typically required.

## Outline

### Pattern recognition

- **Unilateral dislocation:** flexion-rotation; rotational deformity; radiculopathy common.
- **Bilateral dislocation:** flexion-distraction; gross instability; myelopathy/SCI common.

| Feature             | Unilateral facet dislocation              | Bilateral facet dislocation       |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Mechanism           | Flexion-rotation                          | Flexion-distraction               |
| Typical translation | ~**25%**                                  | ~**50%** (or more)                |
| Rotation            | Common                                    | Less prominent                    |
| Neuro pattern       | Radiculopathy common; myelopathy variable | High risk myelopathy/SCI          |
| Stability           | Unstable                                  | **Highly unstable**               |
| Definitive Tx       | Fusion (A/P/combined)                     | Fusion (often combined if severe) |
### Workup priorities

- **Best test:** CT C-spine.
- **Key imaging:** degree of translation (≈ **25%** vs **50%**), locked facets, associated fractures.
- **Rule-out:** Vertebral artery injury (consider CTA neck).

### Treatment roadmap

- **Tx:** attempt **closed reduction** when appropriate.
- **Tx:** if closed reduction fails/contraindicated → **open reduction** (anterior vs posterior guided by **ventral disc compression**).
- **Tx:** definitive **fusion** (anterior/posterior/combined) after reduction.

> [!Decision making]
> 
> - **Translation clue:** unilateral ≈ **25%**; bilateral ≈ **50%** on lateral imaging.
> 
> - **Branch point:** **ventral disc herniation/compression** drives **anterior decompression (ACDF)** priority.
> 
> - **Reduced ≠ stable:** plan **arthrodesis** after any reduction.
>  

## Diagnosis & Workup

### Clinical

- **Dx:** suspect after flexion/rotation trauma + neck pain/deformity ± neuro deficit; confirm with imaging.
- **Red flag:** **progressive neurologic deficit**, respiratory compromise, high-grade translation, hemodynamic instability from SCI.

### Imaging

- **Best test:** CT C-spine (fractures + alignment).
- **Best test (soft tissue):** MRI C-spine (disc/cord/ligaments); SMH slides specify **Sag T2** obtained en route to OR.
- **Key imaging:** perched/jumped facets, translation, rotational malalignment, ventral disc material, cord signal change.
- **Rule-out:** Vertebral artery injury with CTA neck when high-risk injury pattern present.

| Modality     | Best for                | Key imaging                                         | Pitfall                                             |
| ------------ | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| CT           | CT]] C-spine            | **Bony** injury + alignment                         | Perched/jumped facets, facet fractures, translation |
| MRI (sag T2) | **Disc/ligaments/cord** | Ventral disc herniation, cord edema, PLC disruption | Logistics may delay reduction (protocol-dependent)  |
| CTA neck     | Vascular screening      | Dissection/occlusion                                | Use per risk criteria (institutional)               |

| Classic sign                   | Modality/view | Meaning                                           |
| ------------------------------ | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Reverse hamburger bun sign** | Axial CT      | Unilateral facet dislocation (facet malalignment) |
| **Naked facet sign**           | CT            | Facet articulation not apposed (dislocation)      |
| **Bow-tie sign**               | Lateral XR    | Unilateral facet dislocation/rotation             |
| Widened interspinous distance  | XR/CT         | Posterior ligamentous injury                      |
## Management

### Immediate priorities

- **Tx:** rigid immobilization, analgesia, spinal precautions, early neurosurgery/trauma coordination.
- **Timing:** **urgent** reduction pathway when neuro deficit or gross instability.
- **Pitfall:** deep sedation/paralysis can obscure evolving neuro deficit during reduction.

### Reduction approaches 

- Closed reduction** using Gardner-Wells tongs.
- Open reduction**
    - **Anterior:** reduction assisted by Caspar pins + ACDF.
    - **Posterior:** reduction assisted by **towel clips** ± decompression + internal fixation.

| Intra-op finding during U/S-guided posterior reduction                            | Action                                |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Significant ventral disc compression on U/S** **OR** **neuromonitoring change** | **Immediate ACDF**                    |
| No significant ventral compression + stable monitoring                            | **± non-emergent ACDF** (arthrodesis) |
### Closed reduction: indications & precautions 

- **Indication:** acute facet dislocation requiring rapid realignment (especially with neuro deficit).
- **Contraindication:** inability to reliably monitor neurologic status during reduction, or imaging evidence of major ventral disc compression requiring anterior decompression first.
- **Tx:** use serial neuro exams if awake/light sedation; consider neuromonitoring in OR.

#### Failed closed reduction

| Scenario after **failed closed reduction**        | Suggested next step                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Disc herniation / ventral compression present** | **Open anterior discectomy + reduction + fusion (ACDF)** ± posterior fixation |
| **No disc herniation / ventral compression**      | **Open posterior decompression/reduction/fusion**                             |
### Institutional algorithm (SMH)

> [!checklist] Decision pathway (MRI → closed reduction → definitive fusion)
> 
> - **MRI obtained on the way to OR (Sag T2)**.
> 
> - **Closed reduction in OR** with **light sedation**.
> 
> - If **successful closed reduction** → **Anterior**, **Posterior**, or **Combined fusion**.
> 
> - If **failed closed reduction**:
> 
>     - If **disc herniation/ventral compression present** → **open anterior discectomy + reduction + fusion (ACDF)** ± posterior fixation.
> 
>     - If **no disc herniation/ventral compression** → **open posterior decompression + reduction + fusion**.
> 
> - During **ultrasound-guided posterior open reduction** (decompression + internal fixation):
> 
>     - If **significant ventral disc compression on intra-op U/S** or **neuromonitoring change** → **immediate ACDF**.
> 
>     - If **no significant ventral compression** and **stable neuromonitoring** → **± non-emergent ACDF** (arthrodesis).
>  

| Definitive stabilization | Indication                                                                 | Pros                                                        | Cons/Pitfalls                                                       |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Anterior** (ACDF)      | Ventral disc herniation; need anterior decompression; reducible injury     | Direct ventral decompression; restores disc height/lordosis |                                                                     |
| **Posterior fusion**     | No ventral disc issue; powerful reduction needed; posterior element injury | Strong reduction + stabilization                            | Risk if occult ventral disc herniation; consider MRI/U/S/monitoring |
| **Combined (360°)**      | Maximal instability; failed single approach; multilevel                    | Highest stability                                           | More extensive surgery; positioning/operative time                  |

> [!tip]
> 
> - MRI-before-reduction vs immediate reduction is **protocol-dependent**; exam focus = recognize **disc herniation risk** and protect the neurologic exam/monitoring (VERIFY local standard).
>  

## Complications

| Complication                             | When to suspect                                   | Prevention/notes                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Neurologic deterioration**             | New deficit during/after reduction                | Protect neuro exam; use neuromonitoring; address ventral disc compression           |
| [[Vertebral artery injury]]              | Posterior circulation symptoms; high-risk pattern | Screen with CTA per protocol; coordinate antithrombotic plan (institution-specific) |
| Dysphagia/hoarseness (anterior approach) | Post-op swallowing/voice change                   | Technique-dependent; counsel/monitor                                                |
| Nonunion/hardware failure                | Persistent pain, loss of alignment                | Adequate fixation/biology; follow-up imaging                                        |

## Pitfalls & Red Flags

> [!warning]
> 
> - **Pitfall:** missed **ventral disc herniation** before/during posterior reduction → neurologic worsening risk.
> 
> - **Red flag:** **neuromonitoring change** during reduction → stop/reassess; consider **immediate ACDF** per algorithm.
> 
> - **Pitfall:** "reduced = fixed" mindset → inadequate stabilization and recurrent instability.
> 
> - **Red flag:** delayed recognition of vertebral artery injury → posterior circulation stroke risk.
> 
> - **Red flag:** airway/ventilation risk with high cervical injury or significant SCI → anesthesia/ICU planning is critical.
>
