> [!summary] **C1 Fracture Essentials**  
> • C1 fractures are classified by **Landells**: Type I (isolated arch), Type II (Jefferson burst), Type III (unilateral lateral mass).  
> • **Stability hinges on the transverse atlantal ligament (TAL)**—evaluate with ADI and lateral mass displacement.  
> • **Instability markers:** **ADI >5 mm** or **C1 lateral mass displacement >7 mm** (Rule of Spence).  
> • **Dickman I (intrasubstance tear)** → requires **C1–C2 fusion**; **Dickman II (bony avulsion)** → halo vest.  
> • **Stable injuries** (intact TAL) → rigid collar 8–12 weeks; **unstable injuries** → halo or fusion depending on TAL integrity.  
> • MRI is key for differentiating TAL tear type and assessing associated ligamentous injury.  
> • Fusion technique of choice: **Goel–Harms C1 LM + C2 pedicle screws**; transarticular screws used selectively.

## **1. Classification — _Landells Atlas Fractures_**

### **Type I — Isolated Arch Fracture (Anterior or Posterior)**

- **Anterior OR posterior** arch fracture.
- **Most common** C1 fracture pattern.
- ==**Plough fracture==:** anterior arch fracture from odontoid being driven forward into the C1 ring.
- **Stable injury.**
#### **Management**
- **Management:** hard collar

![[Neurosurgery/Spine/_resources/C1atlas_fractures.resources/image.1.png|500]]

---

### **Type II — Jefferson Burst Fracture**

- **Bilateral fractures** of anterior AND posterior arches.
- Classic mechanism: ==**axial load** ==(e.g., diving injury).
- **Key determinant:** transverse ligament integrity (see Dickman classification).

#### **Management**
- **TAL intact:** hard collar.
- **TAL ruptured:**
    - **Bony avulsion (Dickman II):** halo vest.
    - **Intrasubstance tear (Dickman I):** C1–C2 fusion.

![[Neurosurgery/Spine/_resources/C1atlas_fractures.resources/image.2.png|300]]

---
### **Type III — Unilateral Lateral Mass Fracture**

- Involves a ==**single C1 lateral mass**==.
- Mechanisms include axial load + rotation.
- Stability again depends on TAL.
#### **Management**
- **Stable:** hard collar.
- **Unstable:** halo vest (or fusion depending on TAL integrity).

## **2. Dickman Classification — Transverse Atlantal Ligament (TAL) Injuries**

> [!tip] **Critical for determining stability and treatment.**  
> MRI is the definitive modality for classification.

### **Type I — Intrasubstance Tear**

- Complete fiber disruption without bony involvement.
- ==**Highly unstable.**==
- **Management:** C1–C2 fusion (primary).

### **Type II — Bony Avulsion**

- TAL pulls off a fragment of the C1 lateral mass.
- More favorable healing potential.
- **Management:** halo vest or rigid external immobilization.

---

## **3. Imaging Findings (Assessing Stability)**

> [!important] **Two measurements strongly predict TAL rupture:** ADI + Spence

### **Atlantodental Interval (ADI)**

- **ADI > 5 mm** → TAL rupture.
- Measured on lateral x-ray or midsagittal CT.  
    ![[Neurosurgery/Spine/_resources/C1atlas_fractures.resources/image.5.png]]

### **Rule of Spence**

- Sum of **C1 lateral mass displacement > 7 mm** (open-mouth view) → TAL disruption.  
    ![[Neurosurgery/Spine/_resources/C1atlas_fractures.resources/image.6.png|300]]

### **MRI**

- Defines fiber integrity and distinguishes:
    - **Type I (intrasubstance tear)** vs
    - **Type II (bony avulsion)**
- Also evaluates interspinous ligaments, alar ligaments, and ventral soft tissues.

## **4. Management Algorithm**

> [!workflow] **Stepwise Approach**
### **Step 1 — Determine Morphology**
==Landells Type I vs II vs III.==
- Type 1: isolated arch
- Type 2: Jefferson
- Type 3: unilateral lateral mass fracture
### **Step 2 — Assess Stability**
- ==ADI== (>5 mm = unstable)
- Lateral mass displacement (>7 mm = unstable)
- ==MRI== TAL evaluation (Dickman type)
### **Step 3 — Choose Treatment**

#### **Stable Injuries**
- Type I, Type II/III **with intact TAL**  
    → **Rigid collar** for 8–12 weeks  
    → Serial imaging
#### **Unstable Injuries**
- TAL bony avulsion (**Dickman II**)  
    → **Halo vest** (8–12 weeks)
- TAL intrasubstance tear (**Dickman I**)  
    → **C1–C2 posterior fusion**
#### **Additional Indications for Fusion**

- Irreducible displacement
- Progressive ADI/lateral mass widening
- Associated C2 fractures limiting halo application
- Neurological deficit (rare in isolated C1 fractures)

---
## **5. C1–C2 Fusion Pearls (Surgical)**

### **Construct Options**
- **Goel–Harms C1 lateral mass + C2 pedicle screws**
    - Most stable construct; modern standard.
- **Transarticular screws (Magerl)**
    - Require precise alignment; not feasible in all patients.
- **Occiput–C2 fusion**
    - Reserved for complex craniocervical injuries or non-reconstructible C1.

### **Key Technical Points**
- Protect **C2 nerve root** when exposing the C1 lateral mass.
- C2 pedicle screws require careful trajectory planning due to vertebral artery groove.
- Aim for **anatomic reduction** before fixation.
- Ensure **adequate bone decortication** between C1–C2 for fusion.
- Postoperative rigid collar often used for comfort, not stability.
