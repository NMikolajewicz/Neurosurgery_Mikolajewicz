> [!summary] Sagittal Balance Review
> 
> - **Adult spinal deformity (ASD)** includes **scoliosis + sagittal imbalance**; sagittal balance correlates with **quality-of-life** measures.
> 
> - Core radiographic inputs for sagittal balance: **LL**, **PI**, **PT**, ± **SVA**.
> 
> - Alignment targets: **LL = PI ± 9°**, **PT < 20°**, **SVA < 5 cm**.
> 
> - **SRS–Schwab sagittal modifiers:** **PI–LL <10° / 10–20° / >20°**.
> 
> - **SRS–Schwab sagittal modifiers:** **SVA <4 cm / 4–9.5 cm / >9.5 cm** and **PT <20° / 20–30° / >30°**.
> 
> - **Coronal imbalance:** **C7 plumb line >4 cm** from **CSVL** on standing AP scoliosis film.
> 
> - Management options (ASD/sagittal imbalance): **observation**, **focal decompression**, or **surgical correction** (MIS / hybrid / open).
> 
> - **ACR** may increase **LL up to 12°/level** and improve **SVA up to 3 cm**; **do not proceed** if **great vessels tightly approximated** to vertebral bodies/osteophytes.
> 
> - **Smith–Petersen osteotomy (SPO)** can increase **lordosis up to 10°/level** (~**1° per mm** bone resection).
>  

## Definitions & Scope

- Scope: ==adult spinal deformity== (ASD) encompasses coronal plane deformity (==scoliosis==) and sagittal plane deformity (==sagittal imbalance==).
- Why it matters: sagittal balance correlates with quality-of-life measures.
- Core sagittal balance metrics: 
	- lumbar lordosis (LL)
	- pelvic incidence (PI)
	- pelvic tilt (PT)
	- ± sagittal vertical axis (SVA).
- Key alignment objectives (adult): LL = PI ± 9°, PT < 20°, SVA < 5 cm.
- ==Spinopelvic== parameters
    - Pelvic incidence (PI): fixed once skeletal maturity is reached
	    - used to set lordosis target (LL ≈ PI ± 9°).
    - Pelvic tilt (PT): pelvic retroversion/compensation parameter 
	    - angle between vertical and line from femoral head axis to midpoint of S1 endplate
    - Sacral slope (SS): angle between horizontal reference and S1 endplate.
- ==Spinal curvature== parameters 
    - Thoracic kyphosis (TK): angle between top of T4 and bottom of T12
	    - ~41° ± 12°.
    - Lumbar lordosis (LL): standard measurement uses L1 superior endplate to S1 superior endplate 
- ==Global alignment ==parameters
    - Sagittal vertical axis (SVA): horizontal offset of C7 plumb line relative to the sacrum/pelvis 
	    - positive if C7 plumb line anterior
    - PI–LL mismatch: PI − LL
	    - "pelvic harmony" target is PI–LL < 10°.

## Overview

- Core concepts
    - ASD includes scoliosis + sagittal imbalance
    - sagittal alignment predicts disability.
- What to measure (standing radiographs)
    - LL, PI, PT, ± SVA (minimum set).
    - Coronal balance (C7 plumb line vs CSVL).
- Targets to restore
    - LL = PI ± 9°
    - PT < 20°
    - SVA < 5 cm.
- Classification: SRS–Schwab: coronal curve type + sagittal modifiers (PI–LL, SVA, PT).
- Management framework
    - Options: observation, focal decompression, deformity correction (MIS / hybrid / open).
- Lordosis restoration tools
    - ACR and SPO are explicit lordosis-increasing options
    - vascular risk is a key pitfall.

## Diagnosis & Workup

### Clinical

- Dx: symptomatic sagittal imbalance in the setting of ASD/degenerative scoliosis 
	- common presentation = posture-related disability + back pain ± radicular symptoms.
- Rule-out: ==non-spinal drivers of apparent imbalance ==
	- hip flexion contracture, fixed knee flexion, severe hip OA 
- Red flag: 
	- progressive myelopathy
	- cauda equina features
	- rapidly progressive deformity
	- constitutional symptoms (malignancy/infection).

### Imaging

- Best test: 
	- ==standing full-length scoliosis radiographs== (PA + lateral) including pelvis/femoral heads for spinopelvic measurements 
	- apply SRS–Schwab modifiers to ==quantify sagittal deformity severity== (PI–LL, SVA, PT cutoffs).
	- MRI (lumbar stenosis/radiculopathy; thoracic/cervical cord compression)
- Key imaging: 
	- calculate/record LL, PI, PT, ± SVA and compare with targets (LL = PI ± 9°, PT < 20°, SVA < 5 cm).
	- evaluate coronal balance using C7 plumb line vs CSVL
		- >4 cm offset indicates coronal imbalance.
- 

> [!tip]
> 
> - **Always** interpret sagittal imbalance in the context of **compensation**: ↑ **PT** (pelvic retroversion), hip extension, and knee flexion can "mask" true spinal malalignment 
>  

## Management

### Strategy

- Manage based on symptoms, neurologic findings, radiographic severity, and goals of alignment correction.
    - **Options:** **observation**, **focal decompression**, **surgical correction of deformity** (MIS / hybrid / open).
- Alignment goals: aim for **LL = PI ± 9°**, **PT < 20°**, **SVA < 5 cm** when performing deformity correction.

> [!checklist] Approach to sagittal imbalance in ASD
> 
> - Confirm symptom driver: posture-related disability vs neurogenic claudication/radiculopathy vs myelopathy.
> 
> - Obtain standing full-length PA/lateral radiographs and **measure LL, PI, PT, ± SVA**.
> 
> - Quantify severity using **SRS–Schwab sagittal modifiers** (PI–LL, SVA, PT).
> 
> - If predominant stenosis symptoms with limited deformity: consider **focal decompression** (± limited fusion) (VERIFY for case selection).
> 
> - If significant sagittal malalignment + disability: plan **deformity correction** to meet alignment targets (LL/PI/PT/SVA).  
> ^algo-sagittal-balance-approach
>  

### Lordosis restoration techniques 

- ==Anterior column release== (ACR) (often combined with cage placement + posterior compression)
    - Indication: sagittal imbalance requiring lordosis restoration 
    - Expected correction: LL up to 12° per ACR level; SVA improvement up to 3 cm (level-dependent).
    - Contraindication: great vessels tightly approximated to vertebral bodies or osteophytes at planned level.
    - Pitfall: direct/indirect great vessel injury (ALL cut or anterior column lengthening).
- ==Smith–Petersen osteotomy== (SPO) (aka chevron/extension osteotomy)
    - Expected correction: lordosis up to 10° per level; ~1° per mm of bone resected.

| Technique                          | Expected lordosis / alignment change                                             | Key risk / contraindication                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Anterior column release (ACR)**  | **LL up to 12° per level**; <br>**SVA improvement up to 3 cm** (level-dependent) | **Great vessel injury** risk<br>**Contraindication:** vessels tightly approximated to bodies/osteophytes on axial imaging/angiogram |
| **Smith–Petersen osteotomy (SPO)** | **Up to 10° per level**; <br>~**1° per mm** bone resection                       | Overcorrection/instability if poorly selected,<br>general osteotomy risks                                                           |
### Operative planning 

- Choose approach (MIS/hybrid/open) to achieve **alignment targets** while addressing neural compression as needed.
- **Complication:** 
	- residual **PI–LL mismatch**, persistent ↑ **PT**, or persistent ↑ **SVA** → ongoing disability and revision risk 
	- junctional failure (e.g., **proximal junctional kyphosis**) 


## Pitfalls & Red Flags

> [!warning]
> 
> - **Pitfall:** 
> 	- planning deformity correction without documenting **LL, PI, PT, ± SVA** (minimum radiographic dataset).
> 	- ignoring compensation (↑ **PT**, hip/knee strategies) → underestimates deformity severity.
> 	- focusing on coronal plane only; sagittal malalignment is more tightly linked to disability.
> 	- proceeding with **ACR** without confirming safe **great vessel** clearance on axial imaging/angiogram; **do not proceed** if vessels are **tightly approximated**.
> 
> - **Red flag:** 
> 	- new/progressive **myelopathy**, rapidly progressive neurologic deficit, bowel/bladder dysfunction (urgent workup).
> 	- fever, weight loss, night pain, immunosuppression → evaluate for infection/malignancy.
>
