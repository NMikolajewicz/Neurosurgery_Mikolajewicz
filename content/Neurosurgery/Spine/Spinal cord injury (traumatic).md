> [!exam] High-yield
> 
> - **ATLS first** + maintain **spinal precautions/immobilization** until stability confirmed/restored.
> 
> - Document baseline **ISNCSCI/ASIA** **early** (motor/sensory + rectal exam) and **explicitly confirm sacral sparing** (**S4–5**, **DAP**, **VAC**); Early exam can be unreliable in **spinal shock** → **repeat ≥72 h**.
> 
> - Imaging: **CT** for fractures/alignment; **MRI** for cord/ligament/disc/EDH (**STIR** for PLC/disc edema/disruption). Consider **CTA** for selected cervical injury patterns (vertebral artery risk).
> 
> - Prevent secondary injury: avoid **hypoxia** and **hypotension** (avoid **SBP <90**).
> 
> - Hemodynamics: target **MAP 75–80 to 90–95 mmHg for 3–7 days** (AO Spine/Praxis 2024 _suggested_). Know historical target **MAP 85–90 x 7 days** (AANS/CNS 2013).
> 
> - **Neurogenic shock** (often **≥T6**): **hypotension + bradycardia + warm extremities** → **norepinephrine preferred**.
> 
> - **Early decompression ≤24 h** is endorsed (AO Spine/Praxis 2024: _recommended_ option).
> 
> - Start **VTE prophylaxis** as soon as safe; **ICU monitoring** for high cervical and/or complete injuries (respiratory + hemodynamic risk).
> 
> - **MPSS**: not routine; possible small benefit only if **started ≤8 h**; weigh against complications; if used, **24 h** infusion only (avoid 48 h).
>

## Definitions and time course

- **SCI**: spinal cord damage causing temporary or permanent motor/sensory/autonomic dysfunction.
- **Traumatic** vs **non-traumatic** etiologies
- **"Time is spine"**: progressive loss of potentially salvageable tissue in the first hours → urgency in diagnosis and neuroprotection.

**tSCI phases (temporal)**

|Phase|Time window|
|---|---|
|**Acute**|**<48 h**|
|**Subacute**|**48 h–14 d**|
|**Intermediate**|**14 d–6 mo**|
|**Chronic**|**>6 mo**|

## Epidemiology and outcomes

### Incidence (traumatic SCI)

- North America: ~39/million
- Australia: ~16/million
- Western Europe: ~15/million
### Demographics / mechanism

- Sex: ==male ~80%==
- Age: ==bimodal== (peak 15–29, rising peak >50)
- Mechanism (NA, ~2010–2014): ==MVC ~38%, falls ~31%==, sports ~10–17%
- Level distribution: ==cervical ~60%==, thoracic ~32%, lumbosacral ~9%
### Mortality and life expectancy 

- Acute in-hospital mortality: ~4–17%
- Post-discharge annual mortality: year 1 ~3.8%, year 2 ~1.6%, then ~1.2%/yr
- Example life expectancy if injured at age 40:
    - C5–C8: ~23 years
    - C1–C4: ~20 years
    - Ventilator-dependent: ~8.5 years

## Pathophysiology 

### Primary injury (seconds–minutes)

- **Mechanical**: contusion/compression/dislocation/transection
- **Vascular**: ischemia, hemorrhage, disruption of microcirculation
- **Blood–spinal cord barrier** disruption
### Secondary injury (minutes–days → beyond)

- **Inflammation**: early cytokines (e.g., TNF, IL‑1β), inflammatory cell infiltration persisting into subacute phase
- **Excitotoxicity**: glutamate → NMDA/AMPA overactivation → Ca²⁺ influx → cell death
- **Oxidative stress**: free radicals → lipid/DNA/protein injury
- **Ionic dysregulation**: Na⁺/Ca²⁺ influx, edema, apoptosis/necrosis cascades
### Chronic lesion architecture (weeks–months)

- **Cystic cavitation** (ex vacuo) → physical barrier to axonal regrowth
- **Glial scar**: inhibitory ECM (e.g., CSPGs, tenascin, NG2) but also protective (contains/limits spread)
- Myelin-associated inhibitors (**Nogo‑A**, **OMgp**, **MAG**) → **RHOA/ROCK** signaling → growth cone collapse

> [!exam] Clinical translation
> 
> - Acute care priorities (oxygenation, perfusion, decompression) target **secondary injury** and **spinal cord perfusion**.
> 
> - Late-phase disability relates to **scar/cavitation** + inhibitory signaling (basis for investigational therapies).
>

## Clinical manifestations and exam pearls

### Key pearls

- **Fracture level ≠ neurologic level** (especially mid–lower thoracic): vertebral level can mislead segmental cord level.
- **Respiratory compromise by level**
    - **Above C5**: diaphragm risk (phrenic)
    - **Above T11**: intercostal weakness
    - **Above L1**: abdominal muscle weakness (cough/expiratory force)
- **Sympathetic outflow**: T1–L2 preganglionic neurons
    - High lesions → loss of vasomotor tone → hypotension/temperature dysregulation.

---
## Spinal shock vs neurogenic shock (do not conflate)

> [!exam] Shock distinction
> 
> - **Spinal shock** = **transient areflexia/flaccidity** + loss of spinal reflexes (motor/sensory/autonomic) below lesion → early neuro exam may be unreliable; evolves through phases to hyperreflexia.
> 
> - **Neurogenic shock** = **distributive shock** from loss of sympathetic tone (classically **≥T6**) → **hypotension + bradycardia**, wide pulse pressure, warm extremities.
>  
### Bulbocavernosus reflex (BCR) 

- **Absent** in **spinal shock** (early areflexia).
- Helps interpret timing/phase; not a standalone prognostic test.

## Diagnosis and imaging

### ED / trauma bay essentials

- **ATLS** + maintain **spinal precautions**.
- If SCI suspected: **gross motor/sensory exam** of limbs **+ rectal exam** (perineal sensation, **DAP**, **VAC**) and document findings clearly.

### Imaging modalities 

- **CT**: first-line for **bony injury**, alignment, canal compromise; often replaces plain films.
- **MRI**: best for **cord**, **roots**, **ligaments/PLC**, **disc**, **epidural hematoma (EDH)**; guides decompression levels.
    - **STIR (T2)**: sensitive for ligament/disc edema/disruption.
    - Prognostic features: edema vs hemorrhage vs transection (interpret clinically).

> [!exam] "MRI changes management"
> 
> - In thoracolumbar trauma, MRI can change management in ~**25%** (commonly by clarifying **PLC**, **EDH**, instability, or decompression targets).
>  

### CTA

- Consider **CTA neck** in cervical injury patterns with vertebral artery risk (institutional criteria vary).
### Cervical spine clearance in the obtunded adult

- A ==negative high-quality fine-cut CT is generally considered sufficient to remove the collar==; MRI is not required solely for clearance in that context.

---
## Cervical facet dislocation: reduction timing and MRI 

- MRI pre-reduction can identify **disc herniation**, but may **delay decompression**.
- Practical guideline framing:
    - MRI before **open reduction**.
    - MRI before **closed reduction** if **unconscious/uncooperative**.
    - If significant disc herniation → favor **anterior discectomy** (and then reduction/fixation as indicated).

## Neurological classification: ISNCSCI / ASIA

### What to document (minimum)

- **Motor** (key myotomes; MRC grading)
- **Sensory**: light touch + pinprick in **28 dermatomes (C2–S4/5)**
- **Sacral exam**:
    - **S4–5** sensation
    - **DAP** (deep anal pressure)
    - **VAC** (voluntary anal contraction)
- Determine:
    - **Motor level**, **sensory level**, **NLI** (neurological level of injury)
    - **AIS grade**
    - **ZPP** (zone of partial preservation) **only if AIS A**

> [!exam] Sacral sparing = "incomplete"
> 
> - Any of: **S4–5 sensation**, **DAP**, and/or **VAC** = **sacral sparing** → injury is **incomplete** (AIS B/C/D).
>  

### AIS (ASIA Impairment Scale) — exam table

|AIS|Definition (high-yield)|
|---|---|
|**A**|**Complete**: no sensory/motor below NLI **including no sacral function**|
|**B**|**Sensory incomplete**: sensory preserved below NLI **including S4–5 (and/or DAP)**, **no motor** meeting motor-incomplete criteria|
|**C**|**Motor incomplete**: **VAC present** OR motor sparing >3 levels below motor level; **>50%** key muscles below NLI are **<3/5**|
|**D**|**Motor incomplete**: **≥50%** key muscles below NLI are **≥3/5**|
|**E**|Normal exam (prior deficits suspected SCI)|

### Stepwise AIS assignment (fast + reliable)

1. **Sacral sparing?**
    - If **none** → **AIS A** (then record **ZPP**).
        
2. If sacral sparing present: **motor incomplete?**
    - **VAC present** OR motor function >3 levels below motor level.
        
3. If motor incomplete: determine **C vs D** using the **≥50%** key muscles **≥3/5** rule.

> [!tip]- Worked AIS examples
> 
> - **AIS A + ZPP**: no S4–5/DAP/VAC; patchy LT/PP or trace motor below NLI → AIS A; record most caudal sensory/motor levels with any function as **ZPP**.
> 
> - **AIS B (DAP-only)**: absent S4–5 LT/PP, **DAP present**, VAC absent, no qualifying motor sparing → AIS B.
> 
> - **AIS C vs D**: motor incomplete; count key muscles below NLI that are **≥3/5**: **<50%** → C; **≥50%** → D.
>

## Classic SCI syndromes (pattern recognition)

|Syndrome|Typical context|Key findings|
|---|---|---|
|**Central cord**|Older + cervical stenosis; hyperextension fall|**UE weakness > LE**, variable sensory loss, ± bladder dysfunction|
|**Brown-Séquard**|Penetrating / hemicord injury|**Ipsilateral** motor + dorsal column loss; **contralateral** pain/temp loss|
|**Anterior cord**|Often vascular; rarely isolated traumatic|Motor loss + pain/temp loss; dorsal columns spared|
|**Posterior cord**|Rare (vascular > traumatic)|Dorsal column loss; motor + pain/temp preserved|

## Acute management (prehospital → ICU → OR)

### 1) Prevent secondary injury (the "big three")

- Oxygenation: avoid hypoxia (commonly target ==SpO₂ ≥90%== or institutional target).
- Perfusion: avoid hypotension; support ==MAP > 85==.
- Decompression/stabilization: treat mechanical compression/instability early when indicated.

### 2) Immobilization (do it early; stop when safe)

- Maintain spinal precautions initially.
- Avoid prolonged time on rigid spine boards → ↑ pressure injury risk.

> [!warning] Immobilize ≠ leave on board indefinitely
> 
> - Maintain precautions until spine is cleared/stabilized, but minimize prolonged rigid board exposure once safe.
>  

---
### 3) Hemodynamics and perfusion targets

**Concept**

- ==SCPP ≈ MAP − intraspinal pressure== (MAP used clinically as a surrogate).

**Targets to know**

- Avoid **SBP <90 mmHg**.
- **AO Spine/Praxis (2024)**: **MAP 75–80 to 90–95 mmHg for 3–7 days** (_suggested_).
- **Historical (AANS/CNS 2013)**: **MAP 85–90 mmHg for 7 days**.

> [!exam] Hemodynamics
> 
> - "Avoid hypotension (SBP <90) and target MAP augmentation. Contemporary guidance suggests a **range-based MAP 75–80 to 90–95 for 3–7 days**, while older guidance targeted **85–90 for 7 days**. I individualize based on polytrauma/bleeding risk and monitor for pressor complications."
>  

**Neurogenic shock (often ≥T6)**

- Features: **hypotension + bradycardia**, warm peripheries, wide pulse pressure.
- **Vasopressor choice**:
    - **Norepinephrine** preferred (α vasoconstriction + β₁ chronotropy).
    - **Phenylephrine** (pure α) can worsen bradycardia via reflex mechanisms.

**Practical constraints**

- In polytrauma, uncontrolled hemorrhage takes priority over MAP goals.

---
### 4) ICU monitoring: who needs it?

- Strongly consider ICU for:
    - **High cervical injuries**
    - **Complete injuries**
    - Any patient requiring vasoactive support/ventilation
- Continuous **cardiac**, **hemodynamic**, and **respiratory** monitoring.

---
### 5) VTE prophylaxis

- Initiate **DVT prophylaxis as soon as safe** (mechanical early; pharmacologic when bleeding risk acceptable).
- Recognize higher risk with immobility + delayed fixation.

---
### 6) Steroids (MPSS): controversial and time-dependent

**Bottom line**
- Not standard of care; no clear overall benefit signal.
- Possible modest benefit only when started **≤8 hours**; increased infection/complication risk.

> [!warning] MPSS documentation requirement (if used)
> 
> - Document **time from injury (≤8 h)**, shared decision rationale, and why benefits outweigh risks (comorbidity/infection risk).
>  

> [!exam]- MPSS dosing (high-yield)
> 
> - **30 mg/kg IV bolus** over 15 min
> 
> - Wait 45 min
> 
> - **5.4 mg/kg/h** infusion for **23 h**
> 
> - **Do not** extend to 48 h (harm/complications signal; insufficient benefit evidence)
>  

---

### 7) Surgery: goals, timing, and what "good decompression" means

**Goals**
- **Realign**
- **Stabilize**
- **Decompress**  
    Often: reduction + decompression + instrumented fusion (approach depends on injury pattern).

**Timing**
- **≤24 hours**: endorsed/standard in guideline framing (AO Spine/Praxis 2024: _recommended_ option), unless contraindicated.
- Very early (≤8–12 h): potential additional benefit in some cohorts (evidence evolving).

**Adequate decompression
- Outcome depends on **quality/adequacy** of decompression, not just timing.
- Practical definition: **patent subarachnoid space with circumferential CSF around the cord on post-op MRI**.
- Tools: intra-op ultrasound; post-op MRI where feasible.
- If clearly inadequate decompression + persistent compression → consider **return to OR**.

**Unstable spine without neuro deficit**
- Early fixation (often **within 24 h**) is associated with fewer systemic complications (e.g., VTE/PE, pulmonary complications) vs delayed fixation.

## Prognosis and prediction

### Recovery timeline

- Most neurological recovery occurs in the **first 6 months**, but can continue (slower) for years.

### Directionality of outcomes (safe exam framing)

- Better prognosis with: **less severe initial AIS**, **lower level**, **younger age**, less multisystem trauma, lower-energy mechanism.
- **AIS A** has the lowest chance of meaningful motor recovery; conversion from clinically complete to incomplete is uncommon.

### Ambulation: reconcile endpoints

- Ambulation probabilities vary by cohort and definition (**any walking** vs **independent community ambulation**).
    - In general: **AIS A ≪ AIS B ≪ AIS C < AIS D** for walking potential.
### Prediction tools (name + inputs)

- **van Middendorp rule** (ambulation prediction from acute exam; strong discrimination in derivation/validation):
    - Predictors: **Age** (<65 vs ≥65), **L3 motor**, **S1 motor**, **L3 light touch**, **S1 light touch**.
    - Reference: PMID **21377202**.
- **Wilson model**: age + exam + MRI features to predict functional independence (recognize concept).

## Spine trauma classification systems

### TLICS (thoracolumbar)

- Inputs: **morphology** + **PLC integrity** + **neurologic status**.
- Treatment implication:

|TLICS score|Suggested management|
|---|---|
|**≤3**|**Non-operative**|
|**4**|**Indeterminate**|
|**≥5**|**Operative**|
### AO Spine thoracolumbar classification (pattern language)

- **Type A (compression)**
    - **A1**: single endplate, no posterior wall
    - **A2**: split fracture (both endplates)
    - **A3**: one endplate + posterior wall
    - **A4**: both endplates + posterior wall (complete burst)
- **Type B (tension band)**
    - **B1**: osseous posterior tension band (bony Chance)
    - **B2**: ligamentous posterior tension band injury
    - **B3**: hyperextension (often DISH/AS)
- **Type C**: translation/displacement (any plane)

**Neurologic status + modifiers**

- **N0** intact; **N1–N3** increasing deficit; **N4** complete SCI; **NX** unassessable.
- Modifiers: **M1** indeterminate posterior tension band injury; **M2** comorbidities altering management (e.g., ankylosing spine/DISH).

### Subaxial cervical injury: SLIC and AO Spine

- **SLIC**: morphology + DLC + neuro status → score guides operative vs non-operative.
- **AO Spine subaxial**: **A/B/C** framework with **facet modifiers** and key modifiers:
    - **A0** minor; **A1–A4** compression/endplate/posterior wall spectrum
    - **B1–B3** tension band / hyperextension patterns
    - **C** translational injury
- **Facet modifiers**
    - **F1**: nondisplaced (<1 cm or <40% lateral mass)
    - **F2**: potentially unstable (>1 cm or >40%)
    - **F3**: floating lateral mass
    - **F4**: perched/dislocated facet
- Common modifiers (recognize)
    - **M2** critical disc herniation
    - **M3** metabolic bone disease (DISH/AS)
    - **M4** vertebral artery injury

## Neurophysiology and intraoperative monitoring (IONM)

### Why it matters

- Helps localization and tract assessment when clinical exam is limited (e.g., early spinal shock, sedation, polytrauma).
- Detects coexisting **root/plexus/peripheral neuropathy** confounders.
- Intra-op: early detection of cord compromise; requires real-time team communication.
### Modalities (tract mapping)

- **MEPs**: corticospinal tract
- **SSEPs / dermatomal SSEPs**: dorsal columns
- **CHEPs / laser EPs**: spinothalamic pathways
- **NCS/EMG**: peripheral/segmental
- **Sympathetic skin response**: autonomic pathways  
    Multimodal (MEP + SSEP ± EMG) used in higher-risk cases.

## Complications and longitudinal care

### Local spine/cord

- **Post-traumatic syringomyelia**: ~**3%**, months–years; observe if stable; treat if progressive symptoms (surgical strategies to restore CSF dynamics / cavity communication).
- **Charcot (neuropathic) spinal arthropathy**: often **10–15 years** post-injury; deformity ± pain ± neurologic decline → conservative vs fusion depending on progression/instability.
### Cardiovascular / autonomic

- **Orthostatic hypotension**: ~**60%** symptomatic
    - Non-pharm: compression stockings, abdominal binder, hydration/salt
    - Pharm: **fludrocortisone**, **midodrine**; others case-by-case (e.g., ephedrine/droxidopa where used)
- **Autonomic dysreflexia** (urgent)
    - Typically **≥T6**; triggers below lesion (bladder distension, bowel impaction, pressure injury) → dangerous hypertension
    - Immediate: **sit upright**, loosen constrictors, **remove trigger**, treat BP if refractory
    - Prevention: structured bladder/bowel program + skin care
### Respiratory / infection

- High-level injuries → cough impairment, atelectasis/pneumonia risk; chronic respiratory complications are a leading cause of mortality in SCI cohorts.
- High cervical injuries may require long-term ventilation.
- "Immune paralysis" concept: increased infection susceptibility with high lesions (no single accepted targeted therapy; supportive prevention/early treatment).
### GU / GI / sexual function

- Neurogenic bladder (commonly above sacral outflow): retention/incontinence/UTIs
    - Options: intermittent catheterization, anticholinergics, α-blockers, botulinum toxin, diversion/stoma in selected cases
- Bowel dysfunction: major QOL impact
    - Stepwise: fiber/fluids, scheduled bowel program, suppositories/digital stimulation; consider advanced options (stimulator/colostomy) for refractory cases
### Skin / MSK / pain

- **Pressure injuries** (common sites): buttocks, lateral thighs, sacrum, feet/ankles
    - Prevention: daily skin checks, scheduled pressure relief, early mobilization when safe
    - Treatment: wound care, debridement when indicated, optimize nutrition
- **Heterotopic ossification**: **~10–53%**, months; pain/redness/fever/increased spasticity
    - Manage: ROM/therapy, NSAIDs/bisphosphonates (case-dependent), radiation or resection for severe cases
- **Spasticity**: **~65–78%** in chronic SCI (>1 year)
    - PT/stretching first-line; meds (e.g., baclofen, benzodiazepines, α2-agonists such as tizanidine/clonidine); intrathecal baclofen; botulinum; surgical options selectively
- **Neuropathic pain**: up to **~40%**; often delayed onset
    - Pharmacologic + multidisciplinary pain strategies; selected interventional options in refractory cases (e.g., neuromodulation; DREZ lesioning in carefully selected scenarios)

## Rehabilitation and functional restoration

- Requires interdisciplinary rehab: conditioning, respiratory optimization, transfers/mobility, stretching/contracture prevention, bowel/bladder programs, skin care, psychological support.
- **WSLT trial** (12-week intensive walking-specific locomotor training vs similar-intensity rehab): **no difference between groups**; both improved at follow-up (interpret as "intensity matters; modality-specific superiority not proven").
- **FES**: UE function, cycling/walker systems; can improve fitness; implanted systems explored for bowel/bladder in select contexts.
- **Robotics/exoskeletons**: assist gait training; functional gains vary by injury severity and program.

---
## Emerging / investigational therapies 

- **Neuroprotective**: minocycline, riluzole, systemic hypothermia (~33 °C) (investigational).
- **Neuroregenerative**: RHOA/ROCK inhibition (e.g., VX‑210/Cethrin), anti‑Nogo‑A, biomaterial scaffolds, cell transplantation (OECs, Schwann cells, OPCs).
- **Neuromodulation**: epidural stimulation; brain–spine interfaces and adaptive stimulation strategies; combined rehab platforms.
