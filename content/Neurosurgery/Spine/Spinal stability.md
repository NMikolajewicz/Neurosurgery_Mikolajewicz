#spine/stability
### Stable spine
* protects spinal cord
* stands straight
* does not generate pain
by limiting excess motion under physiological load
### Unstable spine
* SCI
* deformity
* pain
allows excess (pathological) motion under physiological load

## **Core Principles of Spinal Biomechanics**

### **1\. Three-Column Concept (Denis Model)**
* **Anterior column**: anterior longitudinal ligament + anterior half of vertebral body/disc.
* **Middle column**: posterior longitudinal ligament + posterior half of vertebral body/disc.
* **Posterior column**: pedicles, laminae, facet joints, ligamentum flavum, interspinous/supraspinous ligaments.
* **Instability** is often defined by failure of ≥2 columns.
### **2\. Load Sharing & Load Transmission**
* Vertebral bodies and intervertebral discs bear ~80% of axial load.
* Facet joints share ~20% load and resist shear/rotation.
* Discs act as shock absorbers; nucleus pulposus distributes force radially.

### **3\. Spine as a Tensegrity Structure**
* Stability is maintained through **balanced tension and compression** among muscles, ligaments, and bones.
* Disruption in one element alters the entire biomechanical environment.
## **Implants & Instrumentation**

### **4\. Pedicle Screws**
* Provide **three-column fixation**.
* Ideal for thoracolumbar stabilization.
* Biomechanically superior due to load transfer through the strongest part of the vertebra.

### **5\. Anterior vs. Posterior Instrumentation**
* **Anterior** constructs are load-sharing.
* **Posterior** constructs (rods, plates, screws) provide load-bearing support.
* **360° fusion** (anterior + posterior) often used for maximum rigidity.
### **6\. Rod Constructs**
* Rod diameter and contouring influence construct rigidity and fatigue strength.
* Cross-links can improve torsional stability.
### **7\. Interbody Cages**
* Restore disc height and promote load transmission through the anterior column.
* Increase surface area for fusion, preserve foraminal height.
## **Clinical Application & Surgical Planning**

### **8\. Stabilization Goals**
* Restore alignment.
* Protect neural elements.
* Promote fusion by limiting micromotion.
* Re-establish sagittal and coronal balance.
### **9\. Motion Preservation**
* Includes disc arthroplasty, dynamic stabilization.
* Aim to maintain segmental motion while preventing degeneration of adjacent levels.
* Biomechanical considerations: must mimic natural disc kinematics.
### **10\. Minimally Invasive Techniques**
* Reduce soft tissue disruption.
* Decrease stress risers and preserve posterior tension band.
* May have lower construct rigidity—careful case selection is crucial.
