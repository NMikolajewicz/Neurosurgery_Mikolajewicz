### Pathophysiology

* caused by many spinal dysraphic malformations
* failure of fibres in filum terminale to lengthen
	* 1\. lack of normal ascent of conus medullaris to L1
	* 2\. ischemic / metabolic disturbance of caudal spinal cord
* ==conus below L2-L3 at any age is abnormal==
* ==filum terminale is abnormal if diameter > 1 mm at L5-S1==
* filum terminale often infiltrated with fatty tissue

### Presentation

* symptoms may occur at any age
	* typically in childhood/adolescence during rapid growth
	* flexion/extension of spine
* back + lower extremity pain
* progressive gait disturbance
* lower extremity spastic weakness
* orthopedic deformity
	* varus/valgus and cavus changes of foot
* sensory loss in sacral dermatomes
* urinary incontinence, impotence
* scoliosis/kyphosis

### Workup

* MRI
	* low conus medullaris (below L2)
	* absent cauda equina
	* lack of intumescentia lumbalis
		* conus tapers gradually into thickened filum
	* thickened  filum terminale
		* \>1-2 mm in diameter at L5-S1 level
	* fat-containing filume terminale
	* conus medullaris does not move forward in spinal canal
		* when MRI done in prone position
* urodynamic studies
	* detrusor hyper-reflexia
	* dyssynergia
	* small bladder size

### Management

* surgical release of tethered cord
	* EMG +/- SSEP
	* single-level laminectomy at L4
	* coagulate and transect thickened filum
	* arachnoid adhesions lysed
* recurrence prevention
	* create large dural sac
	* use metal clips for dural closure
		* less inflammation than silk

### Prognosis

* good operative results if no pain / neuro deficits at presentation and no surgical scarring
* 20-25% retether

Definition

* condition in which ==spinal cord is fastened to an immovable structure==
	* ie: lipoma, vertebra, dura, or skin,
* and results in progressive abnormalities:
	* neurologic
	* urologic,
	* orthopedic
* the term “TCS” was introduced by the 3-H’s in 1976
	* their definition required: ==low conus and filum >2mm in thickness==

Etiology
**1° causes**

* SCM (diastematomyelia, diplomyelia)
* MMC
* dermal sinus & associated dermoid/epidermoid
* lipoma
* neurenteric cysts
* thickened filum, myelocystocoele.
* caudal agenesis

**2° causes**

* arachnoiditis
* trauma
* spinal tumors
* dermoid
* "retethering"

Pathogenesis

* ==filum is formed by 2° neurulation==
	* via canalization caudal to posterior neuropore
	* caudal cell mass involutes, leaving filum
* ==failure of caudal cell mass to involute== produces secondary neurulation anomalies which tether cord
	* thickened filum
	* myelocystocele
	* see caudal agenesis for embryology

Pathophysiology

* fixation/distortion/traction on the neural elements that  worsens with growth
* ==stretching is disproportional==
	* LS segments get stretched most, while C-segment is minimally stretched
* stretching is intermittent
	* not static
* effects of stretching:
	* reduced blood flow
		* reversible with de-tethering
	* impaired mitochondrial oxidative metabolism
	* neuronal and tract degeneration
		* d/t loss of axonal integrity, demyelination, loss of organelles and mitochondria
* gross appearance:
	* cord is stretched, decreased in diameter, pale, with absent pulsations
	* nerve roots leave the cord at 90 degree angles
	* dorsal vasculature is stretched and linear with decreased vessel diameter
	* radicular arteries also appear stretched
	* pial vessels appear thinned
