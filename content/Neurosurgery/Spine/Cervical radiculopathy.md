> [!summary] Quick Review
> 
> - **Disc level → root:** **C5–6 disc → C6 radiculopathy** (typical cervical pattern: _disc affects the root below_).
> 
> - **C5:** lateral arm/medial scapula pain + **lateral deltoid** sensory change; weakness **deltoid/infraspinatus**.
> 
> - **C6:** lateral forearm + **thumb/index** sensory change; **biceps** weakness; reflex often **biceps** ↓.
> 
> - **C7:** posterior arm/dorsal forearm ± dorsal hand pain; **middle finger** sensory change; **triceps reflex** ↓; weakness **triceps/wrist extensors**.
> 
> - **C8–T1:** medial forearm/ulnar digits symptoms; reflex **finger flexors** ↓; weakness **intrinsic hand** (C8–T1).
> 
> - **Dx:** clinical localization + concordant imaging when indicated; **Best test:** [[MRI cervical spine]].
> 
> - **Tx:** no red flags → **conservative first-line**; consider injection for persistent pain.
> 
> - **Operate:** **progressive objective deficit**, **myelopathy**, or **refractory disabling radicular pain** with concordant imaging.
>  

## Definitions & Scope

- **Definition:** [[Radiculopathy]] from the [[Cervical spine]] nerve root → arm pain/paresthesia ± weakness in a **root pattern**.
- **Common etiologies:** [[Cervical disc herniation]]; [[Cervical spondylosis]] with [[Foraminal stenosis]].
- **Level convention (exam):** **disc at Cx–(x+1) → radiculopathy of C(x+1)** (e.g., **C6–7 disc → C7**).
- **Localization strategy:** **dermatome + myotome + reflex** (single finding is rarely definitive).

## High‑Yield Outline

- **Syndrome:** neck pain radiating to arm + dermatomal sensory symptoms ± myotomal weakness.
- **Localization:** map **pain + sensory + reflex + motor** (see [[#^table-cervical-radiculopathy-features]]).
- **Dx:** clinical first; image for red flags, objective deficit, persistent/refractory symptoms, or pre‑procedure planning.
- **Tx:** conservative → injection/selective root block → surgery for deficit/refractory pain; urgent pathway if [[Cervical myelopathy]].

## Diagnosis & Workup

- **Dx:** radicular arm pain + focal neuro exam consistent with a single root | **Best test:** [[MRI cervical spine]] | **Rule-out:** [[Cervical myelopathy]], fracture/instability, infection, malignancy, plexopathy, peripheral entrapment, shoulder pathology | **Key imaging:** disc/osteophyte + foraminal narrowing with **exiting root** compression.
- **Clinical localization (use combined pattern):**
    - Match **sensory change** + **weakness** + **reflex** to a single root (see [[#^table-cervical-radiculopathy-features]]).
    - Consider mimics when symptoms are **non-dermatomal**, **multifocal**, or **pain-dominant** without objective findings.
- **Provocative/relief maneuvers (supportive, not definitive):**
    - **Spurling test:** neck extension + rotation + axial load reproduces radicular pain.
    - **Shoulder abduction relief sign:** symptom relief with hand on head (classically C5–C6 distribution).
- **When to image now:**
    - **Red flag:** progressive motor deficit, signs of [[Cervical myelopathy]], fever/constitutional symptoms, known malignancy, significant trauma, severe unremitting pain.
- **Electrodiagnostics (when pattern is unclear):**
    - **Best test:** EMG/NCS to distinguish [[Radiculopathy]] vs peripheral entrapment/plexopathy; paraspinal denervation supports radiculopathy.
- **Adjunct imaging:**
    - **CT myelography:** if MRI contraindicated or to better define foraminal bony stenosis.

## Management

- **Tx:** stepwise conservative → interventional → surgical | **Indication:** objective/progressive deficit, concordant imaging with disabling refractory pain, or myelopathy | **Contraindication:** uncontrolled infection/medical instability (elective) | **Timing:** urgent if myelopathy/progressive deficit; elective after adequate conservative trial for isolated radiculopathy.
- **Non‑operative (first‑line if no red flags):**
    - Activity modification + analgesia (NSAID/acetaminophen as appropriate) + [[Physiotherapy]] (posture, ROM, stabilization).
    - Short course oral steroids may be used for acute severe radicular pain (institution‑dependent).
    - **Interventional:** [[Epidural steroid injection]] / selective nerve root block for persistent radicular pain (also helps confirm level when exam is equivocal).
- **Operative options (radiculopathy without myelopathy; choose by pathology/anatomy):**
    - **ACDF** ([[Anterior cervical discectomy and fusion]]): ventral disc/osteophyte with foraminal/central pathology; reliable decompression + stabilization.
    - **Posterior cervical foraminotomy** ([[Posterior cervical foraminotomy]]): lateral/foraminal compression with preserved alignment; motion‑preserving option.
    - **Cervical disc arthroplasty** ([[Cervical disc arthroplasty]]): selected single‑level disease without instability or advanced spondylosis.
- **Complication (can't-miss):** postoperative neck hematoma with airway compromise after anterior approaches → urgent evaluation/escalation.

## Pitfalls & Red Flags

> [!warning]
> 
> - **Red flag:** any signs of [[Cervical myelopathy]] (gait imbalance, hand clumsiness, UMN signs) → urgent imaging/escalation.
> 
> - **Red flag:** **progressive motor weakness** (objective) → urgent MRI + surgical assessment.
> 
> - **Red flag:** fever, immunosuppression, IVDU, weight loss, cancer history → rule out infection/malignancy.
> 
> - **Pitfall:** labeling **median/ulnar entrapment** as "C8/T1 radic" without checking proximal dermatomal sensory change + paraspinal EMG.
> 
> - **Pitfall:** confusing **C5 radiculopathy** (deltoid weakness + lateral shoulder sensory change) with primary shoulder pathology.
> 
> - **Pitfall:** treating imaging abnormalities without **clinical-radiologic concordance** (common incidental spondylosis).
>
