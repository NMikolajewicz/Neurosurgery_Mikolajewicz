> [!summary] Cervical radiculopathy review
> 
> - **Disc level → root:** **C5–6 disc → C6 radiculopathy** (typical cervical pattern: _disc affects the root below_).
> 
> - **C5:** lateral arm/medial scapula pain + **lateral deltoid** sensory change; weakness **deltoid/infraspinatus**.
> 
> - **C6:** lateral forearm + **thumb/index** sensory change; **biceps** weakness; reflex often **biceps** ↓.
> 
> - **C7:** posterior arm/dorsal forearm ± dorsal hand pain; **middle finger** sensory change; **triceps reflex** ↓; weakness **triceps/wrist extensors**.
> 
> - **C8–T1:** medial forearm/ulnar digits symptoms; reflex **finger flexors** ↓; weakness **intrinsic hand** (C8–T1).
> 
> - **Dx:** clinical localization + concordant imaging when indicated; **Best test:** MRI cervical spine.
> 
> - **Tx:** no red flags → **conservative first-line**; consider injection for persistent pain.
> 
> - **Operate:** **progressive objective deficit**, **myelopathy**, or **refractory disabling radicular pain** with concordant imaging.
>  

## Definitions & Scope

- **Definition:** Radiculopathy from the cervical spine nerve root → arm pain/paresthesia ± weakness in a **root pattern**.
- **Common etiologies:** Cervical disc herniation; cervical spondylosis with foraminal stenosis.
- **Level convention (exam):** **disc at Cx–(x+1) → radiculopathy of C(x+1)** (e.g., **C6–7 disc → C7**).
- **Localization strategy:** **dermatome + myotome + reflex**

## Outline

- **Syndrome:** neck pain radiating to arm + dermatomal sensory symptoms ± myotomal weakness.
- **Localization:** map **pain + sensory + reflex + motor**
- **Dx:** clinical first; image for red flags, objective deficit, persistent/refractory symptoms, or pre‑procedure planning.
- **Tx:** conservative → injection/selective root block → surgery for deficit/refractory pain; urgent pathway if cervical myelopathy.

## Diagnosis & Workup

- **Dx:** radicular arm pain + focal neuro exam consistent with a single root 
	- **Best test:** MRI cervical spine 
	- **Rule-out:** Cervical myelopathy, fracture/instability, infection, malignancy, plexopathy, peripheral entrapment, shoulder pathology 
	- **Key imaging:** disc/osteophyte + foraminal narrowing with **exiting root** compression.
- **Clinical localization (use combined pattern):**
    - Match **sensory change** + **weakness** + **reflex** to a single root.
    - Consider mimics when symptoms are **non-dermatomal**, **multifocal**, or **pain-dominant** without objective findings.
- **Provocative/relief maneuvers (supportive, not definitive):**
    - **Spurling test:** neck extension + rotation + axial load reproduces radicular pain.
    - **Shoulder abduction relief sign:** symptom relief with hand on head (classically C5–C6 distribution).
- **When to image now:**
    - **Red flag:** progressive motor deficit, signs of cervical myelopathy, fever/constitutional symptoms, known malignancy, significant trauma, severe unremitting pain.
- **Electrodiagnostics (when pattern is unclear):**
    - **Best test:** EMG/NCS to distinguish radiculopathy vs. peripheral entrapment/plexopathy; paraspinal denervation supports radiculopathy.
- **Adjunct imaging:**
    - **CT myelography:** if MRI contraindicated or to better define foraminal bony stenosis.

### Mimics

| Mimic                  | Typical clue                              | Exam discriminator                                                              | Best test                  |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Carpal tunnel syndrome | nocturnal paresthesia, hand‑dominant      | median distribution; no neck provocation; reflexes usually preserved            | EMG/NCS                    |
| Ulnar neuropathy       | 4th/5th digit symptoms, elbow provocation | ulnar sensory/motor without proximal dermatomal pattern; paraspinals normal     | EMG/NCS                    |
| Brachial plexopathy    | patchy, multi‑root                        | involves multiple peripheral nerve territories; often severe pain then weakness | EMG/NCS + targeted imaging |
| Rotator cuff tear      | shoulder‑dominant pain/weakness           | pain‑limited ROM; preserved dermatomal sensory/reflex pattern                   | shoulder US/MRI            |
| Cervical myelopathy    | gait/hand dexterity issues                | UMN signs (hyperreflexia, Hoffmann, clonus), long‑tract findings                | MRI cervical spine         |
## Management

- **Tx:** stepwise conservative → interventional → surgical 
	- **Indication:** objective/progressive deficit, concordant imaging with disabling refractory pain, or myelopathy 
	- **Contraindication:** uncontrolled infection/medical instability 
	- **Timing:** urgent if myelopathy/progressive deficit; elective after adequate conservative trial for isolated radiculopathy.
- **Non‑operative (first‑line if no red flags):**
    - Activity modification + analgesia (NSAID/acetaminophen) + physiotherapy (posture, ROM, stabilization).
    - Short course oral steroids may be used for acute severe radicular pain.
    - **Interventional:** Epidural steroid injection / selective nerve root block for persistent radicular pain 
- **Operative options (radiculopathy without myelopathy; choose by pathology/anatomy):**
    - **ACDF**: ventral disc/osteophyte with foraminal/central pathology; reliable decompression + stabilization.
    - **Posterior cervical foraminotomy**: lateral/foraminal compression with preserved alignment; motion‑preserving option.
    - **Cervical disc arthroplasty**: selected single‑level disease without instability or advanced spondylosis.
- **Complication (can't-miss):** postoperative neck hematoma with airway compromise after anterior approaches → urgent evaluation/escalation.
## Pitfalls & Red Flags

> [!warning]
> 
> - **Red flag:** any signs of cervical myelopathy (gait imbalance, hand clumsiness, UMN signs) → urgent imaging/escalation.
> 
> - **Red flag:** **progressive motor weakness** (objective) → urgent MRI + surgical assessment.
> 
> - **Red flag:** fever, immunosuppression, IVDU, weight loss, cancer history → rule out infection/malignancy.
> 
> - **Pitfall:** labeling **median/ulnar entrapment** as "C8/T1 radic" without checking proximal dermatomal sensory change + paraspinal EMG.
> 
> - **Pitfall:** confusing **C5 radiculopathy** (deltoid weakness + lateral shoulder sensory change) with primary shoulder pathology.
> 
> - **Pitfall:** treating imaging abnormalities without **clinical-radiologic concordance** (common incidental spondylosis).
>

### Answer Key

1. **Middle finger numbness + triceps weakness + ↓ triceps reflex**
	- **Root:** **C7**
	- **Disc level:** **C6–7** (C6–7 HCD → C7 radiculopathy)

2. **Lateral forearm pain + thumb/index sensory loss + biceps weakness**
	- **Root:** **C6**
	- **Most likely disc space:** **C5–6** (C5–6 HCD → C6 radiculopathy)

3. **Lateral deltoid sensory change + weak shoulder abduction to 90°**
	-  **Root:** **C5** (classically from **C4–5** disc)

4. **Two key features distinguishing cervical myelopathy from isolated radiculopathy**
	- **UMN / long‑tract signs (esp. legs):** **spasticity**, **hyperreflexia**, pathologic reflexes (e.g., **Babinski/Hoffman**)
	- **Cord-pattern deficits (not single-root):** gait dysfunction/poor leg control + **sensory patterns below a level** (not dermatomal)

5. **Best imaging test for persistent cervical radicular symptoms with objective deficit**
	- **MRI C‑spine** = **study of choice** for initial evaluation of herniated cervical disc

6. **When is EMG/NCS most useful in suspected cervical radiculopathy?**
	- **When diagnosis is uncertain** / to **exclude mimics** (e.g., **peripheral neuropathy/entrapment**, **ALS**)
	- **When motor deficit is present** (if **motor exam normal**, EMG is **unlikely** to show abnormality)
	- **Timing:** denervation activity typically needs **~10–21 days** → EMG **less helpful in first ~3 weeks**
	- **NCS role:** helps detect **entrapment/polyneuropathy** (CTS vs C6; ulnar neuropathy vs C8)

7. **3 common mimics + 1 discriminator each**
	- **Carpal tunnel syndrome (median neuropathy)**
	    - **Discriminator:** **NCS abnormal** at median nerve; clinical distribution = median territory (often nocturnal) rather than dermatomal
	- **Ulnar neuropathy (often at elbow)**
	    - **Discriminator:** **NCS localizes** ulnar lesion; sensory/motor findings follow ulnar nerve rather than a root
	- **Brachial plexopathy (incl. Parsonage‑Turner)**
	    - **Discriminator:** **EMG**: **paraspinal denervation supports radiculopathy** (paraspinals typically spared in plexopathy); plexopathy often spans **multiple roots/cords**

8. **3 indications for surgery in cervical radiculopathy**
	- **Progressive neurologic deficit** (especially **motor weakness**) despite nonoperative care
	- **Failure to improve** with appropriate non‑surgical management
	- **Persistent, function‑limiting radicular pain/deficit with concordant imaging compression** (the practical "failed conservative therapy + objective correlation" bucket)
