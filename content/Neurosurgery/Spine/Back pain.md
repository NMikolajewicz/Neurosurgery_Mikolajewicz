## **Lumbar Neurogenic Claudication, Radiculopathy, and Mechanical Back Pain**

> [!summary] **Key Distinction**  
> **Neurogenic claudication** results from **central canal stenosis**, while **lumbar radiculopathy** is due to **lateral recess or foraminal encroachment**.

---

## **1. Mechanical Back Pain**

### **Features**

- **==Discogenic== pain:** worsens with **flexion**.
- ==**Facet-mediated== pain:** worsens with **extension**.
### **Management**

- Physiotherapy & core stabilization
- Graded exercise
- Conservative/non-operative management
- Pain control: acetaminophen, NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen, ketorolac), activity modification

---

## **2. Lumbosacral Radiculopathy**

> [!info] **Definition:** Compression or irritation of a spinal nerve root, most commonly from **disc herniation** or **spondylosis**.
### **Etiology**

- Disc herniation (acute or chronic degeneration)
- Foraminal/lateral recess narrowing from spondylosis or facet hypertrophy
### **Clinical Presentation**

- Paresthesia: **63–72%**
- Radiation of pain to lower limb: **35%**
- Numbness: **27%**
- Weakness: **≤37%**
- Reflex changes:  
    – **Ankle reflex absent** in up to 40%  
    – **Knee reflex absent** in ~18%
### **Diagnostic Work-Up**

- **Straight leg raise** (L4–S1)
- **Femoral nerve stretch test** (L2–L4)
    - Pain in anterior thigh; often positive for high lumbar herniations (L2–L4).
- **Romberg test:** ==wide-based gait + positive rhomberg → >90%== chance of lower spine pathology
- **EMG:** sensitivity **50–85%**; helpful for chronic or unclear cases

## **3. Neurogenic Claudication (Lumbar Spinal Stenosis)**

> [!summary] **Classic Triad**  
> Worsens with **standing/walking**, improves with **flexion**, variable/normal **neurological exam**.
### **Pathophysiology**

- Central canal narrowing → increased pressure in the subarachnoid space
- Leads to:
    - Mechanical compression of ==nerve== roots
    - ===Venous=== congestion
    - Reduced ==arterial== flow
    - Impaired axonal conduction
- Walking increases ==metabolic demand== → worsens symptoms
### **Clinical Features**

- Pain with walking, standing, upright exercise
- Relief with flexion ("==**shopping cart sign**==")
- "**Simian stance**": patients adopt flexed posture to relieve symptoms
- Normal neuro exam is common
### **Physical Exam Markers**

- **==Bilateral extensor digitorum brevis (EDB) wasting==** → reliable bedside indicator of longstanding lumbar canal stenosis
- Negative peripheral pulses help differentiate from vascular claudication

### **Management**

- **Decompression alone** for stenosis without spondylolisthesis
- SPORT trial: **laminectomy ± fusion** superior to nonoperative care
- Fusion reserved for patients with:
    - Spondylolisthesis
    - Instability
    - Recurrent stenosis after prior laminectomy
### **Differential Diagnosis**

- **Vascular claudication:** improves with standing rest (not flexion)
- Multilevel lumbar disc protrusions
- Peripheral neuropathy

---

## **4. Key Comparative Summary**

> [!important] **Distinguishing Features**
> 
> - **Radiculopathy:** dermatomal symptoms, positive SLR/FNST, root-specific deficits.
> 
> - **Neurogenic claudication:** posture-dependent, bilateral/segmentally variable, improved with flexion.
> 
> - **Mechanical pain:** activity-dependent axial pain, not radicular, no neurogenic pattern.
