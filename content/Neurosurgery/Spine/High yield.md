### 1. First-pass spine emergency framework

|Syndrome|Key clues|Immediate move|Prognosis anchor|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Myelopathy**|Gait imbalance, hand clumsiness, hyperreflexia, Hoffmann/Babinski/clonus, sphincter late|MRI spine; urgent referral if progressive|Untreated DCM is often stepwise; **20-62% deteriorate neurologically over 3-6 years** if symptomatic and nonoperative. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2192568217700396?utm_source=chatgpt.com "The Natural History of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy ..."))|

### 2. Degenerative cervical spine

#### Degenerative cervical myelopathy

**Diagnosis**

- Clinical myelopathy plus MRI cord compression.
- Symptoms: gait imbalance, falls, hand clumsiness, numbness, neck pain, radiculopathy, sphincter late.
- Signs: hyperreflexia, Hoffmann, Babinski, clonus, spastic gait, proprioceptive loss.

**Severity by mJOA**

- **Mild:** 15-17
- **Moderate:** 12-14
- **Severe:** <=11

**Management**

- **Moderate/severe DCM:** offer surgery.
- **Mild DCM:** surgery or structured rehab/close observation; operate if deterioration or no improvement.
- **Cord compression without myelopathy:** no prophylactic surgery routinely; educate and follow.
- **Cord compression + radiculopathy:** higher risk of myelopathy; surgery or close serial follow-up is reasonable. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29164035/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "A Clinical Practice Guideline for the Management of ..."))

**Prognosis**

- Surgery usually **halts progression** and often improves function, but recovery is limited by long symptom duration, severe baseline deficit, T2 cord signal, older age, and gait/sphincter involvement.
- Natural history is not benign once symptomatic: **20-62% deteriorate** over several years without surgery. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2192568217700396?utm_source=chatgpt.com "The Natural History of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy ..."))

#### Cervical radiculopathy

**Pattern**

- C5: deltoid/biceps, lateral shoulder.
- C6: biceps/wrist extension, thumb.
- C7: triceps/wrist flexion, middle finger.
- C8: finger flexors/intrinsics, ring/small finger.

**Natural history**

- Usually favorable: most improve nonoperatively; systematic review data show about **83% satisfactory function by 3 years**, with most improvement by **4-6 months**. ([NCBI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK441828/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Cervical Radiculopathy - StatPearls - NCBI Bookshelf - NIH"))

**Operate when**

- Progressive motor deficit.
- Myelopathy.
- Intractable radicular pain after adequate conservative therapy.
- Concordant imaging and exam.

**Procedure choice**

- **ACDF:** central/paracentral disc, kyphosis, instability, axial neck pain.
- **Posterior foraminotomy:** lateral/foraminal soft disc or foraminal stenosis, preserved lordosis, no instability.
- **Disc arthroplasty:** selected 1-2 level soft disc/radic, preserved motion, no major facet arthropathy/instability.

#### OPLL

**High-yield**

- Common cause of DCM; risk rises with canal occupancy.
- Occupancy **30-60%** predicts myelopathy risk; **>60%** strongly associated with myelopathy. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4771496/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Ossification of the Posterior Longitudinal Ligament - PMC - NIH"))
- Anterior approach gives direct decompression but higher CSF leak/dural ossification risk.
- Posterior laminoplasty/laminectomy-fusion works best with preserved lordosis and multilevel disease; poor for fixed kyphosis or very high occupying ratio.

### 3. Lumbar degenerative disease

#### Lumbar disc herniation

**Natural history**

- Most radiculopathy improves without surgery; NASS notes the majority improve independent of treatment. ([Spine](https://www.spine.org/documents/researchclinicalcare/guidelines/lumbardischerniation.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Lumbar disc herniation clinical guideline"))

**Operate when**

- Cauda equina.
- Progressive or significant motor deficit.
- Persistent disabling radicular pain after ~6-12 weeks conservative care with concordant imaging.
- Recurrent disabling radiculopathy.

**Pearl**

- **Leg pain > back pain** predicts better discectomy response.
- Weakness recovery depends on severity/duration; pain improves more reliably than motor deficit.

#### Lumbar spinal stenosis

**Classic**

- Neurogenic claudication: worse standing/extension, better sitting/flexion/shopping-cart sign.
- MRI severity correlates imperfectly with symptoms.

**Natural history**

- Often slowly progressive or fluctuating rather than catastrophic.
- Surgery gives better pain/function outcomes than nonoperative care in appropriately selected symptomatic patients; SPORT showed durable benefit through **4 years**, although longer-term differences may narrow. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20453723/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Surgical versus nonoperative treatment for lumbar spinal ..."))

**Operate when**

- Disabling claudication/radiculopathy despite conservative therapy.
- Progressive neurologic deficit.
- Cauda equina.

**Fusion?**

- Add fusion for instability, deformity, significant mechanical back pain from instability, recurrent stenosis with destabilizing decompression.
- Avoid routine fusion for pure stenosis without instability.

#### Degenerative lumbar spondylolisthesis

**Natural history**

- Usually low-grade; slip progression often limited, symptoms usually driven by stenosis rather than translation.
- 2024 NORDSTEN-DS 5-year follow-up: **decompression alone was non-inferior to decompression + instrumented fusion** for selected degenerative spondylolisthesis with stenosis. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39111800/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Decompression alone or with fusion for degenerative ..."))

**Fusion more likely if**

- Dynamic instability.
- High mechanical back pain component.
- Foraminal stenosis requiring facetectomy.
- Sagittal/coronal deformity.
- Recurrent stenosis or prior decompression.

#### Isthmic spondylolisthesis

**Natural history**

- Low-grade pediatric/adolescent slips often stable; adult progression after skeletal maturity is uncommon but can occur with disc degeneration.
- Slip angle is more prognostic than percent slip. ([NCBI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK430767/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Spondylolisthesis - StatPearls - NCBI Bookshelf"))

**Operate when**

- Persistent radiculopathy/back pain despite conservative care.
- Progressive slip, high-grade deformity, neurologic deficit.
- Usually decompression plus fusion if symptomatic adult isthmic slip.

### 4. Trauma and spinal cord injury

#### Acute traumatic SCI

**Initial priorities**

- ABCs, immobilization, CT entire relevant spine, MRI if deficit/compression/disco-ligamentous concern.
- Treat hypotension/hypoxia aggressively.

**2024 guideline anchors**

- **Decompression within 24 h** when medically feasible.
- **MAP augmentation:** lower limit 75-80 mmHg; avoid pushing beyond 90-95; duration 3-7 days.
- Evidence quality for MAP targets remains low/weak, but it is current AO Spine/Praxis guidance. ([Illinois Experts](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/an-update-of-a-clinical-practice-guideline-for-the-management-of-/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "An Update of a Clinical Practice Guideline for ..."))

**Steroids**

- High-dose methylprednisolone is **not standard of care**.
- 2017 AO Spine: may be offered as a **treatment option** only if within 8 h; do not offer after 8 h. This remains controversial and institution-dependent. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5686915/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "A Clinical Practice Guideline for the Management of Patients ..."))

#### Cervical trauma patterns

|Injury|Key exam answer|
|---|---|
|**Occipital condyle fracture**|CT diagnosis; classify by stability/alar ligament involvement; collar vs occipitocervical fusion if unstable.|
|**Atlas burst/Jefferson**|Assess transverse atlantal ligament; rule of Spence historical but MRI/CT-based ligament assessment preferred.|
|**Odontoid type II**|Elderly often collar vs posterior C1-2 fusion depending displacement, nonunion risk, frailty; young displaced often screw or posterior fusion.|
|**Hangman C2 pars**|Extension-distraction; most treated immobilization unless severe angulation/disc disruption.|
|**Subaxial injury**|Use SLIC: morphology, disco-ligamentous complex, neuro status; **<=3 nonop**, **>=5 surgery**, 4 equivocal. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4158633/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Cervical injuries scored according to the Subaxial ... - PMC"))|
|**Central cord**|Elderly spondylotic hyperextension; UE > LE weakness; surgery if ongoing compression/instability.|

#### Thoracolumbar trauma

**Use AO/TLICS logic**

- Compression/burst = anterior column failure.
- Distraction = posterior tension band failure.
- Translation/rotation = unstable until proven otherwise.
- PLC injury, neurologic deficit, kyphosis, canal compromise, and load-sharing influence surgery.

**Operate when**

- Neurologic deficit with compression.
- Unstable morphology: distraction/translation.
- PLC disruption.
- Progressive deformity/kyphosis.
- Polytrauma needing mobilization.

### 5. Spine oncology

#### Metastatic spinal cord compression

**Red flags**

- Known cancer + new severe back pain, night pain, mechanical pain, radicular pain, weakness, sensory level, bladder/bowel symptoms.

**Framework**

- **NOMS:** neurologic compression, oncologic radiosensitivity, mechanical stability, systemic fitness. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4063402/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "The NOMS Framework: Approach to the Treatment of Spinal ..."))
- **SINS:** 0-6 stable, 7-12 potentially unstable, 13-18 unstable; mechanical instability is a surgical disease.

**Immediate management**

- Whole-spine MRI.
- Dexamethasone **16 mg daily** if neurologic signs/symptoms of MSCC while awaiting definitive therapy. ([NICE](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng234/chapter/Recommendations?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Spinal metastases and metastatic spinal cord compression"))
- Surgery favored for mechanical instability, radioresistant tumor with high-grade ESCC, tissue diagnosis needed, progression after RT, or reasonable survival/systemic reserve.

**Evidence pearl**

- Patchell trial: direct decompressive surgery + RT improved ambulation duration compared with RT alone in selected MSCC patients. ([The Lancet](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2805%2966954-1/fulltext?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Direct decompressive surgical resection in the treatment of ..."))

#### Intradural extramedullary tumors

|Tumor|Typical|Imaging|Treatment/prognosis|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Schwannoma**|Sensory radic pain, dumbbell possible|Foraminal expansion, enhancing nerve sheath lesion|GTR often curative; preserve functional root if possible.|
|**Neurofibroma**|NF1, root incorporated|Fusiform root enlargement|Higher root sacrifice risk; plexiform has malignant potential.|
|**Meningioma**|Older women, thoracic, lateral/dorsal|Dural tail, calcification possible|Usually benign/slow; GTR has favorable prognosis and low recurrence. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11011121/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Spinal Meningiomas: A Comprehensive Review and Update ..."))|

#### Intramedullary tumors

|Tumor|High-yield features|Surgical principle|
|---|---|---|
|**Ependymoma**|Adult, central, well-circumscribed, polar cysts/hemosiderin cap|Midline myelotomy; aim GTR if plane exists. EANO: spinal ependymomas are common adult ependymal tumors; management is maximal safe resection. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5909649/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "EANO guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of ... - PMC"))|
|**Astrocytoma**|Child/young adult, eccentric/infiltrative, poor plane|Biopsy/subtotal safe resection; avoid morbidity.|
|**Hemangioblastoma**|Dorsal, cyst/syrinx, flow voids, VHL|Circumferential dissection, en bloc after feeders controlled; do not enter tumor early.|
|**Metastasis**|Rapid pain/deficit, known cancer|Steroids if edema/compression; surgery/radiation depending NOMS/systemic disease.|

### 6. Infection

#### Native vertebral osteomyelitis/discitis

**Diagnosis**

- Back pain + elevated ESR/CRP; fever may be absent.
- MRI with gadolinium.
- Blood cultures x2.
- CT-guided biopsy if blood cultures negative and patient stable.

**Treatment**

- IDSA: most bacterial native vertebral osteomyelitis treated with **6 weeks** parenteral or highly bioavailable oral antibiotics. ([IDSA](https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/vertebral-osteomyelitis/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Vertebral Osteomyelitis"))

**Surgery indications**

- Progressive neurologic deficit.
- Epidural abscess with compression.
- Mechanical instability/deformity.
- Persistent bacteremia or worsening pain despite appropriate antibiotics. ([IDSA](https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/vertebral-osteomyelitis/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Vertebral Osteomyelitis"))

#### Spinal epidural abscess

**Risk factors**

- Diabetes, IV drug use, immunosuppression, bacteremia, recent spine procedure, indwelling catheter. ([AANS](https://www.aans.org/patients/conditions-treatments/spinal-infections/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Spinal Infections"))

**Management**

- Neuro deficit or cord/cauda compression: urgent decompression + antibiotics.
- Medical therapy may be considered only if neurologically intact, organism known/biopsy feasible, stable, and close monitoring possible.

**Prognosis**

- Initial neurologic status and diagnostic delay dominate outcome; missed SEA can cause permanent disability. ([HCA Healthcare Scholarly Commons](https://scholarlycommons.hcahealthcare.com/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1676&context=hcahealthcarejournal&utm_source=chatgpt.com "A Clinical Review on Spinal Epidural Abscess"))

### 7. Vascular spine

#### Spinal dural AV fistula

**Classic**

- Older man, progressive thoracolumbar myelopathy, gait decline, sensory symptoms, sphincter dysfunction.
- Often misdiagnosed as stenosis, neuropathy, MS, transverse myelitis.

**MRI**

- Long-segment T2 cord edema, conus involvement, serpiginous dorsal flow voids.
- Confirm with spinal angiography.

**Natural history/prognosis**

- Progressive venous congestive myelopathy; untreated patients can become disabled within a few years.
- Treatment goal is to **halt progression**; recovery depends on pre-treatment disability and symptom duration. Surgery has high occlusion/low recurrence compared with embolization in many series. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10775532/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Spinal dural arteriovenous fistula: a comprehensive review of ..."))

#### Spinal cavernous malformation

**Pearls**

- Intramedullary "popcorn" hemorrhagic lesion.
- Annual hemorrhage estimates vary roughly **1.7-5.5%** in older series; higher after prior hemorrhage. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6931213/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Conservative and Surgical Management of Spinal Cord ... - PMC"))
- Surgery for symptomatic/recurrent hemorrhage or progressive deficit when accessible; observe incidental/asymptomatic lesions.

### 8. Deformity and osteoporotic fracture

#### Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis

**Natural history**

- Risk depends on Cobb angle + growth remaining.
- Curves **<30 degrees at maturity rarely progress**; curves **>50 degrees** tend to progress in adulthood, often cited around **1 degree/year**. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12347580/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Curve Progression in Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis ... - PMC"))

**Treatment**

- Observe: mild curves, low progression risk.
- Brace: skeletally immature, generally **25-40/45 degrees**.
- Surgery: usually progressive curves around **45-50 degrees** or greater, especially thoracic.

#### Adult spinal deformity

**Natural history**

- Slow progression; one cohort found mean coronal curve progression about **0.83 degrees/year**. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7160806/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "The Natural History of Progression in Adult Spinal Deformity"))
- Surgery is for disabling pain, neurologic compromise, progressive deformity, sagittal imbalance, failed conservative therapy.
- Sagittal alignment predicts disability more than coronal Cobb alone.

#### Osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture

**Natural history**

- Most conservatively treated acute osteoporotic compression fractures have substantial pain improvement in the first **3 months**. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7966424/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Natural History of Pain in Patients with Conservatively Treated ..."))

**Management**

- Analgesia, mobilization, osteoporosis treatment, brace selectively.
- MRI STIR helps confirm acuity and exclude malignancy/infection.
- AAOS strongly recommended **against vertebroplasty** for neurologically intact acute osteoporotic compression fractures; kyphoplasty has weaker/selective support. ([Lippincott Journals](https://journals.lww.com/jaaos/fulltext/2011/03000/aaos_clinical_practice_guideline__the_treatment_of.8.aspx?utm_source=chatgpt.com "AAOS Clinical Practice Guideline: The Treatment ..."))

## 9. Exam one-liners to memorize

- **DCM:** progressive cord disease; moderate/severe = surgery; mild = surgery or close structured nonop, operate if decline.
    
- **Radiculopathy:** most improve; operate for progressive deficit, myelopathy, or refractory concordant pain.
    
- **Lumbar stenosis:** clinical syndrome, not MRI diagnosis; decompression for disabling neurogenic claudication.
    
- **Degenerative spondylolisthesis:** decompression alone often enough if stable; fusion for instability/deformity/mechanical pain.
    
- **Cauda equina:** bladder/saddle symptoms = emergency MRI; decompress confirmed compression urgently.
    
- **Acute SCI:** avoid hypotension/hypoxia; early decompression within 24 h when feasible; MAP 75-80 to 90-95 for 3-7 days.
    
- **MSCC:** MRI whole spine; dex 16 mg if neuro signs; use NOMS/SINS.
    
- **SEA:** back pain + risk factors + ESR/CRP = MRI; neurologic deficit = urgent decompression.
    
- **SDAVF:** progressive myelopathy + conus edema + dorsal flow voids; angiography confirms; treatment stops decline.
    
- **Intramedullary tumor:** ependymoma has a plane; astrocytoma usually infiltrates; hemangioblastoma must be circumferential/en bloc.
    
- **AIS:** curve size + growth remaining predict progression; <30 at maturity stable, >50 progresses.

## 10. Rapid oral exam structure

For any spine case, answer in this sequence:

1. **Localization:** cord, root, cauda, plexus, peripheral nerve.
    
2. **Tempo:** acute trauma/hematoma, subacute infection/tumor, chronic degenerative/vascular.
    
3. **Stability:** mechanical pain, deformity, SINS/TLICS/SLIC.
    
4. **Compression:** myelopathy, cauda, progressive motor deficit.
    
5. **Systemic context:** cancer, infection risk, frailty, anticoagulation, osteoporosis.
    
6. **Imaging:** MRI for neural/infection/tumor; CT for bone; dynamic x-rays for instability; angiography for vascular.
    
7. **Treatment:** decompression, stabilization, biopsy/antibiotics, radiation/systemic therapy, or observation.
    
8. **Prognosis:** baseline neuro status, symptom duration, severity, instability, systemic disease.
