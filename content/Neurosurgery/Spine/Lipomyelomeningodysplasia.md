Pathophysiology

* components
	* myelomeningocele
	* skin-covered subcutaneous lipoma
		* attached to placode
* focal premature dysjunction of neuroectoderm from cutaneous ectoderm allows for migration of mesenchymal tissue into neural tube
	* via dorsal surface of unclosed neural tube
* ectopic mesenchymal cells give rise to fat
* adipose tissue results in spinal cord tethering and vertebral arch nonfusion.

Presentation

* progressive loss of neurological function
* reversal of bladder dysfunction unlikely once established
* 90% develop motor/sensory deficit secondary to tethered spinal cord

Management

* early aggressive surgery
	* steps in surgical management
		* **untether cord using Xomed CUSA and recording from anal sphincter**
		* Free up sides from attachment to dura. 
		* Reduce the bulk of fat using CUSA in the midline. 
		* Tie dura open to sides 
		* Place bovine pericardial graft as dural substitute
* SSEP and EMG, operative microscopy
	* release tethered cord
	* remove lipomatous tissue
	* reconstruct dural sheath
