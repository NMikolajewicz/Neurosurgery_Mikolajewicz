> [!summary] SCIWORA Review
> 
> - **Definition:** Traumatic myelopathy with **normal X‑ray/CT** (no fracture/malalignment) → **suspect SCIWORA** (esp. **children <8–9 y**)
> 
> - **Best test:** **MRI spine** (ideally **≤24 h** if neurologic symptoms); if early MRI normal but symptoms persist → repeat MRI **6–9 days**
> 
> - **Presentation:** Spectrum from transient paresthesia to quadriplegia; **delayed deficits** possible (**minutes–48 h** in ~**50%**, up to **4 days**)
> 
> - **Mechanism:** **Hyperflexion / hyperextension / crush**; pediatric risk = **elastic spine + lax ligaments** → spontaneous self‑reduction
> 
> - **Key imaging:** Cord **edema/contusion** = better prognosis; **major hemorrhage (>50% axial cord)** or **transection** = poor
> 
> - **Tx:** **Immobilize immediately**; treat as acute SCI (avoid hypotension); **collar/brace ~12 weeks (≈3 months)** + no high‑risk activity **6 months**
> 
> - **Tx:** **No routine steroids** in pediatric SCIWORA; surgery **only** for **compression/instability** or neurologic deterioration
> 
> - **Complication:** Recurrent injury can occur even with bracing → strict activity restriction + follow‑up
>  

## Definitions 

- Traumatic spinal cord dysfunction with **normal plain radiographs + CT** (no fracture/dislocation/malalignment)
    - **Key concept:** Juvenile spine **deforms then self‑reduces** → cord injury with **normal X‑ray/CT**
- Predominantly **pediatric**; highest risk **<8 years**; **cervical** most common
    - Adults: "SCIWORA" is **controversial** due to frequent **degenerative stenosis/spondylosis** confounding imaging
- Described in pediatric series (Pang & Wilberger, **1982**); first case report (Burke, **1974**)
- **Modern terminology (WFNS 2020):**
    - **SCIWORA:** Clinic–radiologic mismatch (normal X‑ray/CT) with traumatic myelopathy; **intrinsic cord signal** may be present on MRI
    - **Real SCIWORA / SCIWONA:** Symptoms with **normal MRI** (all imaging negative); consider repeat MRI **6–9 days** if symptoms persist
    - **Non‑SCIWORA SCI without plain radiographic abnormality:** Includes **extrinsic** lesions (e.g., epidural hematoma, ligament rupture, traumatic disc herniation)

## Overview

- **Epidemiology**
    - Pediatric incidence reported **~13–19%** of spinal injuries
    - Predominantly **male** 
- **Pediatric predisposition (<9 y)**
    - **Ligamentous laxity**, **horizontal facets**, **high disc water content**, **unformed uncinate processes**, **growth zones**, **large head:neck ratio**
- **Etiology & mechanism**
    - Causes: **sports**, **MVC**, **falls** (also: "MVA or falls" frequently cited)
    - Mechanisms: **hyperextension**, **hyperflexion**, **crush/traction**, ± **ischemia/vascular injury**
- **Clinical presentation**
    - Deficits range: transient paresthesia/weakness → complete SCI
    - Syndromes: **central cord**, anterior/central/posterior cord patterns, **Brown‑Sequard**, bowel/bladder dysfunction
    - **Delayed symptom onset** is common → serial exams and observation matter
- **Imaging**
    - CT/X‑ray negative by definition; **MRI** detects cord and soft‑tissue injury + predicts outcome
    - Early MRI can be normal → consider **repeat MRI** if symptoms persist/worsen
- **Management**
    - **Conservative** preferred for normal MRI or pure intraneural findings (edema/contusion) **without compression/instability**
    - **Surgery** only for **compression/instability** or deteriorating/non‑improving neurology
- **Prognosis**
    - Best: **normal MRI** or **edema only**
    - Worst: **major hemorrhage** or **transection**

### Pediatric risk factors

| Pediatric risk factors (<9 y)                         | Exam implication                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ligaments more elastic/lax**                        | Transient displacement with **spontaneous reduction** → normal X‑ray/CT despite cord injury |
| **Facets more horizontal**                            | Increased translation risk (less "bony restraint")                                          |
| **Higher water content in discs**                     | Increased intersegmental mobility                                                           |
| Segments slip more readily due to anterior positions  | Greater tendency to transient subluxation/translation (wording varies)                      |
| **Uncinate processes unformed**                       | Increased lateral movement                                                                  |
| **Growth zone susceptible to fracture**               | Subtle physeal injury may be occult on initial imaging                                      |
| **Large head : weak neck strength**                   | Higher cervical loading; higher fulcrum of motion                                           |
| **Higher cervical fulcrum (≈C2–3) in young children** | Upper cervical vulnerability; fulcrum migrates caudally toward **C5–6** with age            |
## Diagnosis & Workup

- **Dx:** Traumatic neurologic deficit (or transient symptoms) with **normal X‑ray/CT** of the suspected region
- **Best test:** **MRI spine** (region of symptoms; consider whole spine if level unclear)
    - **Timing:** Prefer **≤24 h** for clinic–radiologic mismatch; if early MRI normal but symptoms persist → repeat MRI **6–9 days**
- **Rule-out:**
    - Occult bony injury missed on initial imaging
    - **Extrinsic compression**: traumatic disc herniation, epidural hematoma
    - **Ligamentous instability**
    - **Vascular**: vertebral artery injury; spinal cord infarction
    - Peripheral mimics: brachial plexus injury; peripheral nerve injury
    - Congenital/degenerative contributors (esp. adults): stenosis/spondylosis; os odontoideum (if unstable)
    - Non‑accidental trauma (peds) when mechanism inconsistent (VERIFY)
- **Key imaging:**
    - MRI abnormality reported **22–65%** in some series 

### Imaging findings vs. prognosis

| MRI pattern                       | Definition / threshold                                              | Prognosis                                   |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Normal MRI (SCIWO**NA**)          | No cord signal abnormality; consider repeat MRI if symptoms persist | **Excellent**                               |
| Cord edema                        | T2/STIR hyperintensity without hemorrhage                           | **Good**                                    |
| Cord contusion                    | Focal intramedullary injury ± swelling                              | Variable                                    |
| Minor intramedullary hemorrhage   | Hemorrhage **<50%** of cord area on axial MRI                       | Worse than edema; partial recovery possible |
| Major intramedullary hemorrhage   | Hemorrhage **>50%** of cord area on axial MRI                       | **Poor**                                    |
| Complete disruption / transection | Loss of cord continuity                                             | **Very poor**                               |
## Management

| Strategy                 | Indication                                                                             | Key elements                                                           | Timing                                                                          |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Conservative (preferred) | Normal MRI or **pure intraneural** edema/contusion **without compression/instability** | Immobilize; serial neuro exam; prevent secondary injury; rehab         | Collar/brace **~12 weeks (≈3 months)**; no high‑risk activity **~6 months**     |
| Operative                | **Extraneural compression** or **instability**, or worsening/not‑improving neurology   | Decompression ± stabilization                                          | Consider **early** when indicated (often **≤24 h**; **≤8 h** advocated by some) |
| MPSS (controversial)     | **Not routine** in pediatric SCIWORA; adult acute SCI option if **≤8 h**               | **30 mg/kg** bolus over 15 min → 45‑min pause → **5.4 mg/kg/h × 23 h** | Start **≤8 h** if used; avoid **48 h** infusion                                 |

- Immediate immobilization + acute SCI principles
    - **Indication:** Any suspected SCIWORA (neurologic symptoms with normal X‑ray/CT)
    - **Timing:** **Immediately**
    - Targets/support: avoid hypoxia; avoid hypotension; consider **MAP ≥85 mmHg for 7 days** (institution-dependent)
- Definitive immobilization and activity restriction
    - **Indication:** Confirmed SCIWORA or pending MRI with neurologic symptoms
    - **Timing:** External immobilization **up to 12 weeks (≈3 months)**; reassess at ~3 months
    - **Timing:** Avoid high‑risk activities **~6 months**
- Steroids (MPSS) — controversial
    - **Indication:** **Not routine** in pediatric SCIWORA
	    - adult acute SCI **may be offered as an option** if started **≤8 h** (Guideline 2017; weak recommendation)
    - **Dose:** **30 mg/kg IV bolus over 15 min**, **45‑min pause**, then **5.4 mg/kg/h infusion for 23 h**
    - **Contraindication:** **Routine** high‑dose steroids in pediatric SCIWORA; **48‑h infusion** not recommended (Guideline 2017)
- Surgery
    - **Indication:** MRI evidence of **extraneural compression** (disc herniation/epidural hematoma), **ligamentous injury with instability**, progressive deformity, or **worsening/not‑improving** neurologic findings
    - **Timing:** Consider **early decompression** when indicated (often **≤24 h**; **≤8 h** advocated by some)
    - **Contraindication:** Normal MRI or **pure intraneural** edema/contusion **without compression/instability** → conservative preferred
- Rehabilitation + secondary prevention
    - **Indication:** All SCIWORA with neurologic deficits
    - **Timing:** Initiate as soon as medically stable
    - Key elements: bowel/bladder program; pressure injury prevention; DVT prophylaxis; physiotherapy/OT; education on recurrence risk

> [!checklist] Suspected SCIWORA pathway
> 
> 1. **Initial**: immobilize spine; ABCs; serial neurologic exam
> 
> 2. **Imaging**: X‑ray/CT to exclude fracture/malalignment → if neuro symptoms persist/occur → **MRI**
> 
> 3. **MRI result drives management**
> 
>     - Normal MRI or intraneural edema/contusion only → conservative care + immobilization
> 
>     - Extraneural compression/instability → urgent spine surgery evaluation for decompression/stabilization
> 
> 4. **Follow-up**: if early MRI normal but symptoms persist/worsen → repeat MRI **6–9 days**
> 
> 5. **Return‑to‑risk**: no high‑risk sport/activity **~6 months**

## Pitfalls & Red Flags

> [!warning]
> 
> - **Pitfall:** Clearing C‑spine after normal CT in a patient with transient neurologic symptoms → missed SCIWORA
> 
> - **Red flag:** Any **objective neurologic deficit** after trauma with normal X‑ray/CT → **MRI** + maintain immobilization
> 
> - **Red flag:** **Progressive weakness**, new sensory level, or new bowel/bladder dysfunction
> 
> - **Pitfall:** Assuming early normal MRI excludes injury → repeat MRI if symptoms persist/worsen (**6–9 days**)
> 
> - **Pitfall:** Early return to sports/high‑risk activity (<6 months)
> 
> - **Complication:** Recurrent SCIWORA (can occur even while braced); pressure injuries; DVT; late deformity (context‑dependent)
>
