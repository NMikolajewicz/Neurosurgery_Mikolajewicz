> [!summary] Lumbar interbody fusion review
> 
> - **PLIF** = **usually bilateral** graft placement; **TLIF** = **unilateral** graft placement via **facet take-down / neural foramen**
> 
> - **ALIF / LLIF / Ax-LIF** restore **disc height** → **indirect foraminal decompression** (no direct decompression)
> 
> - **TLIF** needs **less nerve root retraction** than PLIF and is advantageous in **re-operations** with **unilateral pathology** (avoids scar via foramen)
> 
> - Many **PLIFs** show **disc space re-collapse** at ~**1 year**; key concerns: **neural injury** and **graft retropulsion**
> 
> - **Stand-alone PLIF/TLIF** may cause **progressive spondylolisthesis** → usually add **pedicle screws/rods**
> 
> - **ALIF**: relative contraindication in **males** due to **retrograde ejaculation (1–2%; up to 45%)**; watch **great vessel injury** esp. **calcified arteries at L4–5**
> 
> - **LLIF** indications include **mild–moderate** central stenosis (NC), foraminal stenosis, **grade I–II spondylolisthesis**, DDD axial pain, deformity correction, adjacent segment failure
> 
> - **LLIF cannot reliably access L5–S1**; L4–5 access limited if **iliac crest > ~halfway up L4 VB**
> 
> - **LLIF plexus safety zones (Uribe)**: L1–4 = **middle of posterior quarter VB**; L4–5 = **midpoint VB**; **psoas hematoma** may present **1–2 days** post-op, **<1%** incidence
>  

## Definitions & Scope

- Lumbar interbody fusion (LIF): arthrodesis across a lumbar disc space using interbody graft/cage (posterior, anterior, lateral, or axial corridors)
- Indirect decompression: increase disc/foraminal height to enlarge neural foramina without direct posterior decompression (e.g., ALIF, LLIF, Ax-LIF)
- Posterior interbody fusion options
    - PLIF: posterior interbody fusion with usually bilateral graft placement
    - TLIF: posterior interbody fusion with unilateral graft placement via facet take-down / neural foramen
- LLIF naming
    - LLIF = generic term; trademarked variants include XLIF / DLIF; OLIF = pre-psoas variant
- Key goals: stabilize painful/unstable motion segment, restore disc height/lordosis, decompress neural elements (direct or indirect), maximize fusion surface area

## High-Yield Outline

- **Approach selection**
    - Favor **indirect decompression** when **foraminal stenosis** dominates, prior surgery makes nerve exposure risky, or disc space is compressed
    - Favor **direct decompression** for **"pinpoint" central canal stenosis** with preserved disc height/foramina
- **When to consider LIF (common indications)**
    - **Degenerative:** foraminal stenosis with disc height loss, **grade I–II spondylolisthesis**, selected DDD with axial pain, adjacent segment failure
    - **Instability contexts:** fracture/dislocation, tumor, infection-related instability (fusion accepted)
- **Procedure menu**
    - **Posterior:** **PLIF** (bilateral) / **TLIF** (unilateral)
    - **Anterior/lateral/axial (indirect decompression):** **ALIF**, **LLIF/OLIF**, **Ax-LIF (L5–S1 only)**
- **Signature complications
    - **PLIF/TLIF:** neural injury; **graft retropulsion**; **disc re-collapse** (PLIF); **progressive spondylolisthesis** if stand-alone
    - **ALIF:** **retrograde ejaculation**; **great vessel injury** (calcified arteries esp **L4–5**)
    - **LLIF:** thigh weakness/numbness; **psoas hematoma** (delayed 1–2 d; **<1%**); lumbar plexus injury (safe zones)

> [!High yield]
> 
> - **Cloward (1943):** original posterior lumbar interbody fusion concept
> 
> - **TLIF** is a **PLIF variation** placed via **foramen** after **complete facet removal** on that side
> 
> - **LLIF**: memorize **L4–5 safe zone = midpoint VB** (Uribe)
> 
> - **LLIF** does **not reliably access L5–S1** (ilium limitation)
>  

## Diagnosis & Workup

### Clinical assessment

- **Dx:** symptomatic segmental pathology where stabilization and/or disc height restoration is intended (radiculopathy, neurogenic claudication, mechanical axial pain, deformity symptoms)
- **Rule-out:** non-spine pain generators (hip disease, vascular claudication, peripheral neuropathy), infection/malignancy (as indicated)
- **Red flag:** progressive motor deficit, cauda equina syndrome, systemic infection signs, suspected malignancy

| Meyerding grade (spondylolisthesis) | Percent slip |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------ |
| **Grade I**                         | **0–25%**    |
| **Grade II**                        | **26–50%**   |
| **Grade III**                       | **51–75%**   |
| **Grade IV**                        | **76–100%**  |
| **Grade V (spondyloptosis)**        | **>100%**    |
### Imaging 

- **Best test:** imaging is pathology-dependent (MRI for neural compression; standing radiographs for alignment/spondylolisthesis; CT for bony anatomy and endplate detail)
- **Key imaging (approach-specific)**
    - **Indirect decompression candidates:** correlate foraminal stenosis with **disc height loss** (supports disc height restoration strategy)
    - **PLIF/TLIF:** relative contraindication when **disc-space height is well preserved**
    - **ALIF:** assess vascular disease/calcification (risk of great vessel injury esp **L4–5**)
    - **LLIF:** confirm target level feasibility
        - **L5–S1:** **not reliably accessible** via LLIF
        - **L4–5:** may be inaccessible if **iliac crest > ~halfway up L4 VB**

|Pre-op study|Best for|Key imaging output for LIF planning|
|---|---|---|
|Standing AP/lateral lumbar (± long-cassette)|alignment, slip|spondylolisthesis grade, sagittal/coronal balance, disc height|
|Flexion/extension radiographs|dynamic instability|motion at intended fusion level (VERIFY thresholds)|
|MRI lumbar spine|neural compression|central vs foraminal stenosis, disc degeneration, Modic change pattern (if present)|
|CT lumbar spine|bony detail|endplates/osteophytes, pars defects, facet anatomy, pre-op corridor planning|
|CTA/MRA/angiography (selected)|vascular mapping|anterior/lateral access risk stratification (esp calcification/close vessels)|
### Adjunctive tests

- **Best test:** **discography is not a stand-alone test**; if used, favor **concordant pain response** + MRI abnormalities; reserve for **equivocal MRI**, especially adjacent to unequivocally abnormal levels
- **Pitfall:** discography can provoke severe LBP in patients without prior complaints
## Management
### Indications & contraindications

- **Tx:** LIF is considered when symptoms correlate with imaging and nonoperative care has failed, or when instability demands stabilization
- **Indication:** 
	- disc height restoration for foraminal stenosis;
	- **grade I–II spondylolisthesis**; 
	- mild–moderate stenosis appropriate for indirect decompression; 
	- deformity/imbalance correction; 
	- adjacent segment failure
- **Contraindication:** 
	- active infection (systemic or local)
	- uncontrolled medical comorbidity
	- poor bone quality (relative)
### Fusion decision in stenosis (guideline pearls)

- **Tx:** decompression alone vs fusion is driven by **instability** and **spondylolisthesis context**
- **Pitfall:** lumbar instability after decompressive laminectomy for stenosis is **rare (~1%)**; fusion is **rarely required solely** to prevent progression in degenerative stenosis
### Decision pathway

| Fusion guidance in lumbar stenosis (selected practice guideline statements) | Recommendation                                                                                                                                    | Evidence                     |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Stenosis without spondylolisthesis**                                      | **Do not** add in situ posterolateral fusion after decompression if **no preexisting instability** or likely iatrogenic instability (facetectomy) | **Level III**                |
| **Stenosis with instability**                                               | In situ posterolateral fusion is recommended when there is **evidence of spinal instability**                                                     | (Level not shown in excerpt) |
| **Instrumentation (posterolateral fusion + decompression)**                 | Pedicle-screw instrumentation is **not recommended** in conjunction with posterolateral fusion following decompression                            | (Level not shown in excerpt) |
| **Stenosis + degenerative spondylolisthesis needing decompression**         | Posterolateral fusion is recommended                                                                                                              | **Level II**                 |
| **Stenosis + spondylolisthesis (when instability/kyphosis anticipated)**    | Consider pedicle-screw fixation as adjunct when **pre-op instability/kyphosis** or **iatrogenic instability** anticipated                         | **Level III**                |

> [!checklist]
> 
> -  Identify dominant compression: **foraminal** vs **pinpoint central canal**
> 
> -  If **foraminal stenosis dominates** OR **prior surgery** makes nerve exposure risky OR disc space is **compressed** → consider **indirect decompression** options (**ALIF / LLIF / Ax-LIF**)
> 
> -  If **pinpoint central canal stenosis** with preserved disc height/foramina → plan **direct decompression** (laminectomy/laminotomy) ± fusion as indicated
> 
> -  If considering **LLIF**, confirm feasibility: **avoid L5–S1**; assess **iliac crest vs L4 VB** for L4–5 access
> 
> -  If considering **ALIF**, screen for **male retrograde ejaculation risk** and **vascular calcification** (esp **L4–5**)
> 
> -  If posterior approach needed and prior midline scarring/unilateral symptoms → favor **TLIF** (less root retraction; avoids scar via foramen)

### Posterior interbody fusion: PLIF & TLIF

- **Approach:** posterior lumbar interbody fusion techniques to stabilize a motion segment and decompress (directly via posterior corridor)
- **Key operative concept (classic):** **Cloward (1943)**: **bilateral laminectomy** + **aggressive discectomy** + graft into **decorticated disc space****
- **Indication:** 
	- posterior exposure already required; 
	- instability requiring posterior instrumentation; 
	- unilateral pathology or revision favors TLIF
- **Contraindication:** 
	- **well preserved disc-space height** (relative)
- **PLIF (high-yield features):** 
	- **bilateral** graft placement
- **TLIF (high-yield features):** 
	- graft placed from **one side** via **neural foramen** after **complete facet removal**; 
	- **less nerve root retraction**; 
	- helpful for **re-operations** and **unilateral pathology**
- **Complication:** 
	- neural injury (intra-op or delayed) 
	- **retropulsion of bone graft**
- **Pitfall:** 
	- many PLIFs show **disc space re-collapse** at ~**1 year** (question benefit vs discectomy)
	- stand-alone PLIF/TLIF** may cause **progressive spondylolisthesis** → usually add **pedicle screws/rods**

### Anterior lumbar interbody fusion (ALIF)

- **Approach:** anterior interbody fusion via **retroperitoneal** corridor, usually through a **Pfannenstiel incision**
- **Indication:** 
	- restore disc height/lordosis for **indirect foraminal decompression**
	- avoid posterior scar when posterior exposure is high-risk (context-dependent)
- **Contraindication:** 
	- **males (relative)** due to retrograde ejaculation risk
- **Key technical pearl:** 
	- left-sided anterior exposure is often favored (easier mobilization: **aorta vs IVC**; **spleen vs liver**)
	- flex ipsilateral leg to relax psoas for safer retraction of ipsilateral lumbosacral plexus
- **Complication:** 
	- **retrograde ejaculation 1–2%** (reported up to **45%** in some reviews)
	- **great vessel injury**, particularly with **calcified arteries** (esp **L4–5**)
- **Timing:** bowel prep **day before** for complex cases

### Lateral lumbar interbody fusion (LLIF / XLIF / DLIF / OLIF)

- **Approach:** minimally invasive **lateral retroperitoneal transpsoas** interbody fusion
- introduced by **Luiz Pimenta (2001)**; variants include **XLIF/DLIF**; **OLIF** uses a **pre-psoas** corridor
- **Indication:** 
	- **mild–moderate** central stenosis with neurogenic claudication
	- foraminal stenosis (indirect decompression)
	- **grade I–II spondylolisthesis**
	- axial pain with DDD
	- deformity correction
	- adjacent segment failure
- **Advantages:** 
	- less tissue trauma
	- minimal blood loss
	- shorter op time
	- fewer wound issues
	- larger cage
	- early mobilization
	- **no durotomy/CSF leak risk**
- **Contraindication:** 
	- **L5–S1 pathology** (LLIF cannot reliably access due to ilium)
	- prior retroperitoneal surgery on planned side (consider contralateral side)
	- possible inability to access **L4–5** if **iliac crest > ~halfway up L4 VB** (table flex + hip bump may help open corridor)
	- thigh weakness (often temporary), knee weakness (uncommon), thigh numbness, graft subsidence/migration
	- psoas hematoma / psoas retraction injury; onset may be **delayed 1–2 days**; incidence **<1%**; recovery can take **>9 months**
	- lumbar plexus injury risk reduced by Uribe "safe zones" (L1–4 posterior quarter; L4–5 midpoint VB)


> [!tip]
> 
> - **Positioning (LLIF):** prior retroperitoneal surgery → consider **contralateral side up**
> 
> - **Scoliosis correction:** usually place **concave side up** (better access to **L4–5**; corridors converge → fewer/smaller incisions)
> 
> - **Fluoro goal:** "true orthogonal" AP with C-arm **0° tilt** and spinous process centered between pedicles
>

| **Indication**                                      | Mild–moderate central stenosis (NC), foraminal stenosis, **grade I–II spondylolisthesis**, DDD axial pain, deformity correction, adjacent segment failure |

### Axial lumbar interbody fusion (Ax-LIF)

- **Approach:** axial interbody fusion option for **L5–S1 only**
- **Indication:** selected **L5–S1** pathology when an indirect decompression strategy is desired 
### Fusion adjuncts & instrumentation

- supplemental fixation is commonly used to prevent progression/instability with interbody fusion constructs
	- **Indication:** avoid **stand-alone PLIF/TLIF** when progression risk is a concern (progressive spondylolisthesis reported)
- **transfacet pedicle screws** (adjunct fixation)
    - **Indication:** adjunct to **ALIF**, **LLIF** (when no lateral plate), **contralateral to TLIF**, **Ax-LIF**
    - **Contraindication:** cannot use where facet removed (e.g., **TLIF side**) or pars defect in upper level
- pedicle screw sizing 
    - Minor diameter should be **≥5.5 mm** (adult lumbar); 
    - screw length targets **70–80% VB**; 
    - **avoid bicortical/anterior penetration** to reduce risk to great vessels/viscera
- bone graft substitutes 
    - **Indication:** in **ALIF + threaded titanium cage**, **autologous bone or rhBMP-2** is recommended (Level I)
    - **Pitfall:** **rhBMP-2** is **FDA-approved (U.S.) only for ALIF**; other uses are off-label
- **Pitfall:** multiple approaches (**anterior + posterior / "360° fusion"**) increase complication risk; benefit is marginal; **not recommended as routine** for LBP without deformity
### Post-op monitoring

- **Complication:** new neurologic deficit (root/plexus) → urgent neuro exam + imaging as indicated
- **Red flag:** 
	- post-LLIF delayed thigh/knee weakness (day 1–2) → consider psoas injury/hematoma or plexus injury
	- post-ALIF hypotension, abdominal pain/distension → consider vascular injury (urgent evaluation)
- **Complication:** 
	- graft subsidence/migration
	- pseudarthrosis
	- infection
	- adjacent segment disease (monitor clinically + imaging)
## Pitfalls & Red Flags

> [!warning]
> 
> - **Pitfall:** using **LLIF** for **L5–S1** pathology (not reliably accessible)
> 
> - **Pitfall:** missing L4–5 access limitation when **iliac crest > ~halfway up L4 VB** (failed corridor)
> 
> - **Pitfall:** omitting consent for possible conversion to posterior approach if LLIF corridor unsafe (often **L4–5**)
> 
> - **Red flag:** post-LLIF delayed (day 1–2) weakness/pain → consider **psoas hematoma** (**<1%**) or plexus injury
> 
> - **Pitfall:** **stand-alone PLIF/TLIF** → **progressive spondylolisthesis**; supplement fixation
> 
> - **Pitfall:** ignoring PLIF concern for **re-collapse** and **graft retropulsion** risk
> 
> - **Red flag:** post-ALIF hemodynamic instability/abdominal symptoms → suspect **great vessel injury** (higher risk with calcified arteries at **L4–5**)
> 
> - **Pitfall:** routine **360° fusion** (anterior+posterior) for isolated LBP without deformity
