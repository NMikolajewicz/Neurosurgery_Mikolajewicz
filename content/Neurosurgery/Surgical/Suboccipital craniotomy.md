## Common Posterior Fossa Approaches

* Midline suboccipital approach (focus of this note)
* Lateral suboccipital (retromastoid) approach
* Supracerebellar approach (modification of the above)

## Key Indications for Midline Suboccipital Craniotomy

1. **Anatomical Regions Accessed:**
	* Vermian region
	* Posterior inferior pontine and medullary regions
	* Foramen magnum
	* Midline cerebellum
	* ==Pineal region (via supracerebellar== route)
	* ==Fourth ventricle (via telovelar== approach)
2. **Pathologies Treated:**
	* Developmental anomalies (e.g., Chiari malformations)
	* Posterior fossa tumors (metastases, meningiomas, ependymomas, astrocytomas, medulloblastomas)
	* Vascular lesions (aneurysms, cavernous malformations, AVMs)
	* Pineal region tumors

## Preoperative Considerations

* Posterior fossa tumors often cause ==obstructive hydrocephalus==
	* CSF pressure management is crucial before dural opening
	* Avoid preoperative shunting
		* tumor resection may resolve CSF obstruction
* Consider intraoperative ==lumbar CSF drainage== for brain relaxation in communicating hydrocephalus
* ==Neurophysiologic== monitoring for lesions near brainstem and cranial nerves
* Study preoperative imaging thoroughly to identify:
	* Displaced cerebrovascular structures
	* Brainstem pial surface invasion
	* Vascular tumors requiring special attention (potential embolization)

## Operative Anatomy

* Superior nuchal line and inion estimate locations of ==transverse sinuses and torcula==
* Asterion is unreliable for ==transverse-sigmoid junction==
* Dura not covered by bone at ==craniocervical junction==
* ==Vertebral artery== segment over C1 lateral arch is exposed (sulcus arteriosus)
	* landmark: suboccipital triangle

* ==Fourth ventricular floor== contains vital functional areas
* Key arteries:
	* SCA (Superior Cerebellar Artery)
	* AICA (Anterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery)
	* PICA (Posterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery)

## Surgical Positioning Options

1. **Prone Position:**
	* Allows two surgeons to work simultaneously
	* Less practical for supracerebellar approach, especially in obese patients
2. **Sitting Position:**
	* Allows gravity retraction (cerebellum away from tentorium)
	* Advantageous for supracerebellar corridors
	* ==Risks: venous air embolism==, uncomfortable arm postures
3. **Park-Bench Position:**
	* Gravity keeps surgical field clear
	* Surgeon can sit during microsurgical portion

## Surgical Technique

1. ### Setup & Incision:
	
	* Incision from inion to C2 spinous process
	* Consider preparing ==Keen's point== for potential intraoperative ventriculostomy

![[Neurosurgery/Surgical/_resources/Suboccipital_craniotomy.resources/image.3.png]]

2. ### Muscle & Bone Exposure:
	
	* Extend incision through deep layers along ==midline avascular fascial planes==
	* Stay in midline to avoid postoperative neck pain
	* Disconnect muscle attachments from superior nuchal line
	* Use ==caution near C1 arch== (vertebral artery risk)
	* Avoid indiscriminate cautery (brisk venous bleeding indicates nearby artery)

3. ### Craniotomy Options
	
	* Two burr holes over transverse sinuses, dissect dura and sinus walls away from inner skull
	* Use craniotome with footplate for bone flap inscription
	* Final cut over sinuses made without footplate
	* Alternative: two small craniotomies on either side of midline septum
	* For foramen magnum lesions: include C1 laminectomy

![[Neurosurgery/Surgical/_resources/Suboccipital_craniotomy.resources/image.2.png]]

4. ### Dural Opening & Exposure
	
	* ==Dural incision==
		* Y-shaped incision for lateral lesions
		* Midline incision for midline lesions (facilitates watertight closure)
	* ==Open arachnoid over cisterna magna== to drain CSF and achieve brain relaxation
	* Use transvermian or preferably telovelar approach for fourth ventricular lesions

![[Neurosurgery/Surgical/_resources/Suboccipital_craniotomy.resources/image.4.png]]

## Closure

* ==Watertight dural closure== using 4-0 Nurolon sutures
* Pericranium preferred over occipital bone for dural grafting
* ==Valsalva maneuver== to check for CSF leakage
* Replace bone using cranial plates if no cerebellar swelling
* Avoid placing neck muscles under tension (prevents necrosis and postoperative pain)
* Gentle muscle approximation, watertight fascial closure

## Postoperative Care

* ICU observation for 1-2 days
* Prophylactic steroids to minimize aseptic meningitis risk
* If preoperative hydrocephalus was present, wean ventricular catheter gradually
* Monitor for cerebellar edema from aggressive retraction
* Watch for posterior fossa tension symptoms requiring decompression
* Consider risks of partial transverse sinus thrombosis and vermian vein sacrifice

## Potential Complications

* Venous air embolism
* Vertebral artery injury
* CSF leak
* Cerebellar edema and tension
* Transverse sinus thrombosis
* Aseptic meningitis
* Cerebellar mutism (referenced in literature, more likely in pediatrics)

## Key Anatomy

### Suboccipital triangle

* superior oblique (==green==)
	* C1 TP to occipital bone
* inferior oblique (==red==)
	* C1 TP to C2 SP
* rectus capitis posterior major (==yellow==)
	* occipital bone (below inferior nuchal line) to C2 SP
