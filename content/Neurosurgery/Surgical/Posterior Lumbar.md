## Lumbar Laminectomy

* * *

### Room setup and equipment

* table: standard radiolucent table with Wilson frame vs. Jackson spine flat top table
* C-arm: c-arm perpendicular to table
* microscope (optional): microscope in from opposite side of C-arm

### Patient positioning

* prone with arms at 90° max abduction and flexion to prevent axillary nerve injury
* foam padding on chest so that nipples are pointing midline straight down
* pads over ASIS and gel pads on knees
* bilateral TED hose and SCDs

### Superficial Dissection to Expose Spinous Process

1. Localize level of incision with anatomic or radiographic landmarks
2. make midline incision
	1. midline incision with 10blade overlying the spinous processes between paraspinal muscles (erector spinae)
	2. ~3-4cm in length for single level
3. Dissect subcutaneous tissue down to fascia
	1. insert cerebellar retractors x2 for fascial exposure
4. Cauterize lumbodorsal fascia over spinous processes to just lateral of midline

### Deep Dissection down to Lamina

1. Subperiosteal dissection with Cobb along spinous processes
2. Subperiosteal dissection of lamina
	1. cranial to caudal down to lamina
3. Place probe under lamina to identify level radiographically
4. Use Cobb to strip laterally along lamina until facet capsules identified but not violated
5. Place deep retractors for better visualization

### Laminectomy and central decompression

1. Remove spinous processes of operative levels with rongeur
	1. save as bone graft for fusion
2. Remove lamina and identify origin of ligamentum flavum
	1. begin with decompression into canal into inferior half of lamina of cephalad vertebrae first with small curette
	2. ligmentum flavum spans from midpoint of rostral SP to superior edge of caudal SP
	3. burr lamina and to thin and then complete resection with Kerrison rongeur
3. Resect ligamentum flavum

* gently retract ligamentum flavum with woodsen elevator
* resect remaining lamina and ligamentum with Kerrison rongeur of cephalad vertebrae
* resect ligamentum from superior lamina of inferior lamina
* use Kerrison to resect caudad lamina from inferior vertebra

### Lateral Recess and Foraminal Decompression

1. Perform Medial facetectomy
	* decompress medial aspect of facet on each side (2-3 mm of medial facet)
2. Decompress lateral recess
	* locating pedicle key to safe decompression
	* kerrison to undercut medial edge of superior facet of caudad vertebra until medial edge of pedicle visualized
	* identify osteophytes that could impinge exiting nerve root around pedicle
	* undercut remaining superior facet using kerrison rongeur
	* no more than 50% superior facet should be resected
3. Confirm exiting and descending nerve roots are well decompressed
	* descending nerve root should be visualized
4. Check to make sure no disc herniation.
	* Dural sac/nerve root may be retracted to see if there is bulging disc is present

### Wound Closure

1. Irrigation, hemostasis, and drain
	* flush out spine with saline bulb irrigation
2. Deep closure
	* close fascia with 1-0 vicryl
	* need water tight closure and need to decrease dead space for hematoma
3. Superficial closure
	* subcutaneous with 2-0 vicryl
	* skin closure with buried 3-0 monocryl
4. Dressing

* soft incision dressings over spine

## Risk Profile

* * *

<https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29576906/>

	complication (any) rate at 3 postoperative months:
		==4.8%== for laminectomy without fusion vs. 8.3% with fusion;
		
	adjacent segment disease
		over 13 years, ==5.6%== incidence of ASD with non-instrumented lumbar fusions vs. an 18.5% rate of ASD with spinal instrumentation.
		
	re-operation rates for laminectomies for lumbar stenosis
	* <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9473837/>
		re-operation rate were ==14%==
		
		5 years re-operation rate:  10.6% for laminectomy without fusion vs. 18.4% for instrumented arthrodeses

Miscillaneous
Preparation

1. General anesthesia
	
2. **Prone on a Wilson frame.**
		avoid compression to the abdomen and thighs.
		
3. The level to be decompressed identified by **intraoperative X-ray.**
	
4. **Prepare and drape** the skin in a sterile fashion
	

Opening

1. Midline skin incision
	
2. Midline fascial incision and a subperiosteal dissection
		goal: to visualize the posterior aspect of the spine (spinous processes and lamina).
		
3. Dissect the paraspinal muscles over the spinous processes and laminae, up to the level of medial half of the facet.
	
4. Self-retaining retractors (e.g., the McCulloch, Adson-Beckman, or Oberhill retractors) to retract the soft tissues over the laminae.
		Bony exposure is not necessary for elements lateral to the facet joints.
		
5. Leksell rongeur is used to remove the interspinous ligament between the spinous processes intended to be resected. The spinous processes and superficial parts of the laminae are also resected with the Leksell rongeur.
	
6. Half of the spinous process superior to the pathologic level may be removed, as well as half of the one inferior to the level of interest.
	
7. The lamina is thinned out with the help of a high-speed drill or a rongeur.
	

Decompression

1. The ligamentum flavum is detached from the lamina with a blunt dissector such as a Penfield or the curved tip of a McDonald dissector or curved curettes, and then gradually removed with Kerrison punches.
	
2. Once the central part of the canal is decompressed the clearance of the lateral recesses is performed:
	1. the medial aspect of the facet joint is removed. It is initially drilled and thinned down and subsequently resected by means of a 45-degree angled Kerrison rongeur.
		
	2. The ligamentum flavum on the medial aspect of the pedicle is removed with the same technique.
		
	3. Foraminotomies can be performed if necessary, identifying the exiting nerve root and following it to the pedicle. Any exophytic bone can removed with either a curette placed between the nerve root and the pedicle with an upward movement or with a Kerrison punch.
		
	4. The foramen is considered free when the tip of a blunt dissector (such as the Woodson elevator) can easily pass between the root and the pedicle.
		

Closure

1. The surgical bed is washed with antibiotic (bacitracin, and in certain cases a corticosteroid).
	
2. Obtaining hemostasis is crucial. The bony edges of the laminectomy can be a source of conspicuous bleeding and should be carefully secured with bone wax.
	
3. The self-retaining retractor is released partially to reduce tension from the paraspinal muscles.
	
4. Bipolar coagulation of the obvious bleeders over the muscles avoids subsequent hematoma formations.
	
5. The muscles are then closed with absorbable sutures in an interrupted fashion while avoiding excessive tension, which could lead to postoperative pain.
	
6. The closure of the fascia is important and is preferably done with interrupted 0 Vicryl sutures.
	
7. The subcutis can be anchored to the fascia to decrease the chances of a virtual space that can fill with blood.
	
8. Skin can be closed in several ways including nylon (Nurolon™, Ethicon, Somerville, NJ) running suture or staples.
