## Burrhole Placement

### Unilateral

(A) Frontal area 2 cm in front of the coronal suture and close to the skin incision
(B) Parietal area just posterior to the parietal bone and close to the skin incision
(C) Temporal squama
(D) Key hole area behind the zygomatic arch of the frontal bone.
![[Neurosurgery/Surgical/_resources/Decompressive_Craniectomy.resources/image.png]]

### Bifrontal

(A) Two burr holes just behind the coronal suture, 1 cm apart from midline on each side
(B) Both key hole areas behind the zygomatic arch of the frontal bone
(C) Both squamous parts of the temporal bones.

## Complications

50% cumulative complication rate

		### CSF absorption disorder
	
	* subdural hygroma
		* unbalance of production and resorption of CSF
		* ruptured arachnoid create one-way valve for CSF flow
	* hydrocephalus
		* 2-30% risk
		* improves with cranioplasty
		### postoperative hematoma
	
	* 7.4% risk
	* risk factors: DIC/platelet dysfunction, secondary to severe bleeding
		### syndrome of trephined
	
	* clinical syndrome: H/A, dizziness, irritability, seizure, discomfort, psychiatric symptoms
	* mechanism: unclear
		* possibly CSF flow abnormalities, direct atmospheric pressure on brain
	* prevention: early cranioplasty
		### surgical site infection
	
	* 7% risk
	* opening of frontal sinus contaminates the surgical field
		### wound healing
	
	* 10% risk of healing complication
	* STA injury reduces flow to scalp flap
		* necrosis of surrounding tissue

## Outcomes

* survival rate increased by DC
* severe disability, including vegetative state, not prevented.
