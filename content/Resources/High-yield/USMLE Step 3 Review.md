# USMLE STEP 3 Last-Minute Study Guide

## HIGH-YIELD TOPICS BY SYSTEM

### CARDIOVASCULAR

**Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS)**

* STEMI: PCI within 90 min (door-to-balloon) or fibrinolytics within 30 min if no PCI available
* NSTEMI/UA: Dual antiplatelet (ASA + P2Y12 inhibitor), anticoagulation, beta-blocker, statin, ACE-I
* Post-MI complications: Free wall rupture (3-5d), VSD (3-5d), papillary muscle rupture (2-7d)

**Heart Failure**

* Acute: Loop diuretics, nitroglycerin if SBP >90, BiPAP if pulmonary edema
* Chronic: ACE-I/ARB, beta-blocker (carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol), spironolactone if EF <35%
* BNP >100 pg/mL suggests HF

**Arrhythmias**

* AFib rate control: Beta-blocker or CCB (diltiazem/verapamil)
* AFib anticoagulation: CHA₂DS₂-VASc score ≥2 in men, ≥3 in women
* VT with pulse: Amiodarone or synchronized cardioversion
* VT without pulse: Defibrillation + epinephrine + amiodarone

### PULMONARY

**Asthma**

* Acute: SABA, ipratropium, corticosteroids (prednisone 40-60mg)
* Chronic: Step-up therapy - SABA PRN → low-dose ICS → LABA/ICS combo

**COPD**

* Acute exacerbation: O₂ (target 88-92%), bronchodilators, steroids, antibiotics if purulent sputum
* Chronic: LAMA or LABA first-line, ICS if frequent exacerbations

**PE**

* Wells criteria: PE likely if >4 points → CT angiography
* Treatment: Anticoagulation (heparin → warfarin/DOAC), thrombolysis if massive PE with hemodynamic instability

### INFECTIOUS DISEASE

**Pneumonia**

* CAP outpatient: Amoxicillin or doxycycline or macrolide
* CAP inpatient: Ceftriaxone + azithromycin OR fluoroquinolone
* HAP/VAP: Anti-pseudomonal coverage (pip-tazo, cefepime, or carbapenem) + vancomycin if MRSA risk

**UTI**

* Uncomplicated: Nitrofurantoin 5 days, TMP-SMX 3 days, or fosfomycin single dose
* Complicated/Pyelonephritis: Fluoroquinolone or ceftriaxone

**Meningitis**

* Empiric: Vancomycin + ceftriaxone + dexamethasone (before or with first antibiotic dose)
* Add ampicillin if >50 years or immunocompromised (Listeria coverage)

### GASTROENTEROLOGY

**GI Bleeding**

* Upper: PPI, octreotide if variceal, antibiotics if cirrhosis
* Lower: Colonoscopy within 24h if hemodynamically stable

**Pancreatitis**

* Acute: NPO, IV fluids (250-500 mL/hr), pain control
* Ranson criteria ≥3 = severe pancreatitis

**Cirrhosis Complications**

* SBP: Diagnostic paracentesis, empiric ceftriaxone if PMN >250
* Hepatic encephalopathy: Lactulose (goal 2-3 BM/day), rifaximin
* Variceal bleeding: Octreotide, antibiotics, band ligation

### ENDOCRINE

**Diabetes**

* DKA: IV fluids → insulin → potassium replacement when K <5.3
* HHS: Aggressive IV fluids (1-1.5L/hr initially)
* A1c goals: <7% general, <8% if elderly/comorbidities

**Thyroid**

* Hyperthyroidism: Beta-blocker acutely, methimazole or PTU (if pregnant 1st trimester)
* Thyroid storm: PTU, propranolol, hydrocortisone, iodine (give 1hr after PTU)

### RENAL

**AKI**

* Prerenal: FeNa <1%, BUN:Cr >20:1
* Intrinsic: FeNa >2%, muddy brown casts (ATN)
* Post-renal: Hydronephrosis on ultrasound

**Hyperkalemia**

* K >6.5 or EKG changes: Calcium gluconate (cardiac protection) → insulin/glucose, albuterol → kayexalate/dialysis

**Acid-Base**

* Anion gap = Na - (Cl + HCO₃), normal 8-12
* MUDPILES: Methanol, Uremia, DKA, Propylene glycol, Iron/INH, Lactic acidosis, Ethylene glycol, Salicylates

### RHEUMATOLOGY

**Septic Arthritis**

* Arthrocentesis: WBC >50,000 (>75% PMN)
* Empiric antibiotics: Vancomycin + ceftriaxone

**Gout**

* Acute: NSAIDs, colchicine, or steroids
* Chronic: Allopurinol (start 2-4 weeks after acute attack resolves)

### NEUROLOGY

**Stroke**

* Ischemic: tPA within 4.5 hrs if eligible, ASA within 48 hrs
* Hemorrhagic: Reverse anticoagulation, BP control (SBP <140)

**Seizures**

* Status epilepticus: Lorazepam → fosphenytoin/valproate → propofol/midazolam
* First seizure: No anti-epileptics unless high risk features

### OBSTETRICS

**Hypertensive Disorders**

* Preeclampsia with severe features: Delivery, MgSO₄ for seizure prophylaxis
* Eclampsia: MgSO₄ 4-6g IV load → 2g/hr maintenance

**Labor**

* Active labor: ≥6cm dilated
* Prolonged second stage: Nulliparous >3hrs (4hrs with epidural), Multiparous >2hrs (3hrs with epidural)

### PEDIATRICS

**Fever Without Source**

* <28 days: Full sepsis workup, admit, empiric antibiotics
* 29-60 days: Blood culture, UA, consider LP
* 60 days: Observe if well-appearing

**Respiratory**

* Bronchiolitis: Supportive care only
* Croup: Dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg (max 10mg)
* Epiglottitis: Secure airway, ceftriaxone

### PSYCHIATRY

**Depression**

* First-line: SSRI (allow 4-6 weeks for response)
* Severe/psychotic features: ECT

**Psychosis**

* Acute agitation: Haloperidol + lorazepam + diphenhydramine
* Maintenance: Atypical antipsychotic (monitor metabolic syndrome)

## PREVENTIVE MEDICINE

### Cancer Screening

* Breast: Mammography age 50-74 q2 years
* Cervical: Pap age 21-65 (21-29 q3yrs, 30-65 Pap+HPV q5yrs)
* Colon: Age 45-75 (colonoscopy q10yrs or FIT yearly)
* Lung: Age 50-80 with 20 pack-year history, annual low-dose CT

### Immunizations

* Influenza: Annual for all >6 months
* Pneumococcal: PCV15/20 for all ≥65 years
* Zoster: Age ≥50 (2 doses Shingrix)
* Tdap: Once as adult, then Td q10 years

## BIOSTATISTICS & ETHICS

### Key Formulas

* Sensitivity = TP/(TP+FN)
* Specificity = TN/(TN+FP)
* PPV = TP/(TP+FP)
* NPV = TN/(TN+FN)
* NNT = 1/ARR
* RR = Risk in exposed/Risk in unexposed
* OR = (AD)/(BC)

### Ethics Principles

* **Autonomy**: Informed consent, respect patient decisions
* **Beneficence**: Act in patient's best interest
* **Non-maleficence**: Do no harm
* **Justice**: Fair distribution of resources

### Capacity Assessment

Must understand:

1. Nature of condition
2. Proposed treatment
3. Alternatives
4. Risks/benefits
5. Consequences of refusing

## CCS CASES HIGH-YIELD TIPS

### Initial Orders (Most Cases)

* VS monitoring
* IV access
* CBC, BMP, LFTs
* UA
* EKG if cardiac concern
* CXR if respiratory concern

### Time Management

* Advance clock appropriately (minutes for acute, hours-days for chronic)
* Order tests → advance time → check results → treat → reassess

### Common Pitfalls

* Forgetting to discontinue home meds that need holding
* Not ordering preventive measures (DVT prophylaxis, GI prophylaxis)
* Forgetting to counsel (smoking cessation, safe sex)
* Not moving patient to appropriate location

### Location Changes

* ED → Floor: Stable patients needing admission
* ED → ICU: Unstable vitals, need for pressors/intubation
* Floor → ICU: Deteriorating patient
* ICU → Floor: Stable for >24 hours

## RAPID REVIEW MNEMONICS

**VINDICATE (Differential Diagnosis)** Vascular, Infectious, Neoplastic, Drugs, Iatrogenic, Congenital, Autoimmune, Trauma, Endocrine
**OPQRST (Pain Assessment)** Onset, Provocation/Palliation, Quality, Region/Radiation, Severity, Timing
**CAGE (Alcohol Screening)** Cut down, Annoyed, Guilty, Eye-opener

## FINAL REMINDERS

1. Read questions carefully - look for "MOST appropriate next step"
2. Choose safe, evidence-based options
3. Consider patient autonomy and shared decision-making
4. When in doubt, choose the least invasive diagnostic test first
5. Always stabilize vital signs before diagnostic workup
6. Document everything in CCS cases
7. Don't forget health maintenance/prevention

Good luck on your exam!

# USMLE STEP 3 Comprehensive Last-Night Study Guide

## EXAM STRUCTURE & STRATEGY

* **Day 1**: Foundations of Independent Practice (FIP) - 232 MCQs in 6 blocks (38-39 questions/block, 60 min each)
* **Day 2**: Advanced Clinical Medicine (ACM) - 180 MCQs in 6 blocks (30 questions/block, 45 min each) + 13 Computer-based Case Simulations (CCS, 10-20 min each)
* **Question Types**: Single best answer, multiple choice, drag-and-drop ordering
* **Score Report**: Pass/Fail with performance profile by competency area

## CARDIOVASCULAR SYSTEM

### Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS)

**STEMI Management**

* Door-to-balloon ≤90 min for PCI (preferred) or door-to-needle ≤30 min for fibrinolytics
* Contraindications to fibrinolytics: Prior ICH, stroke <3 months, bleeding, recent surgery
* All patients: ASA 325mg, P2Y12 inhibitor (ticagrelor preferred), heparin, beta-blocker, statin, ACE-I
* Cardiogenic shock: PCI regardless of time, consider IABP or Impella

**NSTEMI/Unstable Angina**

* TIMI Risk Score: Age ≥65, ≥3 CAD risk factors, known CAD, ASA use, severe angina, ST changes, + markers
* High risk (TIMI ≥3): Early invasive (24-48h)
* Low risk: Conservative with stress test

**Post-MI Complications (Timeline Critical)**

* 0-24h: Arrhythmias (VF most common cause of death)
* 2-4d: Pericarditis (diffuse ST elevation, PR depression)
* 3-5d: Free wall rupture (sudden hypotension, tamponade)
* 3-5d: VSD (harsh holosystolic murmur, step-up on right heart cath)
* 2-7d: Papillary muscle rupture (acute MR, pulmonary edema)
* Weeks: LV aneurysm (persistent ST elevation), Dressler syndrome

### Heart Failure

**Acute Decompensated HF**

* Warm + Wet: Diuretics (furosemide 40mg IV = 80mg PO)
* Cold + Wet: Diuretics + inotropes (dobutamine > milrinone)
* Cold + Dry: Careful fluid challenge, inotropes
* BiPAP for pulmonary edema (decreases preload + afterload)

**Chronic HF with Reduced EF (<40%)**

* Mortality benefit: ACE-I/ARB/ARNI, beta-blocker (carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol), MRA (spironolactone/eplerenone if EF <35%), SGLT2i
* Symptomatic: Loop diuretics, digoxin
* Device therapy: ICD if EF ≤35% + symptoms, CRT if EF ≤35% + QRS ≥150ms + LBBB

**HF with Preserved EF**

* Control BP, volume status, rate control if AFib
* SGLT2 inhibitors show benefit
* No proven mortality benefit for traditional HF meds

### Arrhythmias

**Atrial Fibrillation**

* Rate control: Beta-blocker or non-DHP CCB (diltiazem/verapamil)
* Rhythm control: Consider if symptomatic, new onset <48h, young
* Cardioversion: TEE if 48h-4 weeks, anticoagulate 4 weeks if >4 weeks
* CHA₂DS₂-VASc: CHF (1), HTN (1), Age ≥75 (2), DM (1), Stroke/TIA (2), Vascular disease (1), Age 65-74 (1), Sex female (1)
* Anticoagulate if score ≥2 men, ≥3 women

**Wide Complex Tachycardia**

* Assume VT until proven otherwise
* VT with pulse: Amiodarone 150mg over 10 min or synchronized cardioversion
* VT without pulse: Immediate defibrillation + CPR
* SVT with aberrancy: Adenosine (if regular) or AV nodal agents

**Bradycardia**

* Symptomatic: Atropine 0.5mg → transcutaneous pacing → transvenous pacing
* Pacemaker indications: 3rd degree AV block, Mobitz II, symptomatic bradycardia

### Hypertension

**Hypertensive Emergency (End-organ damage)**

* Goals: Reduce BP by 25% in first hour, then to 160/100 over 2-6h
* Agents: Nicardipine, labetalol, nitroprusside, hydralazine
* Special situations: Aortic dissection: Target SBP <120 in 20 min (esmolol + nitroprusside) Stroke: Permissive HTN unless >220/120 or tPA candidate Preeclampsia: Labetalol or hydralazine, MgSO₄

**Chronic HTN**

* First-line: Thiazide, ACE-I/ARB, CCB
* Compelling indications: DM: ACE-I/ARB CKD: ACE-I/ARB CAD: Beta-blocker + ACE-I Black patients: Thiazide or CCB

### Valvular Disease

**Aortic Stenosis**

* Symptoms (SAD): Syncope, Angina, Dyspnea → AVR indicated
* Severe AS: Valve area <1.0 cm², mean gradient >40 mmHg, jet velocity >4 m/s

**Mitral Regurgitation**

* Acute: Pulmonary edema, surgery urgent
* Chronic: Surgery when symptomatic or EF <60% or LVESD >40mm

**Endocarditis Prophylaxis**

* Only for: Prosthetic valve, previous endocarditis, congenital heart disease, cardiac transplant with valvulopathy
* Procedures: Dental with manipulation, respiratory tract incision, infected skin

## PULMONARY SYSTEM

### Asthma

**Acute Exacerbation**

* Mild-moderate: SABA q20min × 3, oral steroids
* Severe: Continuous nebs, IV steroids, add ipratropium, consider IV Mg
* Impending respiratory failure: Intubate (risk of auto-PEEP)
* Discharge: Peak flow >70%, on steroids + controller

**Chronic Management**

* Step 1: SABA PRN
* Step 2: Low-dose ICS
* Step 3: Low-dose ICS/LABA or medium-dose ICS
* Step 4: Medium-dose ICS/LABA
* Step 5: High-dose ICS/LABA + LAMA or oral steroids
* Step 6: Add biologics (anti-IgE, anti-IL5)

### COPD

**Acute Exacerbation**

* O₂ target 88-92% (avoid CO₂ retention)
* Bronchodilators: Albuterol + ipratropium
* Steroids: Prednisone 40mg × 5 days
* Antibiotics if 2/3: Increased dyspnea, sputum volume, sputum purulence
* BiPAP indications: pH <7.35, PaCO₂ >45, RR >25

**Chronic Management (GOLD)**

* Group A (low symptoms, low risk): Bronchodilator
* Group B (high symptoms, low risk): LAMA or LABA
* Group E (high exacerbation risk): LABA/LAMA/ICS
* Consider roflumilast, azithromycin for frequent exacerbations

### Pulmonary Embolism

**Diagnosis**

* Wells Score: Clinical signs DVT (3), PE most likely (3), HR >100 (1.5), immobilization (1.5), prior PE/DVT (1.5), hemoptysis (1), cancer (1)
* Low risk (<4): D-dimer → if positive, CTA
* High risk (>4): CTA directly
* Pregnant: V/Q scan preferred

**Treatment**

* Hemodynamically stable: Anticoagulation (heparin → warfarin/DOAC)
* Massive PE (hypotension): Thrombolysis (tPA 100mg over 2h)
* Submassive PE (RV strain): Consider thrombolysis case-by-case
* Contraindication to anticoagulation: IVC filter

### Pneumonia

**Community-Acquired (CAP)**

* Outpatient healthy: Amoxicillin 1g TID or doxycycline or macrolide
* Outpatient with comorbidities: Augmentin or cephalosporin + macrolide or respiratory fluoroquinolone
* Inpatient non-ICU: Beta-lactam + macrolide or respiratory fluoroquinolone
* ICU: Beta-lactam + macrolide or Beta-lactam + fluoroquinolone
* Add vancomycin if MRSA risk

**Hospital-Acquired (HAP)**

* Non-MDR risk: Piperacillin-tazobactam or cefepime or levofloxacin
* MDR risk: Dual coverage (pip-tazo + tobramycin) + vancomycin

**Ventilator-Associated (VAP)**

* Clinical Pulmonary Infection Score (CPIS) >6 suggests VAP
* Empiric: Anti-pseudomonal beta-lactam + anti-pseudomonal non-beta-lactam + anti-MRSA

### Interstitial Lung Disease

**IPF**

* UIP pattern on HRCT: Honeycombing, traction bronchiectasis
* Antifibrotics: Pirfenidone, nintedanib
* Avoid steroids unless acute exacerbation

**Sarcoidosis**

* Stages: I (hilar LAD), II (LAD + infiltrates), III (infiltrates only), IV (fibrosis)
* Treatment if symptomatic or organ involvement: Steroids first-line

### Sleep Disorders

**OSA**

* AHI ≥5 with symptoms or AHI ≥15
* CPAP first-line
* Driving risk assessment critical

## INFECTIOUS DISEASE

### Sepsis & Septic Shock

**qSOFA (≥2 = high risk)**

* RR ≥22
* Altered mental status
* SBP ≤100

**Management Bundle (Within 1 hour)**

* Measure lactate
* Blood cultures before antibiotics
* Broad-spectrum antibiotics
* 30 mL/kg crystalloid for hypotension or lactate ≥4
* Vasopressors for MAP ≥65 (norepinephrine first-line)

### CNS Infections

**Bacterial Meningitis**

* Empiric therapy: <1 month: Ampicillin + cefotaxime + gentamicin 1-23 months: Vancomycin + ceftriaxone 2-50 years: Vancomycin + ceftriaxone
	50 or immunocompromised: Vancomycin + ceftriaxone + ampicillin
	
* Dexamethasone: Give with or before first antibiotic dose (reduces mortality in pneumococcal)

**Encephalitis**

* HSV most common: Acyclovir 10mg/kg IV q8h
* Consider arboviruses in season
* LP may show RBC in temporal lobe (HSV)

**Brain Abscess**

* Ring-enhancing with restricted diffusion
* Empiric: Vancomycin + ceftriaxone + metronidazole
* Surgery if >2.5cm, mass effect, or failed medical therapy

### Endocarditis

**Duke Criteria**

* Major: Positive blood cultures (2 separate), echo findings, new regurgitant murmur
* Minor: Predisposition, fever, vascular phenomena, immunologic phenomena, blood cultures

**Empiric Therapy**

* Native valve: Vancomycin + gentamicin
* Prosthetic valve: Vancomycin + gentamicin + rifampin
* Surgery indications: Heart failure, perivalvular abscess, persistent bacteremia, large vegetation, systemic emboli

### HIV/AIDS

**Initial Evaluation**

* CD4 count, viral load, genotype
* Screen: TB, HBV, HCV, syphilis, toxoplasma

**Opportunistic Infection Prophylaxis**

* CD4 <200: PCP prophylaxis (TMP-SMX)
* CD4 <100: Toxoplasma prophylaxis (TMP-SMX)
* CD4 <50: MAC prophylaxis (azithromycin)

**ART Initiation**

* Start immediately in all patients
* Typical regimen: 2 NRTI + INSTI

### Skin/Soft Tissue Infections

**Cellulitis**

* Non-purulent: Beta-lactam (cephalexin, dicloxacillin)
* Purulent or MRSA risk: TMP-SMX, doxycycline, or clindamycin
* Severe: Vancomycin + piperacillin-tazobactam

**Necrotizing Fasciitis**

* Type 1 (polymicrobial): Diabetics, immunocompromised
* Type 2 (GAS): Rapid progression
* LRINEC score ≥6 suggests necrotizing infection
* Treatment: Urgent surgery + broad antibiotics + clindamycin

### UTI

**Uncomplicated Cystitis**

* Nitrofurantoin 100mg BID × 5d
* TMP-SMX DS BID × 3d
* Fosfomycin 3g × 1
* Avoid fluoroquinolones for uncomplicated

**Pyelonephritis**

* Outpatient: Fluoroquinolone × 7d or TMP-SMX × 14d
* Inpatient: Ceftriaxone or fluoroquinolone
* Blood cultures if systemic symptoms

**Catheter-Associated UTI**

* Only treat if symptomatic
* Remove/replace catheter
* 7-14 days antibiotics

## GASTROENTEROLOGY

### GI Bleeding

**Upper GI Bleed**

* Risk stratification: Glasgow-Blatchford score
* Pre-endoscopy: PPI (doesn't improve mortality but reduces rebleeding)
* Variceal: Octreotide + antibiotics + endoscopy within 12h
* Non-variceal: Endoscopy within 24h

**Lower GI Bleed**

* Most stop spontaneously
* Colonoscopy within 24h if stable
* CT angiography if brisk bleeding
* Tagged RBC scan if intermittent

### Liver Disease

**Acute Liver Failure**

* Definition: INR >1.5 + encephalopathy + illness <26 weeks
* King's College Criteria for transplant: Acetaminophen: pH <7.3 or (INR >6.5 + Cr >3.4 + encephalopathy) Non-acetaminophen: INR >6.5 or any 3 of (age <10 or >40, INR >3.5, bili >17.4, duration >7d)

**Cirrhosis Complications**

* Ascites: Diagnostic paracentesis, SAAG ≥1.1 = portal HTN Treatment: Na restriction, spironolactone + furosemide (100:40 ratio)
* SBP: PMN >250, treat with ceftriaxone + albumin
* HRS: Cr >1.5, no response to volume, no shock Treatment: Albumin + octreotide + midodrine or terlipressin
* Hepatic encephalopathy: Lactulose (goal 2-3 BM/day) + rifaximin
* Variceal bleeding: Band ligation + octreotide + antibiotics

**MELD Score Components**

* Bilirubin, INR, Creatinine, Sodium
* 20 = high 3-month mortality

### Pancreatitis

**Acute Pancreatitis**

* Diagnosis: 2/3 of pain, lipase >3× ULN, imaging
* Severity: Ranson's ≥3, APACHE II ≥8
* Management: NPO, aggressive IV fluids (250-500 mL/hr), pain control
* Complications: Pseudocyst (>4 weeks), necrosis (may need necrosectomy if infected)

**Chronic Pancreatitis**

* Pain management: Enzymes, avoid alcohol
* Exocrine insufficiency: Pancreatic enzymes with meals
* Endocrine insufficiency: Brittle diabetes

### IBD

**Crohn's Disease**

* Mild-moderate: Budesonide or mesalamine
* Moderate-severe: Steroids → immunomodulators (AZA/6-MP) → biologics (anti-TNF)
* Perianal: Antibiotics, anti-TNF, consider seton

**Ulcerative Colitis**

* Mild-moderate: 5-ASA (oral + topical)
* Moderate-severe: Steroids → immunomodulators → biologics
* Acute severe: IV steroids × 3-5d → if no response, cyclosporine or infliximab
* Toxic megacolon: NPO, decompress, antibiotics, surgery if no improvement

## NEPHROLOGY

### Acute Kidney Injury

**Prerenal**

* FeNa <1%, FeUrea <35%, BUN:Cr >20:1
* Hyaline casts
* Responds to volume

**Intrinsic**

* ATN: FeNa >2%, muddy brown casts
* AIN: Eosinophils, WBC casts, drug exposure
* Glomerulonephritis: RBC casts, proteinuria

**Postrenal**

* Bilateral obstruction or single kidney
* Ultrasound shows hydronephrosis
* Post-obstructive diuresis possible

**Indications for Dialysis (AEIOU)**

* Acidosis (refractory)
* Electrolytes (K >6.5 with EKG changes)
* Intoxication (methanol, ethylene glycol, lithium)
* Overload (volume)
* Uremia (pericarditis, encephalopathy)

### Electrolyte Disorders

**Hyponatremia**

* Assess volume status first
* Hypovolemic: Normal saline
* Euvolemic: Fluid restriction, salt tabs
* Hypervolemic: Fluid restriction, diuretics
* Correct slowly: <10-12 mEq/L in 24h (risk of ODS)
* Severe symptoms: 3% saline 100mL bolus, may repeat × 2

**Hyperkalemia**

* EKG changes: Peaked T → PR prolongation → wide QRS → sine wave
* Treatment sequence: Calcium gluconate (cardiac protection) Insulin + glucose, albuterol (shift K) Kayexalate, diuretics, dialysis (remove K)

**Hypercalcemia**

* Symptoms: Stones, bones, groans, psychiatric overtones
* Treatment: IV fluids → loop diuretics → bisphosphonates → calcitonin
* If malignancy: Consider denosumab

### Acid-Base

**Primary Disorders**

* Metabolic acidosis: pH <7.35, HCO3 <22 Anion gap: MUDPILES Non-gap: Diarrhea, RTA, ureterosigmoidostomy
* Metabolic alkalosis: pH >7.45, HCO3 >26 Saline-responsive: Vomiting, diuretics Saline-resistant: Hyperaldosteronism
* Respiratory acidosis: pH <7.35, PCO2 >45
* Respiratory alkalosis: pH >7.45, PCO2 <35

**Compensation Rules**

* Metabolic acidosis: PCO2 = 1.5(HCO3) + 8 ± 2
* Metabolic alkalosis: PCO2 increases 0.7 for each 1 mEq/L HCO3 increase
* Acute respiratory: pH changes 0.08 for each 10 mmHg PCO2
* Chronic respiratory: pH changes 0.03 for each 10 mmHg PCO2

### Chronic Kidney Disease

**Stages (by GFR)**

1. ≥90 with kidney damage
2. 60-89 with kidney damage 3a. 45-59 3b. 30-44
3. 15-29
4. <15 or dialysis

**Management**

* BP control: ACE-I/ARB if proteinuria
* Anemia: ESA if Hgb <10
* Bone disease: Phosphate binders, vitamin D
* Acidosis: Sodium bicarbonate if HCO3 <22
* Refer to nephrology: Stage 4 or GFR declining >5/year
