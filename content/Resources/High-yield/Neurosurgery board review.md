## Neurosurgery Board Exam Last-Minute Study Guide

### NEUROANATOMY ESSENTIALS
#### Vascular Anatomy

**Circle of Willis Components**

* ICA → Ophthalmic → PComm → Anterior choroidal
* ACA: A1 (pre-comm) → A2 (post-comm)
* MCA: M1 (horizontal) → M2 (insular) → M3 (opercular) → M4 (cortical)
* Basilar → SCA → PCA (P1-P4 segments)

**Critical Perforators**

* Lenticulostriate (MCA): Basal ganglia, internal capsule
* Thalamoperforators (PCA/PComm): Thalamus
* Recurrent artery of Heubner (ACA): Caudate head, anterior limb IC

**Venous Anatomy**

* Superficial: Superior sagittal sinus → transverse → sigmoid → IJV
* Deep: Internal cerebral veins + basal veins → vein of Galen → straight sinus
* Critical: Vein of Labbé (temporal lobe drainage)

#### Cranial Nerve Anatomy

**Skull Base Foramina**

* Optic canal: CN II, ophthalmic artery
* Superior orbital fissure: CN III, IV, V1, VI, superior ophthalmic vein
* Foramen rotundum: CN V2
* Foramen ovale: CN V3, accessory meningeal artery
* Foramen spinosum: Middle meningeal artery
* Internal auditory canal: CN VII, VIII, labyrinthine artery
* Jugular foramen: CN IX, X, XI, sigmoid sinus/IJV

#### Spine Anatomy

**Ligaments (Anterior to Posterior)**

* ALL → PLL → Ligamentum flavum → Interspinous → Supraspinous
* Cruciate ligament: Transverse (C1) + alar ligaments

**Spinal Cord Blood Supply**

* Anterior spinal artery: Anterior 2/3 cord
* Posterior spinal arteries: Posterior columns
* Artery of Adamkiewicz: T9-L2 (usually left)

### TRAUMA

#### Head Trauma

**GCS Components**

* Eye: 4 (spontaneous), 3 (voice), 2 (pain), 1 (none)
* Verbal: 5 (oriented), 4 (confused), 3 (inappropriate), 2 (incomprehensible), 1 (none)
* Motor: 6 (obeys), 5 (localizes), 4 (withdraws), 3 (flexion), 2 (extension), 1 (none)

**Indications for ICP Monitor**

* GCS ≤8 with abnormal CT
* GCS ≤8 with normal CT + 2 of: age >40, SBP <90, posturing

**ICP Management Tiers**

1. HOB 30°, neck neutral, sedation
2. Osmotherapy (mannitol 0.25-1 g/kg or 3% saline)
3. Hyperventilation (PaCO2 30-35)
4. Barbiturate coma, hypothermia
5. Decompressive craniectomy

**Surgical Indications**

* EDH: >30cc, >15mm thickness, >5mm MLS
* SDH: >10mm thickness or >5mm MLS
* Depressed skull fracture: >1 skull thickness, open, cosmetic

#### Spine Trauma

**ASIA Scale**

* A: Complete - no motor/sensory below level
* B: Incomplete - sensory only below level
* C: Motor <3/5 below level
* D: Motor ≥3/5 below level
* E: Normal

**Instability Criteria**

* Denis 3-column: 2+ columns = unstable
* White & Panjabi point system: >5 points = unstable
* TLICS: ≥5 points = surgery

**Cervical Spine Clearance**

* NEXUS: No imaging if all negative - no midline tenderness, normal alertness, no intoxication, no neuro deficit, no distracting injury
* Canadian C-spine: Multiple criteria including age ≥65, dangerous mechanism

### VASCULAR

#### Aneurysms

**Hunt-Hess Grading**

1. Asymptomatic or mild headache
2. Severe headache, nuchal rigidity
3. Drowsy, confused, mild focal deficit
4. Stuporous, moderate-severe hemiparesis
5. Coma, decerebrate posturing

**Fisher Grade (SAH on CT)**

1. No blood
2. <1mm thick
3. 1mm thick
4. IVH or IPH

**Management**

* Secure within 24-72 hours
* Nimodipine 60mg q4h × 21 days
* Goal SBP <160 until secured
* Triple-H therapy for vasospasm (abandoned at most centers in favor of induced hypertension alone)

**Size Thresholds**

* Anterior circulation: ≥7mm
* Posterior circulation: ≥5mm
* Growth on serial imaging

#### AVMs

**Spetzler-Martin Grade**

* Size: <3cm (1), 3-6cm (2), >6cm (3)
* Eloquence: No (0), Yes (1)
* Venous drainage: Superficial (0), Deep (1)
* Grade I-II: Surgery favored
* Grade IV-V: Conservative or radiosurgery

**Treatment Modalities**

* Surgery: Grade I-III, accessible location
* Embolization: Adjuvant, palliative, or curative for select cases
* Radiosurgery: <3cm, deep location

#### Carotid Disease

**Symptomatic Stenosis**

* CEA: 70-99% stenosis (NNT=6)
* Consider CEA: 50-69% stenosis (NNT=15)
* Medical management: <50% stenosis

**Asymptomatic Stenosis**

* CEA: 60-99% if surgical risk <3% and life expectancy >5 years

### TUMOR

#### WHO Grading (2021)

**Adult-type Diffuse Gliomas**

* Grade 2: IDH-mutant astrocytoma
* Grade 3: IDH-mutant astrocytoma with homozygous CDKN2A/B deletion
* Grade 4: IDH-mutant astrocytoma with necrosis/MVP/CDKN2A deletion; Glioblastoma (IDH-wildtype)

**Molecular Markers**

* IDH mutation: Better prognosis in gliomas
* 1p/19q co-deletion: Defines oligodendroglioma
* MGMT methylation: Better response to temozolomide
* BRAF V600E: Targetable in select tumors

#### Metastases

**Indications for Surgery**

* Single accessible lesion
* ≤3 lesions if all resectable
* Mass effect/hydrocephalus
* Tissue diagnosis needed
* Radioresistant tumor (RCC, melanoma)

**Recursive Partitioning Analysis (RPA)**

* Class I: KPS ≥70, age <65, controlled primary, no extracranial mets
* Class II: All others
* Class III: KPS <70

#### Pituitary

**Functioning Adenomas**

* Prolactinoma: Dopamine agonist first (cabergoline)
* GH: Surgery → octreotide/pegvisomant if residual
* ACTH: Surgery → radiation if residual
* TSH: Rare, surgery

**Apoplexy Management**

* Conservative if no visual deficit or altered mental status
* Urgent surgery if visual deficit or deteriorating

#### Pediatric Tumors

**Posterior Fossa**

* Pilocytic astrocytoma: Cystic + nodule, BRAF alterations
* Medulloblastoma: 4 molecular groups (WNT, SHH, Group 3, Group 4)
* Ependymoma: 9 molecular groups, extent of resection critical

**Craniopharyngioma**

* Adamantinomatous: Children, CTNNB1 mutation
* Papillary: Adults, BRAF V600E

### FUNCTIONAL

#### Pain

**Trigeminal Neuralgia**

* First-line: Carbamazepine
* MVD: Definitive treatment, 80-90% initial success
* Gamma knife: 70-80% initial success, higher recurrence

**Failed Back Surgery Syndrome**

* Trial criteria: >50% pain relief
* Contraindications: Coagulopathy, infection, psychiatric

#### Movement Disorders

**DBS Targets**

* Parkinson's: STN or GPi
* Essential tremor: VIM
* Dystonia: GPi

**Selection Criteria**

* PD: Good levodopa response, no dementia
* ET: Medication refractory, no cognitive impairment

#### Epilepsy

**Surgical Candidates**

* Focal seizures
* Failed 2+ AEDs
* Identifiable focus
* No eloquent cortex involvement

**Temporal Lobectomy Outcomes**

* Engel I (seizure-free): 60-70%
* Memory decline risk with dominant hemisphere

### SPINE

#### Degenerative

**Cervical Radiculopathy**

* C5: Deltoid weakness, lateral arm numbness
* C6: Biceps weakness, thumb/index numbness
* C7: Triceps weakness, middle finger numbness
* C8: Intrinsic hand weakness, pinky/ring numbness

**Cervical Myelopathy Signs**

* Hoffman's sign
* Hyperreflexia
* Clonus
* Babinski
* Lhermitte's

**Surgical Indications**

* Progressive myelopathy
* Moderate-severe myelopathy
* Failed conservative therapy (6-12 weeks) for radiculopathy

#### Lumbar

**Cauda Equina Syndrome**

* Saddle anesthesia
* Bowel/bladder dysfunction
* Bilateral leg symptoms
* Emergent surgery <48 hours

**Spondylolisthesis (Meyerding)**

* Grade I: <25%
* Grade II: 25-50%
* Grade III: 50-75%
* Grade IV: 75-100%
* Grade V: Spondyloptosis

#### Spinal Infections

**Indications for Surgery**

* Progressive neurologic deficit
* Spinal instability
* Failed medical therapy (6-8 weeks)
* Epidural abscess with mass effect

**Antibiotic Duration**

* Diskitis/osteomyelitis: 6-8 weeks IV
* Epidural abscess: 6-8 weeks (may switch to PO)

### PEDIATRIC

#### Hydrocephalus

**EVD Weaning**

* Gradual elevation (5-10cm q12-24h)
* Clamp trial if tolerates 20cm
* Remove if passes 24-48h clamp

**Shunt Complications**

* Infection rate: 5-15% (highest risk first 6 months)
* Malfunction signs: Headache, vomiting, lethargy, upgaze palsy

#### Chiari Malformation

**Chiari I**

* 5mm tonsillar herniation
* Symptoms: Headache, syrinx
* Surgery: Suboccipital decompression ± duraplasty

**Chiari II**

* Associated with myelomeningocele
* Vermian herniation, beaking tectum
* Treatment based on symptoms

#### Craniosynostosis

**Suture-Specific Patterns**

* Sagittal: Scaphocephaly (boat-shaped)
* Coronal: Brachycephaly (bilateral) or plagiocephaly (unilateral)
* Metopic: Trigonocephaly
* Lambdoid: Posterior plagiocephaly

### CRITICAL CARE

#### Neuro ICU Parameters

**ICP Goals**

* Normal: <20-25 mmHg
* CPP = MAP - ICP
* CPP goal: 60-70 mmHg

**Brain Death Criteria**

1. Coma with known cause
2. Core temp >36°C
3. SBP >100
4. No confounders (drugs, metabolic)
5. Absent brainstem reflexes
6. Apnea test positive (PaCO2 >60 or 20 above baseline)

#### Sodium Disorders

**Diabetes Insipidus**

* Urine output >200-300 mL/hr
* Serum Na >145, serum osm >300
* Urine osm <300
* Treatment: DDAVP

**Cerebral Salt Wasting**

* Hyponatremia + hypovolemia
* High urine Na (>40)
* Treatment: Volume + salt replacement

**SIADH**

* Hyponatremia + euvolemia
* Treatment: Fluid restriction, salt tabs, demeclocycline

### SURGICAL APPROACHES

#### Skull Base

**Transsphenoidal**

* Indications: Pituitary, craniopharyngioma, clival lesions
* Limits: 1cm lateral to carotid, above diaphragma

**Retrosigmoid**

* CPA tumors
* Preserve hearing possible
* Risk: Headaches, CSF leak

**Translabyrinthine**

* Large acoustic neuromas
* Sacrifices hearing
* Lower cranial nerve preservation

#### Craniotomies

**Pterional**

* Anterior circulation aneurysms
* Suprasellar lesions
* Landmarks: MacCarty keyhole, superior temporal line

**Orbitozygomatic**

* Basilar apex
* Large suprasellar lesions
* Additional exposure vs pterional

#### Spine Approaches

**ACDF Complications**

* Dysphagia: 50% early, 10% persistent
* Hoarseness: RLN injury
* Horner's: Sympathetic chain

**Lateral Lumbar Complications**

* Hip flexor weakness
* Lateral thigh numbness
* Vascular injury (L4-5 left side)

### HIGH-YIELD FORMULAS

#### Volume Calculations

* Sphere: 4/3 × π × r³
* Ellipsoid: 4/3 × π × a × b × c
* ABC/2 method for hemorrhage volume

#### Radiation

* Single fraction SRS: <4cm diameter
* Fractionated SRT: 4-5cm or near eloquent
* Whole brain: Multiple mets, leptomeningeal

#### Statistical Concepts

* Sensitivity: TP/(TP+FN)
* Specificity: TN/(TN+FP)
* Number needed to treat: 1/ARR

### RAPID REVIEW PEARLS

#### Emergency Differentials

**Acute Headache**

* SAH: Thunderclap, worst of life
* Venous sinus thrombosis: Progressive, papilledema
* Pituitary apoplexy: Visual changes, hormonal
* Cervical dissection: Neck pain, Horner's

**Acute Weakness**

* Cord compression: Level, back pain
* GBS: Ascending, areflexia
* Myasthenia: Fatigable, ptosis
* Stroke: Sudden, focal

#### Surgical Pearls

* Always inspect arachnoid after durotomy
* Identify normal anatomy before pathology
* Preserve veins >3mm when possible
* Gentle bipolar technique: irrigate, low power, short bursts
* Verify cranial nerve function intraoperatively
