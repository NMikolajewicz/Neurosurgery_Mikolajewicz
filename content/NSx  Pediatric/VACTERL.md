> [!summary] **VACTERL Association**  
> • A non-random constellation of congenital anomalies: **V**ertebral defects, **A**nal atresia, **C**ardiac anomalies, **T**racheo-**E**sophageal fistula with esophageal atresia, **R**enal anomalies, and **L**imb abnormalities.  
> • Typically diagnosed clinically when ≥3 component anomalies are present; no single unifying genetic cause.  
> • Vertebral findings often include hemivertebrae, segmentation defects, and scoliosis—relevant for long-term spine growth and deformity surveillance.  
> • TE-fistula/EA presents with feeding intolerance, resp distress, and inability to pass NG tube; early surgical repair is standard.  
> • Cardiac involvement (e.g., VSD, TOF) significantly influences perioperative risk and long-term outcomes.  
> • Renal anomalies range from unilateral agenesis to reflux or dysplasia; renal function should be followed closely.  
> • Limb abnormalities often include radial aplasia, thumb anomalies, or limb length differences.  
> • Workup includes spine radiographs, echocardiography, renal US, and evaluation for TE-fistula; 
> • Prognosis is dictated by severity of visceral malformations; neuro involvement is usually secondary (e.g., spinal deformity, tethering risk).  

## Tethered cord syndrome association
- Often associated with tethered cord syndrome (TCS)
- TCS more likely in individuals with anal atresia or urogenital anomalies
- Work up involves MRI
