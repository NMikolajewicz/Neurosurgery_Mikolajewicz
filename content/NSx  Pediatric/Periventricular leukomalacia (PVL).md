> [!summary] **Periventricular Leukomalacia (PVL)**  
> • Ischemic injury to the immature periventricular white matter, usually in premature infants.  
> • Driven by impaired autoregulation, inflammation, and vulnerability of pre-oligodendrocytes.  
> • Classically presents with cystic or diffuse non-cystic white-matter loss on cranial US/MRI.  
> • Major risk factors: prematurity, hypotension, infection, ventilator instability.  
> • Clinical sequelae: spastic diplegia, cognitive delay, visual deficits; often coexists with IVH.  
> • Management is preventive—optimize ventilation, perfusion, and infection control; no curative therapy.

"leukomalacia" = necrosis of white matter
## Pathophysiology
* selective ==periventricular _loss of oligodendrocytes_== due to hypotension/ischemia
* ==bilateral white matter lesion== of premature infants
	* especially if mechanically ventilated
* affects corticospinal tracts, visual radiations, acoustic radiations
	* multicystic encephalopathy
* ==clinically most significant destructive lesion in immature brain==
	* strong relationship to subsequent handicap
	*

## Presentation
* premature neonates
* Motor control issues and tight/spastic muscles
	* especially in the legs
	* spastic cerebral palsy
* Neonatal seizures
* Intraventricular hemorrhages
* [Respiratory distress syndrome (RDS](https://www.abclawcenters.com/blog/2018/08/20/respiratory-distress-syndrome-rds/))
* Abnormalities in attention or behavior

## Workup
* physical exam may appear normal
* ==cranial U/S==
	* periventricular edema -> periventricular cysts -> atrophy of periventricular white matter -> secondary ventricular dilation

## Grading
- Grade 1: 
	- increased periventricular echogenicity, > 7 days
- Grade 2: 
	- Small periventricular cysts begin to develop
- Grade 3: 
	- Extensive periventricular cysts develop
	- especially in occipital and frontoparietal lobes
- Grade 4: 
	- Extensive subcortical cysts develop in deep white matter
