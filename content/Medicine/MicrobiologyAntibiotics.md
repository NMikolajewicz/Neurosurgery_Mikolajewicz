Gram Stain

* Gram Positives: No outer lipid membrane
	* Cocci in clusters
		* Staphylococcus (MSSA, MRA)
	* Cocci in pairs / short chains
		* Streptococci,   Enterococci
	* Bacilli
		* Listeria, Corynebacterium, Clostridium
* Gram negatives: Outer lipid membrane
	* Bacilli
		* Enterobacteriacea   (E. Coli, Klebsiella, Enterobacter), Pseudomonas
	* Coccobacilli
		* H. influenzae, Pasturella,   Brucella
	* Diplococci
		* Neisseria, Moraxella, Acinetobacter

Anaerobes

* GI/vagina - metronidazole IV/PO (flagyl)
* other - clindamycin IV/PO

Human Flora

* skin
	* S. aureus (MSSA, MRSA); S. pyogenes (Group A streptococcus); S. epidermidis (CNST)
* Nasopharynx
	* S. pneumoniae; H. influenzae; M. catarrhalis; S. pyogenes
* Oropharynx
	* Peptococcus; Peptostreptococcus; Streptococci (viridans); Fusobacterium; Eikenella
* GI (distal)
	* E.coli; Klebsiella spp.; Proteus spp.; Enterococcus spp.; Bacterioides spp.
* Urinary tract
	* E. coli; Klebisella; Enterococcus spp.

Infections (common)

* Pneumonia (community-acquired)
	* Typical   – Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, and Moraxella   catarrhalis.
	* Atypical   – Legionella, Mycoplasma, Chlamydophila
* Meningitis
	* S. pneumonia, N. meningitidis, L.   monocytogenes
* UTI
	* KEEPS: **K**_lebsiella_, **E.** _coli_, **E**_nterococcus_, **P**_roteus mirabilis_ (5%), **S.** _saphrophyticus no_
* Cellulitis
	* Group A Streptococci – more likely   causative organism in non-purulent SSSIs
	* Staphylococcus aureus – more likely   causative organism in purulent SSSIs

Mechanisms of Resistance

* **lactamases**
	* beta-lactamase expressing organisms hydrolyze beta-lactam ring to inactive antibiotics
	* lactamase organisms:
		* ==E.coli, klebsiella spp., h. influenzae, m. cattarhalis==
		* resistant to penicillin, amoxicillin/ampicillin
	* beta-lactamase inhibitors:
		* tazobactam (e.g., pip-tazo)
		* clavulanic acid (e.g., amox-clav; clavulin)
* **Extended-spectrum beta-lactamase (ESBLs)**
	* ESBL organisms:
		* ==E.coli, klebsiella spp.==
	* MOA:
		* hydrolyze all penicillins, beta-lactam/lactamase inhibitors, cephalosporins
* **MRSA**
	* ==MRSA== have mecA gene that produces penicillin binding protein 2a (PBP2a) that has low affinity for beta-lactam antibiotics
	* normally, MSSA has PBP2 that has higher affinity for beta-lactam antiobiotics
* **Broad-spectrum B-lactamase**
	* SPICE
		* ==serratia, indole-positive proteus (proteus vulgaris), providencia spp., citrobacter, enterobacter==
	* MOA
		* inducible gene AmpC
		* SPICE organisms initially sensitive to beta-lactams, but become resistant through exposure.
		* hydrolyze all penicillins, beta-lactams/beta-lactamase inhibitrs, cephalosporins

Treating Resistance Bacteria

* traditional B-lactamase
	* organisms: E.coli, klebsiella spp., h. influenzae, m. cattarhalis
	* use: pip-tazo, amox-clav, 3rd cephalosporin, FQ
	* avoid: penicillin, amox/ampicillin
* ESBL
	* organisms: E.coli, klebsiella spp.
	* use: carbapenem, FQ, septra, AMG
	* avoid: penicillin, cephalosporin
* SPICE
	* organisms: serratia, indole-positive proteus (proteus vulgaris), providencia spp., citrobacter, enterobacter
	* use: carbapenem, FQ, septra, AMG
	* avoid: penicillin, cephalosporin

**Beta-Lactams**

* * *

Members

* Penicillins
* Cephalosporins
* Carbapenems

Antimicrobial Properties

* Bactericidal 
* Time-dependent killing

Mechanism:

* Cell wall active agents – Bind to penicillin-binding proteins (PBP) & inhibit cross-linking

* * *

Penicillins

* * *

1st generation

* Agents
	* ==penicillin V = PO==
	* ==penicillin G = IV==
* Spectrum:  narrow, aerobic gram positive cocci
* Coverage:
	* S. pyogenes (group A strep)
	* treponema pallidum (syphilis)
		* gram negative spirochete
* Not useful against:
	* most gram negatives
	* beta-lactamase producing organisms
		* staph-aureus

2nd generation

* Agents
	* ==amoxiciliin = PO==
	* ==ampicillin. = IV==
* Spectrum: narrow
	* aerobic gram positive cocci + some gram negative
* Coverage
	* gram positive:
		* same as 1st generation
		* also enterococcus, listeria (meningitis)
	* gram negative (HiPEEL)
		* H. influenzae (25% resistant; meningitis, pneumo)
		* Proteus mirabilis (UTI)
		* e.coli (30% resistant; UTI)

==Amoxilicillin-clavulanic acid==

* calvulanic acid: beta-lactamase inhibitor
	* better gram negative coverage
* spectrum: broad
* coverage
	* extends amoxicillin coverage to cover more gram negatives
	* Gram negatives
		* E.coli, H.influenzae, salmonella, shigella
	* Gut anaerobes
		* B. fragilis
* Not useful against pseudomonas

3rd generation

* ==cloxacillin = PO/IV==
* spectrum: narrow
	* same as 1st generation + staph aureus
* Useful against
	* MSSA (methicillin-sensitive S. aureus)
* no useful against:
	* enterococci
	* N. meningitis

4th generation

* piperacillin-tazobactam
	* tazobactam = beta-lactamase inhibitor
* spectrum
	* broadest penicillim
	* aerobic gram-positives
		* including E. faecalis, MSSA
	* gram negatives
	* anaerobes
* useful against
	* pseudomonas
	* gram-negatives
	* most gram positives
* caution with ESBLs
* Not useful against
	* MRSA
	* E. faecium

**Cephalosporins**

* * *

1st generation

* ==cefazolin (IV), cephalexin (PO), cefadroxil (PO)==
* Spectrum: narrow
	* aerobic gram positives (including S. aureus)
	* some gram negative bacilli (PEcK)
		* proteus, E.coli, klebsiella

2nd generation

* ==cefuroxime, cefaclor==
* coverage:
	* gram positives
		* MSSA, streptococcus
	* gram negatives
		* PEcK
		* H.influenzae, moraxella
		* oral anerobes
* no useful against
	* gut anaerobes

3rd generation

* ==ceftriaxone, cefotaxime==
	* spectrum: broad
		* gram positives
			* streptococcus
		* gram negatives
			* n.meningitis, n.gonnorhea (CTX)
			* oral anaerobes
	* not useful against:
		* enterococcus
		* pseudomonas
		* gut anaerobes
* ==ceftazidime==
	* spectrum:
		* less broad than ceftriaxone/cefotaxime
		* gram positives
			* poor coverage
		* gram negatives
			* broad + pseudomonas
	* useful for:
		* pseudomonas
		* empiric gram negatives
	* not useful for:
		* enterococcus
		* gut anaerobes

* * *

Carbapenems

* * *

* "tanks" of beta-lactams
* spectrum: extremely broad
	* most aerobic gram positives
	* most aerobic gram negatives
		* including ESBLs, SPICE
	* most anaerobes
* formulations: all IV
* No coverage for MRSA, VRE
* ==Ertapenem==
	* no pseudomonas coverage
* ==meropenem==
	* covers pseudomonas
* ==imipenem==
	* covers enterococcus, pseudomonas

* * *

Other Agents

* * *

==Clindamycin==

* MOA:
	* inhibits protein synthesis at 50s
	* bacteriostatic
* spectrum: narrow
	* gram-positive: staphylococcus, streptococcus
	* anaerobes: oral anaerobes > gut anaerobes
* useful for:
	* MSSA/MRSA, oral anaerobes
* not useful for:
	* aerobic gram negative
* complications:
	* associated with c.difficile infection

==Vancomycin==

* MOA:
	* inhibits cell wall synthesis
	* bacteriostatic
* Spectrum: narrow
	* only gram negatives
	* aerobes: stephylococci (MRSA, MSSA, CNST), enterococci
	* anaerobes: c.difficile
* complications/considerations
	* IV
		* red man syndrome
		* dose to target trough levels
	* PO
		* no systematic absorption
		* use for c.difficile infections

Macrolides

* ==erythromycin (IV/PO), clarithromycin (PO), azithromycin (IV/PO)==
* MOA:
	* interferes with protein synthesis at 50S subunit
	* bacteriostatic
* spectrum: relatively broad
	* gram positives:
		* streptococci
	* some gram negatives:
		* H.influenza
		* M.cattarhalis
	* atypicals
	* no anaerobic coverage

Aminoglycosides

* ==tobramycin, gentamicin, amikacin (all IV)==
* MOA:
	* inhibits 30S subunit
	* bactericidal
* spectrum: narrow
	* aerobic gram-negatives only
		* including ESBLS
	* synergy with beta-lactam against gram-positives (streptococci, enterococci)
* complications
	* nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity
	* dose-adjusted to CrCl

Tetracyclines

* ==tetracycline (PO), doxycycline (PO), minocycline (PO), tigecycline (IV)==
* MOA:
	* inhibits protein synthesis at 30S
	* bacteriostatic
* Contraindications: pediatric (stain teeth, damage cartilage)
* spectrum: broad
	* aerobic gram positives
		* streptococci, staphylococci (MRSA), listeria
	* aerobic gram negatives
		* E.coli, klebsiella, N.meningitis, brucella spp.
	* other
		* borrelia burgdorferi (lyme disease), brucellosis, rickettsia, mycoplasma, chlamydia
			

==Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole (Septra)==

* MOA: inhibits folic acid synthesis
	* static/cidal
* spectrum: broad
	* gram positives
	* gram negatives
		* including ESBL
	* pneumocystitis, toxoplasmosis, nocardia
* not useful for:
	* pseudomonas
* caution:
	* may cause hyperkalemia, renal impairment (AKI), hemolytic anemia (G6PD), BM suppression, SJS/TEN

==Metronidazole (Flagyl)==

* IV/PO formulation
* MOA:
	* interacts with bacterial DNA
	* bactericidal
* spectrum: narrow
	* mainly anaerobes (gut > oral)
* useful for:
	* intra-abdominal infections
	* c.difficile

Fluoroquinolones

* ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, moxifloxacin
	* urinary FQ: ciprofloxacin (gram neg coverage only)
	* respiratory FQ: moxifloxacin: (gram pos and neg coverage)
* Contraindications: pediatric (impairs bone/cartilage growth)
* MOA:
	* inhibits DNA gyrase
	* interferes with bacterial DNA replications
	* bactericidal
* ==ciprofloxacin (IV/PO)==
	* spectrum: relatively narrow
	* useful for:
		* aerobic gram negatives (pseudomonas)
	* not useful for:
		* gram positive or anaerobes
* ==levofloxacin/moxifloxacin (IV/PO)==
	* respiratory FQs
	* spectrum: relatively broad
		* gram-positive aerobes
			* strep. pneumo
			* MSSA
		* aerobic gram-negatives
		* atypicals
			* chlamydia, mycoplasma, legionella
	* differences
		* gram-negatives: L > M
		* pseudomonas: L only
		* gut anaerobes: M only
* caution
	* associated with arthropathy (<18 years), tendon weakening, rupture
	* QTC prolongation
	* associated with c.difficile
