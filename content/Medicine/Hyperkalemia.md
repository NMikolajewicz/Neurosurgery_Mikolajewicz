Causes: Cellular shift, decreased urinary excretion, increased production

* low aldosterone
	* ACEi, ARB, spironolactone
* iatrogenic supplementation
* potassium ingestion + CKD
* ESRD
* lab artifact (hemolysis)

Normal K levels: 3.5-5.5

Presentation

* muscle cramps, weakness
* focal neurological symptoms
* ECG findings
	* 1) peaked T waves, ST depression
	* 2) loss of P waves
	* 3) widening of QRS and PR prolongation
	* 4) development of sine wave
	* 5) asystole or ventricular fibrillation

Management

* Arrhythmias (K > 6.5)
	* ECG
	* calcium 
		* ==calcium gluconate 1000 mg (10 mL of 10% solution) x 2-3 min==
		* calcium chloride 500-1000 mg IV x 2-3 min
* Shift
	* ==regular insulin 10 U==
	* ==D50W 50 mL x5 min, then D10W 50-75 mL/h==
	* monitor blood glucose q30 min
	* others:
		* beta-agonists (salbutamol)
		* sodium bicarbonate
* Eliminate K+
	* ==fluids + lasix (e.g., 40 mg IV q12h)==
	* GI excretion (slow)
		* laxatives (e.g., PEG)
		* kayexylate
			* complications: bowel necrosis
		* ==lokelma==
			* sodium zirconium cyclosilicate
			* sequesters potassium in GI
	* ==dialysis (last resort)==
