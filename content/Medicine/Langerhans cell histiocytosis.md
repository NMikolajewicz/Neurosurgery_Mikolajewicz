* [Langerhans cell histiocytosis](https://www.cancer.gov/Common/PopUps/popDefinition.aspx?id=471787&version=patient&language=English&dictionary=Cancer.gov) (LCH) is a rare disease that begins in LCH [cells](https://www.cancer.gov/Common/PopUps/popDefinition.aspx?id=46476&version=patient&language=English&dictionary=Cancer.gov).
* LCH cells are a type of ==[dendritic cell](https://www.cancer.gov/Common/PopUps/popDefinition.aspx?id=44948&version=patient&language=English&dictionary=Cancer.gov)== that normally helps the body fight [infection](https://www.cancer.gov/Common/PopUps/popDefinition.aspx?id=45364&version=patient&language=English&dictionary=Cancer.gov).
* Sometimes [mutations](https://www.cancer.gov/Common/PopUps/popDefinition.aspx?id=46063&version=patient&language=English&dictionary=Cancer.gov) (changes) develop in [genes](https://www.cancer.gov/Common/PopUps/popDefinition.aspx?id=45693&version=patient&language=English&dictionary=Cancer.gov) that control how dendritic cells function.
	* These include mutations of the ==_[BRAF](https://www.cancer.gov/Common/PopUps/popDefinition.aspx?id=561325&version=patient&language=English&dictionary=Cancer.gov)_, _MAP2K1_, _[RAS](https://www.cancer.gov/Common/PopUps/popDefinition.aspx?id=45859&version=patient&language=English&dictionary=Cancer.gov)_, and _ARAF_ g==enes.
	* These mutations may cause too many LCH cells to grow and build up in certain parts of the body, where they can damage [tissue](https://www.cancer.gov/Common/PopUps/popDefinition.aspx?id=46683&version=patient&language=English&dictionary=Cancer.gov) or form [lesions](https://www.cancer.gov/Common/PopUps/popDefinition.aspx?id=46324&version=patient&language=English&dictionary=Cancer.gov).

Skeletal manifestations

* The **skeleton** is the most commonly involved organ system in **Langerhans cell histiocytosis** (**LCH**)
* the most common location for single-lesion LCH
* often referred to as **==eosinophilic granuloma==** (**EG**)
* pathophysiology
	* There is proliferation of [Langerhans cells](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/langerhans-cells?lang=us) with an abundance of [eosinophils](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/eosinophil?lang=us), lymphocytes and neutrophils.
	* These cells produce **==prostaglandins==** -> medullary bone resorption
* Epidemiology
		The skeletal system in 60-80% of cases is the only organ system involved.
		
		It primarily occurs in ==older children and young adults==
		
		male to female ratio of 2:1.
		
	Distribution
	* skull: ~50%
	* pelvis: 23%
	* femur: 17%
	* spine: ~15% (range 6.5-25%)
	* ribs: 8% (most common in adults)
	* humerus: 7%
	* mandible: 7%
* Differential Diagnosis
	* [osteomyelitis](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/osteomyelitis?lang=us)
	* [round blue cell tumors](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/small-round-blue-cell-tumours?lang=us)
	* [metastases](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/bone-metastases-1?lang=us)
	* [primary bone tumors](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/missing?article%5Btitle%5D=primary-bone-tumours&lang=us)
	* [lymphoma](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/lymphoma?lang=us)
	* [leukemia](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/acute-lymphoblastic-leukaemia?lang=us)
* Management
	* excision and curettage
	* steroid therapy: intralesional injection
	* chemotherapy
	* radiofrequency ablation
	* radiotherapy for spinal lesion

Imaging Characteristics
Plain radiographs

	Skull
	* solitary or multiple punched out lytic lesions without sclerotic rim
	* double contour or beveled edge appearance may be seen due to asymmetrical involvement of the inner and outer tables ([hole within a hole](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/hole-within-a-hole-sign-1?lang=us)) sign 8
	* [button sequestrum](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/button-sequestrum?lang=us) representing residual bone
	* [geographic skull](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/geographic-skull?lang=us)
	Mandible
	* irregular radiolucent areas mostly involving superficial alveolar bone
	* [floating tooth](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/floating-tooth?lang=us): loss of lamina dura
	Spine
	* [vertebra plana](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/vertebra-plana?lang=us): most common cause of vertebra plana in children; more often in thoracic spine
	Long bones
	* depends on phase of disease which is imaged
	* permeative and aggressive appearing lesion
	* mainly involves diaphysis or metadiaphysis and respects growth plates
	* [endosteal scalloping](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/endosteal-scalloping?lang=us), [periosteal reaction](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/periosteal-reaction?lang=us) (in healing phase it can appear as solid benign periosteal reaction), cortical thinning, intracortical tunneling, and associated soft tissue mass

Ultrasound

	Skull
	* seen as a solid mass lesion
	* minimal vascularity
	* originating from the diploic surface, extending through to the outer table 
	* local subperiosteal spread

CT

	Similar to plain film findings with better demonstration of cortical erosion and soft tissue involvement. Excellent for biopsy and surgical planning.
	

MRI

	Signal characteristics include:
	* T1: typically hypointense to isointense
	* T2: hyperintense 9
	* STIR: hyperintense
	* T1 C+ (Gd): often shows diffuse contrast enhancement
