Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis

* * *

Causes

* MUDPILES
	* methanol, uremia, DKA, paraldehyde, isoniazid, lactate, ethylene glycol, salicylates
* Big 4 (KULT)
	* Ketones, Uremia, Lactate, Toxins

Work up

* Anion gap
	* AG = Na - (HCO3 + Cl)
* Osmolar gap
	* Osm = 2Na + BG + BUN
* VBG
* electrolytes
* serum/urine ketones
* lactate
* blood glucose
* toxic alcohol screen
	* methanol, isopropyl alcohol, ethylene glycol

* * *

Acid-Base Disorders

* * *

Physiology

* The lungs and  kidneys regulate blood pH
* The lungs raise pH by blowing off  CO2, and lower pH by decreasing ventilation and retaining CO2
* The kidneys  raise pH by increasing the production of HCO3\- and excreting acid, and lower pH by eliminating HCO3\- and retaining acid
* Respiratory compensation begins within minutes and peaks in 12 - 24 hours.
* Renal compensation has an acute and chronic phase, where the effect is smaller at first before reaching a peak in 2 - 5 days.

metabolic acidosis

* labs
	* pH < 7.35
	* HCO3 < 22 mEq/L
	* anion gap > 12 mEq/L
* changes
	* ↓ HCO3 and ↓ CO2
* ratios
	* 1:1 compensation
* causes
	* ==MUDPILES==
	* ketoacidosis
		* diabetic, alcoholic, starvation
	* lactic acidosis
		* due to hypoxic metabolism
	* renal failure (under excretion of acid)
	* liver failure (impaired lactate clearance)
	* cell lysis (rhabdomyolysis)
	* methanol (anti-freeze)
	* tylenol / ASA overdose

metabolic alkalosis

* labs
	* pH > 7.45
	* HCO3 > 26 mEq/L
* changes
	* ↑ HCO3 : ↑ CO2
* ratios
	* 1:0.75 compensation
* causes
	* volume responsive (urine Cl < 10)
		* contraction alkalosis secondary to ==diuretics, dehydration, emesis/NG suction==
		* urine Cl is low secondary to increase RAAS activity (Na and Cl are being reabsorbed)
	* not volume responsive (urine Cl > 10)
		* non HTN - Barter's and Gittleman's syndrome
		* HTN - ==hyperaldosteronism==

respiratory alkalosis

* labs
	* pH > 7.45
	* PaCO2 < 35 mmHg
* changes
	* ↓ CO2 and ↓ HCO3
* ratios
	* 10:5 chronic
	* 10:2 acute
* causes
	* ==hyperventilation== (anxiety, mechanical ventilation)
	* hypoxia
	* sepsis
	* CNS disorder
	* salicylate toxicity
	* lung disease (interstitial lung disease)
	* pregnancy
	* hepatic encephalopathy
* MOA
	* excess CO2 removed from lungs
	* kidneys excrete bicarbonate and retain protons.

respiratory acidosis

* labs
	* pH < 7.35
	* PaCO2 > 42 mmHg
* changes
	* ↑ CO2 : ↑ HCO3
* ratios
	* 10:3-4 chronic
	* 10:1 acute
* causes
	* ==hypoventilation==
		* ==opiate overdose, asthma/COPD, reduced muscle strength, OSA==
	* CNS depression (comatose, drug overdose, head injury)
	* thoracic injury (pneumothorax, flail chest)
	* airway obstruction (asthma, COPD)
	* pneumonia
	* pulmonary edema
	* obesity-related hypoventilation
	* neuromuscular weakness (MG, GB)
	* interstitial fibrosis
* MOA
	* CO2 not adequately removed by lungs
	* kidneys retain/add HCO3; protons excreted as ammonia
