**Diabetes**

* * *

T2DM diagnostic criteria

* symptomatic hyperglycemia
	* clinical: polydipsia, polyuria, blurry vision, weight loss)
	* FPG > 11.1 mmol/L
* asymptomatic hyperglycemia
	* FPG > 7.0 mmol/L
	* 2hPG > 11.1 during 75 g OGTT
	* A1C > 6.5%

Workup

* A1C
* fasting lipid profile
* LFT
* urine ACR
* creatinine
* if T1DM: TSH, celiac serology

ABCDES of diabetes care

* A1C targets
	* target: A1C < 7, fasting glucose 4.4 - 7.2 mmol/L, postprandial glucose: <10 mmol/L
	* agents: ==metformin 500 mg 1-2x daily (T2DM)==, insulin (T1DM, or T2DM if A1C > 9-10)
* BP targets
	* target: BP <130/80
	* agents: ==ACEi/ARBs==
* Cholesterol targets
* Drugs for CVD risk reduction
	* ==ASA 75-162 mg PO daily== (if 10 year CVD risk > 10%)
	* ==SGLT2 inhibitors== (empagliflozin, canagliflozin)
* Exercise goals / healthy eating
	* target: 150 min moderate-intensity aerobic exercise per week
* Screening for complications
* Smoke cessation
* Self-management

Complications

* microvascular
	* retinopathy
		* retinal exams
		* laser ablation of diabetic retinopathy
	* nephropathy
		* U/A (ALB/creatinine ratio)
		* ACEi if proteinuria
	* neuropathy
		* monofilament screen
		* gabapentin
* macrovascular
	* CAD
	* PVD

**Hyperglycemia**

* * *

Common causes

* diabetes
* DKA
* medication-induced (commonly steroids)

History

* DM history?
* anti-glycemic medications
* blood sugar trend past 24 h
	* what is their baseline?
* estimated insulin sensitivity factor
	* ISF = 100 / (total daily insulin dose) = change in blood sugar per unit of insulin

Workup

* CBC, lytes
* creatinine
* VBG, anion gap

Management

* increase existing diabetes medications
	* if chronically high BG
* ==insulin sliding scale protocol==
	* check BG TID prior to meals and QHS
	* administer insulin ==aspart or lispro== per sliding scale
	* Sliding scale:
		* BG < 4 = give juice, notify MD
		* BG 4-12 = no insulin needed
		* BG 12-14 = insulin 2 U
		* BG 14-16 = insulin 4 U
		* BG 16-18 = insulin 6 U
		* BG 18-20 = insulin 8 U
		* BG > 20 = insulin 10 U, notify MD

**Hypoglycemia**

* * *

Common causes:

* medication-induced
	* usually hypoglycemics (DM medications)
* poor oral intake
* sepsis

History

* accurate reading?
* symptomatic?
	* confused, drowsy, seizure
* tolerating PO intake? not eating?
* related to medication?

Workup

* blood glucose q1h x 2

Management

* ==D50 1 amp IV push x 1==
* ==juice or oral sugar pack==
* ==glucagon 1 mg IV x 1==
* review medication list
	* hold insulin, sulfonylurea, SGLT-2 inhibtors

**DKA**

* * *

**Definition**: hyperglycemia, acidemia, ketonemia

Triggers/causes

* infection (UTI, pneumonia, gastroenteritis, pancreatitis): 40-50% cases
* insulin deficiency (medication non-compliance, new DM dx)
* infarction (ACS, MI)
* infant (pregnant)
* ischemic (CVA)
* illegal (alcohol, drug/cocaine abuse)
* iatrogenic (prescription drug interactions, e.g., steroids)
* SGLT-2 inhibitors (euglycemic DKA)

Presentation

* presentation (3 P's)
	* polyuria
	* polydipsia
	* polyphagia
* non-specific findings
	* weakness, vomiting, abdominal pain, blurry vision
* physical
	* acetone smell
	* dehydration / tachycardia / hypotension / shock
	* kussmaul's respiration

DKA diagnostic criteria

* hyperglycemia:
	* serum glucose >250 mg/d
* acidosis:
	* pH < 7.3, AG > 10, bicarb < 18
* ketosis:
	* ketunuria
	* ketonemia (measure serum beta-OH-butyrate)

Work-up

* CBC, lytes
* creatinine
* VBG; pH, anion gap, lactic acid
* LFT, lipase
* troponin
* serum ketones
* B-HCG
* HbA1C
* urinalysis, urine culture, urine ketones
* CXR
* ECG

Labs/parameters to trend/track

* VBG q2h
	* monitor pH, bicarb levels
* glucose q1h
* lytes q2h
	* monitor K, PO4, AG, CO2
* insulin infusion rate
* IV fluid rate

Management

* Consults: endocrinology, ICU
* fluid resuscitation:
	* ==2L IV NS bolus==
	* fluid deficit: 100 mL/kg (7-8 L) secondary to osmotic diuresis
* potassium replacement
	* if K < 4, ==add K 20 mEq/L== to maintenance fluids
	* measure K before giving insulin
* Insulin infusion
	* start i==nsulin 10 U IV push, then 0.1 U/kg/hr infusion==
	* continue until AG normalized
	* if AG high but ==glucose < 15, add dextrose== to maintenance fluids

Complications

* hypoglycemia
	* over-treatment
* hyperglycemia
	* under-treatment
* hypokalemia
	* due to insulin
* return to DKA
	* must bridge with SQ insulin dosing
* hyperchloremia
	* due to supraphysiological Cl levels in NS
	* balanced solutions (e.g., plasma-lyte) are preferred in DKA
* cerebral edema
	* 20-50% mortality, 1/3 survivals in vegetative state
	* risk: < 5 years old, new onset DM
	* Sx: AMS, pupillary changes, seizures
	* treatment
		* mannitol 1-2 g/kg
		* consider: intubation, dexamethasone, hypertonic saline (5-10 mL/kg)

**Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS)**

* * *

causes/triggers

* insulin deficiency (med non-compliance, new DM dx)
* infection (pneumonia, UTI, cellulitis)
* inflammation (pancreatitis)
* ischemia (cardiac, neurogenic, intestinal)
* pregnancy

Work-up

* CBC, lytes
* creatinine
* VBG; pH, anion gap, lactic acid
* LFT, lipase
* troponin
* serum ketones
* B-HCG
* HbA1C
* urinalysis, urine culture, urine ketones
* CXR
* ECG

Management

* consults: endocrinology
* same as DKA
