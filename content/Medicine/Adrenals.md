Addison's disease
_low cortisol_

* * *

Pathophysiology

* cortisol deficiency
* primary adrenal destruction

Chronicity

* acute
	* hemorrhage
* chronic
	* infiltrative
	* autoimmune
	* metastatic malignancy

Causes of cortisol deficiency

* dysfunctional anterior pituitary
	* RAAS not affected
* addison's disease
	* primary deficiency
	* due to autoimmune disease or TB

Workup

* presentation
	* hypotension
	* nausea/vomiting
	* coma
	* fatigue
	* abdominal pain
	* low Na, high K
	* hyperpigmentation
		* due to elevated ACTH
* ==early morning _serum_ cortisol==
	* if normal, not addison's
	* if low, continue workup
	* random cortisol is useless
* ==ACTH stimulation test==
	* cosyntropin skin test
	* if anterior pituitary problem, ACTH stimulation raises cortisol
		* order MRI brain
		* tx: replace cortisol
	* if adrenal problem, ACTH stimulation does not raise cortisol
		* order CT/MRI adrenal
		* tx: replace cortisol and aldosterone (fludrocortisone)

Management

* cortisol replacement
	* ==hydrocortisone (GC)==
	* ==fludrocortisone (MC)==

Cushing's syndrome
_high cortisol_

* * *

Etiology (4 causes - 3/4 are malignancies)

* ACTH dependent (↑ACTH, ↑cortisol)
	* paraneoplastic
		* SCC lung tumor
	* cushing's diseases
		* anterior pituitary tumor
* ACTH independent (↓ACTH, ↑cortisol)
	* steroid use
		* history of autoimmune disorder
		* chronic steroid use
	* primary adrenal tumor

Workup

* presentation
	* moon faces
	* straie
	* central obesity
* adrenal incidentaloma (CT imaging)
* refractory HTN
* low K
* Diagnosis
	* establish low cortisol
		* ==24-_urine_ cortisol==
		* ==midnight _saliva_ cortisol==
	* determine etiology
		* ==low dose dexamethasone suppression==
			* if suppressed, normal
			* failure to suppress indicates cushing's syndrome
		* ==high dose dexamethasone suppression==
			* if suppressed, pituitary tumor (cushing's disease)
			* failure to suppress indicated ectopic tumor
				* CT CAP required to identify malignancy
		* ==ACTH levels==
			* if normal, indicates adrenal tumor (primary hypercortisolism)
				* Mx: CT/MRI abdomen, surgical resection
			* if elevated, indicates ACTH dependence

Management

* etiology dependent (primary vs. secondary)

Conn's syndrome
_high aldosterone_

* * *

Pathophysiology

* RAAS decoupled from HPA axis

Excess aldosterone causes

* conn's syndrome
* renovascular HTN
	* fibromuscular dysplasia
	* atherosclerotic disease

Presentation

* HTN
	* secondary to increased Na reabsorption and water retention
	* refractory to anti-hypertensives
* hypokalemia

Workup

* ==aldosterone to renin ratio (ARR)==
	* ARR < 10
		* both elevated
		* renin driving aldosterone
		* causes
			* fibromuscular dysplasia - stent
			* atherosclerosis - no stent
	* ARR > 30
		* aldosterone elevated, renin normal
		* perform ==salt-suppression test==
			* failure for salt to suppress aldosterone supports Conn's
* ==MRI  for adrenal mass==
* ==adrenal vein sampling==
	* confirms diagnosis

* * *

Pheochromocytoma
_high catecholamines_

* * *

Pathophysiology

* catecholamine secreting tumor

Presentation (5 P's)

* paroxysmal
* pain (headache)
* pressure (HTN)
* palpitations (tachycardia)
* perspirations

Workup

* ==plasma-free catecholamines==
* ==24-hour urine metanephrines or VMA==
* CT/MRI abdomen
* adrenal vein sampling

Management

* avoid HTN crisis
* alpha-blockage followed by beta-blockage, then resect

* * *

Incidentaloma
_incidental mass of adrenal glands_

* * *

Workup

* r/o adrenal pathology
	* conn's
	* cushing's
	* pheochromocytoma

Management

* adrenal mass
	* <4 cm: monitor
	* \>4 cm: resect
