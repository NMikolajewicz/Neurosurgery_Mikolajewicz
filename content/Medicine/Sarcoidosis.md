Definition: multisystemic granulomatous disorder, unknown etiology, lung involvement most common (90%)

Etiology:

* ?transmissible
* enviromental: metal dusts, fumes, organic antigens
* genetics:
	* familial
	* USA-blacks, ireland, japan
	* HLA-A1, HLA-DR3 polymorphisms
		

Pathology:

* non-caseating epitheliod cell granuloma
* epitheloid cells:
	* mononuclear phagocytes
	* lymphocytes
* granulomas develop fibrotic changes
	* starts at periphery, progresses to complete fibrosis

Work up

* Biopsy any site
	* lung biopsy preferred: 40-90% yield (trans bronchial biopsy, 4-5x biopsies)
	* surgical lung biopsy
	* mediastinoscopy for lymph node biopsy: 90% yield
* radiological
	* stage I: 98% yield
	* Stage II: 86% yield
* ACE
	* nonsensitive/nonspecific
	* 2x greater than normal
* PFT
	* diffusion capacity, vital capacity
	* restrictive and obstructive abnormalities
* CT thorax
* CXR staging
* Diagnosis
	* tight well-formed granulomas surrounded by a rim of lymphocytes and fibroblasts
	* perilymphatic interstitial distribution of granulomas
		* transbronchial biopsy
	* exclusion of alternative causes

Staging (lungs)

* stage 0: normal CXR
* stage 1: bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy (BHL) (55-90% remission)
* stage 2: BHL + infiltrates (40-70% remission)
* stage 3: parenchymal infiltrates, no BHL (10-20% remission)
* stage 4: advanced fibrosis with honeycombing, hilar retraction, bullae (0% remission)

Treatment

* Steroids (x 12 months minimum)
	* topical
		* skin, uveitis, nasal polyps, airway disease
	* systemic
		* cardiac, neurologic, hypercalcemia
* cytotoxic agents
	* if chronic steroids required
	* methotrexate + azathioprine
	* cyclophosphamide (neurosarcoid)
* antimalarial
	* if chronic steroids required
	* chloroquine (retinopathy + blindness)
	* hydroxychloroquine
* NSAID
	* for MSK, erythema nodosum
* cyclosporine
* radiation therapy
* refractory cases: transplant
	* lungs
	* recurrence occurs in donor organ
		* controlled by immunosuppression

Natural History

* intermittent episodes
* prognosis:
	* spontaneous remission in 2/3 patients
	* 1-5% mortality (usually cardiac cause)
* negative prognostic predictors:
	* blacks, lupus pernio, chronic uveitis, age > 40, chronic hypercalcemia, nephrocalcinosis, progressive pulmonary sarcoid, nasal mucosal involvement, cystic bone lesions, neurosarcoidosis, cardiac sarcoid

Clinical Presentation:

* multiorgan disorder
* non-specific constitutional symptoms
	* fevere
	* fatigue
	* night sweats
	* malaise
	* weight loss
* lungs:
	* \>90% patients
	* SOB, cough, chest pain
	* advanced: fibrosis, honeycombing, hilar retraction, bullae
	* bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy
		* DDx: TB, lymphoma, leukemia, Coccidioidomycosis/histoplasmosis/blastomycosis, Berylliosis
* lymphnodes
	* 33% patients
	* palpable peripheral lymph nodes
		* cervical, axillary, epitrochlear, inguinal
	* discrete, mobile, non-tender
	* splenomegaly
* heart
	* 5% patient
	* benign arrythmias, high degree heart blocks, sudden death
	* fibrous scaring -> diastolic dysfunction, segmental contraction abnormalities
	* Investigations: coronary angiogram, endomyocardial biopsy
* liver
	* 50-80% patients
	* granulomas in liver
	* investigations: LFT abnormalities, liver biopsy
* skin
	* 25% patients
	* erythema nodosum
		* hallmark of acute sarcoidosis
		* 6-8 weeks, rare
		* raised, red, tender bumps (anterior legs)
	* lupus pernio
		* chronic sarcoidosis
		* indurated plaques, discolouration of nose, cheeks, lips, ears
	* other lesions:
		* maculopapular eruptions, plaques, subcutaneous nodules, alopecia, pigmentation changes
* ocular lesions
	* 11-83% patients
	* uveitis common
	* complications: cataract, blindness
* neurosarcoidosis
	* <10% patients
	* early: CN palsies (VII), hypothalamic and pituitary lesions
	* late: space-occupying mass, peripheral neuropathy
	* Investigations: MRI/CT, CSF (lymphocytosis, increased protein, ACE, lysozyme, CD4/CD8 ratio)
* MSK
	* 25-40% patients
	* arthritis
		* knees, ankles, elbows, wrists, small joints
* GI tract
	* <1% patients
	* stomach most commonly involved
* hematological
	* anemia (20%), leukopenia (40%)
	* +/- bone marrow involvement
* endocrine
	* hypocalcemia (2-10%)
	* hypercalciuria (more common)
		* secondary to vitamin D overproduction by macrophages/granulomas
	* other endocrine derangements (hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, hypothermia, adrenal suppression, anterior pituitary)
