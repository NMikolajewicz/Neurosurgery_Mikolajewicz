### C. difficile

* * *

Severity

* age > 65, immunosuppression, temperature > 38C, AKI, WBC > 15, ALB < 30, peritonitis (physical exam)

Management
_per IDSA guidelines_

* initial episode, non-severe
	* ==vancomycin 125 mg PO QID x14 days==

				   or

* fidaxomicin 200 mg BID x10 days

				   or

* metronidazole 500 mg PO TID x14 days

* recurrent episode
	* fidaxomicin 200 mg BID x10 days
* fulminant episode
	* if hypotensive, shock, ileus or megacolon
	* vancomycin 500 mg PO/NG QID

### Cellulitis

* * *

Organisms

* Cellulitis
	* beta-hemolytic streptococcus (group A or B)
		* abscess rare
	* staph. aureus
		* "burrowing", abscess, pus can be cultures
* Diabetic foot ulcer(DFU; polymicrobial)
	* gram+
	* gram-
	* anaerobes

Differential Diagnosis

* statis dematitis
* insect bites
* contact dermatitis
* PAD
* DVT
* necrotizing fasciitis
* gout
* septic arthritic

Management:

* ==cephalexin 500 mg PO q6h x5-7d==
* ==cefazolin 1g IV q8h x5-7d==
* cloxacillin 2g IV q6h
* if beta-lactam allergy
	* 1st line: clindamycin 300 mg PO q6h
	* requiring IV: clindamycin 600 mg IV q8h
* If DUF
	* pip-tazo and vancomycin

		   or

	or
	
* ==moxifloxacin 400 mg PO or IV daily x7 days==

### Endocarditis (EC)

* * *

Organisms

* staph aureus
	* seeds native valves from bacteremia
	* tricuspid valve if IV drug use
* viridens group strep
	* subacute
	* native mitral valve
* strep bovis
* HACEK organisms: Haemophilus, Actinobacillus, Cardiobacterium, Eikenella, Kingella

Workup

* blood culture
* TTE (+/- TEE)
* Duke criteria
	* major criteria: + blood culture (typical organism), +vegetation (echo)
	* minor criteria: predisposing heart lesion / IVDU, fever, vascular/immunologic phenomena, + blood culture (atypical organism)
* colonoscopy (if strep bovis)

Vascular and Immunologic phenomena

* vascular
	* major arterial embil
	* septic pulmonary infarct
	* mycotic aneursym
	* conjunctival hemorrhages
	* janeway lesions
		* painless, palms and soles
* immunologic
	* glomerulonephritis
	* osler nodes
		* painful nodules
	* roth spots
		* retinal hemmorahage

Prophylaxis

* indications:
	* prosthetic value
	* endocarditic history
	* uncorrected congenital lesion
	* oral/throat procedure
* ==amoxicillin==

Complications

* CHF
* septic emboli (to lungs/brain)

Management (by organism)

* Staph aureus
	* naficillin + gentamicin or vancomycin
* strep viridens
	* penicillin x4-6 weeks

Management (by valve status)

* native valve
	* ==vancomycin==
* prosthetic valve < 60d or >365d
	* young and old prosthetics
	* ==vancomycin + gentamicin + cefepime==
* prosthetic vale 30-365d
	* in between prosthetics
	* ==vancomycin + gentamicin==

valve replacement indications

* CHF
* vegetation
	* \> 15 mm if healthy
	* \> 10 mm if embolism (MI, CVA, acute limb ischemia)
	* abscess
	* fungus

### Febrile Neutropenia

* * *

Criteria

* ANC < 0.5 × 109/L
* fever
	* single oral temperature > 38.3 C

				   or

* sustained oral temperature > 38 C x 1h

Workup

* blood cultures
* MRSA screening
* CT chest/abdo
	* bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) if abnormal chest CT
* sputum culture
* NP swab
	* RSV, influenza, parainfluenza
* legionella urinary antigen
* mouth ulcer swabs
* C.difficile PCR

Management

* ==piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV q8h==

		   and

* ==tobramycin 5 mg/kg IV q24h==

### Meningitis

* * *

Organisms (SHiN)

* Strep Pneumo
* H. Influenza
* N. meningitidis
* Others: lysteria, staph (recent NSx), aerobic gram-negative bacilli , cryptococcus, lyme, RMSF, TB, neurosyphilis

Workup

* Exam for elevated ICP/CT
* LP (gold standard)
	* +Gram stain, >1000 WBC is diagnostic
	* High protein and low glucose support bacterial
	* LP contrindications: FAILS
		* focal neurological deficits
		* AMS (cannot report symptoms)
		* immunosuppressed / increased ICP
		* lesion over needle site
		* seizures (cannot report symptoms)
* non-contrast CT
	* if LP unsafe

Management

* Start empiric treatment
	* ==Ceftriaxone 2g IV q12h==

				   and

* ==vancomycin 25 mg/kg IV loading, then 15 mg/kg IV q8h==

* +/- ==dexamethasone 10 mg IV q6h x4 days==
	* if bacterial suspected
* +/- ==ampicillin 2g IV q4h==
	* if >50y, immunocompromised, end stage liver/kidney disease
* Atypical organisms
	* cryptococcus: ==amphotericin==
	* RMSF/Lyme: ==ceftriaxone==
	* TB: ==RIPE== therapy

Atypical meningitis

* rocky mountain spotted fever
	* fever, migrating rash (arms to trunk), tick bites, camping
* lyme disease
	* targetoid rash with arthralgias, arrhythmias
* cryptococcal meningitis
	* HIV patient with fever/headache
	* \>20 cmH2O opening pressure
* TB
	* night sweats, weight loss, hemoptysis, meningitis
	* risk: homeless, prison, endemic areas
* neurosyphilis
	* primary chancre, secondary erythema multiform

### Necrotizing Fasciitis

* * *

If any suspicion of necrotizing skin/soft tissue infection (i.e., necrotizing fasciitis), treat as medical emergency

Organism

* Staph. aureus
* strep

Presentation

* cellulitis
* toxic appearing
* rapidly spreading
* pain out of proportion
* crepitus
* blue-grey discoloration

Workup

* x-ray: visualize crepitus/gas

Management

* Antibiotics
	* ==Piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV q8h== (assuming normal renal function) (_gram positive + negative + pseudomonas_)

				   and

* ==vancomycin 15mg/kg IV q12h== (_gram positives_)

				   and

* ==clindamycin 600IV q8h== (_anaerobes_)

* STAT surgical consult for operative debridement
	* orthopedics or plastic surgery;
	* urology for necrotizing infection of perineum)
* STAT imaging (CT usually fastest) of affected area
	* should not delay surgical management if there is crepitus on examination or rapid progression of illness

### Otitis media

* * *

Organisms (SHiM)

* Strep
* H. flu
* Moroxella

Workup

* Presentation: unilateral ear pain relieved by pulling on pinna
* Dx: CLINICAL by pneumatiC
	* Fluid behind ear

Management

* ==Amoxicillin==
* Amox-clav if recurrent
* Tympanoplasty if ≥3 recurrences in 6 months or 4 in 1 year
* Penicillin allergic = cephalosporin if not anaphylaxis. Otherwise use azithro.

### Osteomyelitis

* * *

Organisms

* staph. aureus (most common)
* salmonella (sickle cell)
* pseudomonas (penetrating wound)
* polymicrobial, including pseudomonas (diabetic foot)
* vibrio fulnificus (oysters in cirrhotics)
* sporothrix (gardening)

Workup

* x-ray
* tagged WBC scan
* bone scans
* biopsy
* ESR/CRP

Management

* source control
	* debridement
* antibiotics x 4-6 weeks
* follow up with ESR/CRP

### Pneumonia

* * *

Organisms

* CAP
	* Typical: SHiM
		* step. pneumo (most common)
		* H. influe (COPD/smokers)
		* M. catarralis
	* Atypical: CML
		* chlamydia
		* mycoplasma
		* legionella (immunosuppressed)
	* others:
		* staph. aureus (post-viral)}
		* klebsiella (aspiration, alcoholics)
* HAP
	* pseudomonas aeruginosa
	* MRSA
* immunosuppressed
	* TB
	* fungal
	* PJP - septra/dapsone +/- steroids

Workup

* Vitals
	* SpO2
* blood/sputum cultures
	* low yield
	* blood cultures 10% positive
* CXR
* Risk stratification (CRB-65)

CuRB-65 (1-4 points):

* confusion
* +/- uremia (BUN > 20)
* RR > 30
* SBP < 90 or DBP < 60
* age > 65

Management:

* CAP
	* in-patient
		* ==cetriaxone==
		* ==azithromycin==
	* out-patient
		* ==azithromycin==
* HAP (MRSA + pseudomonas)
	* ==vancomycin + pip-tazo==

_CAP expanded management_

* _low risk (0 points): 2-4% 30d mortality_
	* _outpatient management_
	* _amoxicillin 1g PO BID x5-7 days_
	* _if B-lactam allergy:_
		* _levofloxacin 750 mg PO daily x 5 day_
		* _moxifloxacin 400 mg PO daily x 5 day_
* _intermediate risk (1-2 points): 13.3% 30d risk_
	* _in patient ward_
	* _amoxicillin-calvulanic acid 875/125 mg PO BID x 5-7 day_
	* _cefotaxime 1 g q8h IV x 5-7 days_
	* _ceftriaxone 1g q24h IV x 5-7 days_
* _high risk (3-4 points): 34.3% 30d risk_
	* _cefotaxime 1 g q8h IV or ceftriaxone 1 g q24h IV_

				   _and_

* _azithromycin 500 mg IV daily_

### Tuberculosis (TB)

* * *

Work up

* CXR
* PPD
	* screen for TB if positive:
		* \>15 mm in healthy
		* \> 10 mm in prison/healthcare/LTCH/DM/ETOH
		* \>5 mm if chronically ill, AIDS, immunosuppressed
* sputum: acid-fast stain
* Adenosine deaminase (ADA)
	* TB biomarker in ascites, pleural effusions

Management:

* RIPE x 6 months (12 if meningitis, 9 if pregnant)
	* ==Rifampin==
		* body fluids turn orange/red
		* CYP450 inducer
	* ==INH (isoniazid)==
		* peripheral neuropathy
		* sideroblastic anemia (prevent with vitamin B6)
		* hepatitis with transaminitis
		* monotherapy if latent TB
	* ==Pyrazinamide==
		* benign hyperuricemia
	* ==Ethambutol==
		* optic neuritis

### Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)

* * *

Organisms

* KEEPS
	* klebsiella
	* e.coli
	* enterococcus
	* proteus mirabilis
	* s. saprophyticus 

Workup

* urine culture if complicated UTI/pyelonephritis or pregnant.
* if uncomplicated UTI send for urine culture only if symptoms persist despite treatment or if recurrent UTI within 3 months of treatment

Management

* asymptomatic
	* no treatment
	* if pregnant
		* ==nitrofurantoin 100 mg BID x5-7 days==

						   or

* ==amox-clav 875/125 mg BID x 5-7 days==

* uncomplicated
	* ==nitrofurantoin 100 mg BID x5 days==
	* trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) 1 DS BID x 3 dasy
	* ciprofloxacin 500 mg BID x 3 days
	* amox-clav 875/125 mg BID x 5-7 days
	* cephalexin 500 mg QID x 5-7 days
* complicated or pyelonephritis
	* ==trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX)== 1 DS BID x 3 days

				   or

* ==ceftriaxone 1g daily==
