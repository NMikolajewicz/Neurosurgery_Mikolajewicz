# Quick guidelines (step-wise)

**1) SBP >100**, oxygenate, normocapnia, euglycemia

* target **PaCO₂ 35–40** (brief hyperventilation only as a herniation bridge)

**2) TXA ≤3 h** if mild–moderate TBI/no major contraindication

* TXA 1 g IV over 10 min, then 1 g IV over 8 h

3) CT + neurosurgery

* Evacuate mass lesions (EDH/SDH/large contusions) with mass effect or neuro-decline.
* **Decompressive craniectomy:**
	* Avoid **early bifrontal DC** for diffuse injury (worse function).
	* Consider **last-tier DC** for refractory ICP with explicit discussion of disability trade-offs.

4) If severe TBI, place monitor/EVD as indicated; **I**

* **CP <22**, **CPP 60–70** via tiered SIBICC measures (hypertonic saline first-line)

5) Evacuate mass lesions; reserve **last-tier DC** for refractory ICP with informed consent
**6) No steroids**, **no hypothermia**; short-course seizure ppx (x7 days)
7) early DVT prophylaxis and nutrition
8) Reassess, repeat CT if change, and use **IMPACT** for counseling (not for early care limitation).

# Summary of  Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) Randomized Trials and Guidelines

* SBP > 100
	* q10 SBP drop in SBP ~ 20% increase in mortality
* Provide TXA within 3h window (CRASH-3 trial)
	* In patients with mild-to-moderate TBI, TXA significantly reduced head injury-related mortality (risk ratio 0.78; 95% CI 0.64–0.95)
	* The greatest reduction in death occurred within the first 24 hours post-injury.​
* Hypertonic saline
* Avoid steroids (CRASH-MRC trial)
	* Corticosteroid treatment increased the risk of death at 6 months compared to placebo (25.7% vs 22.3%), with a relative risk of 1.15 (p=0.0001).
* No benefit for hypothermia

## 1\. **Corticosteroid Randomization After Significant Head Injury (CRASH) Trial**

* **Citation**: Roberts I, Yates D, Sandercock P, et al. Effect of intravenous corticosteroids on death within 14 days in 10008 adults with clinically significant head injury (MRC CRASH trial): randomised placebo-controlled trial. _Lancet_. 2004;364(9442):1321-8[thelancet+1](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(04)17188-2/fulltext)
* **Follow-up Citation**: Edwards P, Arango M, Balica L, et al. Final results of MRC CRASH, a randomised placebo-controlled trial of intravenous corticosteroid in adults with head injury-outcomes at 6 months. _Lancet_. 2005;365(9475):1957-9[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15936423/)
* **Design**: Large, multicenter, international RCT (10,008 patients)
* **Purpose**: Compared 48-hour IV methylprednisolone vs. placebo in patients with head injury
* **Key Findings**:
	* Steroid use increased mortality at 2 weeks (21.1% vs 17.9%, RR 1.18, 95% CI 1.09-1.27; p=0.0001) and 6 months (25.7% vs 22.3%, RR 1.15, 95% CI 1.07-1.24; p=0.0001)[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15474134/)
	* **Recommendation**: ==Steroids are contraindicated in acute TBI==

## 2\. **CRASH-3 Trial (Tranexamic Acid in TBI)**

* **Citation**: CRASH-3 trial collaborators. Effects of tranexamic acid on death, disability, vascular occlusive events and other morbidities in patients with acute traumatic brain injury (CRASH-3): a randomised, placebo-controlled trial. _Lancet_. 2019;394(10210):1713-23[thelancet+1](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(19)32233-0/fulltext)
* **Design**: International, multicenter RCT (12,737 patients from 175 hospitals in 29 countries)
* **Purpose**: Evaluated tranexamic acid (loading dose 1g IV over 10 minutes, then 1g IV over 8 hours) vs. placebo in adults with TBI within 3 hours of injury[thelancet](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(19)32233-0/fulltext)
* **Key Findings**:
	* In patients treated within 3 hours: head injury-related death 18.5% (TXA) vs. 19.8% (placebo), RR 0.94 (95% CI 0.86-1.02)[thelancet](https://www.thelancet.com/article/S0140-6736(19)32312-8/fulltext)
	* **Significant benefit in mild-to-moderate TBI** (GCS 9-15): RR 0.78 (95% CI 0.64-0.95)[thelancet](https://www.thelancet.com/article/S0140-6736(19)32312-8/fulltext)
	* No benefit in severe TBI (GCS 3-8): RR 0.99 (95% CI 0.91-1.07)[thelancet](https://www.thelancet.com/article/S0140-6736(19)32312-8/fulltext)
* **Clinical Impact**: **First pharmacological intervention to show benefit in acute TBI setting**; ==TXA recommended for mild-to-moderate TBI when administered early==[snacc](https://www.snacc.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/SNACC-Article-of-The-Month-December-2019.pdf)

## 3\. **IMPACT Studies (International Mission for Prognosis and Analysis of Clinical Trials)**

* **Primary Citation**: Maas AIR, Steyerberg EW, Butcher I, et al. Advancing care for traumatic brain injury: findings from the IMPACT studies and the mission ahead. _Lancet Neurol_. 2013;12(12):1200-10[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3895622/)
* **Prognostic Model Citation**: Steyerberg EW, Mushkudiani N, Perel P, et al. Predicting outcome after traumatic brain injury: development and international validation of prognostic scores based on admission characteristics. _PLoS Med_. 2008;5(8):e165[journals.plos](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pmed.0050165)
* **Design**: International collaboration analyzing data from 11 large datasets (>8,500 patients) from clinical trials and observational studies[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3895622/)
* **Purpose**: Developed and validated prognostic models for 6-month outcomes in moderate-to-severe TBI[journals.plos](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pmed.0050165)
* **Key Contributions**:
	* **Three sequential models**: Core (age, GCS motor, pupil reactivity), Extended (Core + CT + hypoxia/hypotension), Lab (Extended + glucose, hemoglobin)[tbi-impact+1](http://www.tbi-impact.org/?p=impact%2Fcalc)
	* **Validated across multiple external datasets** with AUC ranging 0.70-0.90 for mortality prediction[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3895622/)
	* **Web-based calculator** available at [www.tbi-impact.org](http://www.tbi-impact.org/) for clinical use [tbi-impact+1](http://www.tbi-impact.org/?p=impact%2Fcalc)
* **Clinical Impact**: **==Gold standard for TBI prognostication==**; widely used for research stratification and clinical decision support[thieme-connect+1](https://www.thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/abstract/10.1055/s-0039-1685137)

## 4\. **DECRA Trial (Decompressive Craniectomy)**

* **Citation**: Cooper DJ, Rosenfeld JV, Murray L, et al. Decompressive craniectomy in diffuse traumatic brain injury. _N Engl J Med_. 2011;364(16):1493-502[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21434843/)
* **Design**: International multicenter RCT (155 patients)
* **Purpose**: Assessed early bifrontal decompressive craniectomy vs. standard care for diffuse TBI and refractory ICP
* **Findings**: Early craniectomy reduced ICP and ICU length of stay but was associated with **==worse functional outcomes at 6 months==** (odds ratio for worse score 1.84, 95% CI 1.05-3.24; p=0.03) compared to medical management[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21434843/)
* **Impact**: ==Cautioned against early decompressive surgery for elevated ICP in diffuse injury==

## 5\. **RESCUEicp Trial**

* **Citation**: Hutchinson PJ, Kolias AG, Timofeev IS, et al. Trial of Decompressive Craniectomy for Traumatic Intracranial Hypertension. _N Engl J Med_. 2016;375(12):1119-30[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27602507/)
* **Design**: International multicenter RCT (408 patients)
* **Purpose**: Evaluated **last-tier decompressive craniectomy** in TBI with refractory ICP (>25 mmHg for 1-12 hours)
* **Findings**: Surgery reduced mortality (26.9% vs 48.9%) but increased rates of vegetative state and severe disability; higher survival with compromised functional independence[research.ed](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/files/27917531/PURE_ID_27917478_NEJMoa1605215.pdf)
* **Clinical Interpretation**: **Informed consent and discussion with family are critical regarding ==tradeoff between survival and quality of life==**

## 6\. **EUROTHERM3235 Trial (Therapeutic Hypothermia)**

* **Citation**: Andrews PJD, Sinclair HL, Rodriguez A, et al. Hypothermia for Intracranial Hypertension after Traumatic Brain Injury. _N Engl J Med_. 2015;373(25):2403-12[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26444221/)
* **Design**: Multicenter RCT (387 patients, stopped early for safety)
* **Purpose**: Hypothermia (32-35°C) to reduce ICP in TBI patients
* **Findings**: Hypothermia did **not improve**, and potentially worsened neurological outcomes at 6 months (adjusted common odds ratio 1.53, 95% CI 1.02-2.30; p=0.04)[pure.ed](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/22098902/Hypothermia_for_Intracranial_Hypertension_after_Traumatic_Brain_Injury.pdf)
* **Guideline Integration**: ==Hypothermia is== **==not recommended==** ==for ICP control in TBI==

## 7\. **BEST TRIP Trial (ICP Monitoring in Resource-Limited Settings)**

* **Citation**: Chesnut RM, Temkin N, Carney N, et al. A trial of intracranial-pressure monitoring in traumatic brain injury. _N Engl J Med_. 2012;367(26):2471-81[neurosurgery.med.wayne+1](https://neurosurgery.med.wayne.edu/pdf/da_-_icp_monitoring_in_tbi_nejm_dec_2012.pdf)
* **Design**: 324 patients, Bolivia and Ecuador, randomized to management guided by ICP monitor vs. clinical/radiologic examination
* **Findings**: ==No difference in outcomes between ICP-guided management and clinical examination plus imaging-guided managemen==t[thebottomline](https://www.thebottomline.org.uk/summaries/icm/best-trip/)
* **Limitations**: Conducted in resource-limited settings with questions about generalizability to resource-rich environments

## 8\. **STITCH(Trauma) Trial**

* **Citation**: Gregson BA, Rowan EN, Francis R, et al. Surgical Trial In Traumatic intraCerebral Haemorrhage (STITCH): a randomised controlled trial of Early Surgery compared with Initial Conservative Treatment. _Health Technol Assess_. 2015;19(70):1-138[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26346805/)
* **Design**: International multicenter RCT (170 patients, stopped early for slow enrollment from planned 840)
* **Purpose**: Early surgical evacuation of traumatic intracerebral hematomas ≥10 mL vs. initial conservative treatment
* **Findings**==: Early surgical evacuation of trauma ICH  had significantly lower mortality at 6 months== (15% vs 33%, p=0.006) and showed trends toward better functional outcomes, though statistical significance was not reached due to early termination[ahajournals+1](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/STROKEAHA.118.022694)

## Clinical Guidelines

## **Brain Trauma Foundation (BTF) Guidelines**

* **Citation**: Carney N, Totten AM, O'Reilly C, et al. Guidelines for the Management of Severe Traumatic Brain Injury, Fourth Edition. _Neurosurgery_. 2017;80(1):6-15[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27654000/)
* **==Key Recommendations==**==:==
	* **ICP Monitoring**: For severe TBI with GCS ≤8 and abnormal head CT or two additional risk factors (age >40, SBP <90 mmHg, posturing)[globalneuro](https://globalneuro.org/uploads/files/Guidelines_for_Management_of_Severe_TBI_4th_Edition.pdf)
	* **ICP Threshold**: Maintain ICP <22 mmHg[globalneuro](https://globalneuro.org/uploads/files/Guidelines_for_Management_of_Severe_TBI_4th_Edition.pdf)
	* **CPP Management**: Minimum cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP) of 60–70 mmHg[globalneuro](https://globalneuro.org/uploads/files/Guidelines_for_Management_of_Severe_TBI_4th_Edition.pdf)
	* **Hyperosmolar Therapy**: Mannitol or hypertonic saline for acute increased ICP[globalneuro](https://globalneuro.org/uploads/files/Guidelines_for_Management_of_Severe_TBI_4th_Edition.pdf)
	* **Decompressive Craniectomy**: Reserve for refractory ICP[globalneuro](https://globalneuro.org/uploads/files/Guidelines_for_Management_of_Severe_TBI_4th_Edition.pdf)
	* **Hypothermia**: Not recommended as first-line therapy[globalneuro](https://globalneuro.org/uploads/files/Guidelines_for_Management_of_Severe_TBI_4th_Edition.pdf)
	* **Seizure Prophylaxis**: Short-term (7 days) phenytoin may be considered[globalneuro](https://globalneuro.org/uploads/files/Guidelines_for_Management_of_Severe_TBI_4th_Edition.pdf)

## **SIBICC (Seattle International Severe Traumatic Brain Injury Consensus Conference)**

* **Citation**: Hawryluk GWJ, Aguilera S, Buki A, et al. A management algorithm for patients with intracranial pressure monitoring: the Seattle International Severe Traumatic Brain Injury Consensus Conference (SIBICC). _Intensive Care Med_. 2019;45(12):1783-94[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31659383/)
* **Design**: **Algorithmic, stepwise approach** to acute intracranial hypertension management using Delphi consensus methodology with 42 international experts[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31659383/)
* **Key Features**:
	* **Three-tier algorithm** for treating elevated ICP with 18 fundamental interventions and 10 treatments not to be used[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31659383/)
	* **Tier 1**: 10 interventions including basic ICP management
	* **Tier 2**: 4 higher-risk interventions
	* **Tier 3**: 3 highest-risk interventions[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31659383/)
	* **Novel elements**: Autoregulation-based ICP treatment guidance and decision-making heatmaps[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31659383/)

## **American College of Surgeons (ACS) Guidelines**

* **Citation**: American College of Surgeons. Best Practices Guidelines: The Management of Traumatic Brain Injury. 2024[facs](https://www.facs.org/media/vgfgjpfk/best-practices-guidelines-traumatic-brain-injury.pdf)
* **Recent Updates**: Include new evidence on triage, transport, and management protocols with emphasis on preventing secondary brain injury[facs](https://www.facs.org/media/vgfgjpfk/best-practices-guidelines-traumatic-brain-injury.pdf)

## Impact and Clinical Translation

**The landmark trials have fundamentally changed TBI management by:**

* **Establishing contraindications**: CRASH trial definitively ruled out corticosteroids[thelancet+1](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(04)17188-2/fulltext)
* **Identifying first effective treatment**: CRASH-3 demonstrated tranexamic acid benefit in mild-to-moderate TBI[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31623894/)
* **Advancing prognostication**: IMPACT studies created validated tools for outcome prediction[journals.plos+1](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pmed.0050165)
* **Refining surgical indications**: DECRA and RESCUEicp trials provided nuanced understanding of decompressive craniectomy timing and patient selection[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27602507/)
* **Challenging established practices**: EUROTHERM3235 disproved routine hypothermia use[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26444221/)
* **Questioning monitoring paradigms**: BEST TRIP raised important questions about ICP monitoring in resource-limited settings[neurosurgery.med.wayne](https://neurosurgery.med.wayne.edu/pdf/da_-_icp_monitoring_in_tbi_nejm_dec_2012.pdf)

**Current evidence-based TBI care focuses on:**

* Early tranexamic acid administration in mild-to-moderate TBI within 3 hours[thelancet](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(19)32233-0/fulltext)
* IMPACT calculator-guided prognostication for clinical decision making[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3895622/)
* Aggressive prevention of secondary brain injury
* Individualized ICP and CPP management using SIBICC algorithms[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31659383/)
* Careful patient selection for surgical interventions
* Avoidance of historically used but now-disproven therapies

The integration of these trial results into contemporary guidelines represents the current standard of evidence-based severe TBI management, with CRASH-3 marking a historic breakthrough as the first successful pharmacological intervention in acute TBI care.[snacc](https://www.snacc.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/SNACC-Article-of-The-Month-December-2019.pdf)
